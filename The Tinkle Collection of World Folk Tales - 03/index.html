    The Clever Soldier

    Tsar Dolmat was furious with his beautiful daughter, Princess Lea. "That's another batch of suitors you've turned away, you foolish girl!" he yelled. "At this rate you'll remain a spinster the rest of your life. It's time I took matters into my own hands."

    The Tsar made a grand announcement: he would give his daughter in marriage to the one who could take care of a hundred hares for an entire month. "But remember," he warned all challengers, "even if one hare escapes, you'll be banished for life!"

    Just then, Boris, a poor discharged soldier, was wandering near the capital. He had found a magical cap with a message scrawled inside: "Wear me and command any animal and wish for any food."

    Boris tested it by wishing for an apple, and one immediately appeared in his hand. Realizing his luck, he rushed to the palace to accept the Tsar's challenge.

    When the hundred energetic hares were released, Boris put on his magic cap. "ATTENTION!" he yelled. "Form up! Quick march!" To everyone's utter disbelief, the hundred hares immediately fell into perfect military formation and marched out to the fields.

    Every day, Boris effortlessly marched the hares to the pasture and brought them all back safely in the evening. As the month drew to a close, the Tsar and Tsarina panicked. They couldn't bear the thought of their royal daughter marrying a common peasant, but they would be the laughing-stock of the kingdom if they broke their word.

    "I have a plan to thwart him," the Tsar whispered. He disguised himself as an old, ragged shepherd and went to the meadow. He offered Boris a handsome price for one hare.

    Boris, recognizing the Tsar beneath the disguise, decided to have some fun. "I'll let you have one, if you fetch me some water and wash my feet," Boris demanded.

    The Tsar swallowed his pride, fetched the heavy water bucket, and humiliated himself by washing the commoner's feet. Boris handed him a hare in a sack. But as the Tsar neared the palace gates, he foolishly opened the sack to check on the hare. It immediately leaped out and fled back to Boris!

    The next day, the last day of the month, the Tsarina decided to try. She disguised herself as a peasant woman.

    Boris recognized her immediately too. "I won't sell you a hare, but I'll give you one if you wash my dirty coat and make me a bowl of soup."

    Unaccustomed to hard work, the Tsarina's hands became red and raw as she scrubbed the stinking coat and cooked the soup. Boris happily ate the delicious meal and gave her a hare. The Tsarina successfully brought it back to the palace, believing she had won.

    But when the month ended, Boris returned with all one hundred hares! He had simply used his magic cap to order the Tsarina's hare back to the flock.

    "I have proved myself worthy," Boris declared. "Pah! Any shepherd could do that!" the Tsar lied, desperately trying to get out of the deal. "I want you to fill these three sacks with words. Only then will you be worthy of the Princess' hand."

    "Very well," Boris said. He opened the first sack and began speaking into it. "Blah blah... I took the hares to the pasture... And then the Tsar came there disguised as a peasant and at my command he..."

    "THAT'S ENOUGH!" the Tsar yelled, terrified his humiliation would be exposed. "The sack is full!" "But it isn't full yet," Boris grinned. "OH, YES, IT IS! If I say so."

    "You still have two more sacks," the Tsar said nervously. Boris opened the second sack. "And the next day the Tsarina came there disguised as a peasant, and she..." "STOP, STOP! THE BAG IS FULL!" the Tsarina screamed in panic. "And she made me a..." "ENOUGH!" the Tsar roared.

    "Your Majesty, I have carried out your commands," Boris smiled innocently. "Yes, yes, the Princess will be your bride, I promise that," the Tsar sighed, defeated.

    And so, the Tsar kept his word. A few days later, the clever soldier Boris and Princess Lea were married, and they lived happily ever after.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 2. The Magic Birch Tree

    In a little mountain village in Russia lived a kindly old peasant named Igor and his greedy, short-tempered wife, Irina.

    One day, Irina ordered Igor to go cut some wood from a large birch tree down the road for the fire.

    Igor trudged out with his axe, but just as he swung it at the tree, he heard a soft, pleading voice. "Please don't hurt me... spare me, and I'll give you anything you desire."

    Shocked to hear a tree talk, Igor immediately dropped his axe. "Please excuse me," he apologized. "I didn't mean to hurt you."

    He returned home empty-handed. When Irina heard his story, she was furious. "You fool! Go back at once! If it doesn't want to be cut, ask it for some twigs for the fire... otherwise tear down its branches!"

    Igor meekly went back to the birch. "My wife says we need some twigs," he asked apologetically. "Go home," the tree replied. "I'll send the twigs." When Igor returned, his yard was full of twigs and wood chips.

    But Irina was still not satisfied. "You silly old man... why didn't you ask for firewood? We'll need it in winter. Go back and ask for logs this time."

    Igor trudged back. "Please, don't be angry," he begged the tree. "She wants logs for the fireplace." "Go home, old man," the tree said kindly. "The logs are already in your shed." And they were, neatly stacked in rows.

    But Irina's greed grew. "What good is wood without food to eat? Go ask the tree for some flour for bread." Igor asked the tree, and immediately, their granary was filled with heavy sacks of corn.

    "This is only corn... it won't make us rich," Irina complained. "You must go back and ask for two chests of gold!"

    Though hesitant, Igor went back. "What does she want now, old friend?" the tree asked. "Gold," Igor mumbled, ashamed. "Two chests of gold." "Go home... the gold is already there."

    When Igor looked through the window of his hut, he saw Irina counting a massive pile of glittering gold coins. As he watched the vast wealth, greed finally entered his own heart too.

    "Now we are richer than the landlord!" Igor cheered. "Perhaps as rich as the king!" "But what if we are robbed?" Irina suddenly worried. "I have a plan. You must go back to the tree."

    Igor rushed back to the tree one last time. "Good friend, we need your help to keep our gold safe," he said greedily. "We want you to make us look ugly and ferocious, so that everyone will be frightened and keep away from us. That way our wealth will be safe."

    The tree paused. "Are you sure, old man... is that what you really want? To keep people away? To be alone?" "YES," Igor insisted. "Go. Your wish has been granted," the tree sighed.

    When Igor returned home, he saw a horrifying creature where his wife used to be. "She looks so... Aah, what's happening to me!" Igor cried out as pain shot through his body. "OWW... GROWL!!"

    The magic birch tree had granted their wish perfectly. To make them truly ugly and ferocious enough to scare everyone away, it had permanently turned the greedy old couple into bears!

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 3. Forced Justice

    Once upon a time in Russia, there lived a poor, hard-working farmer. One day, he chopped far too much firewood in the forest to carry back himself. He reluctantly went to his wealthy, arrogant neighbor to borrow a horse.

    "All right. You can take the horse this once," the rich neighbor scoffed. "But don't come pestering me again."

    As the poor farmer led the horse away, he realized he had forgotten to borrow a horse-collar. Terrified of his neighbor's sharp tongue, he didn't dare go back. Instead, he foolishly tied the heavy cart directly to the horse's tail!

    When he shouted "Giddap!", the horse lunged forward with all its might. SNAP! The poor horse's tail tore right off!

    The rich farmer was absolutely furious. Refusing to accept payment for the accident, he dragged the poor farmer to the local judge. "I don't want your money," he yelled. "I'll see that you're punished heavily!"

    The poor farmer received the court summons with dread. "I know this judge," he worried. "He's famously corrupt and he'll fine me heavily, I'm sure of it!"

    The next day, as the poor farmer walked across a high bridge on his way to the courthouse, he accidentally slipped and fell over the edge! CRASH! He plummeted off the bridge and landed squarely on an old man sleeping in a cart passing below.

    The injured cartman leaped up in a rage. "You scoundrel! Tried to murder me, eh? Come with me to the court!" "But it was an accident!" the poor farmer protested.

    Knowing his fate was sealed, the poor farmer picked up a heavy, jagged rock and slipped it into his pocket. "They'll cast me into prison for life or even hang me," he thought desperately. "In any case, if the corrupt judge rules unfairly, I'll knock him out with this rock! I might as well be hanged for a sheep as for a lamb."

    In the courtroom, the rich farmer and the cartman began screaming their complaints loudly.

    The poor farmer stood silently, but kept pointing meaningfully to the heavy bulge in his pocket, glaring at the judge.

    The corrupt judge noticed the gesture. "Eh! Why does that peasant keep pointing to his pocket?" the judge wondered greedily. "Could it be a bag of gold coins that he has there? Perhaps he wants to bribe me. I wouldn't mind even if they were silver! I must not judge this case hastily!"

    Hoping to win the "bribe," the judge slammed his gavel down. "Enough! I have come to a decision!"

    He turned to the rich farmer. "Let the poor farmer keep the tailless horse... until it grows a new tail!" "Eh! But I need it to plough my fields now!" the rich man complained.

    The judge ignored him and turned to the angry cartman. "And as for you... to make things fair, you must leap off the exact same bridge and try to land directly on the poor farmer's head!" "But... but... how can I be sure I'll land on his head?" the terrified cartman stammered. "I'll be killed if I land on the ground!" "That's your problem, not mine!" the judge declared. "The case is closed!"

    Outside the court, the desperate rich farmer gave the poor man a hundred coins just to get his horse back immediately. The terrified cartman also gave the poor man a hundred coins to cancel the "fair" punishment of jumping off the bridge.

    The corrupt judge happily called the newly-rich poor farmer aside. "Now, let me see the bribe you have in your pocket!" he whispered eagerly.

    The farmer reached in and pulled it out. "Sure. A rock!" "A rock?" the judge gasped. "Yes, a rock!" the farmer glared. "If you had ruled unfairly against me, I would have flung this rock straight at your head!"

    The terrified judge realized how close he had come to a cracked skull. "It's a good thing I judged the way I did, or I would not be alive now!" he stammered, hurriedly dismissing the man.

    The poor farmer walked home with two hundred coins in his pockets, chuckling to himself. "I've had justice," he smiled, "but it's a pity I had to force it out of them!"

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 4. The Magic Horse

    A Russian farmer had a terrible problem: somebody was trampling upon his wheatfields at night. Desperate, he ordered his eldest son to stand guard.

    The eldest son went to the field, but thinking no culprit would come, he fell fast asleep. The next morning, the wheat was ruined again. He lied to his father, claiming he had stayed awake but seen nothing.

    The next night, the farmer sent his second son. He too shirked his duty and fell asleep. The wheat was trampled again.

    Finally, it was Leo's turn, the youngest son, whom his brothers often called a fool.

    Leo stayed wide awake. Around midnight, the ground began to shake, and a strange, glowing light approached. It was a magnificent, magical horse!

    Brave Leo leaped out, threw a rope over the horse's head, and jumped onto its back. "Got you!" he shouted.

    "Please let me go," the horse surprisingly spoke. "I'll help you when you're in need." "If I let you go, where will I find you?" Leo asked wisely. "Go to an open space, whistle through your fingers three times, and call out: Chestnut-grey, hear and obey! and I'll come at once."

    Leo agreed but made the horse promise to never eat or trample his father's wheat again.

    A few days later, the Tsar made a grand announcement: his beautiful daughter, Princess Anna, would sit at the window of her incredibly high tower. Any man who could ride his steed, leap high enough to reach the window, and take the precious ring from her finger would win her hand in marriage.

    Leo's arrogant brothers laughed at him when he asked to borrow their old stable horse to try his luck. They refused to help him.

    But Leo remembered his magical friend. He went to an open space and called out, "Chestnut-grey, hear and obey!"

    The magnificent horse instantly appeared in a flash of glowing light. "I'll help you," the horse promised. "But first we must turn you into a noble youth." He told Leo to close his eyes and say a magic phrase. Instantly, Leo's ragged clothes transformed into those of a handsome prince.

    Leo rode Chestnut-grey to the palace, where all the noblemen were failing miserably to jump even halfway up the incredibly high tower.

    Suddenly, Leo and Chestnut-grey charged. With a mighty leap, the magical horse soared effortlessly up the side of the tower! But just as Leo reached for the ring, a cold wind made the Princess cough and cover her mouth, causing him to miss.

    They circled around and leaped a second time. This time, Leo successfully slipped the glowing ring off her finger!

    To everyone's shock, the mysterious rider didn't stay to claim his prize; he simply rode away. Leo later thanked Chestnut-grey and returned to his normal, ragged self, hiding his bandaged hand where he kept the ring.

    Three days later, the furious Tsar ordered every single person in the land to attend a grand banquet, under pain of death. Leo's brothers were forced to take him along.

    At the banquet, Princess Anna personally poured wine for every guest, searching for her mysterious champion. When she came to Leo, she noticed his bandaged hand.

    "What happened to your hand?" she asked kindly. "I... er... I hurt myself," Leo stammered. "A thorn..." "Take the cloth off and show me," she insisted gently.

    Reluctantly, Leo removed the bandage, revealing the brilliant, glowing ring!

    "The ring! So, you're the one!" Princess Anna smiled, immediately leading him to the Tsar.

    The Tsar was overjoyed. Leo's brothers, suddenly realizing the "fool" was now royalty, shamelessly tried to claim they had always supported him. The Tsar angrily told them to shut up and learn to be nicer to people.

    A few days later, Leo and Princess Anna celebrated their royal wedding, and they lived happily ever after.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 5. The Firebird

    Long ago in Russia, a Tsar owned a magical tree that bore golden apples. When the apples began disappearing every night, the Tsar ordered his sons to stand guard and catch the thief.

    The two eldest sons, Peter and Vassily, fell fast asleep on their watch. But the youngest son, Ivan, bravely stayed awake. Suddenly, a blinding, glowing light appeared. It was the magnificent, magical Firebird! Ivan grabbed at it, but only managed to pull out a single, glowing feather as it flew away.

    "Whoever gets me the Firebird shall get half my kingdom!" the Tsar declared after seeing the feather. All three brothers immediately set out on a quest to find it.

    After days of travel, Ivan found a stone with an ominous inscription: 1. Go ahead and you will be hungry. 2. Go left and you will die. 3. Go right and you will lose your horse.

    Ivan bravely chose to go right. Almost immediately, a massive wolf leaped out of the woods and devoured his horse!

    "You frightened my horse!" Ivan cried in dismay. "Never mind," the beast replied gently. "I, Greywolf, will carry you wherever you wish to go."

    When Ivan explained his quest, Greywolf revealed that the Firebird belonged to Tsar Afron. Greywolf carried Ivan to the castle and gave him strict instructions: "Catch the bird only when it is out of its cage. On no account must you touch the cage!"

    But when Ivan saw the Firebird, he was so mesmerized that he grabbed the cage itself. An alarm instantly sounded, and the guards captured him.

    Tsar Afron, impressed by Ivan's courage, made a deal: "Get me the Horse with the Golden Mane from Tsar Kusman, and I will give you the Firebird."

    Greywolf carried Ivan to Tsar Kusman's castle. Again, he gave a strict warning: "The horse will follow anyone who strokes its mane. On no account must you touch its bridle."

    But Ivan, excited by the magnificent horse, foolishly grabbed the bridle. The horse loudly neighed, and Ivan was captured again.

    Tsar Kusman also made a deal: "Bring me Princess Helena, the daughter of Tsar Dolmat. If you succeed, your reward will be the Horse with the Golden Mane!"

    Greywolf carried Ivan to Tsar Dolmat's castle. Knowing Ivan's track record, Greywolf told him to stay outside. The massive wolf sneaked in, grabbed the terrified Princess, and carried her out to Ivan.

    As they traveled back, Ivan and Princess Helena fell deeply in love. Ivan couldn't bear to give her to Tsar Kusman.

    Greywolf, seeing their love, used his magic to transform himself into an exact replica of Princess Helena! Ivan presented the "Princess" to Tsar Kusman, received the Horse with the Golden Mane, and rode away with the real Helena.

    As soon as Tsar Kusman approached his new bride, Greywolf transformed back into a wolf, terrifying the Tsar before escaping to rejoin Ivan!

    Next, they reached Tsar Afron's castle. Ivan didn't want to part with the magnificent horse either. So, Greywolf transformed himself into the Horse with the Golden Mane. Ivan traded the "horse" for the Firebird and rode away with Helena on the real horse.

    As soon as Tsar Afron mounted his new prize, Greywolf transformed back into a wolf! The Tsar fell off in shock, and Greywolf escaped to join Ivan once more.

    Having successfully helped Ivan secure the Firebird, the Horse with the Golden Mane, and Princess Helena, Greywolf bid his friend a fond farewell.

    Exhausted, Ivan and Helena fell asleep on their journey home. Unfortunately, Ivan's wicked older brothers stumbled upon them. Jealous of Ivan's prizes, they killed him, stole the treasures and the Princess, and warned her never to tell the truth.

    Sometime later, Greywolf found Ivan's body and magically revived him, though Ivan had lost his memory. When Greywolf finally found him wandering, he restored his memory and rushed him back to the capital.

    They arrived just in time to stop Vassily from forcibly marrying Princess Helena. Ivan revealed the brothers' treachery to the Tsar.

    Furious, the Tsar banished Peter and Vassily forever. He proudly named Ivan his heir and happily agreed to his marriage to Princess Helena.

    Ivan called out to thank his truest friend, but Greywolf had already returned to the deep woods, ready to help other travelers in need.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 6. Young Frost Learns a Lesson

    Young Frost, the son of the powerful Old Frost, was a terrible braggart who thought the world of himself.

    "Heh heh! There is no one in the world as powerful as me," he boasted as he floated as a chilly spirit over the snow. Spotting a fat, well-fed nobleman bundled up in a carriage, Young Frost decided to play a prank.

    "You can wrap yourself in furs, my friend, but it won't save you... hee hee," Young Frost sneered. He unleashed a terrifying, freezing gust of wind onto the carriage.

    "Y-eow! It's c... c... cold!" the nobleman shivered. "It's f... f... freezing. Co... co... coachman, drive faster!"

    After thoroughly chilling the nobleman to the bone, Young Frost proudly flew back to his father. "Yahoo! Look at me, father! I'm so strong!" he yelled. "I just froze a big fat nobleman. You could never do it, but I did it! I did it!"

    Old Frost sighed. "Don't brag so much, my son. Do you see that skinny woodcutter over there? Go and try to freeze him!"

    "Hah! I'll freeze him in a flash, see if I don't!" Young Frost laughed, flying toward the thin man chopping wood in the forest.

    Young Frost began to harass the woodcutter. He flew up and tickled his nose with an icy blast, then flew down and nipped at his feet.

    "Brr... it's cold," the woodcutter noticed. "I'll chop faster to warm up."

    The harder Young Frost tried to freeze him—creeping under his collar and blasting his neck with freezing wind—the faster the woodcutter swung his axe, generating more and more body heat.

    "Phew! I'm beginning to feel hot," the woodcutter panted. "Let me take off my gloves."

    Seeing the gloves on the snow, Young Frost had a nasty idea. "This is my chance! I'll hide in his gloves. Won't he be surprised when he puts them on!" He magically slipped inside the gloves, freezing them solid.

    Soon, the woodcutter finished his work. "Well, I've done enough for today. I'll be off now." He picked up his gloves and frowned. "Eh! My gloves have become as hard as steel with the cold."

    "And when you put them on, they'll freeze your hands... hee hee hee," Young Frost giggled from inside.

    "But I'll soften them up!" the woodcutter grunted. He picked up his heavy axe handle and began viciously pounding the frozen gloves against a log. THUMP! THUMP!

    Trapped inside the gloves, Young Frost was brutally beaten black and blue by the heavy blows!

    "Phew! I'm off! He's a terror, this fellow!" Young Frost cried, zooming out of the gloves and flying back to his father as fast as he could.

    "What's the matter, son?" Old Frost chuckled. "You look all shaken up!" "Ooh! Aah!" Young Frost groaned, covered in bruises. "I'm completely winded, trying to freeze that woodcutter."

    "I hope this will cure you of your habit of boasting," his father smiled. "Believe me, son. Nothing good can ever come out of bragging."

    Young Frost did not answer. He had finally learned his lesson the hard way.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 7. The Flying Ship

    Once upon a time in Russia, an old peasant lived with his wife and three sons. The two older sons were considered smart and clever, but the youngest, Ivan, was considered slow and dull.

    One day, the Tsar's crier made an amazing announcement: "The Tsar will marry his daughter to the man who will bring him a flying ship!"

    The two older brothers immediately set off to try their luck. Their mother lovingly packed them fine white rolls, sweets, and a large bottle of wine for their journey.

    "Mother, can I go too?" Ivan asked eagerly. "Don't be stupid! The wolves will eat you up," she scolded. But Ivan insisted until she finally gave in. To humor him, she gave him a meager bundle of dry rusks and a flask of water, sure he would turn back in a day or two.

    But Ivan was earnest. As he walked, he met an old man who asked where he was going. "The Tsar has promised to marry his daughter to the man who will bring him a flying ship!" Ivan explained. "Do you know where you can get a flying ship?" the old man asked. "No! But I am going because everyone else is doing so," Ivan admitted cheerfully.

    The old man asked to share his food. Ivan gladly offered his poor fare, but when he opened his bundle, the dry rusks had miraculously turned into delicious cakes and sweet-meats!

    "See! God has not forsaken you," the old man smiled after they ate a hearty meal. "Now go to the forest. Approach the first tree you see, cross yourself thrice, and strike the tree with an axe. Then lie down and sleep. When you wake up, you will find a splendid ship that will fly you anywhere you like. But you must take on board every wayfarer you meet."

    Ivan followed the instructions exactly. When he woke up, a wonderful flying ship was waiting for him!

    As Ivan sailed the skies, he picked up a series of extraordinary passengers, just as the old man commanded: 1. Sharp Ears: A man who could hear what was happening in the underworld. 2. Giant Steps: A man who kept one leg tied to his chest because, if untied, he could cross the world in a single stride. 3. Bullseye: A man who could accurately shoot a target a thousand versts away. 4. Gorger: A man carrying a massive sack of bread who claimed it was just a mouthful for him. 5. Bibber: A thirsty man who could drink an entire lake in one gulp. 6. The General: A man carrying a magical bundle of twigs that could instantly turn into a massive army. 7. Cool Cat: A man carrying magical straw that, when spread on hot ground, instantly covered everything in ice and snow.

    When Ivan's flying ship landed at the palace, the Tsar was dismayed. He couldn't marry his precious daughter to a ragged peasant! His minister suggested setting impossible tasks to get rid of them.

    "Fetch me a flask of the Water of Life before I finish my breakfast," the Tsar commanded.

    Giant Steps untied his leg and fetched the water in a trice. But on his way back, he decided to rest near a windmill on the other side of the earth and accidentally fell fast asleep!

    As the Tsar's breakfast neared its end, Ivan panicked. Sharp Ears pressed his ear to the ground and heard Giant Steps snoring. Bullseye immediately fired his gun at the distant windmill. The BANG woke Giant Steps, who rushed back and delivered the water just as the Tsar was finishing his meal.

    Furious, the Tsar set another task: "Consume a thousand loaves of bread and fifty barrels of wine in one hour!" Gorger and Bibber happily polished off the entire feast in just ten minutes, complaining it was barely a light snack.

    Desperate, the cunning minister lured Ivan and his crew into an iron bath-house that had been heated until it glowed red-hot, planning to burn them alive. But Cool Cat went in first and spread his magical straw. The searing bath-house instantly turned ice-cold, and the crew comfortably waited near the stove to keep warm.

    The next morning, the shocked minister set one final, impossible condition: "You must come to the wedding ceremony with a complete battalion of soldiers!"

    Ivan thought he was finally defeated, but The General smiled. He spread his magical twigs in the nearby woods, and a massive, perfectly drilled army sprang up!

    Seeing the instant army, the Tsar and his minister finally gave in. "This man is indeed extraordinary!" the Tsar realized. "He can do the impossible. He could do wonders for our kingdom."

    And so, Ivan the "fool" married the Princess amid great pomp and ceremony. With his incredible flying ship and his wonderful, loyal friends, he lived happily ever after.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 8. The Greedy Fisherman

    Once in Russia, there lived two brothers. The older brother was very rich and arrogant, while the younger brother was very poor.

    One day, the younger brother's wife cried, "There's nothing in the house for the children to eat. What shall we do?" "I'll take the boat out," he promised. "I may catch some fish."

    The poor man fished the whole day but caught absolutely nothing. "How can I go home empty-handed?" he worried as evening approached. "I'll try my luck one last time."

    When he pulled up his net, he found a large, magnificent fish with silver scales. "Please let me go!" the magical fish suddenly spoke. The fisherman was shocked. "But what about my children? They're hungry."

    "Put your hand into my mouth, reach down into my throat and pull out the ring that you will find there," the fish instructed.

    The fisherman did as he was told and pulled out a simple ring. "Now throw the ring down," the fish said.

    When the ring hit the bottom of the boat, it instantly changed into a massive pile of gold coins! THUMP! "Take them," the fish smiled. "Now your family will never be hungry again."

    The grateful man thanked the fish, gently lowered it back into the water, and rushed home with his shirt bulging with gold. "Wife, we're rich!" he cheered.

    It was not long before news of the younger brother's sudden prosperity reached the rich, older brother. "How did he become so rich, so quickly?" his wife asked. "Why don't you go and ask him?"

    The elder brother immediately went to the younger brother's house, pretending to care. "How I missed you! So many times I thought of visiting you, but you know how busy I am." After a fine dinner, he asked, "Tell me, brother, how did you acquire so much wealth so quickly?"

    The honest younger brother innocently told him everything about the magic fish and the ring that turned into gold coins.

    Before he even finished the story, the greedy elder brother rushed out. He bought a boat and a fishing net and went fishing the very next day.

    He fished all day but caught nothing. On his last try, however, he caught a fish. It was the exact same magical fish with silver scales that his brother had caught!

    "Please let me go," the fish asked. "I will. If you give me the ring you gave my brother," the greedy man demanded. "Your brother was poor," the fish replied. "You are already rich." "I want to be richer than him!" the man yelled. "Now give me the ring or I'll take you home and fry you. Open your mouth!"

    The greedy man forced his arm deep into the fish's mouth, groping wildly for the ring.

    Suddenly— CHOMP! "AAAAH!" the man screamed in agony.

    The powerful fish bit his arm clean off! It then leaped out of the net and swam away into the deep water. And the greedy man who had gone out in search of gold had a very hard time rowing home with only one arm.

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 9. A Story of Fools

    Igor, a Russian woodcutter, lived with his simple-minded wife, Irina, near a forest. One chilly day, before leaving, Igor told her, "Irina! Watch the fire till I return."

    When he returned, the room was freezing cold. "Irina! This room is cold. Why didn't you watch the fire like I told you?" he scolded. "But I did, Igor," she replied innocently. "I watched it till it died."

    "You foolish woman!" Igor yelled, stomping out. "Now light the fire again while I go and cut some wood."

    A trickster passing by witnessed this exchange. "Foolish, is she? Then I'll trick her into parting with that warm coat hanging on the peg," he plotted.

    The cheat knocked on her door. "Brr... It's a cold day. May I warm myself by your fire, ma'am?" "Of course, you can. Where have you come from, stranger?" "From heaven," the cheat lied smoothly. "Really?" Irina gasped. "My father died a few months ago. I'm sure he is in heaven too. How is he?" "He is well. But it is very cold in heaven. We are neighbors, and he often wishes for a warm coat." "My poor father..." Irina cried. "Will you take my husband's coat and give it to him?" "Of course I will," the cheat chuckled, taking Igor's coat and leaving.

    When Igor returned and heard the ridiculous story, he threw his hands up in despair. "Bah! I'm leaving home. I shall only return if I find a greater fool than you, Irina!"

    Igor tracked the cheat's footprints to a farmhouse. He saw the cheat go inside, leaving his wife tending to some pigs outside. Igor walked up to the largest pig and bowed. "Greetings, O great pig!"

    The cheat's wife stared at him. "What are you doing?" "This pig resembles my wife's long-lost brother," Igor said solemnly. "Who knows, he might have changed into this pig... Can I take the pig home so that my wife can talk to him?"

    The woman giggled, thinking Igor was a complete idiot. "Of course, you can take your brother-in-law home." "Thank you. But it is bitterly cold. My wife will feel sorry to see her brother with no warm clothing." "Here! Cover your dear brother-in-law with this coat," the woman offered generously, handing over Igor's own stolen coat! "And why don't you take him in our horse carriage? My dear wife will be so pleased."

    When the cheat came out and heard what his wife had done, he was furious. "You foolish woman! You have given him the coat, the pig, and the horse carriage. I am going to get them back, and I shan't come back till I find a greater fool than you!"

    The cheat ran into the forest, hoping to catch Igor. Meanwhile, Igor had hidden the carriage, horse, and pig, and was sitting on the ground next to a hat.

    "Puff! Pant! Gasp!" the cheat arrived. "Have you seen a man passing this way in a carriage with a pig inside?" "He went that way," Igor pointed. "It should take me half an hour to catch up with him, champion runner though I am." "Champion runner, are you? Then will you catch him for me?" the cheat begged. "Catch him and one of the three items shall be yours." "But I can't leave this hat here," Igor protested. "Take it with you." "Oh no! I can't do that. My master has trapped a golden bird under it and has warned me not to let it go. Not even if someone gave me a gold coin."

    Hearing about a golden bird, the greedy cheat offered, "Here! Take five gold coins and be off."

    "I can go home now!" Igor smiled to himself, taking the coins. "I've found a greater fool than Irina." He raced home with the pig, the coat, the carriage, and the five gold coins.

    Meanwhile, the cheat sat carefully guarding the hat. "I'll take the golden bird and put a stone in its place," he plotted. He lifted the hat carefully. "Ulp! There's no bird here. I've been tricked!"

    Dejectedly, the cheat went home to his wife. "You are back! So you did find a greater fool than me," she said. "I did," he sighed. "It is myself."

    The Tinkle Collection of World Folk Tales - 03 - 10. Ninochka and the Swan Geese

    Ten-year-old Ninochka lived with her baby brother, Sasha, and her parents in a village in Russia.

    One day, her parents told her, "Ninochka, your father and I are going to work. Take good care of Sasha and I'll buy you a new kerchief." Ninochka agreed, but no sooner had her parents left than she put Sasha in his cradle and selfishly ran out to play with her friends.

    Suddenly, a flock of wicked Swan Geese swooped down through the open window, grabbed little Sasha, and carried him away!

    When Ninochka came home and found an empty cradle and a large feather, she realized the terrible truth. The elders had often warned about the wicked Swan Geese who stole children. She immediately set out to find him.

    Following the distant specks in the sky, she ran up a hill until she reached a magical apple tree. "Apple tree, tell me where the Swan Geese have flown," she asked. "Not until you've eaten one of my wild apples," the tree replied. "But at home we don't even eat garden apples!" Ninochka scoffed arrogantly. "Then I cannot tell you," the tree said.

    Ninochka ran on until she reached a magical Milk River with Jelly Banks. "Milk river, tell me where the Swan Geese have flown," she demanded. "Eat some of my jelly with milk, then I'll tell you." "I cannot do that. At home we don't even eat jelly with cream!" she retorted proudly. "Then I cannot tell you."

    As the sun began to set, she finally saw a strange cottage spinning round and round in circles. Inside, a little old lady was spinning yarn, and little Sasha was playing with golden apples nearby. Ninochka knew this was no ordinary old lady. She bravely knocked on the door and offered to help spin yarn to warm herself up.

    A magical mouse scurried up to her. "Little girl, give me some bread and I will tell you something," it squeaked. When Ninochka gave it bread, the mouse warned her, "That is Baba Yaga, the witch. She has gone to light a fire. She will wash you, steam you and eat you! Make haste. I will spin the yarn for you."

    Terrified, Ninochka quickly tied Sasha to her back and fled. When Baba Yaga realized she had been tricked, she screamed and sent the wicked Swan Geese to bring them back.

    Ninochka saw the terrifying flock approaching just as she reached the Milk River. "Milk river, hide me!" she cried. "You must first eat some of my plain fruit jelly," the river replied. Having learned her lesson, Ninochka quickly ate the jelly, and the river hid them safely under its banks until the geese flew past.

    She ran on, but the geese spotted her again. "Apple tree, please hide me!" she begged as she reached the hill. "Eat one of my wild apples and I will hide you." Ninochka quickly ate the wild apple, and the tree kindly hid them deep within its thick branches until the geese finally gave up and flew away.

    When the coast was clear, Ninochka ran the rest of the way, reaching home just in time to meet her returning parents.

    "You have looked after Sasha well," her mother smiled, handing her the new kerchief. "Phew! That was a close shave," Ninochka thought to herself, hugging her baby brother tightly. "I will never leave Sasha alone again."
