# The Magic Chant - 1. The Magic Chant

A wise pandit and his pupil were once passing through a dense forest when they were ambushed by a gang of ruthless robbers. 

"We have no money. Please let us go," the pandit pleaded. 
"Ha! Ha! You cannot deceive us," the robber chief laughed. "Your pupil can go to your house and bring us a thousand gold coins. We will keep you tied to this tree until he returns."

As the pupil prepared to leave, he whispered a warning to his master. "Master, I'll come back as soon as possible. Please do not tell these thugs that you know the *Vaidarbha mantra*!" 

The *Vaidarbha mantra* was a powerful, secret chant. If uttered when the stars were in a specific, favourable position, it would magically produce a shower of precious gems from the sky. 

That night, the pandit looked up at the sky and realized the planets were perfectly aligned. Desperate to be free, he ignored his pupil's warning and called out to the robber chief. 

"If I start a shower of gems, will you let me go?" the pandit offered. 
The chief didn't believe him at first, but agreed to let the pandit bathe and recite the chant. To the robbers' absolute amazement, a shower of sparkling jewels rained down from the sky! 

The robbers eagerly gathered the gems and kept their promise, setting the pandit free. 

However, before they could leave the forest, they were surrounded by a second, larger gang of robbers! 

"Give us your gems!" the second gang demanded. 
The first gang pointed to the pandit. "He's invaluable! He knows a magic chant that will bring down a shower of jewels. That's how we got ours. Take him and let us go!"

The second gang agreed, letting the first gang leave with their loot. They turned to the pandit and demanded, "Produce a shower of gems for us too!"

"I... I cannot," the pandit stammered, terrified. "The chant is effective only at a specific time. The auspicious moment will not occur again for another year."

"Liar! Scoundrel!" the robbers roared, and beat the poor pandit to death in their anger. "If only I had listened to my pupil's advice!" were the pandit's last, regretful thoughts. 

Realizing they had lost their chance at magic gems, the furious second gang chased after the first gang to steal their jewels. A fierce, bloody battle ensued. The second gang slaughtered the first gang, but then greed took over, and they began fighting amongst themselves for the treasure. 

Soon, only two robbers remained alive. Exhausted, they agreed to share the booty and hid it in a hollow tree. One robber stayed to guard the treasure while the other went to a nearby village to buy food. 

But greed poisoned both their minds. The robber guarding the treasure hid behind a rock, planning to ambush and kill his partner when he returned. Meanwhile, the robber buying food ate his fill and then sprinkled deadly poison over the rest of the food, planning to kill his companion and keep all the gems. 

When the second robber returned with the food, the first robber jumped out and killed him instantly. Then, hungry from the fighting, he greedily devoured the poisoned food and died in agony moments later. 

Two days later, the pupil returned to the forest with the ransom money, only to find the area littered with corpses and the cursed treasure hidden in the tree. 

"My guru used the chant for the benefit of undeserving people!" the pupil sighed sadly. "Greed destroyed them all. If only he had heeded my advice!"


# The Magic Chant - 2. The Drummer

A skilled drummer and his young son once travelled to the great city of Varanasi to play their drums at a lively festival. 

They gave a wonderful performance, and the delighted audience rewarded them generously with money, precious jewels, and fine new clothes. 

On their journey back home through a dense forest, the young boy was overjoyed with their new wealth. Feeling on top of the world, he began beating his drum loudly and cheerfully as they walked. 

"Don't make such a din, son!" his father warned anxiously. "This forest is full of robbers. You will attract them!"

"Don't worry, father! I'll play a fierce martial beat to frighten them off!" the boy replied confidently, drumming even louder. 

Deep in the jungle, a gang of thieves heard the loud, rhythmic drumming. 
"It appears a royal party is hunting in the forest," the thieves muttered, deciding to stay hidden. 

After a while, the boy stopped drumming. "Enough, son!" his father sighed in relief. "Don't do it again! A martial beat should only be played once to sound like a passing royal army."

But the boy was too proud and didn't listen. "Oh, father, you worry too much! I'll scare the thieves good and proper!" he laughed, and began beating the drum loudly once more. 

When the thieves heard the exact same drumbeats echoing through the forest again and again, they realized their mistake. 

"That is no royal party!" the robber chief chuckled. "It is just somebody who has made a lot of money and is feeling foolishly happy! Let's attack him!"

The thieves rushed out of the bushes and easily surrounded the drummer and his son. 

"Stop! Don't move! Give us all you have!" the thieves demanded, threatening them with axes. 

The poor drummer and his son could do nothing but watch helplessly as the thieves stripped them of their hard-earned money, their new clothes, and their precious jewels. 

As the thieves ran off with all their loot, the father turned to his foolish son. "I told you to stop, didn't I? But you wouldn't listen. In your pride, you foolishly invited your own doom by playing at the wrong time!"

"I have learnt my lesson now, father!" the boy wept, realizing that there is a time and a place for everything.


# The Magic Chant - 3. The Sadhu and the Ram

One day, there was great excitement in Varanasi. A large crowd of people had gathered to watch a fierce ram-fight. 

A sadhu, dressed in holy robes, happened to pass by. Curious about the commotion, he pushed his way through the cheering crowd to see what was going on. 

In the center of the ring, he saw two rams locked in combat. One of the rams, preparing to charge with maximum force, began stepping backward, lowering its head. 

The foolish sadhu, full of pride and vanity, completely misunderstood the animal's actions. 

"He is stepping back! He is bowing before me!" the sadhu thought delightedly. He stepped forward and spread his arms. "O, noble beast! Only you, in this whole crowd of people, know a great soul when you see one!"

A man in the crowd realized the danger and called out a warning. "Be careful! He is not bowing before you...! He is preparing to attack! Please step back!"

But the vain sadhu refused to listen. "No, I won't! The poor animal wants me to bless him!" he declared stubbornly. 

A moment later, the ram charged forward with incredible speed and slammed its hard horns directly into the sadhu, tossing him through the air. 

"A-A-H!" the sadhu screamed as he crashed to the ground, bruised and battered. 

As the crowd rushed to help him up, the sadhu groaned in realization. "I have invited this agony on myself! I was foolish and vain! Now I have paid the price for my pride!"


# The Magic Chant - 4. The Day the Earth Broke Into Two

One day, a nervous little rabbit was relaxing under a bilva tree, resting on a bed of dry palm leaves. 

As he lay there, a strange, terrifying thought struck him. "I wonder what will happen if the earth breaks into two?" he worried. 

Just at that exact moment, a heavy bilva fruit fell from the branches above and landed on the dry leaves with a loud THUD! 

The terrified rabbit jumped up in alarm. "Wh... what is that? Is the earth really going to break? I must run away to a safe place!"

He bolted through the forest as fast as his legs could carry him. Another rabbit saw him sprinting and asked, "What's the matter? Where are you going?"

"Don't ask me, brother. Just run for your life! The earth is about to break!" the first rabbit cried. 

Believing him, the second rabbit began to run too. Soon, a whole horde of frightened rabbits was racing through the jungle. 

A deer saw the panic and asked what was wrong. "The earth is breaking!" the rabbits yelled. The deer joined the stampede. 

Soon, pigs, buffaloes, tigers, and even elephants joined the mad rush, blindly believing the rumour that the world was ending. 

A wise old lion saw the panic-stricken troupe fleeing through the forest. "Hmm... but that is impossible! The earth isn't breaking," he thought. 

He climbed to the top of a hill and let out a mighty, terrifying roar: GR-GR-GR! 

The animals immediately froze in their tracks. "Why are you running? What is the matter?" the lion demanded. 

"The earth is breaking, O mighty one!" the animals replied in unison. 
"Who saw this happen?" the lion asked logically. 

The elephant said the panther reported it; the panther blamed the tiger, who blamed the deer, until finally, the rabbits pushed the first little rabbit forward. 

"Did you really see the earth breaking, little one?" the lion asked gently. 
"Yes, my lord. I saw it with my own eyes. It was near my burrow under a bilva tree in a palm grove. I heard a deafening noise and fled," the rabbit squeaked. 

The lion smiled knowingly. "A palm grove... a bilva tree... Hmm.... I am sure he has made a mistake. I'll go with the rabbit and find out what really happened. Wait here till I come back."

The lion carried the nervous rabbit back to the bilva tree. He looked closely at the ground and spotted the fallen bilva fruit resting on the dry palm leaves. 

"Ah! Just as I thought!" the lion deduced. "The sound of the heavy fruit falling on the dry leaves frightened the rabbit, and he thought that the earth was breaking."

Returning to the waiting animals, the lion told them what he had discovered. 

"Friends, you did not check if this was true," the wise lion scolded them gently. "Blindly relying on a false rumour, you would have paid a heavy price today."


# The Magic Chant - 5. The Foolish Tree

Long ago, two large trees lived in peace and friendship in a dense forest full of wild beasts. Lions and tigers roamed freely and preyed on other animals, leaving carcasses behind and causing a foul stench. 

"Friend, these animals are such a nuisance! I feel suffocated by the smell," one tree complained to the other. 

"Yes, it is becoming difficult to live here," the second tree agreed. 

"I can't bear it any longer! I'll drive these animals away," the first tree declared. 

"Oh, no! Please don't!" his wise friend warned. "The wild animals protect us, and keep men away! You don't know what you are doing!"

"But just imagine how pure and pleasant the air will be! I'll do it! I'll drive them away!" the foolish tree insisted. 

One night, a strong breeze began to blow. "Here is my chance!" the foolish tree thought. He shook himself vigorously, making a terrifying, loud noise: SHR-SHR-SHR! 

The animals sleeping beneath him woke up in a panic. "Look at that tree! How it is swaying!" the tigers cried. 
"The tree is possessed! Let's run away!" the lions roared. 

Terrified of the possessed tree, all the wild animals fled the forest that very night. 

The next morning, the foolish tree was delighted. "At last, I've got rid of those animals! Already, the air smells fresher!"

Many days passed in quiet peace. But then, one afternoon, a cowherd passed that way, chasing after a stray calf. 

"Come back, you naughty one! The lion will eat you up!" the boy yelled. However, as he ventured deeper into the forest, he noticed something strange. 

"I hope a tiger doesn't pounce on me!" he thought, but then he realized, "But there seem to be no tigers here! And no animal footprints anywhere around!"

The boy went home and told his family about his discovery. The men returned the next day to investigate. "It looks as if no animals have lived here for ages!" they concluded excitedly. "This land could be very useful to us."

The wise tree sighed. "You see what they are planning? I told you to leave the wild beasts alone."

"Don't be silly," the foolish tree scoffed. "I can frighten those men away, just as I did the beasts!" 

The tree shook itself violently, making its loud, terrifying sound. SHR... SHR... SHR... 

"What is that awful sound, father?" a young boy asked. 
"It's nothing, son! Just the breeze, rustling through the leaves!" his father replied calmly. 

The tree tried again, shaking even harder. Twigs and branches snapped off and hit the men. "Hey! Watch out!" they yelled. 

"Oh! It's nothing to worry about! Just a few twigs have fallen down!" the men laughed. "We'll cut down these trees. The wood will come in useful."

Soon, the forest was full of people from the nearby village with axes, chopping down the trees to clear the land. 

"We'll tackle this big tree tomorrow," the men decided, pointing at the foolish tree. 

That night, the foolish tree wept in terror. "Help, friend! Help me, please! You heard them, didn't you? They are coming for me tomorrow!" 

"It is too late!" his wise friend replied sadly. "Now you must bear the consequences of your foolish action!" 

And the next day, the woodcutters returned, and that was the end of the foolish tree.


# The Magic Chant - 6. The Lost Gram

Once, a powerful king and his large army had encamped outside a city for the night. High up in a tree above the camp, a hungry monkey watched them closely. 

"I have been feeling hungry all day, but now it appears I'll have a feast!" the monkey thought excitedly. 

A little later, the soldiers brought out large pots. "Let's roast some gram for the horses," they said. 
"Ha! Just as I expected. How lucky I am!" the monkey thought. 

"We'll feed the horses when the gram gets cold," the soldiers decided, leaving the roasted gram to cool by the fire while they walked away to do other chores. 

"This is my chance!" As soon as the soldiers turned to leave, the monkey swooped down from the tree and landed next to the pots. 

He quickly grabbed a handful of the roasted gram and ate it. "This is delicious!" he thought happily. 

But though his hunger was satisfied, his greed was not. 
"I must take away as much as I can!" he decided greedily. 

He stuffed his mouth full with the gram and then grabbed even more with both of his front paws. "I'll have enough for many more days!" he thought proudly. 

With his mouth and hands full, he scrambled back up the tree. However, as he climbed, one single grain of roasted gram rolled out of his paws and fell to the ground below. 

"Oh... oh, my gram! It's gone!" the monkey gasped. 

Without thinking about the huge amount of food he already held, the greedy monkey became obsessed with that one dropped grain. "I won't allow this! I must get it back!" 

In his panic, he threw away all the gram he held in his hands and spat out all the gram in his mouth, just to free himself so he could search. 

He sprang down from the tree to look for the one fallen grain. "Where could it have gone? It must be somewhere around here!" 

He searched high and low, but the tiny grain was lost in the dirt and leaves. "I can't find it anywhere!" he cried in despair. 

And then he looked up and realized what he had done. "And, alas, I have lost all the rest of the gram too!" 

Thus, the foolish monkey, in being niggardly and greedy over just one single grain of gram, lost absolutely everything he had.
