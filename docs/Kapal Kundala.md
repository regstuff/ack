# Kapala Kundala

During the reign of the great Mughal Emperor Akbar, a lone pilgrim boat was returning from Gangasagar. Early one morning, a dense fog descended, blinding the boatmen. Trapped in the swirling mist, the terrified passengers realized they were hopelessly lost at sea.
"Oh God! We are lost if we strike a rock!" a passenger cried out. 
"It will be worse if we are washed ashore," another wailed. "We'll be at the mercy of wild beasts in the jungles."

Only one young man named Navakumar remained calm. "Please! Remain calm. We should find our way by daybreak," he reassured them. "Doesn't the beauty of the sea make you forget your fears?"

Eventually, the boat drifted toward a desolate, forested shore. Starving and exhausted, the passengers needed firewood to cook their food, but everyone was too terrified of tigers to enter the dense jungle. Bravely, Navakumar volunteered to go alone. 

Armed with a small axe, Navakumar ventured deep into the woods. He gathered a heavy load of wood, but when he returned to the beach, he was stunned. The boat was gone! The rising tide had threatened to strand the vessel, and his cowardly companions had sailed away, abandoning him to his fate.

Lost and wandering as night fell, Navakumar spotted a flickering fire in the distance. Approaching it, he found a terrifying figure—a *Kapalik*, a formidable tantric ascetic who worshipped the Goddess Kali and practiced human sacrifice. Desperate, Navakumar begged the Kapalik for food and shelter. 
The Kapalik gave him fruit and allowed him to sleep in his hut, but sternly commanded, "Don't leave the cottage till I return."

Exhausted, Navakumar slept for a full day. When he finally woke up, the Kapalik had not returned. Driven by hunger, Navakumar left the hut to search for fruit but quickly lost his way again. Wandering along the beach, he suddenly encountered a breathtakingly beautiful maiden with long, untamed hair. 

"I am Kapala Kundala," she said softly. "Come with me!" She led him back to the Kapalik's cottage, then vanished into the trees as quickly as she had appeared. 

That night, Navakumar heard soft whispers outside the hut. It was Kapala Kundala. "Don't go with him!" she warned urgently. "He is going to sacrifice you to Goddess Kali."
Unfortunately, the Kapalik overheard her. Bursting from the shadows, he overpowered Navakumar, bound him firmly, and dragged him to the sacrificial altar. Leaving his victim tied before the blazing fire, the Kapalik went to fetch his heavy scimitar.

Suddenly, Kapala Kundala emerged from the bushes, holding the Kapalik's scimitar in her hand. "Sh-shh! Don't make a noise!" she whispered, swiftly cutting Navakumar's ropes. 

She led the rescued youth through the dark forest to a secluded temple and told the resident priest the entire story. The priest was deeply concerned. "The young lady will be in mortal danger from the Kapalik if she stays here after helping you escape," he warned Navakumar. "You must marry her and take her with you." 
Though hesitant, Navakumar agreed upon learning she was of noble Brahman birth. The priest performed a hasty wedding and escorted the newlyweds to the nearest village, providing them with money to hire a palanquin. 

As they traveled toward Navakumar's home in Saptagram, Navakumar lagged behind the palanquin carriers. Night fell, and he stumbled upon a broken palanquin abandoned on the road. Inside, a wealthy woman lay bound with ropes. Navakumar untied her. 
"My servants left me and fled when dacoits attacked," the woman explained. Navakumar escorted her safely to a nearby village inn. 

At the inn, the woman introduced herself as Mati Bibi, a wealthy noblewoman from the Mughal court in Agra. When Navakumar introduced himself as Navakumar Sharma of Saptagram, Mati Bibi was secretly stunned. She gifted Kapala Kundala expensive jewelry as a reward for her rescue. 

When her guards finally arrived, Mati Bibi revealed her true identity to her maid. "He is not a stranger. He is my husband," she confessed. Mati Bibi's real name was Padmavati. She was Navakumar's first wife, whom his family had disowned years ago after her father converted to Islam. Now a powerful figure in the Mughal court, she had never forgotten Navakumar. 

Meanwhile, Navakumar and Kapala Kundala reached Saptagram, where they were joyously welcomed by his family. Navakumar affectionately renamed his wild, forest-born bride 'Mrinmayee'. However, Mrinmayee found domestic life stifling and deeply missed her carefree days in the forest. 

Years passed. When Emperor Akbar died and Prince Salim was about to be crowned, Mati Bibi left the political intrigues of Agra and settled in Saptagram to win back her husband. She summoned Navakumar and dramatically revealed her true identity as Padmavati. 
"Take me as your wife," she pleaded. 
"How can you suggest such a thing? You know I am already married," Navakumar refused, rejecting all her wealth and power. 

Furious and humiliated, Mati Bibi swore revenge. Disguising herself as a young man, she ventured into the forest and sought out the evil Kapalik, who was still hunting for Kapala Kundala. "I also desire her death," the disguised Mati Bibi told the dark ascetic. "I will tell you how to bring about her death."

Executing her wicked plan, Mati Bibi befriended Kapala Kundala under a false identity. One evening, she told the innocent girl, "I need a rare medicinal herb from the forest. It must be plucked by a woman exactly at midnight, and she must be alone." 

That night, Kapala Kundala crept out to the forest. Suspicious, Navakumar followed her. Deep in the woods, he saw his wife meeting a mysterious young man—who was actually Mati Bibi in disguise! Before Navakumar could intervene, the Kapalik emerged from the shadows. 

The Kapalik intercepted Navakumar and poisoned his mind with devastating lies. "She comes here to meet this young man!" the Kapalik hissed. "She is unfaithful! You displeased Goddess Kali by running away. You can atone by sacrificing your unfaithful wife to Kali."

Blinded by intense jealousy and rage, Navakumar agreed to the horrific pact. 
The next evening, when Kapala Kundala prepared to leave the house, Navakumar confronted her. "I know where you are going! You are going to the forest!" he snarled. "I'll take you there."

He forcefully marched her toward the Kapalik's sacrificial fire, intending to end her life for her perceived sins. Kapala Kundala, though innocent, remained remarkably calm and made no attempt to defend herself against the accusations. 

However, as they neared the blazing fire and Navakumar saw the terrifying Kapalik waiting with his scimitar, reality shattered his jealous delusions. "That is the man who wanted to kill me!" Navakumar realized with a sudden, horrifying jolt. 

The fog of manipulation and jealousy finally lifted from Navakumar's mind. He turned to his wife, finally understanding the depth of her innocence and the cruelty of the deception played upon him. "Mrinmayee! Why didn't you show me the light?" he cried, begging for her forgiveness. "The forest makes us take leave of our senses. Let's go away from here quickly."

Turning their backs on the evil Kapalik and the treacherous shadows of the forest, Navakumar and Kapala Kundala walked away hand-in-hand, returning to the safety and light of their lives together.
