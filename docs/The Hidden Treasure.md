# The Hidden Treasure - 1. The Hidden Treasure

One day, long long ago, a tired woman came walking dejectedly down the road from the city of Mithila. She wore the simple garb of a maid, but her graceful manner suggested noble birth. 

"How far are you going, sir? Can you take me to Kalachampa?" she asked a passing carter. 
"I'll take you there. Please get into my cart," he offered kindly. 

Exhausted, the woman fell asleep in the cart. She woke up at the southern gate of the city. As she sat alone, wondering where to go, a teacher and his pupils passed by. 

"Who are you? You seem to be in distress," the teacher asked gently. 
"Please don't hesitate. Tell me the truth. I may be able to help you," he added kindly. 

"I am the Queen of Mithila," she confessed, weeping. "My husband was killed in battle by his enemy, Poljanaka. I had to flee the city in disguise for the sake of my unborn child."

The Queen stayed and gave birth to a son, Mahajanaka. He grew up to be strong and brave. 

When he was older, he asked his mother about his father. "You are a prince," she told him. "A few days before you were born, your father had to leave to do battle with his enemy, Poljanaka. We lost, and I fled to save you."

"I must work hard. One day I will rule over Mithila," Mahajanaka vowed. He studied hard under the wise teacher. 

Years passed, and he grew into a handsome, learned man. He decided to sail overseas to trade, hoping to earn enough money to raise an army and reclaim his throne. 

Meanwhile, King Poljanaka of Mithila was seriously ill and had no son. "The man my daughter Shivali chooses for a husband shall be the king," he declared on his deathbed. 

"Her suitor will have to perform three tasks to prove his intelligence, strength, and wisdom," the king added. "First, he must point out the head of the princess' square bed. Then he must string a heavy bow which requires the strength of a thousand men. Lastly, he must decipher sixteen clues to find sixteen pots of gold hidden around the palace."

After the king died, many nobles tried to win the princess, but they all failed. The princess found them all lacking in courage or dignity. 

Around this time, Mahajanaka's ship was destroyed in a terrible storm. He swam for days and finally washed ashore near Mithila, exhausted. 

As he slept outside the city gates, the desperate ministers of Mithila sent out the sacred royal chariot. "It will lead us to the right man," the royal priest believed. 

The horses galloped out of the city and suddenly came to a halt right next to the sleeping Mahajanaka! The ministers woke him with a deafening uproar of drums and horns. 

Mahajanaka awoke calmly, without panic or fear. "He is brave, wise and noble," the priest observed, impressed. 

They explained the tasks to him, and Mahajanaka agreed to try. When he was led to Princess Shivali, he walked slowly and regally, refusing to be rushed or intimidated by her. 

"How serene and confident his manner! How majestic! He is the man for me!" Shivali thought, instantly falling in love. 

Mahajanaka faced the first task. He took a small hairpin and handed it to the princess. Naturally, she placed it at the head of her square bed. "There is the head of the bed," he announced simply. 

Next, he was taken to the armoury. He picked up the massive bow and strung it with ease, displaying incredible strength. 

Finally, the minister gave him the clues for the hidden gold. "Where the sun rises; and the second where the sun sets..."

Mahajanaka realized the "sun" referred to the "sun of knowledge" — the ascetics and scholars. He quickly found the pots buried where the late king used to receive and see off the wise men. He effortlessly deciphered all sixteen clues and found all the gold. 

"You have proved yourself on every score, my lord. You must now marry the princess, and make us happy," the minister announced joyfully. 

"First, I want my mother and my uncle to be brought here from Kalachampa," Mahajanaka requested. 

When they arrived, Mahajanaka and Shivali were married. As his first act as the new King of Mithila, he made a grand announcement. 

"I declare that the gold found in the pots will be distributed among the needy," he proclaimed. 

"Mother, by giving away the gold to the needy, I have found a more valuable treasure," King Mahajanaka smiled. "The goodwill and blessings of my people!"
