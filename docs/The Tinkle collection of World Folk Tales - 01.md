# The Giant Killer

Hundreds of years ago, the land of Israel was attacked by the Philistines. It was a long and exhausting war. The Philistine camp stood on one hill, and the Israelite camp stood on another, with a deep valley between them.

One day, there was a great uproar in the Philistine camp. The Israelites came running out to see what the matter was. 

"What creature is that?" an Israelite soldier gasped in terror. 

A massive, towering figure stepped forward from the Philistine ranks. "Look at me! You white-faced, lily-livered cowards! Don't run away," he boomed. "Send one, just one puny Israelite to fight with me..."

It was Goliath of Gath, the champion of the Philistines. He was a giant of a man, heavily armored and wielding a spear as thick as a weaver's beam. 

"If your champion wins, we will be your servants," Goliath challenged, his voice echoing across the valley. "But... if he is killed, you will be our servants forever!"

The Israelites trembled in fear. "He could crush us like nuts with his bare hands," they murmured among themselves. "Who could kill him? Only a fool would try."

Every day, Goliath came out and shouted the exact same challenge. "Puny men! Weak-kneed creatures! So, not one of you dares fight me? Cowards!"

Many days passed, but no one dared to accept Goliath's challenge, and every day after sunset, the giant went back to his camp. 

One day, a young shepherd boy named David came to the Israelite camp. 

"What do you want here?" a soldier asked him. 

"I am looking for my brothers," David replied. 

He soon found them among the troops. "David! What are you doing here?" his eldest brother scolded. "This is not a safe place for you!"

"How is father?" another brother asked. 

"He's well," David replied. But before they could speak further, a thunderous voice interrupted them. 

"Come and fight, you weaklings!"

"Who is that?" David asked, startled. 

"It's Goliath... the champion of the Philistines," a soldier whispered in awe. "He wants an Israelite to fight him in single combat."

"Then why doesn't someone take up the challenge?" David asked innocently. 

"Who would dare to face him?" the soldiers laughed bitterly. 

"I would!" David declared. 

The soldiers stared at him in disbelief. "You are a fool, David. You'll never have any sense in your head!" his brother yelled. "Go and look after your sheep, David. Leave the fighting to us."

But instead of going back home, David walked down to a stream nearby. He carefully chose five smooth, round stones from the riverbed and put them in his pouch. Taking out his shepherd's sling, he walked bravely towards the giant. 

"Goliath! I will fight with you!" David shouted. 

When Goliath saw the young boy coming, he threw his head back and roared with laughter. "So they have sent a boy... HOHOHO! Run off, now, before I decide to crush you!"

But David only went nearer. 

"That poor boy!" the Israelites cried out from their camp. "David! Foolish boy, come back! The giant will grind him to dust!"

"Goliath! You can't refuse to fight!" David called out, standing firm. "It was you who demanded single combat... and I have taken up your challenge!"

The giant gave a great roar of fury and advanced towards David. "You...! You dare to challenge me? I'll..."

But Goliath did not finish his sentence. Quick as a flash, David fixed a stone in his sling and began to swing it rapidly above his head. The next moment, the stone came flying through the air... and hit Goliath right on his forehead with a sickening *THWACK!*

Goliath swayed like a mountain in an earthquake... and fell heavily to the ground, dead. 

"Goliath has fallen!" the Israelites cheered in disbelief. "Look, the Philistines are running away! Quick, after them!"

Seeing their invincible champion defeated by a mere boy, the Philistine army panicked and fled. The Israelites pursued the Philistines and won a great victory that day. 

David became a great hero to his people, and when he grew up, he became one of the greatest kings of the Israelites.


# A Friend Indeed

In ancient Rome, a slave named Androcles worked under a cruel and abusive master. Unable to bear the torture any longer, Androcles ran away one day. 

*His soldiers will soon be after me,* Androcles thought, running desperately through the countryside. *I must run away as far as I can.*

He fled deep into a dense jungle and took shelter in a dark, quiet cave. He lay on the rocky floor, exhausted and terrified. 

Suddenly, a terrifying sound shook the cave walls. 

"ROOOARR!"

Androcles froze. A massive lion stepped into the cave. 

*A... A lion!* Androcles thought in panic. *This must be his cave! I'm trapped!*

The lion approached him, but it did not attack. Instead, it sat down and whimpered, holding out its huge front paw. 

Androcles looked closer. *He's holding out his paw... oh, there's a huge thorn stuck in it!*

Slowly and carefully, Androcles moved towards the mighty beast. "My poor friend... how it must pain you!" he whispered gently. "Let me take it out!"

The lion sat perfectly still as Androcles gripped the sharp thorn. With a swift tug, he pulled it free. "There!"

After Androcles had taken out the thorn, the lion licked his hand gratefully and quietly went away. 

He returned after a while, dropping a freshly caught animal at Androcles' feet. 

"Oh, you've brought me food!" Androcles smiled. 

From that day on, Androcles stayed with the lion in his cave. They shared their food and lived together peacefully. But one day, when the lion was away hunting, a group of Roman soldiers discovered the cave. 

"Soldiers!" Androcles gasped, trying to run. 

"Don't try to run away," a soldier shouted, grabbing him. 

The soldiers tied Androcles up and took him back to his cruel master. 

"You know how slaves who run away are punished!" his master sneered, sitting on his lavish couch. He turned to his guards. "Throw him to the lions!"

One of the most popular pastimes of the ancient Romans was to watch bloody fights between men and ferocious lions. These fights used to take place in a massive, crowded stadium called the Arena. 

Some days later, poor Androcles was pushed into the center of the dusty Arena. 

"Out you go! Look at the crowds that have come to see you," a guard mocked, slamming the heavy iron gates shut behind him. 

The crowd cheered excitedly. 

"Is that the slave? He doesn't look very strong to me," a spectator commented. 

"Look, they're opening the lion's cage!" another pointed. 

As soon as the cage door was raised, a massive, roaring lion rushed out into the sunlight. 

"They starved him for three days. He'll tear the slave to pieces," a man chuckled. "What a huge creature!"

The lion indeed was very hungry. With a terrifying roar, it rushed straight towards Androcles. 

*I have no chance against this beast,* Androcles thought, bracing himself. *But I won't run... I'll stand here and fight.*

The lion lunged... but suddenly stopped right in front of Androcles. It didn't attack. Instead, it began to rub its head affectionately against the slave's legs!

The crowd gasped. "What! He doesn't want to attack!" "Could it be...? Is it...?"

Androcles fell to his knees, tears in his eyes, and hugged the massive beast. "It is you!"

"This is amazing! The ferocious lion has become as gentle as a lamb," the Emperor marveled from his box. 

"So they caught you too," Androcles whispered to his friend. "You have become so lean. They have been starving you, have they?"

It was the very same lion from whose paw Androcles had removed the thorn!

The entire crowd was deeply moved by the beautiful scene of friendship and loyalty. They stood up and began to chant, "FREE THEM! YES, LET THEM GO!"

Yielding to the demands of the people, the Emperor ordered that Androcles and the lion be set free. 

It became a common, heartwarming sight thereafter to see the huge, fearsome beast happily following Androcles around like a small puppy on the streets of Rome.


# The Learned Liars

Once upon a time, a wealthy King invited two renowned scholars to his palace. One of them was called Pandit Gyanraj, and the other was called Pandit Vidyaraj. 

They were learned men indeed, and they had something new and fascinating to tell the King and his courtiers every single day. 

One day, the King thought to himself, *Such great men should be given rich rewards.* He decided to hold a grand ceremony in their honor. 

The King invited several hundred people to his palace. 

"We are here today to honour and reward two of the most learned men in our land," the King announced proudly to the gathered crowd. 

But as he looked at the two scholars, the King suddenly realized that he did not know much about either man personally. *Pandit Gyanraj is... er...* he thought, struggling to find the right words of praise. *Pandit Vidyaraj will be able to help me out.*

He called Pandit Vidyaraj aside privately. "Pandit Vidyaraj, please tell me all about your friend."

"Well... I... er..." Vidyaraj stammered. *I must be careful,* he thought greedily. *If I praise Gyanraj, the King might give him a better reward than the one he gives me.*

"Your Majesty, I do not like to say it, but Pandit Gyanraj is an... an ass!" Vidyaraj lied smoothly. "He doesn't know anything. He just repeats what he hears from me."

*That's a lie!* the King thought, hiding his surprise. *I have heard Pandit Gyanraj speak and I know he is learned.*

"Anyway," the King said aloud, "let's see what Gyanraj has to say about his friend."

The King dismissed Vidyaraj and called for Pandit Gyanraj. "Pandit Gyanraj, please tell me all about your friend."

"He is... er..." Gyanraj hesitated. He had the exact same greedy thought as his companion. *I must be careful. If I praise Vidyaraj, the King might give him a better reward than the one he gives me.*

"Your Majesty, since you ask me, I must tell you that Pandit Vidyaraj is a... a donkey!" Gyanraj declared. "He doesn't know anything. He just repeats what he hears from me."

The King frowned. *These scholars have read many books, but what is the use of all their learning? They are no better than any of us here. How can I honour such greedy, deceitful men?*

"Listen carefully and do as I say," the King whispered a command to his royal servants. 

Sometime later, two servants walked into the grand hall carrying large silver plates covered with beautiful silk cloths. They placed one plate respectfully before each pandit. 

The two scholars rubbed their hands in glee, expecting gold and jewels. They eagerly pulled away the silk cloths...

"What is this, Your Majesty?" Pandit Gyanraj asked, horrified. 

"Fresh, green grass!" the King replied cheerfully. 

"You yourselves have told me that one of you is an ass and the other a donkey," the King announced loudly for the entire court to hear. "So I have given you the reward asses and donkeys would like best—the finest grass that could be found in the kingdom!"

The two learned liars felt deeply ashamed of their greed and jealousy. They left the palace in disgrace, and no one ever heard of them again.


# The Witty King

Once upon a time, in southern India, there was a brave King called Vijayaraj. One day, his kingdom was fiercely attacked by the Sultan of a neighbouring country. 

Vijayaraj and his men fought bravely, defending their land with all their might, but finally, outnumbered and overpowered, they lost the battle to the Sultan's forces. 

King Vijayaraj was captured and brought before the victorious Sultan. 

The Sultan looked at the defeated king with a mixture of respect and sternness. "I admire you for your bravery," the Sultan declared, "but you will have to die. Do you have a last wish?"

"Yes," Vijayaraj said calmly. "I wish to drink a cup of water."

"Is that all?" the Sultan asked, surprised by the simple request. He turned to his guard. "Soldier, bring water!"

The soldier fetched a simple clay cup filled with water and handed it to Vijayaraj. 

Before taking a sip, Vijayaraj looked at the Sultan. "Sultan, please order your men not to kill me until I have finished this water."

The Sultan agreed to the condemned man's request. "No one will touch you till you finish drinking," he commanded his soldiers loudly. 

Vijayaraj immediately tilted the cup and poured out all the water directly onto the dry, sandy floor. It soaked into the earth instantly. 

"You must let me live now that I can't drink it anymore!" Vijayaraj declared triumphantly. 

The Sultan stared at the wet patch of sand, completely taken aback. He thought it over for a moment, and then burst into hearty laughter. 

"You're as clever as you're brave!" the Sultan chuckled. He ordered his soldier to bring another cup. "Now drink this water and live."

Impressed by Vijayaraj's quick wit and unshakable courage in the face of death, the Sultan returned Vijayaraj's kingdom to him. From that day forward, the two great rulers became good friends and lived in peace.
