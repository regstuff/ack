# The Legend of Maarthaanda Varma - 1. The Legend of Maarthaanda Varma

In the early eighteenth century, the kingdom of Travancore was plagued by intrigue and rebellion. The reigning Maharaja was seriously ill, and a powerful group of feudal lords known as the "Group of Eight," led by a man named Kutaman, conspired to seize control of the state. 

They plotted to ensure that the Maharaja's eldest son, Padmanaabhan Thampi, would ascend the throne instead of the rightful heir, the Maharaja's nephew, Yuvaraja Maarthaanda Varma, breaking the land's matrilineal tradition of inheritance. 

Thampi, greedy for power, employed a ruthless assassin named Velu to eliminate Maarthaanda Varma. Velu ambushed the Yuvaraja and his loyal companion, Ananta (the son of the powerful Lord Pillai). Maarthaanda escaped, but Velu seemingly murdered Ananta. 

To turn Lord Pillai against Maarthaanda, the conspirators spread malicious rumours that Maarthaanda had murdered Ananta over a dancing girl. 

Maarthaanda Varma, forced to go into hiding, sought refuge at the house of a loyal chieftain named Kurup. However, the conspirators tracked him down, locked Kurup in a room, and set fire to the house. 

Just as all seemed lost, a mysterious, mad Chaannaan (a low-caste tribal) appeared, grabbed a sword, and helped Maarthaanda and his bodyguard fight off Velu's men and escape. 

Meanwhile, Kutaman's niece, Subhadraa, discovered the conspirators' dark plans. Though related to Kutaman, her loyalties lay with the rightful heir, Maarthaanda. She also deeply loved Ananta's betrothed, Paarukutty, who refused to believe Ananta was dead. 

Subhadraa secretly listened to the conspirators and learned that Thampi planned to assassinate Maarthaanda that very night. She immediately dispatched a warning letter to Maarthaanda, saving his life once again. 

Soon after, the old Maharaja finally passed away. Maarthaanda Varma swiftly took command, issuing his first royal decree to consolidate the state's warriors under his direct control, stripping the conspirators of their military power. 

Furious, the Group of Eight decided to attack the palace that night with a thousand soldiers. Subhadraa, risking everything, rushed to the palace to warn the new King. 

"You must flee the palace this very moment," she urged Maarthaanda. She helped disguise the King and his men as paid labourers and escorted them through the dangerous streets of Trivandrum. 

As they neared her house, they were confronted by Lord Pillai, who still believed the rumours that Maarthaanda had murdered his son. 

Subhadraa bravely stepped forward to reveal the truth. "The persons who did attempt to murder your son are Thampi's men," she explained. "After Velu attacked him and left him to die, Ananta was rescued by the Pathaans. He had to promise the old Pathaan that he would never disclose his identity to his own people for their protection."

She revealed that the mysterious Chaannaan who had repeatedly saved Maarthaanda's life was none other than Ananta in disguise! 

Overjoyed to learn his son was alive and loyal, Lord Pillai allied with King Maarthaanda. 

Just then, the forces of Thampi and the Group of Eight arrived to attack the King. But Maarthaanda was no longer alone. Ananta, leading a fierce band of Pathaan warriors, entered the fray. 

The conspirators were decisively defeated. Sundaram, Thampi's evil servant, was killed, and Thampi and the Group of Eight were taken captive. 

Remembering his debt to Subhadraa, King Maarthaanda ordered Lord Pillai, "Set Kutaman free. I promised Subhadraa I'd forgive him." 

Ananta was finally reunited with his beloved Paarukutty, and peace seemed restored. 

But tragedy struck at Subhadraa's house. The ungrateful Kutaman, enraged by his niece's betrayal of their family's plot, struck her down with his sword. "You traitor!" he yelled, killing her instantly. 

When King Maarthaanda heard the shocking news of Subhadraa's death, he wept for the brave woman who had sacrificed everything to save his life and his kingdom. 

"No loyal subject of mine shall ever suffer your cruel unjust fate in my reign, Subhadraa," he vowed. 

True to his word, Maarthaanda Varma proved to be a just, strong, and benevolent ruler, bringing peace and prosperity to the troubled land of Travancore.
