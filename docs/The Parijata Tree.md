# The Parijata Tree - 1. The Parijata Tree

News had reached the celestial realm that Krishna had slain the evil demon Narakasura. Lord Indra and the Devas were waiting at the city gates to joyously receive Krishna and his wife, Satyabhama. 

"What a relief it is to know that he will never again trouble us!" Indra sighed. 
"I have brought back Mother Aditi's earrings," Krishna said, handing back the precious jewels the demon had stolen. 

While Krishna was being praised, Satyabhama felt slighted. Shachi, Indra's wife, was deliberately ignoring her. "Indra's wife should have been here to receive me," Satyabhama thought bitterly. "After all, I did help Krishna in the fight."

As they were leaving, Krishna and Satyabhama flew past Indra's beautiful garden, Nandana, on their mount, Garuda. They stopped to admire it. 

"Krishna, look! The Parijata tree!" Satyabhama gasped. "Golden bark! Copper leaves! And such fragrant flowers! Krishna, let's take it to Dwaraka!"

The chief of Indra's guards overheard them. "No mortal may touch that tree!" he shouted. "This Parijata tree belongs to Shachi. Keep off if you do not want to provoke Indra's hostility."

Offended, Satyabhama snapped, "Have you forgotten that Krishna is the slayer of Naraka? We are taking away the tree. Stop us if you can!"

The guard ran to Shachi and repeated Satyabhama's words. Furious, Shachi complained to Indra. "Mere mortals daring to carry away the celestial tree! Stop them!"

Indra marched to Nandana with his entire celestial army. 

"Indra will come himself and offer the tree to you, Bhama," Krishna said. But seeing the army, he realized Indra had other intentions. Krishna blew his conch, and the Devas answered with a shower of arrows. 

Krishna effortlessly cut the arrows to pieces. Garuda tore a magical noose to shreds and sent the Devas fleeing in terror. When the god Yama stepped forward, Krishna smashed his mace. 

Seething with anger, Indra himself came forward and hurled his mighty thunderbolt at Krishna. Standing calmly, Krishna merely raised an arm and caught it! At that same moment, Garuda attacked Indra's majestic elephant, Airavata, causing it to run amuck. 

Defeated and humiliated, Indra prepared to flee. 
"Wait, Indra. Don't go away," Krishna called out. "You are the Lord of the Celestials. You must not suffer humiliation. I only started this fight to teach Shachi a lesson for ignoring Satyabhama. Forgive me. Let the Parijata tree remain where it stands."

Indra, deeply moved by Krishna's grace in victory, replied, "Here is your thunderbolt. I should have offered you the Parijata. Instead, I chose to fight you! Well, it is not too late. If you have truly pardoned me, take this Parijata to Dwaraka! You cannot refuse!"

And so, Krishna and Satyabhama flew back to Dwaraka, proudly carrying the celestial Parijata tree.


# The Parijata Tree - 2. Lakshmana

The hand of the fair and beautiful Princess Lakshmana was sought by many mighty kings across the land. Her father, King Brihatsena, had asked her to choose one of them. 

"Your suitors are getting impatient for an answer to their proposals," her father told her. "Have you made up your mind?"
"I have, father," Lakshmana replied firmly. "I do not want to marry any of them. My heart is set on marrying... Krishna."

Brihatsena was surprised but pleased. "I respect him as a person and have a great regard for his prowess in archery. But I am worried about the wrath of all those kings, each of whom wants to marry you."

"Don't worry, Lakshmana," he smiled, finding a clever solution. "I have found a way out. We will hold a *swayamvara* with a contest! A contest in which only one can emerge victorious and claim your hand: Your Krishna!"

Invitations were sent to all the mighty suitors and to Krishna. When they assembled in the grand arena, Brihatsena explained the incredibly difficult rules. 

High above the arena was a revolving machine with a wooden fish inside. "You have to look at the fish's reflection in the water below," Brihatsena announced, "and shoot down the fish in the machine!"

Many great kings tried, but the incredibly difficult task of shooting a moving target by only looking at its reflection proved impossible for them. 

Then came Krishna. With a confident smile, he stepped forward, easily strung the heavy bow, looked at the reflection in the water, and let his arrow fly. 

His aim was perfect. The fish came down in pieces! 

Lakshmana happily stepped forward and garlanded him. After taking leave of her loving and resourceful father, she got into Krishna's chariot, and they rode to Dwaraka, where his other wives, Rukmini and Satyabhama, were waiting to welcome her.


# The Parijata Tree - 3. Tulabhara

One morning, Krishna's wealthy and proud wife, Satyabhama, was giving away alms when the mischievous Sage Narada arrived. 

"You have come at an auspicious moment!" Satyabhama greeted him. "Please do me the honour of accepting a gift. Tell me what I can give you that is worthy of being accepted by you."

"Will you give me whatever I ask?" Narada challenged. 
"I will."
"Then give me what is most precious to you!"

Satyabhama proudly pointed to her vast collection of ornaments and celestial gems. "Is this necklace most precious to you... or these diamonds?"

"What do *you* consider most precious to you?" Narada insisted. 

"What is most precious... to me... is... Krishna!" Satyabhama finally admitted. 
"There you are, Satyabhama. My question is answered. I will take Krishna."

Satyabhama was horrified. When Narada explained the situation to Krishna, he playfully agreed to go with the sage. 

"O sage, please don't ask me for Krishna!" Satyabhama begged. 
"Well, if you love him so much, give me something which is equal in weight to him," Narada proposed. 

"Thank you, O sage!" Satyabhama laughed confidently. "Krishna cannot weigh more than all the gold I have."

A huge balance scale (*Tulabhara*) was brought out. Krishna calmly sat on one of the pans. Satyabhama began piling heap after heap of her heavy gold jewelry onto the other pan. 

But to her shock, the pan with Krishna didn't budge. She brought out every single piece of gold, every jewel, and every celestial ornament she owned, piling them high. Still, the pan on which Krishna sat did not move even a hair's breadth off the ground!

Desperate and having nothing left, she didn't know what to do. Just then, Rukmini, Krishna's wise and seniormost consort, arrived. 

"How could you use gold as a measure!" Rukmini scolded her when she heard the story. "Krishna is not a commodity to be possessed, evaluated, and disposed of. If at all he has to be evaluated, only the power of his own name uttered with selfless love could be the measure."

Rukmini ordered all the heavy gold and jewels to be removed from the pan. 

With her hands folded in deep devotion, she softly chanted, "Krishna... Krishna... Krishna..." and gently placed a single, small Tulsi leaf on the empty pan. 

And lo! The pan with the single Tulsi leaf immediately went down, lifting Krishna up! The scale was perfectly balanced by pure love. 

As Krishna stepped down, Narada picked up the Tulsi leaf. "This was what I sought, Satyabhama. This symbol of divine love and devotion. I now take leave of you."

Satyabhama, humbled and changed, hugged Rukmini. "Oh, sister! Until today I was proud of all my wealth. Now I know better. All my wealth is mere dust compared to the wealth of selfless love."
