# Tales Of Misers - 1. Ilisa, The Gildmaster

Ilisa, the gildmaster, had more money than he could ever spend, but he was incredibly tight-fisted. 

One day, walking through the streets of his city, Ilisa frowned. *I've stayed out longer than I should,* he thought. *I hope my wife has not bought anything for herself in my absence. She is so extravagant. If I didn't keep a constant watch on them they would eat me out of house and home. And as for my worthless children, all they can think of is eating.*

"Help a hungry man, sir," a beggar suddenly pleaded, holding out a bowl.

"Out of my way, you wretch! You won't get a thing from me!" Ilisa yelled, raising his walking stick to strike the poor man.

*Everyone is after my money,* Ilisa thought bitterly as he hurried away. 

Suddenly, a sweet aroma caught his attention. He saw a street vendor frying fresh sweets. *What luscious jalebis!* he thought, his mouth watering. *But if I buy some I'll have to share them with my acquaintances there. Tcha! If I send my servant to buy some I'll have to share them with my wife and children.*

He paced back and forth. *Unless I eat them somewhere else...* 

Ilisa called his servant over. "Go and buy some jalebis from the sweetmeat shop," he whispered.

"Yes, master."

"But don't bring them here... take them to the bushes near the lake. I'll wait there for you."

As Ilisa was making his elaborate, secretive plans just to eat jalebis alone, a magical event occurred. A man who was his exact likeness presented himself before the king of the city. 

"Maharaj, I have come to seek your permission... to give away some of my great wealth," the impostor announced.

"What!" the King exclaimed, stunned. "You want to give away wealth?"

"Yes, maharaj. Hoarded wealth is of no use to anyone," the impostor smiled.

*The greatest miser in the land wanting to give away his wealth!* the King thought in disbelief. *Well, well! Wonders will never cease!* 

"You may do so," the King agreed.

The impostor then went to Ilisa's house. *There's a stranger in town impersonating me,* the impostor told his servants. "If he comes here, beat him and drive him away."

"Yes, master," the guards agreed.

The impostor then went inside and looked at Ilisa's wife. "It's a shame that Ilisa's wife should wear such dowdy clothes," he said warmly. "Go to the market and buy whatever you want... buy something for the children too."

His wife stared at him in absolute shock. "Are... are you all right?"

"I've never felt better," the impostor beamed. "It's just that I am in a generous mood today. Tell everyone you meet that I am giving away half my wealth to the poor and needy."

*He has taken leave of his senses,* his wife thought, completely bewildered, *but I'd better buy something for myself before he changes his mind!*

Soon, people began to stream into Ilisa's house from all over the city. 

"Master... master..." the villagers cried. 

"Come in, come in," the impostor welcomed them cheerfully. "Pick up whatever you want and go."

"Whatever we want!" the people gasped in joy, rushing into the storerooms. 

*He may change his mind any moment,* people whispered as they grabbed treasures. *Yes, we all know what a miser he is.*

One man worked more methodically than the others. Instead of rushing into the house, he chose a bullock-cart for himself, loaded it with heavy valuables from the storeroom, and drove away, singing the praises of his benefactor.

"There is no one greater than the noble Ilisa!" the man sang loudly as he drove his cart out of the city. "I've got a fine pair of bullocks, and a cartload of treasure!"

Meanwhile, the real Ilisa was sitting alone in the bushes near the lake, secretly stuffing his face with jalebis. Hearing the man singing, Ilisa paused. 

*Someone's praising me! What!* he thought. He stepped out of the bushes and hailed the cart driver. "Thank you, my good man!"

"You are right. I can see that you are a good judge of men," the driver laughed.

Ilisa looked closely at the cart. "I just have to look at a person to know..." he began, before his eyes widened in horror. "My bullocks! My cart! And my belongings!"

Ilisa grabbed the man. "Thief! How dare you call me thief!" the driver yelled, punching Ilisa squarely in the face. *WHAM!*

"You couldn't have. I am Ilisa!" Ilisa screamed, nursing his jaw.

"I got all these things from the noble Ilisa. You! You are a scoundrel!" the driver retorted, kicking Ilisa into a ditch before driving off.

*I'd better rush home and see what's going on there,* Ilisa panicked. He ran all the way back to his estate. "Stop! Thief! Thief!"

When he arrived, he saw dozens of people walking out of his house with his prized possessions. "Stop! What are you doing?" he screamed at the guards. "Stop them!"

"It's the impostor the master warned us about," the guards yelled. They grabbed the real Ilisa and began beating him mercilessly with their staffs. *WHACK! THUD!*

"Hey, you, take that back to the house! It's mine!" Ilisa yelled at a villager as the guards threw him out into the street. "You can't do this to me... You are all going to pay dearly for this! I'll come back with the king's men."

Ilisa rushed straight to the palace. "Maharaj, I am being looted! People are carrying away my property!"

The King looked at him in total confusion. "You yourself came here and asked for my permission to give away your wealth!"

"But gildmaster... Maharaj, have you ever known me to give away *anything*?" Ilisa cried frantically. "Some impostor has taken my place! I will have him brought here."

When the impersonator was brought to the palace, the king and his courtiers found themselves gazing at two men who looked identical in all respects. 

"Now who is who?" the King sighed.

"My wife could identify me, maharaj," Ilisa said confidently. 

But when Ilisa's wife was brought to the palace and asked to identify her husband, she immediately pointed to the generous impostor. "That's my husband!" she declared.

"Ungrateful woman! After all I've done for you!" Ilisa shrieked. "Now who... what... why, my barber of course!"

The royal barber was brought to the palace. "I'll have to examine their heads, maharaj," the barber said. After checking both men, he shook his head. "I cannot identify the gildmaster, maharaj."

"What do you mean! I have a wart on my head. Have you forgotten?" Ilisa demanded.

"You have a wart... but so does he!" the barber pointed at the impostor.

Ilisa felt his world crashing down. *Even my wife has identified him as her husband! I'll be thrown out of my own house... and I'll lose all my wealth... oooh!* Overwhelmed by the shock, he fainted dead away.

When Ilisa was restored to his senses with a splash of water, the impostor smiled kindly at him. "I shall not frighten you any more. I am your father... come down from heaven."

"F-father!" Ilisa stuttered in awe.

"All the wealth you possess is mine," his celestial father declared. "I was generous to the poor and needy... but you have put all my wealth under lock and key and it is of no use to anyone! I will take away all my wealth if you persist in such behaviour!"

"I... I won't, father!" Ilisa begged, falling to his knees. "Give me another chance!"

Then Ilisa's father returned to the celestial world in a golden chariot. And Ilisa returned home, completely transformed into a generous and considerate man.


# Tales Of Misers - 2. Kesiya

Long ago, there lived an incredibly stingy miser named Kesiya. Despite his enormous wealth, he was the king's treasurer, but he never spent a single coin more than absolutely necessary.

One day, as he was walking home from the palace, Kesiya caught a whiff of sweet frying batter. He saw a man happily eating fresh *poodas* (pancakes) from a street vendor.

*It's such a long time since I have eaten poodas,* Kesiya thought, his mouth watering. *I could ask my wife to make some... but the cost!*

When Kesiya reached his house, his shoulders were slumped in misery.

"You look sad. What's the matter?" his wife asked, concerned.

"Nothing," Kesiya grumbled.

His wife wouldn't let it go. "Tell me what's wrong. Is the king angry with you for some reason?" 

"No."

"Have any of the servants asked for a raise?" 

"Oh, no! They're all good people," Kesiya sighed. 

"What's the matter, then?" she pressed.

Finally, Kesiya broke down. "Well, if you must know... I have a terrible craving for poodas."

His wife burst out laughing. "Poodas? Is that all? I'll make some for you right away. In fact, I'll make enough for the whole neighbourhood!"

Kesiya's eyes bulged out of his head. "What! Have you taken leave of your senses, woman! I... I..."

"All right, all right, I'll make just enough for us," she soothed him.

"For us? For the children, for you, and for me?" Kesiya asked nervously. "Leave the children out."

"They may not care for poodas anyway," his wife agreed.

"Then I'll make just enough for the two of us," she said.

Kesiya hesitated, sweating profusely at the thought of the wasted ingredients. "You don't really want to eat poodas, do you?"

"Er... well..." she sighed. 

"I thought as much," Kesiya said eagerly. "Make just enough for me."

"Wait!" his wife suddenly realized. "We must be careful. If you make the poodas in the kitchen, the sweet aroma will attract the neighbours... and then we'll be forced to give them some. I'll tell you what we'll do. We'll take everything we need up to the terrace, and you can make the poodas up there."

"Perfect!" Kesiya agreed.

So Kesiya and his wife secretly carried the utensils and ingredients required to make the poodas all the way up the stairs to the highest terrace.

"Nobody here... good!" Kesiya whispered, looking around. "Now you can get to work."

Sometime later, the smell of frying poodas filled the terrace. "Are they ready?" Kesiya asked hungrily.

"Almost. But let them cool," his wife said. Suddenly, she pointed up at the sky. "Oh, we have a guest."

"A guest?" Kesiya shrieked. 

Hovering gracefully in the air above the terrace was a serene Buddhist monk. 

"What do you want? Go away!" Kesiya yelled, waving his arms. "Do you think you can get a pooda by hovering up there? I wouldn't give you one even if you were standing on this terrace."

The monk calmly floated down and landed on the terrace. 

"Do... do you think I'll give you a pooda just because we are standing on the same terrace?" Kesiya stuttered, stepping back. "I wouldn't give you one even if you were standing beside me."

The monk quietly walked over and stood right beside him.

"I won't give you one! I mean it!" Kesiya yelled.

His wife tugged his sleeve. "Give him a small one and let him go away," she whispered.

Kesiya grumbled, but pinched off a tiny, marble-sized piece of dough. But as he looked at it, he shook his head. "Not that one! That's too big!"

"I'll make a small one for him," his wife sighed. She took a tiny drop of batter and placed it in the pan. 

But as soon as the batter hit the oil, she gasped. "I can't help it! The paste keeps on spreading over the whole pan. There's something very strange going on here." The tiny drop had expanded into a massive, golden pooda.

"You're just a bad cook, that's all!" Kesiya accused. He pointed to the stack of finished poodas. "Give him that one on top and let him go away."

His wife tried to lift the top pooda, but it wouldn't budge. "They are all stuck together."

"Pull!" Kesiya grunted, grabbing the bottom of the stack while his wife pulled the top. But no matter how hard they pulled, they couldn't separate a single pancake. Kesiya pulled so hard he fell backward onto the floor.

"My wife was right. Something very strange is going on here," Kesiya realized, looking up at the serene monk. "It is clear that we are not dealing with an ordinary man. You can have all the poodas, respected sir."

The monk smiled. "Sit and eat. I want them not for myself... but for my Lord Buddha and his disciples. They are waiting for me. Come with me if you wish to meet him."

The monk raised his hand, and a glowing, magical staircase appeared, leading directly up into the sky. 

Kesiya and his wife, carrying the tray of poodas, followed the monk up the staircase till finally they reached a divine garden where the Lord Buddha sat surrounded by hundreds of monks.

Buddha warmly accepted the offering of poodas, and they were distributed among his monks. Kesiya and his wife too got their share. 

"This is the first time I have shared my food with strangers," Kesiya said softly. "And strangely, I feel happy..." 

He paused, noticing his wife staring blankly at the tray. "Er, what are you staring at?"

"The poodas," she whispered. "Everyone has eaten, but the tray is as full as before."

Kesiya's eyes filled with tears of joy. "Never have I seen such a wonder! This is the greatest day of my life!"

Kesiya returned home a profoundly changed man. The very next morning, he smiled at his wife. "Wife, tomorrow you will make poodas for the whole neighbourhood."
