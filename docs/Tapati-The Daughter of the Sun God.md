# Tapati-The Daughter of the Sun God - 1. Tapati

Samvarana, the noble ruler of the great kingdom of the Bharatas, sat on his throne and heard with an incredibly heavy, broken heart the terrible reports from his worried ministers of the massive sufferings of his poor subjects.

"Terrible famine, endless drought and deadly disease heavily stalk the entire land, my Lord," his chief minister reported sadly. 

"Our good people are tragically dying by the hundred every single day..." another minister wept.

King Samvarana buried his face in his hands. "...And those unfortunate few that are still barely alive, surely consider their terrible, starving plight to be infinitely worse than death itself," he groaned. "Ah! What an absolutely wretched, terrible king I am that I desperately must sit here and safely see my beloved people suffer thus!"

"But incredibly worse is yet to quickly come, Maharaj," the chief minister warned nervously. "For... the... the rival Panchalas have officially declared brutal war on us."

Samvarana's heavy heart completely sank as he desperately led his starving, weakened army to the bloody field of battle to directly face the incredibly mightier force of the Panchalas.

*Their massive army is incredibly much bigger, and their fresh soldiers are infinitely better fed and rested,* Samvarana thought miserably, riding his royal elephant. *Yet, as their sworn king I completely have absolutely no other choice but to bravely fight.*

Samvarana and his loyal men bravely and desperately fought the invading enemy. 

However, the terrible odds heavily against them were simply far too great. Thousands of Bharata soldiers fell. 

"It's absolutely all over for us," Samvarana cried out, seeing his army completely crushed. 

Humiliated and entirely grief-stricken, King Samvarana desperately fled for his life with his few remaining men and his royal family deep into the thick forest safely on the far banks of the Sindhu (The Indus) river. 

And the exhausted Bharatas quickly built a rough stone fort there and quietly lived in it.

For many incredibly long, bitter years Samvarana sadly lived deep in the harsh wilderness, quietly nursing the terrible, painful wound of his massive defeat.

Then, one quiet day, the venerable, incredibly famous sage, Vasishtha, unexpectedly visited the hidden fort. Samvarana happily received him with absolute reverence.

"Great, holy one," Samvarana wept, washing the sage's feet. "We have tragically lost our beloved kingdom. We are completely unable to properly practise our royal dharma in this wild forest."

Samvarana folded his hands tightly. "Please, generously be our royal guru, revered sage. Your holy presence and your powerful prayers will surely magically help us quickly regain our lost kingdom."

Vasishtha smiled gently. "So safely be it."

"I am indeed incredibly blessed," Samvarana sighed in pure relief. 

"Blessed did you actually say, Samvarana?" Vasishtha frowned sharply. "That proud king is truly blessed who bravely rules entirely over the grand kingdom of his noble forefathers in all absolute dignity and royal honour... Not one who cowardly lives hiding safely in forest exile."

"Revered one..." Samvarana started, his face turning red with shame. 

"Exactly why have you weakly allowed your past defeat to completely shatter your spirit so?" Vasishtha demanded angrily. "Bravely challenge the cruel Panchalas to a massive battle and forcefully regain your rightful throne like a true warrior."

Vasishtha stood up. "My simple presence here will absolutely not work easy miracles specifically for you, Samvarana. It is strictly by your very own fierce efforts that your deep desire will ever be successfully fulfilled."

Samvarana's eyes flashed with sudden, fiery resolve. "Great sage, your incredibly wise words give me massive courage. This very, exact day, I will immediately start eagerly making grand preparations for brutal war."

"Good!" Vasishtha smiled proudly. "I will strongly pray for your absolute victory."

Filled with massive, new energy, Samvarana successfully rallied his loyal soldiers and proudly declared sudden war on the completely surprised Panchalas. He violently fought like one divinely inspired. 

High up in the bright heavens, Samvarana's incredible vigour and brilliant valour completely won the deep admiration, and the very heart of the incredibly beautiful Tapati, the divine daughter of Surya (the Sun-God).

"How incredibly brave he is!" Tapati gasped, watching him fight below. "And so incredibly strong and unbelievably handsome too!"

Taken completely by surprise but definitely not displeased, Lord Surya smiled warmly to himself and looked gently at her glowing face. 

"Tapati, my sweet child..." Surya called. 

"Um?" Tapati mumbled, not taking her eyes off Samvarana. 

"Tapati, look directly at me," Surya laughed softly. "...Or do you completely have bright eyes for absolutely none else today?"

Tapati blushed bright red. 

"Never before have I ever seen you so completely entranced by the mere sight of a simple mortal," Surya smiled. "Do you truly passionately desire him for a husband, my child?"

"Yes, loving father," Tapati admitted shyly. 

"But, father... he is a mortal... I am divine... how do I even know if he would love me?" she worried.

"We will definitely very soon comfortably find out," Surya promised. 

Samvarana successfully won the massive, bloody war and proudly ascended his grand throne once more in absolute glory. 

One peaceful day, months later, Samvarana excitedly set out on a grand hunt with a few close companions. 

"Look—a swift deer!" Samvarana shouted. "After it!"

The massive chase began. Samvarana, riding his incredibly fast horse, very soon completely left all the others far behind... and quickly found himself riding absolutely all alone on the thickly wooded mountainside. 

"How incredibly fiercely Lord Surya shines so brightly today!" Samvarana panted, wiping sweat from his brow. "I am completely exhausted. My poor horse absolutely must be so, too."

It unfortunately was. For the overworked horse suddenly tottered weakly on its legs and violently fell heavily to the ground, dead. 

Samvarana gently stroked his dead horse. "My poor, loyal one, it has tragically been far too much for your brave heart. Come," he said to himself, standing up, "let us quickly look for a cool stream."

At a sparkling mountain stream, Samvarana thankfully drank his absolute fill. 

Then, as Samvarana slowly rose to his tired feet... he was completely dazzled by an incredibly brilliant, glowing sight. It was a beautiful woman—the absolute most incredibly radiant one he had ever, ever set mortal eyes on. 

Bathed completely in her divine, glowing splendour, the very green creepers and plants around her seemed to have magically turned into solid, shining gold. 

*Such an incredibly great, flawless beauty absolutely must have been perfectly fashioned by brilliantly churning out the absolute best from Deva, Asura and man!* Samvarana thought in pure awe. *Or is she the magnificent Goddess Sri (Lakshmi - the Goddess of Wealth) herself magically visiting the earth?*

Samvarana found himself unconsciously walking in a pure daze directly towards her. 

"O absolutely flawless one, exactly who are you?" Samvarana whispered. "Absolutely no glowing Deva, fierce Asura or beautiful human maiden I have ever seen can possibly compare with you in absolute beauty!"

She smiled softly, her jewels sparkling in the sunlight. 

"Though beautifully decked out in perfect jewels, you effortlessly emerge the absolute most coveted jewel of them entirely all," he praised. "You are infinitely lovelier than the bright moon."

He fell to his knees. "I am... completely filled... with... with intense longing..."

But exactly as he reached out his hand... she was completely gone! Magically vanished into thin air!

Completely driven to absolute distraction by her incredibly sudden disappearance, Samvarana desperately wandered hither and thither completely through the thick forest, frantically searching exactly for her. 

"Where are you!" he cried, until finally, utterly exhausted... he heavily fainted. 

At long last, as he lay completely dejected and deeply grief-stricken on the forest floor... 

"Rise, O noble best of kings!" a gentle, musical voice called. "It is absolutely not remotely proper for you, who are so incredibly famed throughout the entire land, to so easily lose your royal composure thus."

Samvarana gasped and sat up. She had returned! 

"I absolutely cannot live completely without you, O beautiful one," Samvarana begged passionately. "Be my loving wife! Become entirely mine... right now... by the swift Gandharva rites! It is famously said to be the absolute best of all forms of marriage."

Tapati smiled gently but shook her beautiful head. "O noble king, I respectfully do completely not wish to secretly wed you by the private Gandharva rites (a private contract as opposed to a marriage with proper Vedic rites). I safely have a very loving, powerful father..."

"...Who will definitely absolutely not proudly stand in our way," she promised softly. "Properly approach him and respectfully ask for my hand in formal marriage."

"I will!" Samvarana agreed instantly. "Where is he?"

"Approach him purely through deep penance and holy meditation. For..." Tapati pointed gracefully up at the blinding, bright sun. "...My great father is the supreme illuminator of the entire universe. I am Tapati, the proud daughter of Lord Surya."

"Tapati!" Samvarana gasped in pure awe. 

Samvarana immediately sat firmly down exactly where he was, crossed his legs, and fiercely began to deeply worship the Sun-god constantly by bright day and by dark night, without food or water. 

On the gruelling twelfth day of his intense, fiery penance, his royal guru Vasishtha suddenly came flying to him from the sky. 

"Samvarana, I perfectly know exactly what your incredibly deep desire is," Vasishtha smiled proudly at his dedicated student. "It absolutely shall be successfully fulfilled."

Vasishtha raised his holy hands. "I shall safely in person directly approach the glowing Sun-god for you."

Vasishtha magically flew straight up into the blinding sky. 

"I am the sage Vasishtha, O mighty Surya," he announced before the Sun-god. "The incredibly noble King Samvarana respectfully solicits the beautiful hand of your divine daughter Tapati in formal marriage."

Lord Surya smiled brightly. "O holy Sage, when the absolute best of mortal monarchs so respectfully seeks the beautiful hand of the absolute best of divine women, exactly what could possibly be more incredibly proper than generously blessing their perfect union."

Later, when the exhausted Samvarana finally opened his eyes and looked hopefully up into the bright sky, his heart completely overflowed with immense gratitude and absolute joy as he saw Vasishtha safely returning with Tapati. 

"O revered Sage," Samvarana wept happily. "First you brilliantly inspired me to bravely win back my lost kingdom. Now you have generously helped my incredibly intense penance wonderfully bear sweet fruit."

Vasishtha blessed them both warmly. "May you happily both comfortably live completely together in absolute, perfect harmony and eternal bliss."

And there safely on the quiet mountainside, in the holy presence of the glowing gods and the wise sages, King Samvarana proudly married the divine Tapati, the incredibly beautiful daughter of the mighty Sun-god.


# Tapati-The Daughter of the Sun God - 2. Ruru and Pramadvara

One incredibly hot, sunny day, a tired group of young men came walking up to a quiet hermitage deep in the forest. 

"We are incredibly tired and terribly thirsty," one young man said, wiping his brow. 

"Will someone please kindly give us some cool water?" another called out towards the small hut. 

Inside the hut, a beautiful young girl was gently nursing a wounded pigeon. "Wait safely here, my sweet little one, till I quickly attend to them," she whispered to the bird. 

She opened her hands. "No. You are completely well now. You may happily fly away."

The pigeon fluttered its wings and flew directly out the window, right past the waiting young men. 

The beautiful girl stepped out of the hut, holding a clay water pot. She looked up and smiled at one incredibly handsome young man standing at the front. "Did my little one completely startle you?" she asked, her voice like soft music.

The young man, whose name was Ruru, was completely stunned by her incredible beauty. He simply stared at her, completely speechless. 

She frowned slightly. "What's the matter? Why do you simply stare at me so? Didn't you desperately want some water?"

"Oh... y... yes," Ruru stammered, his face turning bright red as he took the cool clay pot from her hands. 

He drank deeply. "That was incredibly the absolute sweetest water I have absolutely ever tasted!" he sighed. 

She laughed softly. "You were just very incredibly thirsty, sir."

The young men formally thanked her and slowly walked away back into the forest. 

"Hey, Ruru! Exactly what are you so deeply thinking about?" his friend asked, waving a hand in front of Ruru's dazed face. 

"Uh? Oh, absolutely nothing," Ruru lied, but he kept looking back over his shoulder at the quiet hermitage. 

Ruru was the proud son of the respected Brahmana, Pramati. 

That late evening, when he and his friends safely met in his room—

"I will simply absolutely have to openly tell them," Ruru sighed happily, leaning his chin on his hand. "How else will I safely let my father proudly know!"

"I absolutely cannot completely forget her. That beautiful face..." Ruru sighed. 

"Which face? Exactly whose beautiful face?" his confused friend asked. 

"That lovely girl's face..."

"Exactly which girl?" another friend demanded. 

"The beautiful girl safely at the quiet hermitage..." Ruru smiled. 

"Exactly which hermitage?" 

Ruru threw a pillow at them. "Don't cruelly tease me. You perfectly know exactly who I sincerely mean!"

His friends laughed loudly. "We definitely do; we do very well, indeed."

"Please, formally tell my father that I would absolutely love to happily marry her. Will you generously do that for me?" Ruru pleaded. 

The very next day, when the wise Pramati happily heard the incredibly good news directly from Ruru's friends—

"I am incredibly pleased to happily know that my brave son has finally successfully chosen a beautiful bride completely for himself," Pramati smiled. "Come, let us all formally go directly to the quiet hermitage at once."

The beautiful girl, meanwhile, was happily busy gently tending her green plants outside her hut. 

"This strong wooden stick will definitely make you safely grow incredibly straight and tall. There!" she smiled, tying a small plant to a stick. 

She looked up and gasped. "Oh... it's... it's him!" she blushed, seeing Ruru arriving with his father. 

Pramati walked respectfully up to the old sage who ran the hermitage. 

"Sthulakesha," Pramati bowed. "I have formally come to respectfully ask for the beautiful hand of your lovely daughter specifically for my dear son, Ruru."

Sthulakesha smiled warmly. "Pramati, my old friend, I could honestly happily wish for absolutely no infinitely better husband specifically for my sweet child."

Sthulakesha sighed softly. "But you must also absolutely truthfully know that she is strictly my daughter simply by adoption. One quiet day I was peacefully walking slowly along the muddy bank of a rushing river when..."

There were absolutely none happier completely in that entire forest that incredibly bright day, than Ruru and Pramadvara.

But incredibly little did poor Ruru completely suspect that cruel fate would tragically soon abruptly end his immense happiness with exactly one incredibly cruel, violent stroke. It tragically happened just a few short weeks completely before the happy day of the grand wedding. Pramadvara was happily playing blind-man's buff with her laughing friends. 

"I am Ruru," one friend teased, dodging away from the blindfolded Pramadvara. "Come here, Pramadvara."

"Ha, ha, ha!" the girls laughed happily.

The snake struck violently. "AAAAH!" Pramadvara screamed in agony.

Sthulakesha ran out of his hut. "NO!" he wept, falling to his knees beside her.

Then, exactly as if terrible death, completely with its absolute worst, cruel weapons, absolutely could not permanently crush her pure beauty, Pramadvara's completely lifeless, pale body miraculously bloomed beautifully again and incredibly looked even lovelier than it had in bright life. 

Exactly like a sweet blossom, brutally withered by a cruel, sharp wind, Pramadvara lay quietly there, completely crumpled, pale and completely dead.

The terrible news spread rapidly. Completely filled with massive, deep compassion, the wise dwellers of that entire forest, the great sages Kushika, Uddalaka, Bharadwaja, Goutama and absolutely many others, came sadly to the quiet hermitage. 

"Take brave heart, O Sthulakesha," they comforted the weeping father. 

"Poor child!"
"Poor, completely innocent child!"

Just then, Ruru arrived. When he saw her dead body, he completely collapsed in absolute shock. 

"Ruru, exactly why has this terrible thing completely happened? Why?" Sthulakesha wept. 

Ruru couldn't even speak. He completely turned and desperately left the incredibly sad place... and blindly, frantically made his desperate way deep into the darkest part of the forest. And exactly there—

"Woeful and utterly miserable am I that strictly with these very eyes I tragically saw my beautiful bride completely lying pale and dead," Ruru wept bitterly, falling to his knees. 

"She who gently healed other living, wounded creatures is now tragically completely beyond absolutely all earthly healing," he sobbed.

"If I have ever been truly charitable; if I have ever successfully observed the sacred, difficult penance; if I have absolutely ever shown completely proper respect to my noble elders... Let the holy merit of exactly these pure acts completely restore my beloved bride safely to life!"
