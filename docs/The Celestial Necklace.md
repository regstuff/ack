# The Celestial Necklace - 1. The Celestial Necklace

King Shrenika of Rajagriha was a pious king who had done many good deeds. One day a Deva appeared before him and rewarded him with a necklace of precious stones. 

"Treat this necklace with care..." the Deva warned him, "...for if it breaks, the man who repairs it, will die at once!"

King Shrenika was very pleased to receive the necklace and he gave it to his favourite wife, Queen Chellana. 

"It shines like the stars in the Milky Way—it's heavenly!" she gasped. "I got it as a gift from a Deva," the king said. 

"Look after it well," he cautioned. 
"I will. Never, even for a single day, will I part with my necklace!" she promised. 

Queen Chellana wore the necklace every day. But one morning, as the Queen was about to put on the necklace... the string gave way. 

"Oh, my beautiful necklace!" she cried. The Queen's maid carefully picked up the precious stones strewn on the floor. 

"The king will be angry when he hears of this!" the maid said. 

"Don't worry, my queen. He will soon have it repaired," the king's servants tried to console her. 

But when the king was informed, he was very disturbed. "I don't think we can have your necklace repaired," he sighed. 

"But why not?" she asked. 

"Because the Deva, whose gift it was, warned me that the man who repairs the necklace will die the moment the work is done. It is out of the question. I can't risk the life of any of my subjects!" the king explained firmly. 

"You are saying this only to avoid paying a reward for the repair of the necklace!" the Queen accused him angrily, walking away. 

The king turned helplessly to his minister, Abhay Kumar. "Women are so unreasonable! How can I lie about the fate awaiting the man who repairs this necklace? Who would then be so foolhardy as to accept such an offer?" 

"The fate that awaits whoever repairs the necklace must not be hidden, my king," Abhay Kumar agreed. "Issue a proclamation any way, and then let's see what happens."

The next day the king's criers announced the reward— "One lakh of gold coins for stringing Queen Chellana's necklace! But, remember, the man who completes this task will die immediately after."

"One lakh! Just for stringing a necklace? I'll do it!" a man shouted excitedly, before hearing the condition. 
"Oh! Then it's not for me!" he quickly retracted. 

For a long time no one came forward to do the work. Then an old goldsmith who was very poor, decided to accept the king's offer. 

*I am at the end of my life in any case. If I die to earn the reward, my dear sons will benefit!* the old man thought bravely. 

So the goldsmith asked for permission to speak to the king. 

"Friend, I am happy you have agreed to do this service for the queen," the king said softly. The king turned to his guard. "Give him the casket containing the precious necklace. Here is a part of the reward. The rest will be paid when the work is completed. I hope you succeed."

"With God's help, my king, I hope to succeed," the goldsmith bowed. 

The goldsmith returned home with the casket and told his sons what he had decided to do. 

"Father, don't do it!" his sons pleaded. "Your life is worth a lot more to us than the money."

"Don't try to stop me, my sons. My mind is made up," the goldsmith said firmly. "Only remember, when I have completed my task and I am no more, take the necklace to the king and claim the rest of the reward."

The goldsmith then shut the door and began his work. Spreading the sparkling jewels in front of him, he prepared to thread them. But—

"Oh! Oh! This is an unexpected problem," he gasped, examining a stone. "It's impossible to string these stones. The holes are so tiny, only an ant could go through them!"

He paused, then smiled. "...An ant! That's it! I have an idea!"

"First, I'll dip one end of the string in honey... and then I'll place it on the ground like this, with the precious stones in a row. Now, let's see if my plan works," he whispered. 

After a while, an ant came by... picked up the honey-coated string... and passed with it through the tiny holes in the precious stones! 

"Perfect! My plan is perfect!" the goldsmith rejoiced. 

When the ant finally emerged with the string from the last stone... the goldsmith gently picked up the two ends and fitted up the clasp. 

He held up his beautiful handiwork and rejoiced in it for just one moment. 

The next moment, he was dead. 

The four sons of the goldsmith mourned the death of their father. "Poor father! He loved us more dearly than life itself!"

After some time, remembering their father's last words, the four boys took the celestial necklace to King Shrenika. 

"The necklace has been repaired! Good! The queen will be delighted!" the king smiled. 

"Then may we have the rest of the reward?" one of the sons asked respectfully. 

"Reward? But where is the old man who repaired the necklace?" the king frowned. 

"The old goldsmith was my father. As soon as he had repaired the necklace, he died, as was fated. He told us to collect the money from you after his death."

"But how do I know you are really his sons? For all I know you may have stolen the necklace from the poor old man!" the king declared suspiciously. "Go away, before I have you put into my dungeons!"

"But, sir...!" 

Sadly, the goldsmith's sons left the king's palace. "One is powerless against a king...."

Some years later, they were passing through a dense forest. 

"Poor father! If he knew the king refused to pay us the money he would be very unhappy," one of them sighed. 

Now, the old goldsmith had been reborn as a monkey. This monkey chanced to be sitting on a tree above and, hearing them, the memory of his past life flashed upon him with blinding suddenness. 

*Ah, my dear sons! So the king has done you out of the reward I paid for so dearly!* the monkey realized angrily. *The king has no right to the necklace. Till he pays for the work, it belongs to my sons. I must get hold of the necklace.*

The monkey went to the palace, wondering how he could get hold of the necklace. *If I go into the palace, I will be spotted at once. I will wait outside near this tank.*

Not long after this, Queen Chellana came to the tank to bathe. She placed her necklace and other ornaments in the care of her maid. 

"Wait here till I return from my dip in the tank, Kapila. We won't be long."

The monkey patiently waited till the queen had left and the maid, Kapila, was alone. Then he swooped down so swiftly... and picked up the necklace so deftly... that the maid noticed absolutely nothing. 

The monkey then went directly to his old home... and threw in the necklace through the window! *CLUNK!*

"What's that?" the sons jumped up. "It's the celestial necklace!"

One of the goldsmith's sons sprang to the window and saw the monkey. "There's a monkey here! He must have brought the necklace!"

"Don't be alarmed son. It is I, your father. I was born again in this form...." the monkey explained softly. "Hide the necklace. The king has no right to it till he pays the promised reward."

"There's no need to be afraid. Do as I say and all will be well," the monkey assured them. "Farewell, my sons."

The boys decided to bury the necklace in the house. "We'll dig a hole here. This place should be quite safe. There! We've finished! Let's put the charpoy over it."

"Now the patch of dug earth is well hidden," they agreed happily. 

Meanwhile in the palace gardens— 

"Kapila, where's my necklace?" the Queen demanded. 
"Necklace...? It... it's gone...!" Kapila wept. 
"You are trembling, Kapila! Why such fear? I know you couldn't have taken it!" the Queen said kindly. "But the necklace was taken and you noticed nothing? Strange! Were you here all the time?"
"I've been sitting right here since you left!" Kapila swore. 

"I can't understand it. But the king will find the answer. Let's go back to the palace."

"The celestial necklace has been stolen? We'll find it soon enough," the King reassured her. "Such a unique necklace cannot be hidden for long."

He turned to his virtuous minister. "Don't you agree, Abhay Kumar?" 
"Yes. It should take less than a week to find the thief."
"Then get to work as soon as possible. Find the thief and recover the necklace. I will order an immediate search in the city."

Some time later— "No sign of the necklace yet?"
"No. We've ransacked the houses of all the known thieves... and we are certain they are innocent of this crime," the guards reported tiredly. "What's more, they were shocked that any one dared to take the celestial necklace. They know it is impossible to sell such a necklace."

Abhay Kumar reported to the king. "The necklace will be difficult to recover. Whoever took it, has no intention of selling it."

*Hmm.... The necklace hasn't been stolen by a regular thief, that's certain. Whoever took it had another motive for doing so,* the King thought deeply. 

As there seemed to be no other way of recovering the necklace, the king cleverly issued a proclamation. 

"There is a death sentence awaiting the man who took the queen's necklace..." the criers announced in the streets, "...but if he comes forward and surrenders the necklace, he will be granted a full pardon."

The monkey heard the proclamation from a nearby tree. *Oh! My sons shall not give up their claim so easily,* he worried. 

The monkey went to speak to his sons, and found them busy digging up the hole again. They had already heard of the king's proclamation. 

"We had better return the necklace before we are discovered!" they panicked. 
"Let's do it soon. I am afraid...."

"No, dear sons. Don't return it," the monkey advised calmly. "Give me the necklace. I'll take care of this matter."

The monkey carried away the necklace to the forest. All day long he hid in the hollow of a large tree. At sunset he ventured out again and came to a quiet park near a large temple. 

*I must think of a safe place to hide this necklace,* the monkey pondered. 

Just then, Acharya Suhasti, a renowned ascetic, peacefully emerged from the temple... and, safely standing under the exact same tree, was deeply lost in pure meditation.
