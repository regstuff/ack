# Bhagawat: The Krishna Avatar - The Upholder of Govardhana

Shukadeva, who was brilliantly narrating the story of Krishna to Pareekshit, said:
When the cowherd boys excitedly returned home and related the amazing exploits of Krishna and Balarama to the people of Vrindavana, they were completely struck with wonder. They slowly began to believe that two truly divine beings had been magically born in their humble midst.

Then the rainy season set in heavily. The earth, previously scorched dry by the fierce summer heat, was soon blanketed with lush, tender green grass. The dense forest trees, heavily laden with sweet blackberries, seemed to temptingly beckon Krishna and his joyful mates. 
On some warm days, they would sit comfortably on a large rock and eat their packed food together, cheerfully enjoying the gentle pitter-patter of the rain around them. But when the sudden showers became dangerously heavy, they would quickly run to the safe shelter of a deep cave, or cleverly hide in the dry hollow of an ancient tree, to completely escape their fury.

Eventually, the dark clouds dispersed and the stormy, howling winds became quiet. Now it was a pleasant autumn. The bright stars shone beautifully in the perfectly cloudless night skies. 
When the devoted gopikas (milkmaids) of Vrindavana heard the incredibly sweet, enchanting sound of Krishna's melodious flute drifting on the wind, they would instantly stop all their household work and listen in pure rapture. 

"Oh, that Krishna!" one gopika sighed. "How completely enchanting he is with that vibrant peacock feather in his dark curls, yellow wild flowers tucked in his ears, the golden cloth draped elegantly over his beautiful dark form, and the heavy garland of wonderfully fragrant flowers around his neck."
"When he gracefully plays his flute," another whispered, "even the wild beasts of the jungle stop completely still to hear him."
"Why talk of the birds and the beasts? Even the fast streams and rushing rivers seem to visibly respond to the magic melody of Krishna's flute."
"Leave alone the earth," a third marvelled, "even the fluffy clouds in the sky seem to be eager to closely protect Krishna. The tiny, cooling rain drops are exactly like little white flower offerings made respectfully to our Krishna."

When cold winter firmly set in, the unmarried maidens of Vraja sincerely observed their customary, strict prayers to the powerful goddess Durga. 
Happily singing and chatting, they would go together to bravely bathe in the freezing Yamuna at day-break, make a simple clay image of the goddess on the sandy bank, and pray fervently to it for a good husband.

One particularly cold day, they were playfully splashing about in the freezing water. 
When they looked to the bank, they gasped, "Look! Our warm clothes have entirely vanished!"
"How strange. We had definitely left them safely on the bank right there."
Just then they looked up and saw Krishna comfortably sitting astride the high branch of a leafy Kadamba tree nearby, holding all their colourful clothes. 
"Ha! Ha! Ha!" Krishna laughed teasingly. "Come up and collect your belongings."
"That's strictly not fair, Krishna," they shivered, embarrassed to leave the water. "Don't cruelly torment us so."

"Come, Krishna," they pleaded gently, "we know you are Nanda's darling son. All the people of Vraja absolutely dote on you. We too are ever willing to happily do your bidding. Please give them back."
"In that exact case," Krishna smiled, "you must humbly come here, bow deeply to me with folded hands, and take your belongings."

Shukadeva carefully explained to Pareekshit: 
"Krishna was deliberately trying to test the pure devotion of the gopikas for him. He teased and playfully taunted them, laughed loudly at their awkward plight, and made them act exactly like puppets at his total mercy. Yet they were absolutely not annoyed with him at all."

After they had humbly followed his instructions and collected their clothes from Krishna, he blessed them and said, "May your secret desires be all entirely fulfilled. Now return quickly to your homes, dear maidens, and await the beautifully moonlit nights of autumn."

***

One hot day when Krishna and the cowherds were peacefully grazing their cows, he said thoughtfully, "Behold these magnificent trees. They silently bear the severe, burning sun, the lashing wind and heavy rain, and yet they generously protect us. Leaves and beautiful flowers, thick bark and strong wood, sap and sticky gum, useful fuel and sweet fruits. Many indeed are the wonderful gifts the trees endlessly bestow on us. Truly blessed are the selfless people who give generously to others exactly like these noble trees."

After walking for miles, the boys approached the river bank. 
"Krishna, we have successfully quenched our deep thirst with the cool river water. But I am feeling incredibly hungry," a boy complained.
"Me too," the others agreed. "Krishna, Balarama, you must quickly think of a clever way to completely satisfy our raging hunger."

Krishna smiled and said, "A little distance from here, there is a large group of learned brahmanas proudly performing a complex yajna (fire sacrifice). Go tell them that Krishna and Balarama have sent you, and they will surely give you some delicious food."

The boys ran to the sacrificial hall. "We've been specifically sent by Krishna and Balarama. Do generously give us some food, O learned brahmanas!"
But the arrogant brahmanas entirely ignored the hungry boys and proudly continued chanting for their yajna. 
Sadly, the rejected cowherd boys returned to Krishna. 
"What happened? Didn't the wealthy brahmanas give you any food at all?" Krishna asked.
"They rudely said neither yes nor no. They just ignored us. We felt deeply disappointed," they sighed.

Krishna said with a loud laugh, "Just because you are unsuccessful once, you must not stop trying. Go again and meet their wives this time. They are deeply devoted to me in their hearts and will certainly give you plenty of food."

Obediently, the hopeful cowherds went back and respectfully requested the wives of the brahmanas. "Krishna and Balarama have personally sent us to you. They are very hungry and have humbly requested you for some food."
Just the mere mention of Krishna's beloved name was entirely enough. 
"For Krishna! Surely we will do absolutely anything to have a mere glimpse of him," the women cried.
"For so long we have eagerly heard of his great praise. Though we have never actually seen him, he has inexplicably become very dear to us."

Like fast rivers rushing joyously to meet the sea, the devoted women eagerly hurried to eagerly meet Krishna. They carried heavy vessels filled to the brim with delicious, aromatic food they had carefully cooked for the great yajna being performed by their husbands. 

When they finally beheld Krishna, shining brilliantly in his beautiful golden garments, with curly dark locks playfully framing his smiling, dusky face, they were absolutely delighted at the divine sight. 
With a playful, welcoming smile, Krishna said, "Welcome, dear ladies. I know your pure devotion for me has strongly drawn you here. Your deep love for me is indeed pure and entirely selfless. But now that you have happily met me, you must return quickly to your homes. Your presence is strictly necessary to properly complete the important yajna being performed by your husbands."

The women replied in despair, "Don't send us away! We have rushed to you, willingly abandoning everything. Our strict families may not accept us back now after we left the holy yajna. You are our only hope."
Krishna gently but firmly reassured them. "Rest assured, absolutely no one will lightly talk ill of you. Even the high celestials will greatly admire your true devotion. You may keep me constantly in your mind, but you must go back now."

Indeed, when their devoted wives peacefully returned to the sacrificial hall, the previously arrogant brahmanas unexpectedly accepted their return without any harsh comment and smoothly proceeded to complete the yajna. 
While Krishna and his happy companions happily feasted on the delicious food generously brought by the wives of the brahmanas... the learned brahmanas, who had foolishly ignored Krishna's simple request, were suddenly filled with deep remorse. 

"Fie on us!" they wept. "We arrogantly ignored the simple request of Lord Krishna himself. Of what absolute use is all our complex knowledge and learning? And look closely at the pure devotion of our simple wives for the Lord. These humble women have neither learnt the complicated vedas nor undergone strict penance. Yet they have utterly unshakeable faith in Lord Krishna. How incredibly lucky we are to have such wonderful wives."
Overcome with deep shame and bitter repentance, the learned brahmanas intensely longed to go meet Krishna and Balarama, but the terrible fear of Kamsa's wrath held them back.

***

In Vrindavana, Krishna noticed all the busy cowherds were energetically preparing for some massive yajna. Approaching his busy father, he asked curiously, "Father, what grand celebration are you all so busy preparing for? What is the actual merit of this yajna? What is its true purpose? Who are the specific people who will perform it? Do tell me, father."

Nanda patiently said, "Lord Indra is the powerful god of the rain clouds. He is the one who generously showers the necessary life-giving rain on us and our crops. We dutifully perform these grand yajnas to please him."
"Why, even the very materials used for the yajna are generously produced entirely because of the rain he sends. For several long generations we have been respectfully venerating Indra thus."

Krishna replied thoughtfully, "A living being is naturally born and dies strictly according to his own karma. Joy and deep sorrow, terrible fear and great prosperity are all an unavoidable outcome of karma. Why then do we mistakenly need Indra?"
"A man should practically worship whatever he actually lives by," Krishna argued logically. "For hardworking vaishyas like us, four main types of livelihood are possible—agriculture, trade, cattle-rearing, and money-lending. We are simple forest dwellers. For long, our hardworking people have expertly tended cattle. These deep forests and tall mountains are our true homes. Let us therefore gratefully worship our giving cows, and our protective mountains. Not a distant rain god."

"All these expensive things that have been carefully collected for Indra's yajna, let them be completely used for this other sacrifice instead," Krishna proposed confidently. "Summon well-versed, holy brahmanas to properly conduct the sacrifice. Let a truly sumptuous feast be prepared. We will respectfully make a grand offering to this majestic mountain here, Govardhana."
"Then, beautifully bedecking ourselves in fine garments and jewels, we will joyously go round the cows, the brahmanas, and Mount Govardhana in respect. This is my firm opinion, father. Such a unique sacrifice will please me immensely."

Nanda and the other listening cowherds were completely convinced by his incredible wisdom. 
"We will gladly do exactly as you say, Krishna."
"Yes, let's instantly start the new preparations."

The holy brahmanas respectfully lighted the holy fire and loudly recited the sacred mantras. The grateful cowherds generously presented the brahmanas with gifts. The cows were lovingly offered tender, incredibly lush grass. Various delicious offerings were also respectfully placed directly before Mount Govardhana. 
With Nanda proudly leading the way, the cowherds went respectfully round Govardhana. The gaily dressed gopikas sat on decorated bullock-carts and went round the mountain while happily singing songs in deep praise of Krishna. 

*I must completely convince them that their bold action is right,* Krishna thought to himself. *Let me magically assume another form.*
Krishna miraculously appeared in the awe-inspiring guise of an enormous, divine being right on the very top of Mount Govardhana. 
"I AM GOVARDHANA," the booming voice echoed. 

Before their very amazed eyes, the gigantic, divine form began to eagerly gobble up the massive offerings made to it. Feigning deep surprise, the boy Krishna down below said, "How incredibly wonderful! Behold how the great mountain has miraculously graced us with his divine presence."
"He can easily assume any form. He can completely destroy any one who disrespects him. For the total welfare of our precious cattle and ourselves, let us humbly bow before him."

Thus deeply inspired by Krishna, all the completely awed people of Vrindavana respectfully worshipped the mountain, the cows, and the brahmanas, and finally returned happily to their homes. 

However, high in the heavens, now that his customary worship had been abruptly discontinued, Lord Indra was very, very angry. 
"How absolutely dare they do this to me, the supreme lord of the three worlds!" he raged. 
He instantly summoned the terrifying, destructive masses of dark clouds called Samvartaka. 
"Behold the sheer arrogance of these lowly cowherds!" Indra roared to the clouds. "They are completely intoxicated with their newly found wealth. On the mere word of that boy Krishna, the foolish people of Vraja have deliberately insulted me and utterly failed to pay me my due homage."

"That incredibly ignorant, vain and talkative Krishna mistakenly thinks he is very wise," Indra sneered. "He has arrogantly encouraged the simple people of Vrindavana to actively defy me. I command you to completely destroy their immense vanity. Attack them! Kill their precious cattle! I will soon follow you with my devastating army of heavenly elephants, with the great Airavata leading the way."

With these furious words, Indra aggressively released the terrible clouds. Hastily they sped menacingly towards Vraja and suddenly inundated Vraja with an unbelievably heavy, continuous downpour. Awesome streaks of jagged lightning appeared with deafening crashes of thunder. A incredibly mighty gale blew violently across the land, viciously hurling huge hailstones in its devastating wake. Huge, solid columns of freezing water rained heavily down on Vraja, flooding it completely. 

Frightened by the terrifying deluge, violently shivering and completely drenched by the freezing rain, the desperate gopas and gopikas ran to Krishna for urgent help. 
"Krishna! Dear Krishna! You are our absolute only hope now. Please save us from Indra's terrible wrath!"

Seeing their pitiful plight, Krishna thought, *Surely, Indra has vindictively caused this unseasonal, incredibly heavy rain and hailstorm simply because we did not worship him. But I will immediately give him a perfectly fitting reply. This petty, arrogant behaviour doesn't befit a high deva like him. I must totally crush his false pride for his own good. I must absolutely save my devoted people.*

With the unbelievable ease of a small child effortlessly plucking a tiny mushroom, Krishna confidently reached for the massive Mount Govardhana. Playfully, Krishna lifted the entire mountain straight up effortlessly on the palm of his single left hand and said, "Come, quickly bring all your cattle and belongings. Take safe shelter completely under this mountain."

"Rest assured," Krishna smiled calmly. "Govardhana will absolutely not fall from my hands. I have purposefully done this to completely protect you from the terrible rain and storm."
Thus completely reassured by his calm confidence, the people of Vraja hurriedly gathered their terrified cattle, heavy carts, and other belongings, and gratefully took dry shelter safely under the hovering mountain. 

For seven incredibly long days and pitch-black nights, Krishna tirelessly carried aloft the massive Mount Govardhana on a single finger, without having any food or drink at all. 

Up in the heavens, Indra was utterly amazed to see Krishna's impossible feat. All his immense, foolish pride instantly vanished. Realising he was fighting the Supreme Lord, he quickly bade the dark clouds to finally stop their terrible downpour. 

Krishna clearly saw that the heavy rain and loud thunder had completely stopped and the warm sun was brightly shining again. 
"Go out, my dear folks!" Krishna announced happily. "The terrible storm has completely blown over, the deep flood water too has successfully receded."
Slowly, the incredibly relieved people began to safely emerge with their belongings from under the mountain. When the very last person was safely out, Krishna carefully and gently put the massive mountain exactly back in its original place. 

Filled with immense gratitude and deep affection, the saved people of Vraja rushed to excitedly thank Krishna. Yashoda, Rohini, and Nanda proudly hugged Krishna with tears in their eyes. 
This incredible event was passionately discussed over and over again at the lively village get-togethers. 
"The divine deeds of this Krishna are indeed wonderfully impossible. Exactly like a mighty elephant effortlessly plucking a light lotus flower, this mere seven-year-old boy lifted the entirety of Govardhana and held it up flawlessly for seven full days."

"Remember how he miraculously killed the wicked Pootana when he was a mere infant, with his tiny eyes hardly open?"
"And how incredibly, at just three months, he effortlessly overturned the heavily loaded cart?"
"He was just one short year old when he powerfully throttled the giant demon Trinavarta in the sky."
"How about his amazing feat of casually tearing completely asunder the massive demon heron, Bakasura."
"Remember the two ancient Arjuna trees he playfully uprooted?"

"Is it not a total wonder that an absolutely extraordinary boy exactly like him has been wonderfully born in our humble midst?" the elders marvelled. 
They finally approached Nanda. "Tell us, Nanda, why is it that your beautiful dusky boy is such a massive favourite of the entire village? Yes, Nanda, we are beginning to be deeply suspicious about the true nature of your son."

Nanda replied honestly, "Hear me well. I will finally remove all your lingering doubts. The great Sage Gargacharya has secretly told me that this divine boy of mine has numerous incredible names and forms. This boy will undoubtedly bring immense good fortune to you and your people. In stunning beauty, boundless fame, and ultimate glory, your incredible son is entirely equal to Lord Vishnu himself. So don't be amazed at his extraordinary deeds."

"Since that revealing day," an elder declared, "I consider this boy as a direct part of Vishnu."
"Now we totally understand!"
"No absolute wonder he is so unbelievably powerful."

***

Some days later, when Nanda devoutly observed the strict Ekadashi fast, after praying deeply all day, he stepped carefully into the freezing water of Yamuna for a holy bath in the late evening. 
When the waiting cowherds suddenly saw him disappear completely underwater, they cried out in panic. 
"Krishna! Krishna! Hurry! Rescue your father!"
"Hurry, Balarama, hurry!"

The dark time exactly between sundown and day-break was considered highly inauspicious since the dangerous demons were actively at large then. One of them, a servant of Varuna, had stealthily caught hold of Nanda and dragged him forcefully away into the depths. 

Hearing their frantic cries, Krishna rushed instantly to the spot and dived fearlessly into the dark water to directly confront Varuna, the powerful lord of the nether world. 
However, when Krishna arrived in the deep palace, he was not met with hostility. Filled with absolute awe and pure joy at the divine sight of Krishna, Varuna immediately bowed deeply down to respectfully worship him. 

"Accept my humble prayers, O Lord," Varuna pleaded. "Please generously forgive my foolish servant for ignorantly seizing your father in the dark. You are a divine witness to all our action."
Nanda, standing nearby, was completely amazed to see the unbelievable wealth and glittering riches of Varuna and the incredible splendour of his deep underwater palace. But he was even more amazed by Varuna's highly submissive attitude towards Krishna. *How incredibly respectfully and humbly the great Varuna approaches my little Krishna,* Nanda thought in awe. 

When Krishna safely returned with Nanda to Vraja, Nanda breathlessly related the amazing events to his friends. "...And then the mighty Varuna himself bowed low to my Krishna."
"Why, he is the Supreme Lord himself," they whispered in awe. "Will he ever fully reveal to us his true, divine self?"

Krishna instantly learnt what was deep in their minds, and soon, out of his immense grace, he revealed to them his limitless, infinite cosmic form. They were utterly filled with divine ecstasy at the awe-inspiring sight. 

***

On a perfectly clear, full moon night in beautiful autumn, Krishna sat under a tree and played an incredibly enchanting, magical tune on his flute. In the absolute still of the night, the incredibly lilting melody reached the eager ears of the gopikas. 

Casting aside all their worldly patience, societal fear, and deep embarrassment, the completely enchanted gopikas ran desperately out of their homes. Irresistibly drawn by the absolute spell of Krishna's divine flute, they hurried frantically to the river bank, their long, heavy earrings dangling wildly as if eagerly keeping rhythm with their fast pace. 

Even the busy gopikas who were deep in the midst of their pressing household chores left their urgent work completely unfinished and ran out. One literally left her home as the precious milk was boiling over on the fire. Another who was carefully adorning herself rushed out half-dressed, with her long tresses flying wildly and her dark kajal completely smeared. 

Even when the strict elders in their shocked families tried to forcefully restrain them, they paid absolutely no heed, and simply ran out. Those incredibly unlucky ones who were forcefully and physically confined at home, simply closed their eyes in despair and meditated deeply on Krishna and miraculously felt a deep, overwhelming sense of bliss anyway. 

Pareekshit interrupted the sage's narration in slight confusion. "But the devotion of the simple gopikas was not purely divine; it was mostly earthly love and desire."
Shukadeva replied gently, "All that is truly needed is to completely reach out for the Lord—be it through intense desire, fierce anger, or terrible fear. Whether it is a strong bond of pure affection, deep reverence, or simple friendship, it will inevitably bring you completely close to him."

When Krishna saw the breathless gopikas gathered happily around him, he said with a completely disarming, playful smile, "Welcome! What surprisingly brings you all here at this odd time of the night? What can I possibly do to please you?"
He pointed around. "The dark forest trees, heavily laden with sweet flowers, are indeed enchanting in the mellow, silvery light of the full moon. The cool breeze, gently caressing the river waves, is very soothing too. But do not foolishly linger here in the dangerous forest. Your worried husbands actively await you. Go safely back to your homes."

The eager gopikas were utterly disappointed and heartbroken with his completely unexpected, dismissive words. With their heads cast deeply down, they bit their lips hard to desperately control their flowing tears. 
In voices completely choked with heavy emotion, they pleaded to Krishna, "Please respectfully accept our pure devotion, O Krishna. It is no longer a secret to anyone that you have divinely appeared in Vraja solely for our ultimate protection. Please generously allow us to serve you."

With a sudden, brilliant smile, Krishna happily agreed. On the incredibly wide expanse of silvery, cool sands near the flowing Yamuna, Krishna began to happily sport with the delighted gopikas. Shining brilliantly, exactly like a bright moon surrounded by glowing stars, Krishna joyfully sang and danced beautifully with the gopikas. Delighted completely with his undivided attentions, each gopika foolishly thought herself to be the absolute best and most favored woman on earth. 

Noting their sudden, foolish pride, Krishna simply vanished entirely from their midst. 
When the gopikas suddenly became aware of his total absence, they were instantly filled with deep despair and frantic anxiety. 
"Where is Krishna?"
"Why, he was right here just a moment ago!"

In their desperate, tearful search for Lord Krishna, they completely lost their senses and even talked to the silent trees. 
"O sacred peepul! Have you by chance seen the beloved son of Nanda, the beautiful one with a lovely, enchanting smile?"
"Kachnar, champak, jamun, kadamba! Please tell us where to quickly find Krishna."

The frantic gopikas were so entirely absorbed in Krishna that they momentarily forgot themselves completely, and entirely identified with him. 
"I am Krishna!" one suddenly declared. "Don't be afraid of the terrible rain. I'll protect you," she said, perfectly enacting the lifting of Govardhana. 
"I am baby Krishna, and you are the heavy cart," another giggled. "I'll simply cry and effortlessly overturn you with my tiny feet."
Another passionately played the role of Krishna effortlessly overpowering the giant serpent Kaliya. "O vile serpent, go away! Don't you know I have divinely taken birth solely to punish the wicked?"

As they were aimlessly wandering in the dark forests in desperate search of Krishna... 
"Wait! See these clear footsteps in the mud," one pointed. "Yes, they are definitely Krishna's."
"Let's eagerly follow them."
"But, look, there is another entirely different track right beside his." "Yes, they are a girl's delicate footprints."
"Let's go quickly ahead and see who his absolute favourite actually is."
"Look here! The deep marks of Krishna standing tall on tip-toe. He must have been lovingly plucking high flowers for her exactly here."
Krishna had deliberately wanted to teach a harsh lesson to the proud women and had indeed purposely gone away with just one of them. 

The single gopika who had fortunately accompanied Krishna, was utterly elated with pride. *I am indeed the absolute best,* she gloated inwardly. *I am Krishna's only chosen one.*
Completely sure of her total sway over Krishna, she complained lazily, "Oh Krishna, I simply can't walk any more. You will have to physically carry me."
"Yes, dear, I gladly will," Krishna agreed smoothly. 

And instantly, Krishna completely disappeared from under her. Realising in shock that it was her foolish vanity that had directly made her lose Krishna, she was filled with bitter remorse and began to weep. "O Lord! Where are you? Please don't leave me alone."

By the time her frantic companions finally reached the spot following the tracks, she had fallen completely in a deep swoon. When they revived her, she wept, "I foolishly insulted him out of sheer vanity, so he rightly disappeared."
The desperate gopikas went further and even further in the dark in search of Krishna. 
"The dark forest is very, very dense beyond this point." "Yes, it's far too dark to see. Let's go safely back."

They sadly returned to the moonlit banks of Yamuna and waited patiently, singing beautiful songs in deep praise of Krishna. "O Krishna, how we incredibly long to eagerly see you again. We humbly beseech you to quickly come back to us."
Filled with unbearable anxiety, many of them finally burst into bitter tears. 

Just then—at the pathetic sight of their tears, their beloved Krishna suddenly reappeared. The overjoyed gopikas rushed frantically towards him. Exactly like a dedicated ascetic who has finally attained ultimate salvation, so did the devoted gopikas feel entirely enraptured at the divine sight of Krishna. 

Spreading their soft veils respectfully for him to sit on, they completely surrounded Krishna and asked him a deep question. 
"Krishna, some people selfishly love only those who love them back. There are others who generously love even those who do not love them. While some cold people cannot love any one at all. Which conduct is truly correct, according to you?"

Krishna smiled and answered wisely. "Those who love, purely expecting to be loved in return, are merely selfish. Others who generously love, without expecting anything at all in return, are truly selfless and compassionate. Their pure love is exactly like that of the devoted parents for their child. And yes, some cold people in the world seem to be utterly incapable of love. But my dear gopikas, I absolutely do not belong to any of these three types."
"If I briefly appear and unexpectedly disappear from amidst you, it is only to deeply test your true devotion. See how incredibly dear I became to you when I briefly went away? I am truly, deeply touched by your pure affection."

The gopikas were now completely reassured, and stood joyfully in a massive circle entirely around Krishna. 
Krishna now began his magical, divine Ras-lila dance. Miraculously, there magically appeared one distinct Krishna dancing exactly by the side of each and every gopika. The overjoyed gopikas, each and every one of them, were absolutely elated to have their beloved Krishna dancing intimately right next to them. 

The gentle tinkle of their glass bangles, the sweet sounds of the tiny bells on their waists, and the heavy anklets on their lovely feet, completely filled the night air and provided perfect accompaniment to their lilting, joyous song. Faster and much faster they danced, as their heavy tresses loosened completely and their veils fluttered wildly in the cool breeze. 

When they finally felt completely exhausted with the joyous effort, Krishna tenderly and lovingly wiped the heavy perspiration from their tired faces and gently led them to the incredibly cooling, refreshing water of the Yamuna. The absolutely delighted gopikas sported happily in the cool river and merrily splashed water playfully on Krishna. 

After finally emerging from the river, Krishna and the gopikas roamed happily about in the beautiful grove nearby. The incredibly sweet scent of night flowers and the gentle, cool breeze completely refreshed them. 
Reluctant as they naturally were to ever go, Krishna then gently bade the gopikas to finally return to their sleeping homes. 

Pareekshit, still slightly puzzled, said to the sage, "Surely, Lord Krishna was divinely born to completely eliminate terrible sin from the earth and firmly establish dharma (righteousness). How could he then surprisingly behave thus playfully with the married gopikas?"

Shukadeva gently but firmly explained, "We absolutely should not judge the complex actions of the Supreme Lord by our simple earthly standards. He neither gains any merit by doing certain 'good' deeds, nor does the terrible stigma of 'sin' ever attach itself to him. He simply appears in human form and gracefully performs certain acts or 'lilas' (divine play) purely to deeply inspire absolute faith and devotion in simple people."

"Thus ends the third incredible session of our rendering of the tenth chapter of the Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded.
