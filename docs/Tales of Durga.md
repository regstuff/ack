# Tales of Durga - 1. Durga - The Slayer of Mahisha

The incredibly powerful Asura, Mahisha, once performed severe and painful penances for many years to propitiate Lord Brahma, the creator of the universe. 

Pleased with his intense austerities, Brahma finally appeared before him in a flash of divine light.

"Lord, make me immortal," Mahisha immediately demanded, bowing before the god.

Brahma shook his head. "All who are born must die. You cannot escape death. Ask for something else."

Mahisha became thoughtful. *What shall I do? Have I performed all those difficult penances for nothing? Ah! I have it!* he thought with a wicked smile.

"If I must die, lord, let it be only at the hands of a woman," Mahisha requested.

"So be it," Brahma granted, and he left for his celestial abode.

As Brahma disappeared, Mahisha threw his head back and laughed loudly. *How can a woman, a helpless, weak creature, kill me?* he thought triumphantly. *You have as good as granted me the absolute boon of immortality!*

Jubilantly, Mahisha hurried back to the nether world and addressed his waiting armies. "From now on, the devas are completely powerless against me," he roared to his men. "On to heaven! Victory to Mahisha Asura!"

As Mahisha and his massive, terrifying army approached heaven, the heavenly guards panicked. "The Asuras! Quick! Indra must be informed!"

A little later, Lord Indra rushed out of Amaravati, his beautiful capital, leading his own divine army. 

"You can't go any farther, O Mahisha. Turn back," Indra warned the demon king. "You've tried time and again to take Amaravati and have failed. Why do you persist?"

"Indra, I've come to fight, not to talk," Mahisha sneered. "Use your weapons instead of your tongue!"

Stung by Mahisha's mocking words, Indra darted forward, brandishing his powerful weapon—the thunderbolt. "Attack, O Devas. Let no asura escape!" he yelled.

Then ensued a fierce, bloody battle between the devas and the asuras. But empowered by his new boon, Mahisha was unstoppable. Severely beaten and completely overwhelmed, the devas had to run for their lives, abandoning their heavenly home.

After driving the devas out of Amaravati, Mahisha proudly ascended Indra's throne. 

"I am the Lord of Lords," Mahisha declared to his followers. "Henceforth I alone shall be worshipped in the three worlds. The worship of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva should be stopped forthwith."

Mahisha's cruel men spread across the earth, destroying temples and beginning to brutally harass those pious men who continued to worship Vishnu or Shiva. 

"Worship Mahisha or die!" the asuras threatened the sages. "Replace that Shiva-Linga with an idol of Mahisha! Or else..."

"O God of Gods! When will you save us from this terrible plight?" the humans and devas cried in despair.

Meanwhile, unknown to the asuras, the defeated devas had already assembled at Kailasa, the snowy abode of Lord Shiva.

"We are totally helpless against Mahisha," Indra pleaded with the great gods. "Will evil triumph over righteousness?"

Even as the devas spoke, an intense, blinding light issued forth from the enraged faces of Shiva, Vishnu, and Brahma. It was joined by similar brilliant rays of light from Indra and all the other devas. 

And lo! The next moment, out of that concentrated, divine light, a magnificent, beautiful female form with a thousand arms came into being! It was Devi Durga, the Supreme Goddess.

"O Devi, you are the origin of all the worlds," the devas bowed before her. "You are Durga. You are the inner force, the absolute source of all strength. Destroy Mahisha and protect us!"

Shiva drew forth a flaming trident from his own trident and offered it to Durga. Vishnu and the other devas, too, offered their most powerful celestial weapons to her. 

"O Devi, accept this discus," Vishnu said.
"And this thunderbolt," Indra offered.

Armed with the mighty celestial weapons and mounted on a fierce, golden lion, Durga emitted a terrifying, blood-curdling roar. 

"I'll seek out and destroy the evil Mahisha!" she declared.

Her roar was so powerful that the seas trembled... the earth shook, and the mountains rocked. 

In his stolen palace in Amaravati, Mahisha's heart missed a beat. "What's that?" he gasped, feeling an unfamiliar spike of fear. He rushed out of his palace to find out.

When he saw the goddess, he laughed in relief. "Ho! A mere female!"

"No! I am no mere female!" Durga called out, her eyes blazing. "I have come to fulfil Brahma's boon. You wanted to die at the hands of a woman, didn't you?"

Mahisha hastily stepped back, a chill running down his spine. He called to his men. "Get her! Kill her!"

As the thousands of asuras hurled various deadly weapons at her, Durga simply breathed hard. As she exhaled, thousands of fully-armed divine soldiers came into being from her breath and fought at her side. 

Her golden mount strode fearlessly through Mahisha's army, destroying thousands of asuras with its sharp claws and teeth. 

Mahisha seethed with fury. "I'll annihilate this monster and her hordes!" he roared. 

Assuming the magical form of a colossal buffalo, Mahisha charged forth, snorting and bellowing. He brutally trampled upon Durga's men and lashed at them with his heavy tail. 

Enraged, Durga flung a magical noose at him and caught him tight. But Mahisha shook himself with all his might, instantly transformed himself into a ferocious lion, and pounced upon Durga. 

As Durga swiftly chopped off the head of the lion with her sword, Mahisha rose in his own true, demonic form and charged at her again. 

Later in the fierce battle, he assumed the massive form of an elephant and violently tugged at Durga's lion mount with his heavy trunk. Durga cleanly lopped off the trunk with her sword. 

Bleeding, Mahisha again assumed the form of the giant buffalo and snorted with incredible rage, preparing for another charge.

Durga looked down at him from her lion with utter contempt. "Snort, O fool! In a moment, the devas will roar in triumph at this very place, when you are slain!"

Enraged beyond reason, Mahisha rushed blindly at her. But Durga gracefully leapt from her lion directly upon him and pinned the massive buffalo firmly down with her foot. 

Mahisha violently struggled to free himself from her divine grip. As his true demonic form—half of him—began to emerge from the mouth of the buffalo to escape, Durga raised her heavy, gleaming sword. 

With one mighty swing, she struck. The next moment, Mahisha fell dead at the foot of Durga. 

The watching devas were overjoyed. "O Durga, upholder of virtue, destroyer of evil, we humbly salute you!" they cheered. "O Devi, continue to protect us!"


# Tales of Durga - 2. Chamundi

On another occasion, years after Mahisha's defeat, the devas were once again driven out of heaven, this time by Shumbha, the powerful new Lord of the Asuras.

Chanda and Munda, Shumbha's two most trusted and cruel commanders, secretly followed the fleeing devas. 

"Let's find out where they go and why," Chanda whispered to Munda as they tracked the gods through the mountains.

The devas went to the holy Mount Himavat and began to pray fervently to Durga. "O Durga, protector of the virtuous, destroy the evil Shumbha and restore righteousness to heaven," they chanted.

Just then, Parvati, Shiva's gentle consort, who was on her way to the river to fetch water, happened to pass by the praying devas. Hearing their desperate pleas, she paused. 

And lo! The very next moment, the fierce Goddess Ambika (a powerful incarnation of Durga) magically sprang forth directly from the body of Parvati! She stood before the devas, radiant and incredibly beautiful, armed and ready for battle.

Hiding behind the rocks, Chanda and Munda were stunned. 

"What a breath-taking beauty! We should inform Shumbha," Chanda gasped.

"Let's run to our master," Munda agreed.

When they rushed to the palace of Shumbha and described Ambika's unparalleled beauty to him, the arrogant asura king's eyes gleamed with desire.

"That beautiful one shall be mine! Bring her to me!" Shumbha commanded.

Chanda and Munda rushed back to Mount Himavat and approached the goddess. "O beautiful one," Chanda said smoothly, "Shumbha, the Lord of the three worlds who possesses unequalled wealth, wants to marry you."

Ambika smiled a mysterious smile. "I've taken a strict vow that I'll marry only him who can conquer me in battle and humble my pride."

Munda scoffed at the delicate-looking woman. "Do you truly seek to battle with the great asura who has vanquished even the mighty devas? Be wise and submit gracefully to our master."

"I know I am foolish," Ambika replied, pretending to sigh, "but I am bound by the ill-considered vow taken long ago. He must defeat me."

"We'll inform our master," the commanders growled, turning back.

When Chanda and Munda conveyed Ambika's message to Shumbha, he was furious. "If it's battle she wants, she shall have it," he roared. Then he turned to another of his fierce commanders, Dhumralochana. "Go, and bring her to me."

Dhumralochana took a massive army to Mount Himavat and aggressively confronted Ambika. "If you don't go to my lord of your own free will, I'll have to take you to him by force," he threatened.

Ambika pretended to be afraid, shrinking back. "Alas! Spare me. I am a helpless woman."

"Then marry our lord!" Dhumralochana demanded.

"My vow does not permit me to marry him without fighting him," she replied firmly.

Without wasting another word, Dhumralochana drew his weapon and rushed towards her to strike. 

With utter contempt for the charging asura, Ambika simply looked at him and uttered a single, powerful syllable of divine energy: "Hum!"

The next moment, a handful of ashes smouldered on the ground exactly where the mighty asura had once stood. He had been completely incinerated by the sound!

"Flee! Flee!" cried his terrified army, running back down the mountain.

But Chanda and Munda, who had been waiting below, stopped the retreating asuras. "Our lord has ordered us to capture this villainous woman," Chanda yelled, rallying the troops. "She is but one, we are many. Do not give up. We'll surround her and seize her."

When the massive army of asuras closed in on Ambika, who was now seated calmly on a fierce lion, she simply frowned in deep anger.

Suddenly, out came the terrifying, dark goddess Kali (another fierce incarnation of Durga) bursting directly from Ambika's forehead! 

Kali roared and charged at the asuras. She was a force of absolute destruction. Snatching a massive war elephant with just one hand, she hurled it effortlessly at the asuras, crushing dozens of them to death instantly.

Seeing his army being decimated, Chanda drew his sword and rushed at Kali. 

Kali effortlessly dodged his blow, seized him tightly by the hair... and cleanly beheaded him with a single stroke of her curved blade.

Blind with fury at his brother's death, Munda rushed forth... only to meet with the exact same fate! Kali tossed his severed head onto the ground.

With their leaders dead, the remaining asuras fled in absolute terror. 

Ambika smiled warmly at the blood-soaked Kali. "You have killed Chanda and Munda," Ambika proclaimed. "Henceforth, for this great deed, you shall also be eternally known as *Chamundi*."


# Tales of Durga - 3. How Durga Slew Shumbha

When the asura king Shumbha learned that his two best commanders, Chanda and Munda, had been killed by a mere woman, he was furious. He decided to come in person to deal with Ambika (Durga). His huge, seemingly infinite army completely surrounded Ambika and Kali on Mount Himavat.

Seeing the sheer number of enemies, the *Shaktis*—the inner divine energy of various male gods—issued forth, assuming fierce female forms to aid in the battle. Each of these forms is considered to be an incarnation or aspect of Durga.

Out of Brahma emerged Brahmani, riding a swan.
Out of Vishnu emerged Vaishnavi, riding an eagle.
And out of Maheshwara (Shiva) emerged Maheshwari, riding a bull. 

Finally, out of Ambika herself emerged the fierce Chandika. Soon, the sky was completely filled with hundreds of powerful Shaktis.

In the great battle that followed, the Shaktis proved devastating. Brahmani sprinkled magical water from her *kamandalu* (water pot) on the enemies, instantly making them weak and inert. Vaishnavi fought fiercely with her spinning discus, Maheshwari impaled demons with her trident, and various other Shaktis fought with their respective celestial weapons. 

Unable to face this overwhelming divine onslaught, the asuras began to flee in terror.

"Fleeing from women! Shame on you! Turn round and fight!" a massive, terrifying asura roared at the retreating troops. It was the terrible asura general, Raktabeeja. 

As Raktabeeja rushed forth to attack, Indra's Shakti struck him squarely with her crackling thunderbolt. 

Raktabeeja bled from the deep wound, but a terrifying magical curse was revealed. As each drop of the blood flowing from his wound touched the ground... there instantly rose a fully grown, mighty asura, his exact replica, ready to fight the Shaktis!

As the Shaktis struck and wounded each of the new asuras thus born, out of *their* spilled blood rose many, many more asuras. And soon, the battle scene was completely filled with thousands of identical Raktabeejas, who relentlessly fought the Shaktis.

Seeing the impossibility of the situation, Chandika turned to the fearsome Kali. 

"O Chamundi," Chandika called out, "these Raktabeejas can be annihilated only if we prevent their blood from falling to the ground."

Kali grinned, her tongue rolling out. "Leave it to me. Not a drop of their blood shall stain the earth again."

As Chandika and the other Shaktis furiously attacked the thousands of Raktabeejas, hacking them to pieces, Kali ran beneath them. With her massive, magically extended mouth and tongue, she swallowed every single drop of blood before it could touch the ground, thereby preventing the birth of any more asuras.

Soon, the hundreds and thousands of clone Raktabeejas that had arisen were completely wiped out. The original Raktabeeja was furious and desperate. 

*I must kill her before she strikes the fatal blow,* Raktabeeja thought. 

He rushed madly towards Chandika with his sword raised. As Chandika struck him with a fatal blow, Kali once again prevented his blood from touching the earth, drinking it all. And Raktabeeja fell dead, to the immense joy of the watching devas. 

"Victory to Chandika!" the devas cheered.

Back at the palace, the asura king Shumbha and his brother Nishumbha were stunned when they heard the news of Raktabeeja's death.

"Nishumbha, you alone are capable of killing her," Shumbha said. 

"I'll take her on, brother," Nishumbha promised. 

But even the most powerful asura, Nishumbha, was absolutely no match for Chandika. He was swiftly slain on the battlefield.

"Alas! My brother is dead!" Shumbha cried out in grief when he arrived. Desperate and seeing his entire army destroyed, he yelled at the goddess. "O Durga, there is no true glory in your victory! Helped by all these others, you've destroyed my men. You could not defeat me alone!"

Chandika smiled calmly. "I am alone," she stated. "The goddesses you see are but different forms of myself."

Then, to the utter amazement of Shumbha, the various Shaktis all seamlessly merged back into Durga's single form. 

With a roar, Shumbha charged. Seizing Durga, Shumbha used his dark magic to rise high into the sky, attempting to throw her to her death. But after a fierce, world-shaking battle in mid-air, it was Durga who effortlessly flung the asura down to the earth below. 

She landed gracefully beside him and cleanly slew him with her spear. 

Rejoicing, the devas, headed by Indra, appeared on the scene and bowed deeply. "We salute you, O Mother Durga, destroyer of evil," they prayed in endless gratitude.
