"Let's simply jump completely down," Balarama suggested calmly.

With a single, impossibly huge leap right over the raging flames, they safely reached the far foot of the hill completely unseen. 
"Jarasandha and his foolish men are desperately looking for us on the burning slopes. Let's quietly run!"

Krishna and Balarama then swiftly and safely made their secret way back to impenetrable Dwaraka.

"At last!" he gloated. "Krishna and Balarama have been completely burnt to terrible death in that massive fire. I can now finally go back to Magadha in absolute peace and victory."

That's exactly where you will be easily able to meet me and take me away." (Such a daring marriage was called 'Rakshasa Vivaha' and had perfect social sanction for warriors).

He immediately stood up. "But there's absolutely no time to lose. I must urgently call my swift charioteer. Daruka! Daruka!"

Krishna happily accepted Satyabhama and married her with completely due, grand ceremony. "I absolutely cannot accept the gem, however," Krishna refused firmly. "Let it securely remain with you exactly as a divine gift of Surya. However if you truly wish, you may simply give us the pure gold generously produced from it."

Satyabhama was very, incredibly beautiful and many a wealthy young man of Dwaraka had fervently hoped to happily marry her. Among them were the powerful Akrura and Kritavarma. 
"Satrajit had specifically promised to definitely marry Satyabhama to one of us," they fumed angrily. "And now he's suddenly married her exactly to Krishna. We absolutely must carefully take revenge."

They went secretly to Shatadhanva, another powerful Yadava, who had also deeply hoped for Satyabhama's lovely hand. "Why don't you brutally kill the treacherous Satrajit and simply take away the priceless Syamantaka gem for yourself?" they urged him wickedly. 
Shatadhanva greedily agreed. In the complete dead of the dark night he stealthily went to Satrajit's unguarded house and murdered him. 

Next morning—"Oh! My poor, dear father has been brutally killed!" Satyabhama wept over his bloody body. "And the priceless gem has been completely stolen!"
Meanwhile Krishna and Balarama had temporarily gone on a state visit to Hastinapura. The grieving Satyabhama quickly followed them exactly there. 
"I feel so terribly helpless and completely alone," she sobbed. 
"Don't worry at all," Krishna comforted her fiercely. "We will immediately go back to Dwaraka and definitely find the cruel killer and the stolen gem."

Shatadhanva was absolutely terrified to hear of Krishna's sudden return. He desperately ran for urgent help to Kritavarma. "Help me! Save me securely from Krishna's terrible wrath."
"No! I will absolutely not ever confront the mighty Krishna," Kritavarma refused completely. 

When Kritavarma flatly refused to possibly help, Shatadhanva pleaded desperately with Akrura, but entirely in vain. 
"No! Who would possibly dare to face Krishna who playfully held entire Mount Govardhana effortlessly on his single hand for seven long days. Absolutely not me!"
"Then I absolutely must desperately flee!" Shatadhanva panicked. "At least keep this dangerous gem safely with you."

Shatadhanva sped frantically away on swift horseback. But soon the relentless Krishna and Balarama were exactly on his dusty trail. 
"There he is! He's just jumped quickly off from his exhausted horse."
"Let's run swiftly after him."
Krishna and Balarama got quickly down from the heavy chariot and ran incredibly fast after the fleeing Shatadhanva. Krishna accurately aimed his deadly, spinning *chakra* exactly at Shatadhanva and beheaded him. 

But when they carefully searched his bloody clothes for the gem—"Oh! He absolutely doesn't have the gem on him at all. We need not have necessarily killed him," Krishna sighed. 
"He must have definitely given it secretly to someone else entirely for absolutely safe keeping," Balarama deduced. 

Krishna returned calmly to Dwaraka entirely alone while Balarama paid a long visit to Mithila. Meanwhile the guilty Akrura and Kritavarma had secretly and quickly left the town in absolute fear. 
"Krishna has definitely killed Shatadhanva. He will definitely come after us now. Let's run far away."

Krishna sent royal guards to quickly fetch the fleeing Akrura back. "Uncle, I perfectly know that Shatadhanva desperately left the gem exactly with you. Rightfully it belongs to Satrajit's direct descendants and therefore rightfully to my own son with Satyabhama. However you may safely keep the Syamantaka gem. Just temporarily allow me to simply show it to absolutely everyone in Dwaraka and finally clear my good name of any theft."

"Here, have it," Akrura sighed, handing it over safely. 
Krishna then publicly and proudly showed the brilliant jewel to all the suspicious people of Dwaraka. Having finally cleared all lingering doubts completely from the minds of the people, he generously and safely returned the gem exactly to Akrura. 

"Thus exactly ends the truly fascinating sixth session of our detailed rendering of the tenth chapter of the sacred Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded.
