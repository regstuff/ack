# The Rainbow Prince

Princess Rupanjali was beautiful to behold, and her laughter was like a babbling brook. But her mother, the Queen, was deeply worried. 

"Where will we find a prince good enough for our darling daughter?" she wondered.

"My bards will find her a handsome prince," the King reassured her. 

So the bards were sent to kingdoms near and far in search of a match for their princess. When they returned, they presented their findings. "Your majesty, we have brought portraits of the most handsome of the princes we saw. Let the princess choose from them."

The pictures were hung up in the palace art gallery, and the King and Queen took Rupanjali there. 

"We leave the choice to you, Rupanjali," the King said gently. "Tell us who among these princes you wish to marry."

Rupanjali walked around the gallery, examining each portrait. 

"Well? Which one do you like the most?" her mother asked. 

"None!" Rupanjali declared haughtily. "Not one of them is worthy of my hand." Then, pointing to the sky, she announced, "The prince I marry should be as handsome as the rainbow."

The next morning, a stranger appeared in the city, playing a stringed instrument. His music was so exquisite that people gathered around him in awe, wondering where he was from. The stranger went straight to the palace, where the King rose from his throne to greet him. 

"My heartiest welcome to you," the King said. "This is indeed a fortunate day for my daughter. I am happy to accept your offer."

"O King, I come from the land of the rainbow," the stranger explained. "I have been looking for a bride worthy of our prince. I have just heard of Princess Rupanjali and have come with a marriage proposal. But wait! Before you accept, there is one thing I must tell you. Some time ago, a wicked old witch came to our land and cast a spell on our prince, which put him to sleep for a long time. Then she breathed the consciousness of the prince into a blood-red parrot and let the bird fly away."

The King was dismayed. "Our prince will not awake until the red parrot is found," the stranger concluded. 

"How can I marry my only daughter to a prince who may never wake up again?" the King said sadly. 

When the news reached Rupanjali, however, she was resolute. "I will marry no one but the rainbow prince. And he must be aroused from his sleep. So I must have the red parrot!"

"But, my child, we do not know where it has flown away," her father reasoned. 

Rupanjali stamped her foot impatiently. "Every single bird in the kingdom must be caught. You are certain to find the red parrot among them."

The King was shocked. "But we can't do that, child. It would be cruel."

"I don't care," she replied coldly. "If you love me, father, you will do it."

Unable to deny his daughter, the King reluctantly agreed. "All right, my child." 

Orders were immediately sent out for all birds to be trapped, and the King's men ensured that not a single bird escaped. Soon, the nests became empty, and the happy chirping of birds no longer filled the air. 

A massive special cage was erected to hold the millions of birds. The entire cage echoed and re-echoed with their heartbroken, frightened cries. Nilanjana, Rupanjali's gentle maid, heard the cries and was moved to tears. 

"My poor dears," she whispered to the trapped birds. "I will go to the princess and plead for you. She is kind and gentle. She will set you free."

Nilanjana rushed to the princess. "Oh kind princess, set the poor birds free! Don't you see how wretched they are?"

Rupanjali flew into a rage. "How dare you question my actions, you wretch!"

Nilanjana was shocked to see the change that had come over her princess. *This is not the princess I admired!* she thought. *This is not the gentle Rupanjali whose smile held the colours of the rainbow.* 

She tried again, pleading, "Dear princess, how can you find your life's happiness at the cost of such pain to the poor creatures? Set them free."

Rupanjali's eyes flashed dangerously. "Well-spoken, merciful one! You love those birds, don't you?"

"Yes, princess. My heart goes out to them," Nilanjana answered earnestly. 

Rupanjali turned to the guards with a cruel smile. "Throw her into the cage. Let her be in the company of the birds she loves so much!"

As the guards dragged Nilanjana away, Rupanjali muttered to herself, "I must find out whether the red parrot is among the birds caught."

The guards threw Nilanjana into the massive cage, where she cried herself to sleep among the fluttering birds. 

A little while later, she was awakened by a gentle voice. "Nilanjana! Wake up, Nilanjana!" She opened her eyes to find a bright red parrot speaking to her. 

Nilanjana gently took the bird into her hands. "Poor little one! How you have suffered!"

"Set me free, Nilanjana," the parrot begged. "Just see how the thread has bruised my wings. How cruel you human beings are!"

"I will cut the thread and set you free," Nilanjana promised without hesitation. "I don't care what happens to me."

At that very moment, Rupanjali was on her way to the cage, hoping that the red parrot had been caught. As she approached, she saw Nilanjana holding the bird. "It's the red parrot!" she thought gleefully. "Nilanjana has found the bird!"

But before she could reach the cage, Nilanjana released the bird into the sky. 

"Nilanjana! What are you doing?" Rupanjali screamed as the bird flew away. "Oh, my red parrot! You wicked girl! You have set my parrot free! You shall die for this!"

"I only saw how it suffered," Nilanjana defended herself softly. "I know it would have died if I had not set it free."

"You released the parrot, and you must die!" Rupanjali declared furiously. 

The tears and pleas of her maid left Rupanjali unmoved. Nilanjana was taken to the top of a high hill to be thrown into the rocky stream far below. The guards gave her a push. But even as she fell, two strong arms caught her, protecting her from the fall. 

The next moment, Nilanjana found herself standing safely on the hill again. Beside her stood a handsome prince. 

"Nilanjana, I am the rainbow prince," he said warmly. 

Rupanjali, who had followed them, came running forward. "Ah, my prince! I have been waiting for you."

Then the prince took off his garland and stepped forward. "I am the red parrot! And I belong to you, Nilanjana," he said. 

"No!" Rupanjali cried out in disbelief. "She is only a maid. I am the princess you are looking for..."

Ignoring Rupanjali, the rainbow prince placed the garland around Nilanjana's neck. "You are my queen. I took the form of the red parrot only to test the worth of the girl I would choose to marry." He turned to Rupanjali. "Cruel Rupanjali is a princess only by birth. But you, my kind, brave, loyal girl, are a princess at heart!"

He looked lovingly at Nilanjana. "You glow with an inner beauty. Rupanjali's beauty is only skin-deep. Come, let us go to the land of the rainbow."

"But the birds... in the cage..." Nilanjana hesitated, looking back. 

"I have already set them free. Look!" the prince smiled, pointing to the sky. A massive flock of birds was soaring joyously into the clouds. 

"I am so happy!" Nilanjana beamed. 

Then the prince placed Nilanjana in his golden chariot, and they set off for the land of the rainbow. Rupanjali was left behind, a sadder and wiser girl. If only she had been a little more considerate, she might have found her own happiness. But from that day on, she learned to be kind and gentle to others, knowing that true beauty lies within.


# The Faithful Shepherd

There once lived a prince whose best friend was a shepherd. The two boys spent a great deal of time together, playing and romping about in the hills and fields. 

"When you grow up and become king, you won't let people shoot down these birds, will you?" the shepherd asked one afternoon. 

"Indeed, I will not!" the young prince promised. 

"And you will not forget me?" 

"How can I?" the prince smiled. "You are my best friend. I will make you my minister."

One day, the prince came to the fields with a heavy heart. "Friend, I have to go away to the gurukula for my studies and training."

The two friends bade each other a tearful farewell. "Come back soon, friend," the shepherd said. "The brook, the hill, the birds, and I will be waiting."

The years flew by. The shepherd never made another friend; he simply waited for his prince to return. 

One day, as the shepherd was taking his flock home, a villager called out to him. "Don't you know your friend has returned? He is to wed Princess Kanak Mala tomorrow."

The shepherd's face lit up with joy. "My prince has returned! He will surely come and visit me soon, just as he used to."

But many months passed, and the prince did not come. 

"The prince must be very busy," the shepherd reasoned. "I will go and meet him." 

While the shepherd was gathering fresh flowers to take to his friend, the prince was being formally crowned as King at the palace. When the shepherd arrived at the palace gates, there were many people waiting outside to catch a glimpse of the newly-crowned King. 

"My friend will come out now," the shepherd thought excitedly. 

After some time, the King emerged. The shepherd quickly stepped forward from the crowd. "Friend!" he called out brightly. 

The King heard him and recognized him instantly. *It's the shepherd!* the King thought to himself. *He wishes to take advantage of a childhood friendship. I must not encourage him.*

And the King walked right past the shepherd without even glancing at him. 

"My friend! Don't you remember me?" the shepherd called out again. 

But the guards held the shepherd back with their spears. "Quiet! Don't pester his majesty!" they scolded. 

The shepherd went away quietly, tears streaming down his face. *My friend has forgotten me!* he thought, heartbroken. 

That night, the King could not sleep. He was deeply troubled by his conscience. *It was a pity I had to send him away,* he thought, tossing in his bed. *Perhaps I should have told the guards not to be harsh with him.* But soon, his pride took over, and he managed to justify his actions. *But if he were a good friend, he would have understood my plight. How can a shepherd address a king as 'friend' in public?*

When the King woke up the next morning, he cried out in agonizing pain. He found his entire body covered with millions and millions of sharp needles. 

"Aa...aah... Kanak Mala!" he screamed. 

His Queen rushed into the room. "My lord!" 

"I am in agony, Kanak Mala. Help me!" the King wept. 

"I will pluck out these needles—every one of them!" the Queen promised, sitting by his side. But she soon realized it was a hopeless task. "In the place of every needle I remove, two more appear!" she cried in despair. 

Very soon, the entire kingdom came to know about the King's bizarre and terrible affliction. People came in their hundreds to the palace and tried in vain to pull out the needles, but nothing worked. 

"Oh, Kanak Mala, what shall we do? I can't even see!" the King sobbed. 

"Have courage, my lord. I will keep on pulling out the needles," she promised, refusing to give up. 

But the King could see her exhaustion. "No, your hands must be bathed in blood. Please take some rest now," he insisted. 

"I won't leave your side, my lord."

"A dip in the river will refresh you. Go, Kanak Mala," he urged. 

Reluctantly, the Queen went to the river. As she stood on the banks, a young girl approached her. "Do you need a maid, fair queen? I have no family and nowhere to go. I shall do all your odd jobs."

"Will you help me pluck out the needles on the King's body?" Kanak Mala asked desperately. 

"Yes, I will," the girl agreed. 

"Then you may come along with me after I have bathed," the Queen said, stepping into the water. 

Kanak Mala had just waded into the river when she heard an arrogant voice behind her. "Be quick, woman! You've to get to the palace and do all my odd jobs."

Kanak Mala looked up in shock. The young girl had put on the Queen's royal clothes and ornaments! "What! How dare you! Put back my clothes and ornaments!"

The girl only laughed mockingly. "I am the queen now. I have tricked you cleverly, haven't I? No one will know that I am not the real queen! Ha, ha, ha!"

As Kanak Mala was a newlywed bride, she had always worn a veil in public. Only three people could have recognized that the other girl was an impostor. *But the old King and the Queen are away on a pilgrimage,* Kanak Mala realized with dread. *My husband's eyes are sewn up with needles! Who else can expose her?*

"Why are you wasting time? Come out of the river quickly," the impostor snapped. 

*I will go to the palace as her maid,* Kanak Mala decided silently. *I must be near my husband. He needs me.*

But upon reaching the palace, the impostor locked the door to the King's room from the inside. "You cannot go inside," she ordered Kanak Mala. 

"Please allow me to serve my lord," Kanak Mala begged. 

"And pull the needles out? No. It would not be in my interest to let you do so!" the impostor sneered. She then made Kanak Mala work hard as a lowly maid all day long. 

That evening, exhausted and heartbroken, Kanak Mala went to the river and cried her heart out. Suddenly, she heard a voice drifting through the trees:

"If I had a hundred needles, I would buy a crown.
If I had a thousand needles, I would buy a town."

Kanak Mala pricked up her ears. *Who is singing about needles? How I dread that word!* she thought. 

The voice continued: 
"If I had a million needles, I would dance and sing.
If I had a billion needles... I would be a king!"

Kanak Mala got up and looked around for the singer. Sitting under a tree was a young shepherd. 

"Ah! There he is!" she thought. She rushed up to him. "Do you really want a billion needles? If you are willing to pluck them out yourself, you can have them. Come, follow me."

The shepherd silently followed Kanak Mala back to the palace. 

"Can you get in through the window?" she whispered, pointing up at the palace walls. "The King is locked in that room. His body is full of needles."

The shepherd climbed a nearby tree, swung over to the balcony, and quietly entered the King's room. The King lay groaning on the bed, covered in needles. 

Taking a long thread from his pocket, the shepherd began to chant: 
"Wicked needles, move off, do!
Come, thread, be quick at last
And pull out every needle fast."

The next moment, the thread swung out magically, threading itself through the eyes of the needles, and pulled them all out at one stroke. 

The King gasped as the pain vanished. He opened his eyes and saw his childhood friend standing before him. "My friend! You have saved my life!"

The King embraced the shepherd tightly. "How happy I am to see you again! I shall never forgive myself for having forgotten you."

"Do you remember me then?" the shepherd asked with a gentle smile. 

"Tell me, was it you who punished me with these needles?" the King asked, deeply ashamed. 

"No, never! I am your friend. How could I hurt you?" the shepherd replied. "It was your own conscience which punished you. Then destiny took a hand, turning the pricks of your conscience into actual needles."

"I deserved it all!" the King admitted, tears in his eyes. "And I am happy that the same destiny brought you again to my rescue!"

Meanwhile, outside the room, the courtiers were puzzled to find the King's door locked. "How strange! Let us break open the lock and find out what's happening," one suggested. They managed to force the door open and entered the room. 

"What are you doing here?" a courtier demanded, seeing the shepherd. 

Hearing the commotion, the impostor came running into the room. 

The King pointed at her furiously. "Who are you? Where is my queen? Bring her to me at once!"

Trembling all over in fear, the impostor was forced to fetch Kanak Mala. As the Queen entered, the impostor fell at her feet. "Oh queen! Save me! Show me mercy," she wailed. 

"Kanak Mala!" the King cried out, rushing to her side. 

"My lord!" she smiled, overjoyed to see him healed. 

The wicked impostor was banished from the kingdom forever. The King and Queen lived happily ever after, and the faithful shepherd remained by his best friend's side to serve as his most trusted minister.
