# Gajendra

Indradyumna, the virtuous Pandyan King, decided one day to leave his worldly duties behind. Calling a meeting of his counsellors, he announced, "I have decided to retire to the forest and devote all my time to the worship of the Lord." Despite their pleas, he confidently handed over the kingdom and retreated to the Kulachala mountains, where he built a small cottage.

Immersed in his devotion, Indradyumna took a vow of silence. One day, the great Sage Agastya and his disciples passed by his door. Lost in meditation, the king failed to offer the customary courtesies to welcome the esteemed sage. 

Enraged by what he perceived as a slight, Agastya cursed the king: "Your arrogance shall not go unpunished! May you turn into an elephant!"

High in his celestial abode, Lord Vishnu smiled upon hearing the curse. "Agastya, you have unknowingly done my devotee a great service," the gentle god mused.

Down on earth, Indradyumna instantly transformed into a massive elephant. Roaming the deep forests, he soon encountered a wild herd. Displaying the might of a king, he easily overpowered the herd's leader and was accepted as their new chief, earning the name 'Gajendra'. 

Under Gajendra's leadership, the mighty herd took the Trikuta forest by storm. One hot day, seeking relief, the elephants waded into a large, serene lake for a refreshing bath. However, this lake was the territory of a ferocious crocodile. 

As Gajendra moved deeper into the waters, the crocodile attacked, clamping its powerful jaws around the elephant king's leg. "It's a crocodile! He's caught my leg!" Gajendra trumpeted in alarm.

A desperate struggle ensued. Gajendra trumpeted loudly and thrashed about, trying to shake the predator off, but the crocodile's grip was like iron, seemingly blessed with divine strength. Seeing their leader trapped and unable to break free, the rest of the herd panicked. "It's a pity, but we'll have to desert our leader," they decided, fleeing to the safety of the shore.

Left alone, Gajendra fought tirelessly until his strength began to fail. Desperate and exhausted, he realized that physical might alone could not save him. Remembering his past devotion, he plucked a lotus flower from the lake with his trunk and raised it towards the heavens. 

"O Vishnu, come to my aid! You are my only refuge," Gajendra prayed with all his heart.

Hearing the desperate plea of his devotee, Lord Vishnu instantly mounted his divine eagle, Garuda, and descended from Vaikuntha. Hovering over the lake, Vishnu hurled his brilliant Sudarshana Chakra (discus). The weapon struck true, and the crocodile was slain instantly, freeing Gajendra.

Offering the lotus to his savior, Gajendra bowed deeply before Lord Vishnu. At the divine touch of the Lord, the curse was broken, and Gajendra regained his human form as King Indradyumna.

"Arise, Gajendra," Vishnu smiled.

Overwhelmed with gratitude and devotion, Indradyumna pleaded, "Lord, I wish to behold you all the time. Take me with you to Vaikuntha."

"So be it," Lord Vishnu blessed him, taking his faithful devotee to his eternal celestial abode.


# Ambarisha

King Ambarisha was a devout follower of Lord Vishnu. After fasting for three days in the sacred forest of Madhuvana, the king prepared to break his fast on the highly auspicious day of Dwadashi. Just as he was about to eat, the notoriously short-tempered Sage Durvasa arrived at the ashram.

"Arise, O King," the sage commanded.

"Holy one, you have come on the auspicious day of Dwadashi," Ambarisha greeted him respectfully. "Please be my guest and bless me."

"I will," Sage Durvasa agreed. "But first, let me bathe in the holy Yamuna."

The sage left for the river, but hours passed and he did not return. As noon approached, the learned men and courtiers assembled there grew worried. 

"My lord, if the fast is not broken before noon, you will incur demerit," they warned the king.

Ambarisha was caught in a terrible dilemma. "This is a fine predicament, indeed! What shall I do? If I don't wait for the sage, I'll incur his wrath. But if I don't break the fast in time, the religious merit is lost."

The courtiers deliberated and finally offered a solution. "My lord, you could take a mere sip of water and break the fast. The sage will not be offended because you will eat your meal only after serving him."

"What you advise seems to be the only way out," Ambarisha agreed. Taking a little water in his palm, he raised it to his lips and drank a single sip to formally break the fast. 

Almost immediately, Sage Durvasa returned. Knowing through his mystical powers what had just transpired, he exploded in rage. "You are known to be a virtuous king! And yet, you could not wait for your guest! Not a drop of water should have been drunk before feeding the invited guest!"

The sage tore off a lock of his matted hair and hurled it to the ground. "You shall pay for your arrogance, Ambarisha!" he roared. 

Instantly, the lock of hair transformed into a terrifying, fiery discus that sped towards the king. The courtiers were aghast, but Ambarisha stood calmly, his faith unwavering. "Don't worry," he told his men. "I think he will soon get over his fit of rage."

As the deadly discus came whirring towards Ambarisha, another brilliant, divine discus suddenly appeared in the sky. "Look! Another discus!" the courtiers pointed.

It was Lord Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra, sent to rescue his devotee. In a flash, the Sudarshana Chakra destroyed Durvasa's weapon. But it did not stop there—it then turned its blazing fury towards Sage Durvasa himself!

Terrified, the sage broke into a run, pursued relentlessly by the divine weapon. He fled across the earth and up to the celestial realms, seeking refuge with Lord Brahma. 

"I am being scorched! I cannot bear it," Durvasa pleaded. But Brahma shook his head helplessly. "Brahma is my only refuge," Durvasa thought, but he was turned away, for no one could stop Vishnu's weapon.

Next, Durvasa fled to Mount Kailasa, the icy abode of Lord Shiva. "Lord, protect me!" he begged.

"My child, seek His protection whose anger you have invited upon yourself," Lord Shiva advised calmly. "None can protect you now. Lord Shiva won't fail me! But even he cannot stop it."

With nowhere else to turn, the exhausted sage rushed across the cosmic ocean to Lord Vishnu, who sat resting on the great serpent Adishesha. 

"O Lord! I have offended your devotee. Pardon me! Protect me!" Durvasa cried out.

Vishnu looked at him sternly. "O sage, he who employs his power to injure the virtuous, invites evil upon himself. Only Ambarisha has the power to save you. Seek his protection."

Defeated and humbled, Durvasa retraced his steps all the way back to the forest of Madhuvana. Finding King Ambarisha exactly where he had left him, the great sage fell to his knees. "Ambarisha, take pity on me! Call off this discus!"

Moved by the sage's plight, the noble king closed his eyes and appealed to the divine weapon. "If Lord Vishnu has been pleased with my devotion, let Sage Durvasa no longer be troubled by you." 

Instantly, the Sudarshana Chakra vanished into thin air. 

Durvasa stood up, deeply awed. "The discus of Vishnu, which could not be controlled by either Shiva or Brahma, respects your words," he marvelled. "What is the secret of your power?"

King Ambarisha smiled gently. "The love of Lord Vishnu." Thus did the Lord demonstrate his immense love and protection for his true devotees.


# Vrikasura

The foolish but mighty Asura, Vrika, once decided to propitiate Lord Shiva to gain ultimate power. Retreating to a desolate forest, he lit a sacrificial fire and began a gruesome penance. Day after day, he cut off pieces of his own flesh and offered them to the fire. 

"Lord, with this offering, may you appear before me," he chanted, throwing bloody chunks into the flames. 

But Lord Shiva did not appear. Growing impatient and desperate, the Asura concluded, "He does not appear. Perhaps it is only the offering of my head that will propitiate the Lord."

Drawing his heavy sword, Vrikasura prepared to decapitate himself. "When he was about to sever his head from the body," Lord Shiva finally materialized from the flames, his hand raised in blessing.

"Stop, Vrika!" Shiva commanded. "There is no need to kill yourself to please one who is satisfied with a mere offering of water. What is the boon you seek?"

The ambitious Asura smirked. "I wish to conquer the world. May anyone on whose head I lay my palm, die."

"So be it," Lord Shiva granted the strange request, bound by his divine promise.

But the very next moment, the treacherous Asura's eyes gleamed with malice. Deciding to test his newfound power on the very god who had granted it, Vrika reached out his hand towards Lord Shiva's head!

"No!" Shiva cried out, dodging the treacherous blow. Realizing the danger he had put himself in, the great god broke into a run. 

Vrikasura chased him relentlessly across mountains and valleys. Desperate, Lord Shiva sought out Lord Vishnu, who was resting on the cosmic serpent amidst the ocean. "Protect me from the wicked Asura," Shiva urged. 

"Leave him to me," Vishnu smiled, instantly devising a plan. 

Assuming the form of a young, innocent-looking Brahmachari (celibate student), Lord Vishnu intercepted the running Asura. 

"Where are you running to? Whom are you seeking?" the Brahmachari asked kindly. "Rest for a while. You seem tired."

Panting, Vrika stopped and boasted of the deadly boon he had just received from Lord Shiva, and how he intended to use it on the granter himself.

The Brahmachari laughed out loud, a sound of pure disbelief. "What! Shiva duping a devotee! I can't believe it."

"What do you mean?" Vrika asked, confused.

"That's because the moment you placed your palm on his head, you would realize that he had duped you," the young boy explained innocently. "He's just tricking you. Then why don't you place your palm on your own head and see?"

The dull-witted Asura pondered for a moment. "A good idea!" he agreed.

Without stopping to think, the foolish Asura placed his heavy palm firmly on his own head. The next moment, the deadly boon activated, and Vrikasura fell dead to the ground, a victim of his own treacherous ambition. 

Just then, Lord Shiva arrived, relieved to see the threat neutralized. The clever Brahmachari transformed back into the majestic Lord Vishnu. 

"I am sorry that the unfortunate Asura had to die," Lord Shiva sighed. 

"It was inevitable," Lord Vishnu replied calmly. "He who secures a boon to destroy others seeks his own destruction."


# Rantideva

In the high heavens of Vaikuntha, Lord Brahma, Indra, and the other Devas once called on Lord Vishnu with a curious question. 

"Lord, who do you consider your greatest devotee?" they asked.

Lord Vishnu smiled gently. "Rantideva."

The Devas were surprised and a little skeptical. "That king? Just because he has given up his kingdom and fasted for forty-eight days?" they murmured. "Allow us to test him, my Lord."

"Do so," Lord Vishnu nodded knowingly. "In the process, you'll learn why I consider him a great devotee."

Down on earth, King Rantideva, having given away all his immense wealth and possessions in charity, was living a life of extreme asceticism. He had been fasting for an astonishing forty-eight days. Finally, a small meal was prepared for him, and he sat down to break his fast.

"It is hard to practise charity when one is hungry. Let's see how Rantideva reacts," the Devas whispered to each other, descending in disguise.

Just as Rantideva raised the food to his lips, a mendicant approached. "Master, I am hungry," he pleaded.

Without a moment's hesitation, Rantideva offered him a generous portion. "Lord, may you be pleased. I take this food as your prasada," he prayed, seeing the divine in his guest.

After the mendicant left, Rantideva prepared to eat the remainder. But before he could, a weary farmer came along. "Holy one, I am dying of hunger," the man cried.

"You shall share what I have," Rantideva smiled warmly, handing over another portion of his meager meal.

As the farmer departed, Rantideva was left with barely enough to satisfy a child. "Master, do you have something for me and my poor dogs?" a ragged beggar called out. 

Rantideva offered all the remaining solid food to the beggar and his animals. "Glory to the Lord," he thought peacefully. "I was able to serve Him by feeding the hungry. Let me have some water and break my fast."

But as he raised the small copper pot of water to his parched lips, a fourth visitor stumbled towards him. It was a man from an outcast community. "Master, I am thirsty," he gasped.

Rantideva immediately extended the pot. "Take this and quench your thirst."

The visitor hesitated, pulling back. "No! I shouldn't deceive you. People call me an untouchable. I will pollute it if I touch it."

"How can you pollute anything?" Rantideva asked softly. "Doesn't the all-pervading Vishnu reside in you too, brother? I do not even seek moksha (liberation)! I deny myself only to free others from misery."

At these selfless words, the visitor's disguise vanished. In a blinding flash of light, Lord Vishnu, Lord Brahma, Indra, and all the other Devas appeared before Rantideva in their true, radiant forms.

"Rantideva is indeed a great soul," the Devas admitted, deeply humbled. "He is the greatest devotee of the Lord."

Lord Vishnu looked upon Rantideva with boundless love. "Rantideva, I am pleased with you. You truly see me in all creatures. You have attained Moksha!"
