# The Fearless Boy - 1. The Fearless Boy

Samkicca was a young novice who lived peacefully with a community of holy monks. They used to go out and beg for food every morning, and then eat their meals together at the monastery. 

One day as they were about to eat, Samkicca looked up and saw a stranger watching them intently. 

"He appears to be hungry!" Samkicca thought compassionately. 

He approached the head monk. "Revered sir, we have a guest."
"We will share what we have with him, Samkicca," the elder monk agreed readily. 

They invited the man over. "Where do you come from, brother?" an elder asked. 

"A distant place, sir. I am on my way to my elder daughter's house," the stranger replied sadly. "I was staying with my younger daughter, but she has fallen on hard times... and I had to leave."

"You appear to be hungry. Eat with us," the monks told him warmly. 

"Go get a leaf and each of us will give you a portion of rice," one monk instructed. Soon, the man had a large mound of food. 

"Ah, that was a very good meal!" he thought happily after eating. 

"Revered sir, have you come from a grand feast?" the man asked. 

"No, brother. We have not come from a feast," the monk replied simply. "This is the kind of food we collect every day. The people here are good to us."

The stranger was amazed. "I could never hope to get food like this even if I worked from dawn to dusk," he pondered. 

"Revered sir, I would like to live with you here," he requested humbly. 
"You may stay with us," the monks agreed. 
"I shall perform any task you assign me," he promised. 

The stranger made himself quite comfortable in the monastery where the monks lived. But several weeks later— 

"How I long to see my daughter! And my sweet grandchildren," he sighed, feeling homesick. "Will these monks give me permission to visit her? I doubt it. But why do I have to ask them at all?"

The man collected his few belongings and quietly left the monastery. His path lay across a dense, dark forest. 

When he had walked halfway through it— "Robbers!" he gasped in terror as a gang of armed men surrounded him. 

"P... please don't hurt me," he begged. "I don't have anything of value. See for yourselves."

"He speaks the truth," the robbers muttered after searching him. "There's nothing here."

The robber chief grabbed him. "Who are you and where are you going?"
"I am just a poor man..." he stammered. 
"Ah! That is just what we need!" the chief grinned wickedly. 

They quickly tied the poor man to a large post. "The forest spirit has to be appeased... by a human sacrifice," the chief declared. "NO!" the man screamed. 

The chief raised his large sword. "Have mercy on me, master!" the man pleaded. 

"A wretch like me would make a terribly poor sacrifice! Even the spirit would be offended!" the man reasoned desperately. "There's a monastery not far from here where dwell thirty men of noble birth. Offer one of them instead and you will please the spirit beyond measure."

The robbers agreed. "He is right. Untie the wretched fellow," the chief ordered. 

"I... I will forever be indebted to you," the relieved man bowed. "If ever you need my help..."
"I do," the chief demanded. "You will lead us to the monastery."

"C-certainly!" the man stammered, leading the fierce robbers to the peaceful monastery. 

"There! That's the place," he pointed from a distance. 

"I don't see any monks around," the chief frowned. 
"They are in their cells. Sound the gong and they will come out," the man explained, quickly running away. 

The chief struck the large gong loudly. The monks hurried out of their cells. 

"Who struck the gong, brothers?" the elder monk asked. 

"I did," the fierce robber chief stepped forward, surrounded by his men. "We are a gang of robbers who dwell in the forest. And the forest spirit has to be appeased with a human sacrifice. We have come to choose a victim."

"Take me," the old elder monk offered instantly without hesitation. 

"No, revered sir, we cannot spare you. Take me instead," another monk pleaded. 
"NO! Take me!" cried another. 

Soon, all thirty monks were begging to be taken as the sacrifice, much to the robbers' complete surprise. 

"They are all incredibly brave men," the chief remarked. 

"That is because they have absolutely no earthly attachments," one of the robbers noted softly. 

"I will take the youngest among you," the chief decided. 

"That is me," a monk stepped forward. 
"You are mistaken, brother. Samkicca!" the novice called out gently. "I am the youngest person here, revered sir. I shall go. Do not stop me, I entreat you."

And so young Samkicca departed bravely with the fierce robbers. 

Soon they arrived at the robbers' jungle temple. "Sit!" the chief ordered roughly. 

Samkicca sat peacefully on the ground and immediately went into a deep trance. 

"He is saintly and deserves a merciful, quick death," the chief thought, drawing his heavy sword. "I shall despatch him with one single blow."

"Receive this sacrifice, O great spirit and shower thy blessings on us!" he shouted, swinging the sword down with all his might. 

*THUD!* 

The sword bounced off Samkicca's neck without leaving a scratch. "Did I miss? My hand slipped. This time I'll cleave his head in two!" the chief roared in frustration. 

He swung the sword again, even harder. 

*THUD! CRACK!*

The heavy steel sword shattered into pieces against the novice's neck! 

"H-he is no ordinary mortal," the terrified chief gasped, staring at the broken blade. 

"I... I have broken my strong sword on your head. Yet you remain so perfectly calm. Have you no fear at all?" the chief asked in awe. 

Samkicca calmly came out of his trance. "He that is free of worldly desire has no mental suffering. To one who is perfectly free of attachment, death has absolutely no terror."

The chief fell to his knees in reverence. "I shall quickly give up my lawless life and follow you," he promised. 
"We too," the other robbers echoed humbly. 

And Samkicca, who had walked into the dangerous forest with a fierce band of robbers, returned safely to the monastery with a peaceful band of devoted disciples.


# The Fearless Boy - 2. The Two Disciples

An elder monk had two disciples who lived with him. One was terribly lazy, and the other was incredibly hardworking. 

Early one morning, the hardworking disciple woke up. "Wake up, brother! Come with me to the river to fetch water for the master's bath," he gently shook the lazy one. 

"Oh, he sleeps like a log!" the diligent disciple sighed when the other didn't move. 

The dutiful disciple fetched the heavy pots of water himself... and set it to heat over a wood fire. 

Sometime later when the lazy disciple finally awoke— "That's the water steaming. Time to go to the master," he thought slyly. 

He walked into the master's room. "Master, the water for your bath is ready. Come, have your bath before the water gets cold," he said helpfully. 

As the master walked to his bath, the diligent disciple watched. "He knows. He has come for his bath. This lazy brother does absolutely no work, yet cleverly takes all the credit for mine," he thought in annoyance. "It's time I exposed him."

That afternoon, as the lazy disciple lay fast asleep after a heavy meal, the diligent disciple heated water for the elder's wash. 

When the water was boiling, he quickly poured almost all of it out... and took the bucket away, safely leaving only a very small quantity steaming in the vessel directly on the hot fire. 

When the lazy disciple awoke and saw the steam rising— "Ah, it's time to go to the master," he smiled lazily. 

"Master! Master, the water is ready," he announced. 
"You may come for your wash," the lazy disciple added. 

"I feel so drowsy," the master yawned, rubbing his eyes. "Perhaps I should gently go back to sleep. Brother..."

The master walked out. "Where is the water? Eh?!" he asked loudly, looking at the empty pot. "Is there no water in the vessel?"

"What?" the lazy disciple ran outside, shocked. "Oh, the rascal! I thought he had...!" 

Just then the diligent disciple walked up with the bucket of hot water. "Here, master. I have brought the hot water for your wash."

The master looked at the two disciples and understood immediately. 

"Now I see it all. This boy has been doing all the hard work and..." the master thought. 

He turned to the lazy disciple sternly. "Brother, you must never falsely take credit for the work another has done."

The lazy disciple felt deeply offended. "That was no way to speak to me. And all for a few drops of water! Hmph!" he thought angrily. 

And the youth walked away in a huff, never to return! 

The wise and hardworking disciple continued to study diligently under the master and later became a great teacher himself.


# The Fearless Boy - 3. The Great Architect

Prince Bodhi was enchanted with his new palace and was all praise for the architect who built it. 

"Never have I seen such splendour in stone! You have created a marvel! Tell me, have you ever built a palace like this for anyone else?" the prince asked. 

"No, Yuvaraj! For no other have I built a palace such as this," the architect replied. 

The prince turned triumphantly to his friend, a nobleman named Sanjikaputta. "Did you hear that, Sanjikaputta? The palace is unique!" 

As they walked away, the prince said, "And I shall see to it that it always remains so!" 

"How will you do that?" Sanjikaputta asked. 
"Oh, there are ways...!" the prince smiled. 

Sanjikaputta realized the prince meant to slay the architect. He went back to the architect and warned him. "Your talent is a sword at your throat. Be warned!"

The next day, the prince sent for the architect. "Have you finished your work on our palace?"

"No, Yuvaraj," the architect lied. "It is something which you haven't even dreamt of."

"But right now I need some timber," the architect requested. "Seasoned timber, with the sap well dried out, Yuvaraj." 

"I shall have it sent to you," the prince agreed. 

"From now on, I must work in privacy. With the exception of my wife and my son, no one should be permitted anywhere near the new palace," the architect insisted. The prince agreed. 

When he had got the timber, the architect shut himself up in a courtyard of the new palace and set to work. He secretly built a large wooden mechanical bird. 

One day, his wife arrived looking frightened. "Soldiers have surrounded the palace," she said. 

"The prince must be getting suspicious," the architect said. "Go home and sell everything of value in the house. Tomorrow, bring our son with you, and also all the gold we have."

The next day, she returned with their son and the gold. They climbed inside the large wooden bird. 

"Are we going to fly?" the son asked. "Yes, my son."

The architect pulled a lever and up they went in the flying bird. 

The prince and his guards saw them. "Yuvaraj! The architect is trying to escape!" 

"Shoot him down!" the prince ordered. 

The archers fired, but the arrows missed. The architect brought his craft down safely in another country. And there he resumed his career, building several palaces, each better than the other.
