# Bhagawat: The Krishna Avatar - Victory Over Kamsa

Pareekshit was completely engrossed in eagerly listening to the long, detailed narration of the sage Shukadeva. 
"On the auspicious day of Shivaratri," Shukadeva continued, "Krishna, Nanda, and the other cowherds happily visited Ambikavana, a dense, holy forest nearby. After a purifying holy dip in the sacred river Saraswati, they devoutly worshipped Lord Shiva."

Abstaining firmly from any food or water to strictly observe the Shivaratri fast, the tired cowherds went to sleep peacefully on the soft sandy river bank. 
Suddenly, in the dark of the night, a huge, terrifying python silently slithered out of the woods and violently pounced on the sleeping Nanda. 
"Help, Krishna! Help! Hurry!" Nanda screamed, jolted awake. "The giant python is completely swallowing me."

The other cowherds woke up in deep alarm and were horrified to find their beloved leader Nanda trapped in the crushing coils of the massive python. 
"Quick! Attack the python!" 
"Save Nanda!"
They desperately beat the thick python with heavy sticks and burning logs from their campfire, but entirely in vain. The python simply ignored them and continued to swallow Nanda. 

Then Krishna calmly came forward and simply gave a very gentle kick to the huge python. Miraculously, with the mere, divine touch of his foot, the terrifying python instantly vanished into thin air. 
In its place stood a beautiful, heavenly youth in all his radiant glory, who immediately bowed respectfully to Krishna. 

"Who are you?" the amazed cowherds asked. "How did a wonderful, radiant being exactly like you assume the terrible body of a python?"
"I am a celestial Vidyadhara named Sudarshana," the youth explained humbly. "One day as I was proudly driving in my golden chariot, I saw some revered rishis (sages) of the Angiras group. Too proud of my immense wealth and good looks, I arrogantly laughed at seeing their ugly, deformed faces. They rightfully cursed me to instantly become a loathsome python."
"But now I finally realise how completely full of mercy they truly were. They told me I would be freed by the Lord. The mere, blessed touch of your foot has finally released me from their long curse. Allow me to gratefully return to my world, O Lord."
"You may do so," Krishna smiled. 

Bowing deeply to Krishna once more, the youth completely disappeared. The cowherds were utterly overjoyed to see yet another incredible, miraculous feat performed effortlessly by Krishna. They returned happily home, loudly singing his great praises.

***

One beautiful, clear day, Krishna and Balarama were happily roaming in the forest accompanied by the joyous gopas and gopikas. It was a perfectly moonlit night and the cool air was heavily filled with the sweet scent of blossoms. The gopikas joyously burst into song. 
So incredibly melodious was their sweet music that Krishna and Balarama too happily began to sing along, completely filling the hearts of the gopikas with pure joy. 

Just then, Shankhachooda, a powerful, wicked devotee of Kubera (the lord of wealth), suddenly appeared from the shadows. Before anyone knew what was happening, Shankhachooda violently grabbed several gopikas and ran off with them. 
"Help!" they cried aloud in terror. "Oh Rama!" "Oh Krishna!"

Krishna and Balarama quickly looked around for a suitable weapon. 
"Here, Krishna. Take this massive sala tree to attack him," Balarama said, uprooting a whole tree. 
"Fear not, dear gopikas," Krishna called out. "We are coming to help you."

The two angry brothers rapidly approaching with huge, entire trees in their strong hands looked incredibly fearsome to Shankhachooda. One terrified look at them and he immediately dropped the women and ran desperately for his very life. 
"Balarama, stay here and protect the gopikas," Krishna commanded. "I will follow him and punish him."

Shankhachooda ran as fast as he possibly could through the dark forest, but the swift Krishna relentlessly pursued him. *I must get hold of him and take his precious crest jewel,* Krishna thought. 
Catching up to the fleeing demon, then, with a single, incredibly powerful stroke of his bare hand, Krishna completely knocked Shankhachooda's head off his shoulders. 
Krishna casually took the shining, priceless gem that was firmly fixed on Shankhachooda's head and presented it lovingly to Balarama when he returned.

Every single day, when Krishna went to the deep jungle with his massive herd of cows, the gopikas felt terribly forlorn and lonely. They would try to console themselves by endlessly talking about Krishna and his amazing feats. 
"How incredibly sweet are the magical melodies, Nanda's darling boy plays upon his flute."
"Even the timid deer and the grazing cows are so utterly charmed by its melody that they remain completely rooted to the ground. Pricking up their ears, they stand totally still like a painted picture on a wall."
"With the bright peacock feather tucked beautifully into his curly locks, and bedecked with fresh green twigs, Krishna looks exactly like some great, handsome wrestler, as he lovingly calls each of his cows by their names."
"The very waters of the river Yamuna seem to purposely rise as if they want to respectfully gather the holy dust from his feet."

***

One hot day, when Krishna was peacefully returning from the forest as usual, a huge, aggressive bull suddenly confronted him on the path. It was the powerful demon Arishtasura in the terrifying form of a bull. As he violently stamped the ground with his enormous, heavy hooves the very earth trembled in fear. 
"GRRR!" he bellowed. 

Krishna looked around and saw the strong stone fences which had been completely demolished, and the tall crops which had been ruthlessly trodden underfoot by the mad bull. 
"He has deliberately caused terrible havoc everywhere. Save us," the cowherds panicked. 
"That deafening roar strikes absolute terror in our hearts!" 
"Krishna! Krishna! Protect us!"

Krishna gently comforted them. "Be calm. There is absolutely nothing to fear."
He stepped forward and called out to the bull, "O foolish one! Leave these innocent cows and cowherds alone immediately. I am here to completely crush the false pride of wicked creatures exactly like you."

Having boldly challenged the massive demon, Krishna stood by perfectly calmly, casually resting his arm around a friend's shoulder. 
The enraged creature leapt violently towards Krishna. His heavy hooves aggressively digging the ground beneath, and his raised, thrashing tail scattering the clouds high above, he rushed menacingly at Krishna, exactly like a deadly thunderbolt forcefully hurled by Indra. 

As the bull reached him, Krishna effortlessly caught hold of his sharp horns, and exactly like a mighty elephant fighting its equal rival, drove the massive asura back incredibly forcefully. 
The stunned bull fell. Krishna now quickly stepped heavily on the body of the demon and powerfully twisted its thick neck around as if he were simply wringing a wet cloth. With a final, deft movement, Krishna forcefully pulled out its sharp, deadly horns. 

Krishna then ruthlessly attacked the demon with its own horns and killed him instantly. The relieved cowherds gathered happily around Krishna and loudly sang his praises.

***

Meanwhile, far away in Mathura, the meddling sage Narada visited King Kamsa. 
"Kamsa," Narada revealed, "Krishna, who safely lives in Vraja, is actually the eighth son of Devaki, while Balarama is Rohini's son. It was specifically to protect them from your wrath that Vasudeva secretly placed the two boys in the care of his dear friend Nanda. All the powerful asuras sent by you to kill them have been effortlessly killed themselves."

On suddenly hearing this shocking truth, Kamsa trembled violently with pure rage and immediately picked up his sharp sword. "I will kill that lying, treacherous Vasudeva!"
Narada hastily dissuaded him from immediately killing Vasudeva. "But—" Kamsa roared, "Throw Vasudeva and Devaki deep in the darkest jail again, bound tightly in heavy fetters and thick iron chains!"

After Narada went away, Kamsa urgently called for Keshi, a terrifying asura. "Go to Vraja immediately and kill Krishna and Balarama."
"As you wish, O king," Keshi bowed. 

Then Kamsa hastily summoned the incredibly hefty Mushtika, Chanura, and other giant wrestlers, as well as his ministers and the royal keeper of elephants. 
"Listen carefully, O brave Chanura and Mushtika. In Vraja there secretly live the two hidden sons of Vasudeva, Krishna and Balarama. I am reliably told they are specifically ordained to kill me. When they are brought here, you must completely kill them under the clever pretext of a friendly wrestling match with them."

He then addressed the royal keeper of elephants. "O mahout! Let your most vicious, mad elephant Kuvalayapeeda stand ready at the main gate. As soon as my enemies come, the elephant can crush and kill them."

Then he turned to his ministers. "Let grand preparations be started immediately for the massive celebration of the 'bow sacrifice' on the eve of the full moon day. Let bloody sacrifices be made to Lord Shiva to absolutely ensure our success."

Then Kamsa secretly sent for Akrura the Yadava, a close, trusted kinsman of Krishna. 
"Only you among the Yadavas have my absolute welfare at heart," Kamsa lied smoothly. "It is to you I turn for crucial help just as Indra turns to Vishnu. Go to Vraja where the two hidden sons of Vasudeva secretly live. Take this royal chariot and fetch them here. Right away."
"Once they are here, I will cleverly have them killed by the mad elephant Kuvalayapeeda. If they somehow escape, my incredibly strong wrestlers will surely slay them. Thereafter I will completely kill Vasudeva and the other rebellious Vrishnis, my useless old father Ugrasena, and all my remaining enemies."
"Then my dear Akrura, with the strong help of allies like Jarasandha, my powerful father-in-law, and the terrifying asuras, I will completely conquer the entire earth. Hurry up and fetch Krishna and Balarama. But simply tell them they are respectfully invited here to celebrate the grand bow sacrifice and to see the wonderful sights of the great city of Mathura."

Akrura, secretly a devotee of Krishna, bowed his head. "Indeed you act thus to cleverly avert your prophesied death. A man must be prepared for both utter failure and great success. I will do exactly as you say."

***

Meanwhile, the demon named Keshi had assumed the terrifying form of a mighty, giant horse and galloped aggressively into Vrindavana. Viciously pawing the ground in pure fury, he neighed so unbelievably loudly that the terrified people trembled in absolute fear. His huge form closely resembled a dark mass of storm cloud and his gaping mouth was exactly like the dark hollow of a massive tree. The very earth violently quaked under his heavy feet. 

Krishna bravely confronted him alone. "GRRR!" Keshi rushed incredibly fast at Krishna with his enormous mouth wide open as if he would completely devour the entire sky. So impossibly fast was he that it appeared impossible to even catch him, let alone bring him under control. He aggressively tried to brutally kick Krishna with his front hooves. 
But Krishna deftly and smoothly eluded the heavy blow. Then he swiftly caught hold of the demon firmly by his hind legs, and easily threw him scornfully at a great distance, exactly like a mighty eagle casually tossing a small serpent. 

After a short while, Keshi dizzily pulled himself up and furiously tried to attack again. Krishna simply gazed at him perfectly calmly with a gentle smile. As the horse bit at him, Krishna effortlessly thrust his entire left arm deeply into the open mouth of the demon. His delicate, soft hand now magically resembled a searingly heated iron rod. 

At the agonizing touch of Krishna's burning hand, Keshi's huge teeth began to completely shatter and fall out. Then Krishna's arm miraculously began to swell massively inside the mouth of the demon. Larger and much larger it grew, till it completely choked the demon's throat, cutting off his air entirely. 
Keshi fell heavily to the ground throwing up his legs desperately in the air as he suffocated. He finally collapsed, and his massively swollen form literally burst open like a ripe, over-stuffed cucumber. 

***

A few days after easily killing Keshi, Krishna was happily playing with his friends on a sunny hilltop. 
"Let's play an exciting game of hide and seek," a boy suggested. "You five will be the clever thieves, seven of us will be the brave guards, while these little boys will pretend to be the sheep."

They were completely, deeply engrossed in the fun game when Vyomasura, a new, sneaky demon appeared there. Cleverly disguised as a normal cowherd, he seamlessly joined in the game as one of the 'thieves'. 
Pretending perfectly to play the game, he would stealthily take away the little boys who had become the 'sheep'. One by one, he secretly hid them deep in a dark cave nearby, completely covering the mouth of the cave with a massive, unmovable rock. 

As the long game progressed, the number of players strangely dwindled till there were only four or five cowherds left. Krishna had of course completely realised the terrible truth. 
*I will properly catch him this time,* Krishna thought. *His little, deadly game is completely over now.*

As soon as Vyomasura tried to sneakily take away another 'sheep', Krishna pounced violently on him, exactly like a fierce lion attacking a sneaky wolf. 
Caught, the demon instantly assumed his true, incredibly fearsome, giant form. But much as he desperately struggled, Vyomasura could absolutely not free himself from Krishna's iron grip. Krishna caught him incredibly roughly and powerfully pinned him down to the hard ground, crushing the life out of him. 
Having easily killed the sneaky demon, Krishna went straight forth to the hidden cave where his terrified companions had been trapped. Effortlessly breaking the massive rock at the entrance into pieces, Krishna joyfully freed all the boys trapped inside.

***

Meanwhile, Akrura too had finally started for Vraja in his chariot. *Oh, how I incredibly long to have a mere glimpse of Lord Krishna,* he thought happily. *Kamsa has actually done me a massive favour by sending me on this mission.*
When at sunset he finally reached his destination, he saw the beautiful Krishna and Balarama resting inside a cowshed. Akrura jumped hastily down from the chariot and tearfully prostrated himself entirely at their divine feet. 

The two brothers affectionately lifted him up and led Akrura to their house. Thereafter, he was served a truly sumptuous meal and highly honoured as a very welcome guest. 
Nanda said sadly to Akrura, "How do you possibly spend your days in the cruel, oppressive rule of Kamsa? His terrified subjects are exactly like the poor sheep reared by a ruthless butcher. So we will not even dare ask whether you are truly happy."

Krishna too gently enquired, "Yes, do tell us dear uncle, how everyone is faring at Mathura. We are incredibly happy indeed to see you here. Do tell us the true purpose of your sudden visit."
Akrura honestly related the terrible events at Mathura. "So when the sage Narada finally told Kamsa about the absolute truth of your birth, he sent me here to immediately fetch you on the clever pretext of inviting you to a grand wrestling match."

When they heard of Kamsa's foolish plan, Krishna and Balarama burst out laughing loudly. 
"Did you hear father, what our dear uncle Kamsa has in his clever mind?" Krishna chuckled. 
"Yes, I did," Nanda replied seriously. "Let us prepare to leave for Mathura immediately. I will tell all our people to gather the best gifts for Kamsa to keep him pleased."

Early the exact next morning, Akrura happily ascended onto his royal chariot to return to Mathura. Following him closely were many carts heavily laden with pots of sweet milk, fresh curd, and soft butter, to be presented formally to Kamsa. The cowherds seemed incredibly happy at the exciting idea of visiting the great city of Mathura. 
To the weeping gopikas, looking incredibly sadly at him, Krishna said affectionately with a highly charming smile, "Do not weep. I will absolutely be back soon."
Hoping desperately that Krishna might change his mind and quickly come back, the gopikas waited awhile on the road. Then with incredibly heavy hearts, they finally returned to their empty homes. 

Krishna and Balarama sped away fast on Akrura's chariot. On the long way, they stopped to rest by the river Yamuna. After the two brothers had refreshed themselves, Akrura said, "I will go and have a holy bath while you comfortably rest here in the chariot."

As he ceremoniously bathed in the cool waters of the Yamuna, he opened his eyes underwater. "Why, there I can clearly see Krishna and Balarama sitting perfectly inside the water. How can that possibly be! I am absolutely sure they are resting in the chariot."
Akrura peeped confusedly out of the water. "Yes, there they are, sitting perfectly comfortably in the chariot. Did I simply imagine that they were inside the water?"

Once again he dived deeply into the water. Now he miraculously beheld a truly splendid, divine sight. 
"Oh! There is the great Lord Shesha (the cosmic serpent) himself. Nestling perfectly in his massive coils is Krishna, wearing his beautiful yellow garment heavily decorated with priceless jewels."
Akrura was completely overwhelmed at the divine sight, and respectfully bowed deeply before them underwater. "Lord, different people foolishly worship you in many different ways. But just as all flowing rivers finally reach the single ocean, so also all the different, sincere forms of worship are ultimately directed only to you."

Just as a clever actor hides completely behind a curtain after a grand performance, Lord Krishna suddenly withdrew the divine vision inside the water. 
Akrura came completely out of the water in a stunned daze. 
"Have you perhaps seen something extraordinary, uncle?" Krishna teased. "Why are you looking so completely wonder-struck?"
"O Lord," Akrura smiled knowingly, "all the truly wonderful things of the earth, sky, or deep ocean are already perfectly present in you. What else is possibly left for me to ever see after I have truly beheld you?"

With these deeply respectful words, Akrura enthusiastically drove the chariot ahead. By late evening they finally reached the grand gates of Mathura. Nanda and his companions, who had gone swiftly ahead in their carts, eagerly met them at the city gates. 
Krishna said politely to Akrura, "Dear uncle, go safely to your house now. We will confidently get down here to see the amazing sights of the city."
"No, I absolutely do not want to go home without you two," Akrura pleaded. "Please come with me and totally bless our humble home with your divine presence."
"Indeed I will surely come with Dau (Balarama) to your house," Krishna promised. "But only after finally slaying Kamsa, the terrible oppressor of the Yadavas."

Akrura then went directly to Kamsa's palace. "O king! I have faithfully carried out your strict orders. Balarama and Krishna have been successfully brought to Mathura."

Meanwhile, enthusiastically accompanied by Balarama and the other cowherds, Krishna went casually around the great city of Mathura. 
"See how incredibly high the crystal gateways are!" the forest-dwelling boys marvelled. 
"Even the simple houses have big, heavy gates beautifully embossed with solid gold."
"The tall buildings are richly decorated with clear crystal, blue sapphires, red corals, white pearls, and green emeralds."

While the simple cowherds of Vraja were totally admiring the grand sights of the city of Mathura, they in turn were being deeply admired by the sophisticated women of Mathura. 
"How incredibly lucky are the simple gopikas of Vraja to be wonderfully able to behold these delightful, handsome faces everyday," the women whispered from their high balconies. 
As Krishna strode on incredibly gracefully along the path, they excitedly showered him with fragrant flowers. Some of them even stopped Krishna and Balarama right on the street to respectfully offer worship. 

Just then, Krishna saw the royal washerman pass haughtily by with a massive bundle. "Wait!" Krishna called out. "You will greatly prosper by generously giving us some suitable, clean clothes."
But the arrogant washerman, a loyal servant of Kamsa, said incredibly angrily, "You ill-mannered, dirty fools! Run away. You who live like animals in forests and mountains, how can you possibly think of dressing in such incredibly fine royal clothes? If the king's strict officers see arrogant people exactly like you, they will instantly take you prisoner and kill you."

When the washerman spoke so incredibly rudely, Krishna simply slapped him casually on his face. The single, powerful slap instantly killed the arrogant man. The washerman's terrified assistants fled in absolute fright when their master fell down dead. 
Krishna casually picked up the massive bundles, thrown by the fleeing washermen, and generously distributed the incredibly fine royal clothes among the excited cowherds. "Here, Balarama, take these."

When they went a little happily ahead, they came upon a friendly tailor. He expertly fitted the highly colourful, royal garments perfectly on Krishna and the other cowherds, in a most incredibly becoming fashion. The dark Krishna and the fair Balarama looked exactly like a majestic pair of elephants, one completely black and the other pure white, richly decorated for a grand festival. Krishna was very, very pleased with the kind tailor and deeply blessed him. "May you truly have excellent health, immense wealth, and total prosperity."

Thereafter, Krishna and Balarama went happily to the house of Sudama, the royal flowerman, who enthusiastically welcomed the two brothers and respectfully worshipped them with beautiful garlands of fresh flowers, cooling sandal-paste, and sweet betel leaves. 
"O Lord, I am completely blessed by this wonderful visit of yours," Sudama wept with joy. He then generously presented Krishna and Balarama with massive garlands of the absolute finest and most incredibly fragrant flowers. 
Thereupon, O Pareekshit, Krishna happily bestowed on the devoted Sudama several incredible boons, including ever-growing, limitless prosperity, a very long life, and endless fame. 

As Krishna and his brightly dressed friends were cheerfully sauntering along on the main, busy road of Mathura, they saw a woman with a terribly humped back carrying a tray. 
"O beautiful one," Krishna teased gently, "who are you? For whom are you carefully carrying this incredibly sweet-smelling sandal-paste?"

The poor hunchback was completely captivated by Krishna's stunning looks, his gentle, kind smile, and total friendliness. "My name is Trivakra, O incredibly handsome one! I am a highly favourite servant of King Kamsa. I carefully make this special sandal-paste and soothing ointments strictly for him. But none is absolutely more worthy of being beautifully anointed by these ointments than the truly divine two of you. Please, gratefully accept this."

With the bright yellow paste beautifully contrasting with Krishna's entirely dark complexion, and the deep red coloured paste on Balarama's very fair body, they looked incredibly attractive. 
Krishna then gently placed his two feet perfectly on the toes of the hunchback. He raised his hands and with his fingers, he pushed up her chin firmly with a slight, sudden jerk. "I must help her by making her twisted body perfectly straight."

At the incredibly miraculous touch of his divine hand, Trivakra instantly lost her terrible hump, and stood perfectly before him, her body entirely erect and beautifully well-proportioned. Holding a corner of Krishna's yellow garment, she pleaded passionately, "You both must absolutely come to my house. I simply cannot go away leaving you here."
"I will certainly come," Krishna promised gently, "but only after my important task here is completely over." Thus with incredibly sweet words, Krishna finally took leave of her. 

***

At last they finally reached the grand site of the bow sacrifice. On entering the massive hall, they saw a truly large and incredibly mighty bow. Resplendent exactly like a bright rainbow, it was heavily studded with incredibly expensive jewels. Several heavily armed guards were standing constantly around, fiercely protecting it. 

Completely ignoring their angry protests, Krishna reached confidently for the mighty bow. With just his left hand, he deftly lifted the incredibly heavy bow, effortlessly strung it, and in a matter of mere minutes, casually bent it so far that he split the massive bow completely into two. SNAP!

By now, the angry guards were fully alerted, and aggressively surrounded Krishna. "Catch him!" "Don't let him go alive!"
Krishna and Balarama simply picked up the broken pieces of the massive bow and casually began to effectively use them as incredibly heavy weapons. After easily vanquishing the numerous guards with their makeshift weapons, Krishna and Balarama stepped calmly out of the ruined hall and casually roamed about the city again. 
"Look at these two incredibly brave men," the citizens whispered in awe. "Surely they are of truly divine origin."

At sunset, they peacefully returned to their camp outside the city, where their simple carts had been safely kept. After washing up cleanly and happily eating a delicious meal of delicacies made of rich milk, they slept perfectly peacefully. 

Meanwhile, when Kamsa heard in horror that Krishna and Balarama had not only effortlessly broken the sacred, unbreakable bow, but also brutally killed his elite guards with utter ease, he was terribly worried. He spent a completely sleepless, terrifying night amidst several terrible ill omens. When he fearfully looked in the mirror, he gasped, "Why, I can't even see my own head in the reflection!" 
When at last he finally drifted into a fitful, exhausted sleep, he had absolutely terrible nightmares, and got up with a terrible fright. 

Recovering a little fake courage at dawn, Kamsa proudly inaugurated the grand festival of wrestling matches. The massive arena place was incredibly well decorated with beautiful garlands, bright banners, and colourful bunting. Melodious, exciting music was being loudly played in the background. Benches were comfortably provided for all the citizens. On the absolute highest point was placed the grand royal throne for Kamsa, who was internally still in a deeply disturbed, terrified state. 
Then massive, terrifying champions like Chanura and Mushtika, arrived proudly in the arena one by one, flexing their massive muscles. 

***

When Krishna arrived at the arena site, he saw the massive, mad elephant Kuvalayapeeda completely blocking the main entrance. In a loud, commanding voice he called out, "O mahout! Allow us to safely pass!"
When there was absolutely no response, he raised his powerful voice in clear anger. "Can't you hear? Make way immediately or I will definitely send you and your useless elephant to your terrible death."

The arrogant mahout deliberately provoked the mad elephant to violently attack Krishna. The enraged elephant aggressively wrapped his massive trunk securely around Krishna. But Krishna nimbly and incredibly smoothly wriggled out of the elephant's crushing hold, and cleverly hid right between its massive legs after giving it a truly good, painful blow. 

The giant elephant was absolutely furious at losing sight of his tiny victim, and frantically began to ferret him out with his sweeping trunk. But Krishna and Balarama casually took firm hold of his small tail and powerfully dragged him completely backwards for quite a long distance. 
For a long time, Krishna playfully teased and taunted the exhausted elephant. Then with just one powerful hand he pulled violently at the trunk and completely floored the massive beast. Krishna then effortlessly pulled out the heavy, sharp tusks, and with them, he easily and brutally finished off both, the giant elephant and its wicked keeper. 

Casually holding the massive, bloody tusks on his shoulder like a prize, Krishna confidently entered the wrestling arena. People of Mathura began to excitedly whisper among themselves. 
"He is the incredible one who killed Pootana."
"Also Trinavarta, Keshi, and Dhenuka."
"He is the very one who bravely conquered the terrible Kaliya."

Addressing Krishna and Balarama, Chanura, the massive ace wrestler, said mockingly, "Welcome, Krishna and Balarama. Our great king has specifically invited you here to publicly show your skill."
"Yes Chanura, we would absolutely love to do so," Krishna smiled innocently. "But we are mere children. It would definitely not be proper or fair for a giant like you to forcefully compete with youngsters."
Chanura sneered and said, "Hah! You and Balarama are absolutely neither children nor innocent youngsters. Why, only moments ago you effortlessly and playfully killed the incredibly mighty elephant Kuvalayapeeda. Come on Krishna, bravely try your might on me, while Balarama fights Mushtika."

The unfair challenge was calmly accepted. The brutal fight began in complete earnest as the mismatched pair of wrestlers violently turned and twisted, aggressively rolled and jumped, and powerfully pulled and pushed at each other. 
The entire audience, especially the soft-hearted women, were deeply disturbed as they saw what seemed an incredibly unequal, brutal combat. 
"How can cruel King Kamsa possibly allow this terrible injustice?"
"Let us go away immediately. I simply can't bear to see this slaughter."
"Yes. These poor, beautiful young boys being unfairly pitted against massive, expert wrestlers."

Vasudeva and Devaki clearly overheard this panicked conversation as their dark prison was very near the arena. They were entirely filled with terrible worry and deep anxiety. "What will possibly happen to our dear boys," Devaki wept. "Imagine fighting with those impossibly powerful men."

By now, however, the sweating, panting Chanura was actually beginning to deeply regret his arrogant challenge to Krishna. He could not move him an inch. In utter frustration, he violently attacked Krishna directly on the chest with his massively clenched fists. Unperturbed entirely, Krishna calmly caught firm hold of his thick arms and effortlessly flung him brutally on the hard ground after easily turning him around several dizzying times high in the air. Chanura's neck snapped, and he died instantly.

Meanwhile Balarama had easily managed to defeat and crushingly kill not only Mushtika, but also Koota, who foolishly came to fight him next. With a single swift, powerful kick, Krishna completely beheaded Shala, and easily tore the entire body of Toshala, the two other massive wrestlers who foolishly came forward. 
Chanura, Mushtika, Koota, Shala, and Toshala—the five incredibly strong, unbeatable men of Kamsa—all lay completely dead in the dust. 

Kamsa could absolutely not bear to see this impossible disaster, and desperately issued a rapid series of panicked orders to his terrified men. "Drive these two dangerous sons of Vasudeva completely out of town! Capture Nanda immediately! Take away absolutely all the belongings of the cowherds. Kill Vasudeva! Kill Ugrasena too!"

Even as the terrified Kamsa was frantically issuing these desperate orders, Krishna incredibly leapt completely up to his incredibly high royal seat in a single bound. Kamsa tried desperately to defend his life, wildly brandishing his sharp sword. But catching Kamsa effortlessly by his hair, Krishna violently threw him completely down into the arena. Krishna then jumped incredibly heavily directly on Kamsa, crushing and killing him that very instant. 

Krishna then dragged Kamsa's dead body effortlessly on the dirty floor of the arena amidst incredibly loud, shocked cries from the massive audience. 
Kamsa had eight younger, equally wicked brothers, who furiously rushed at Krishna and Balarama with drawn swords to take immediate revenge... but Balarama effortlessly and brutally killed them all in moments. 

Thereafter, Krishna and Balarama hurried joyously to the dark prison to finally free Vasudeva and Devaki. Though absolutely filled with unbelievable joy to finally see Krishna, his parents, having just seen him effortlessly slay the greatest warriors and the king, found it incredibly difficult to simply accept the Supreme Lord as their own mortal son. 

"Thus ends the fourth incredible session of our rendering of the tenth chapter of the Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded.
