# Thugsen - 1. Thugsen

Long ago in the kingdom of Avanti, King Vikram lived with his seven wives. He loved the youngest queen, Shashikala, the most. When the other six queens discovered she was pregnant, their jealousy turned into a wicked plot. Fearing the King would completely ignore them if Shashikala bore a son, they spread vile rumors and slander about her to the King. 

Enraged and feeling betrayed, King Vikram banished the innocent Queen Shashikala to a remote jungle fortress without even listening to her defense. The Prime Minister, who secretly hoped to become the next ruler, eagerly carried out the order and ignored the pleas of Shashikala's loyal maid, Devaki. 

Months later, deep in the jungle fortress, a healthy prince was born. Queen Shashikala and Devaki raised the boy, vowing to teach him everything they knew. Devaki taught him the arts of mimicry, disguise, and trickery, calling him "Thugsen" (master thief/trickster), while his mother taught him music and the arts of a prince. 

When Thugsen turned sixteen, his mother finally revealed the truth about his royal birth and his unjust banishment. Determined to force his father and the Prime Minister to make amends, Thugsen set out for the capital city of Avanti. His mother gave him a single precious jewel for his journey. 

On the way, five robbers tried to steal his belongings. Thugsen cleverly offered to share a meal with them first. He then asked to take a quick dip in a nearby lake. When he emerged, he held his mother's jewel, claiming he found it at the bottom of the lake where "hundreds more were waiting to be picked." The greedy robbers all dived in, allowing Thugsen to easily steal their horses and their entire booty!

Arriving in Avanti, Thugsen disguised himself as a simple villager and befriended an old street singer, sharing his humble hut. 

That night, Thugsen snuck to the palace and used his musical skills to sing a soothing lullaby, putting the royal guards to sleep. He entered the palace, stole the necklaces of the six wicked queens, and defiantly drove a nail into the main doorpost before slipping away. 

The next day, the furious King offered a massive reward of ten thousand rupees for the thief's capture. Two famous wrestlers, Balabhima and Hanuman, bragged they would catch him. 

Thugsen disguised himself as a fortune-teller. He visited Balabhima and predicted the thief would be at the cemetery at midnight, wrapped in a black rug and carrying a stick. Then, he visited Hanuman and predicted a pot of gold awaited him at the cemetery at midnight, advising him to wrap himself in a black rug and carry a stick for protection!

That night, the two wrestlers violently beat each other up in the dark cemetery, each thinking the other was the thief or a robber. When the King heard how easily his champions were fooled, he was astonished. 

The Prime Minister proudly promised to catch the thief himself. But Thugsen was delighted to match wits against his mother's enemy. 

Thugsen disguised himself as a wealthy merchant from the South and visited the city's royal jewelers. He asked to take their finest ornaments to his lodgings to decide, leaving a locked steel box filled with "money" as a deposit. Two days later, the jewelers opened the boxes to find only stones! 

Furious that the thief had struck again, the King ordered the city gates closed early and a house-to-house search initiated. 

Anticipating this, Thugsen disguised himself as a desperate washerman and tricked the guards into letting him out of the city gate. He went to the river, threw a hollow gourd into the water, and hid in a tree. 

The Prime Minister soon arrived, spotted the floating gourd in the dark, and assumed it was the thief swimming away. He stripped off his clothes, tied his horse to a tree, and dove in. While the Prime Minister was swimming fruitlessly after the gourd, Thugsen climbed down, stole the Prime Minister's horse and clothes, and rode back to the city gate!

Disguised as the Prime Minister, Thugsen warned the guards that the thief was coming. A shivering, naked man soon arrived, claiming to be the Prime Minister, but the guards threw him in the dungeon as the "thief"! The King himself had to come down and order the humiliated Prime Minister's release. 

Now, the King himself decided to hunt down the thief. Thugsen intercepted the Prime Minister in the forest, disguised as a royal messenger. He tricked the Prime Minister and the King into taking different, incredibly thick paths into the deep jungle. As it grew dark, Thugsen stole the King's horse and left the two powerful men hopelessly lost, exhausted, and hungry in the forest overnight. 

When the King finally returned, he discovered the thief had secretly returned all the stolen jewels to the merchants. Realizing this was no common thief, the King made a proclamation: the young man would not only be pardoned but rewarded if he presented himself. 

The next day, Thugsen confidently strode into the packed royal hall. The crowd recognized him as the loitering villager, the fortune-teller, and the wealthy merchant. 

"I am the prince of Avanti—your son!" Thugsen declared to the shocked King. 

"I am serious. My mother is Queen Shashikala!" Thugsen explained. "She was unjustly banished by you seventeen years ago. My pranks, as you call them, were meant to attract your attention and to demand justice. My mother was innocent—the victim of the six queens!"

The King, who had long suspected the truth, stepped down and tearfully embraced his son. He immediately ordered the six wicked queens and the corrupt Prime Minister punished. 

A special chariot was sent to bring Queen Shashikala and the loyal maid Devaki back to the palace with full honors. 

To celebrate the joyous reunion, the old King announced his retirement. His clever, brave son, Prince Thugsen, was officially installed on the throne, ready to rule Avanti with wisdom and justice.
