# Thugsen: A Marathi Folktale

Long long ago, in the kingdom of Avanti, lived a king named Vikram. He had seven wives, but while he loved them all, he loved his youngest, Queen Shashikala, the most. This made the other six queens intensely jealous, especially when they discovered she was expecting a baby. "If she has a son, the king will never look at us again!" they whispered.

Plotting her downfall, the six queens approached King Vikram that evening with false tears. "It's Shashikala, my lord," they lied. "She has spread vile slander about you! We are ashamed to say more."

Enraged and feeling betrayed, the king stormed into Shashikala's chamber. When the gentle queen, confused and speechless, couldn't defend herself, King Vikram believed the lies. "Take her away!" he ordered his Prime Minister. "Imprison her in my jungle fortress. She may take her maid with her."

As they were led away, the loyal maid, Devaki, pleaded with the Prime Minister. "She is expecting a baby! It might turn out to be the long-desired heir. Please, plead on her behalf." But the Prime Minister, secretly hoping to usurp the throne himself, silenced her, refusing to help.

Months later, in the remote jungle fortress, Shashikala gave birth to a healthy baby boy. "Alas, Devaki, how shall we bring up a prince in this jungle?" she wept.
"We shall bring him up like the prince he is," Devaki vowed. "You teach him the arts you know. I'll teach him what I know."

And so they did. Devaki taught the boy the arts of mimicry, disguise, and deception, until he could flawlessly act as a terrifying highway robber. "What an excellent thug you'd make!" Devaki laughed. "Thugsen! That's what we'll call you!" Shashikala taught him music, singing, and literature.

Sixteen years passed. One day, Shashikala sadly remarked, "It's over sixteen years since the king, my husband, banished me from the palace."
Thugsen, who had never known his true lineage, insisted on hearing the whole story. When his mother finished, his eyes blazed with determination. "Then you are a queen and I am a prince! That scoundrel of a Prime Minister and my father shall pay for wronging us. I will go to Avanti and force them to make amends!"

Despite his mother's pleas to forgive and forget, Thugsen set off for Avanti, armed only with a single precious jewel from his mother and some food from Devaki.

On his journey, Thugsen stopped by a lake to rest. Five ruthless robbers surrounded him, demanding his belongings. Quick-witted, Thugsen invited them to share his food first. "I never eat without bathing," he added, diving into the lake. Fifteen minutes later, he emerged with his fist tightly clenched, revealing his mother's ruby. 
"Where did you get that?" the robbers gasped.
"At the bottom of the lake, of course!" Thugsen lied smoothly. "There are hundreds of them waiting to be picked!"
Greedy and foolish, the five robbers dove into the water. Chuckling, Thugsen grabbed their loot, mounted the best of their five horses, and rode off toward the city.

In Avanti, Thugsen befriended a kind old street singer and shared his humble hut. That night, Thugsen snuck into the palace grounds, singing a melody so soothing that the guards fell fast asleep. He slipped into the chambers of the six wicked queens, stealing their necklaces right off their necks, and drove a nail into the palace's main doorpost as a mocking signature.

The next morning, the city was in an uproar. The king announced a massive reward for the thief's capture. Two famous wrestlers, Balabhima and Hanuman, boasted they would catch the rogue. 

Hearing this, Thugsen disguised himself as a fortune-teller. He visited Balabhima, predicting, "The thief will be at the cemetery tonight, wrapped in a black rug and carrying a stick. Be bold and attack him!" Then, he visited Hanuman, predicting, "Tonight is your lucky day. Go to the cemetery at midnight in a black rug with a stick, and you will find a pot of gold!"
That night, the two wrestlers met in the dark cemetery. Mistaking each other for the thief and a rival robber, they beat each other senseless while Thugsen watched from afar.

Frustrated, the Prime Minister himself vowed to catch the thief. Thugsen, eager to punish the man who had exiled his mother, disguised himself as a wealthy merchant and visited the royal jewellers. Using sleight of hand, he swapped cases of priceless gems for boxes full of stones. The ruined jewellers flooded the king's court with complaints.

Furious, the Prime Minister ordered the city gates locked at sunset to conduct a house-to-house search. Anticipating this, Thugsen disguised himself as a poor washerman and begged the gate guards to let him out to work. Once outside, he climbed a tree overlooking the river, holding a large, hollow gourd. 

When the Prime Minister rode up on his patrol, Thugsen tossed the gourd into the river. Thinking it was the thief swimming away, the Prime Minister stripped off his clothes, tied his horse to a tree, and dove in. While he vainly chased the floating gourd, Thugsen climbed down, stole the Prime Minister's horse and clothes, and rode back to the city gates. Wearing the Prime Minister's clothes, he easily got back inside. 

Shivering and humiliated, the real Prime Minister returned to the gates later, only to be arrested by his own guards who had been warned by Thugsen about a "naked impostor." The king had to personally intervene to free his freezing minister.

Thugsen wasn't done yet. He disguised himself as a royal messenger and intercepted the Prime Minister, leading him deep into the thickest part of the jungle under the pretense of meeting the king. Then, he found the king's hunting party and led the king into the same dense forest, claiming the Prime Minister was waiting. Leaving them both lost and exhausted, Thugsen stole their horses and rode back to the city, laughing, "I must thank you, father, for letting me grow up in a jungle!"

When the battered king and his minister finally returned to Avanti, they discovered that all the stolen jewels and necklaces had been mysteriously returned to their rightful owners. Realizing this was no ordinary thief, the king sent out a city-wide proclamation: "His Majesty wishes to meet the young man who has made a fool of us all. He will be pardoned and rewarded!"

The next day, the royal hall was packed. Suddenly, Thugsen stepped forward. "Here I am!"
The king stared at the bold young man. "Who are you?"
"I am the Prince of Avanti—your son!" Thugsen declared. "My mother is Queen Shashikala. She was unjustly banished by you seventeen years ago. My pranks were meant to attract your attention and demand justice. My mother was innocent—the victim of the six jealous queens!"

The king, who had long suspected the truth, stepped down from his throne and embraced his son with tears in his eyes. "Forgive me. Those who were responsible for the injustice shall be punished."

Queen Shashikala and the faithful Devaki were brought back to the palace with immense honor. The six wicked queens were punished, and the treacherous Prime Minister was dismissed. 

At a grand feast celebrating their reunion, King Vikram made a final proclamation: "I am old and wish to retire. My son, Prince Thugsen, shall be installed on the throne!" And the clever prince who fought for justice ruled Avanti wisely for many years.
