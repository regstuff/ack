# Tales Of Shiva - 1. Shiva and Arjuna

Quiet, peaceful Indrakeela, nestled high in the Himalayas, was a serene retreat of holy ascetics. 

One day, a stranger arrived.

"Who could this stranger be?" an ascetic whispered.

"Doesn't he know that weapons have no place here?" another asked, pointing to the stranger's bow and quiver.

The young ascetics followed the stranger to the river bank and watched him closely. 

"Look at his bow! Could he be Arjuna, the Pandava?"

"If he is, why is he here?"

"The Pandavas are in exile, having lost the game of dice to the wily Duryodhana," an older ascetic explained. "Perhaps the poor Pandava is seeking divine grace!"

It was indeed Arjuna, who had come to the remote mountains to propitiate Lord Shiva.

He built a small linga out of the earth and sat before it. "Om Namo Shivaya. Om Namo Shivaya," he chanted. 

For days he persisted. "Om Namo Shivaya."

Four months later, the spiritual heat generated by his intense penance had grown unbearable.

"We cannot go any nearer," an ascetic gasped, sweating profusely. 

"The heat of the terrible penance is spreading far and wide."

Soon, magical smoke billowed out and choked the whole forest. Unable to bear it, the sages of Indrakeela set out for Kailasa, the divine abode of Lord Shiva.

"Lord, grant Arjuna his wish, and relieve us of this suffering," the sages pleaded before Shiva.

"So be it," Lord Shiva nodded.

When the sages departed, Goddess Parvati turned to her husband. "What does Arjuna want, my lord?"

"He wants celestial weapons," Shiva explained.

"Can he wield them, my lord?" Parvati asked doubtfully.

"I will find out by testing him," Shiva smiled. "I'll appear before him as a Kirata, a simple mountain hunter, and engage him in a duel."

"May I accompany you?" Parvati asked.

"You may, but in disguise."

"I shall come as a Kirata-woman," Parvati agreed.

When the divine hordes of Shiva heard about it, they rushed to him. "Lord, we would like to witness the great combat. May we accompany you?"

"You may, in the guise of Kirata women," Shiva permitted.

Soon, the disguised divine hunting party approached Indrakeela. 

"See that boar running wild, my lord," Parvati pointed.

"Hah! A fit target for my arrow!" Shiva laughed, drawing his bow. 

But the wily, swift boar outdistanced the Kirata and charged straight into the quiet hermitage, driving the ascetics helter-skelter.

"It is Mookasura again! Now in the form of a boar!" an ascetic screamed, recognizing the shape-shifting demon Mooka.

His deep penance disturbed by the din, Arjuna opened his eyes, immediately raised his bow, and took aim at the charging beast.

"Stop! It's mine," Shiva shouted, bursting into the clearing as the Kirata. "Leave it to me. I was stalking it."

"Shoot it and claim it," Arjuna retorted coldly. "Hunters do not beg."

The boar was caught exactly between the two unerring arrows, which struck it simultaneously. The beast fell dead, instantly reverting to the hideous, true form of Demon Mooka.

"Your arrow killed it," Parvati proudly told Shiva.

"Victory to our lord!" the disguised Kirata women cheered.

The wild exultation of the Kirata women amused Arjuna. "O Kirata, does not this thick forest terrify your women folk? And you their only escort?" he mocked.

"Young man, we fear nothing," Shiva replied calmly.

"Perhaps *you* are terrified," Arjuna smirked. "You do appear soft!"

"Soft? Me? Didn't you see the force of my arrow piercing the boar?" Shiva challenged.

"It was our chief's arrow that killed the boar," the Kirata women yelled at Arjuna.

Arjuna was enraged by the insult. "They speak the truth, young man," he sneered at Shiva. "Your arrow hit a dead boar."

"All right, Kirata," Arjuna declared, drawing an arrow. "We will settle once and for all who is the better shot."

Arrows whizzed past as the two great archers matched their skills. 

After a while, Arjuna paused, astounded. *My quiver is empty and there is not a scratch yet on the Kirata,* he thought in disbelief.

"O mighty archer, shall I lend you a few arrows?" Shiva mocked gently.

Infuriated, Arjuna abandoned his bow. In a deft move, Arjuna caught the Kirata in his bowstring to strangle him. But the next moment, the Kirata effortlessly wrested the bow from Arjuna and threw it away!

The Kirata women danced for joy. "The ascetic is beaten!" they chanted.

Undaunted, Arjuna drew his heavy sword and rushed towards the Kirata. "Kirata, think of the Lord at the last moment of your life and prepare to die!"

But as Arjuna smote the head of the Kirata with his heavy sword, the blade shattered into pieces!

Shorn of his arms, Arjuna continued the fight by uprooting entire trees and hurling them at his opponent. When that failed, in a desperate attempt, Arjuna charged at the Kirata with his bare hands. 

But the Kirata remained completely unscathed. 

With a simple flick of his wrist, the Kirata lifted Arjuna into the air and flung him effortlessly to the ground.

Lying on the dirt, a humbled Arjuna thought of Shiva and his grace. Right on the spot, he quickly made a linga from the earth and began to worship it. 

"Om Namo Shivaya," he prayed deeply.

Instantly, a new divine power surged through his limbs. A rejuvenated Arjuna leapt to his feet and again challenged his rival.

"Kirata, your time is up!" Arjuna yelled, raising his fists.

But he stopped mid-stride, as if transfixed. 

*The flowers I just offered to my Lord Shiva on the linga... they are resting perfectly on the Kirata's head!* Arjuna realized, his eyes widening. *I see now. You are none other than him!*

Arjuna fell weeping at the feet of the Kirata. "O Lord, pardon me and my vanity."

Shiva then revealed himself in his true, glorious form, and so did Parvati in hers.

"I am pleased with your devotion and courage," Shiva smiled, blessing the Pandava prince. "I shall impart to you the secret of the Pashupata missile which will help you in the hour of need."

Shiva's word came true. Years later, in the great Mahabharata war, it was only with the divine Pashupata that Arjuna could finally defeat and kill his mighty arch-rival, Karna.


# Tales Of Shiva - 2. Shiva the Fisherman

Once, in the serene heights of Kailasa, Lord Shiva started expounding the profound mystery of the Vedas to his beloved wife, Goddess Parvati, who sat listening attentively. 

The divine discourse was so complex and detailed that years passed by, and Shiva continued his explanations without a single break.

Gradually, in spite of her best efforts to focus, Parvati's attention flagged. Her mind began to wander, and her eyes glazed over. 

Noticing this, Shiva was immensely annoyed. "The Vedas are not for you," he snapped angrily. "Since you are no better than a common fisherwoman with your lack of focus... may you be born as one!"

Due to his powerful divine curse, Parvati instantly vanished from Kailasa. 

The very next moment, Shiva's anger completely evaporated, replaced by deep regret. "What have I done!" he lamented, clutching his head. "In thoughtless haste have I cast away one whose love for me was peerless."

Shiva's depressed state of mind did not escape Nandi, his trusted servant and mount. *Now my master will know no peace until mother Parvati returns,* Nandi thought sadly.

Meanwhile, Parvati had already reached the earth. She lay as an abandoned human infant under a *punnai* tree near a coastal village, where she was soon found by the chief of the Paravars, a clan of local fishermen.

"What a lovely child!" the chief gasped, picking her up gently. "No doubt it is God's gift to me. I'll call her Parvati."

Little Parvati grew up happily by the sea. She used to eagerly go with her foster father whenever he went out fishing. "Look! A huge fish!" she would point out. 

As she grew up, she even learnt to row the boat herself. "You are a real help to me, Parvati," her proud father would say. 

The villagers admired her greatly. "A pretty girl!" they would say. "Strong and clever too! Lucky is the man who weds her!"

Meanwhile, back at Kailasa, Shiva was miserable. "Nandi, I can no longer live without Parvati!" he cried.

"My lord, why don't you go down to earth and claim her?" Nandi suggested reasonably.

"How can I, Nandi?" Shiva sighed. "Because of my curse, she will now marry a fisherman. I cannot interfere."

Nandi narrowed his eyes in thought. *Then I will see to it that you are forced to become a fisherman,* he plotted silently.

Assuming the form of a massive, terrifying shark, Nandi headed straight for the coast where the Paravars lived. 

"A shark!" a fisherman yelled from a boat. 

"Quick! We must get it!" another shouted, grabbing a spear. 

But before the fishermen could act, the massive shark attacked, smashing their boat to splinters, and the two fishermen found themselves thrashing in the water.

As the days went by, the shark became a regular, unstoppable menace to the village. "Our nets are torn. And our boats in pieces!" the fishermen complained to their chief. "What shall we do?"

At last, the desperate chief of the Paravars came up with an award. "I will offer the hand of my beautiful daughter to the one who captures the shark," he proclaimed.

Many a brave young man tried to capture the beast... and utterly failed, their boats destroyed by the monstrous fish.

The desperate Paravars at last sought divine help. They gathered before a Shiva linga. "O compassionate one, save us from the shark," the chief prayed.

His daughter, Parvati, too prayed fervently. "Lord, come to our rescue. Don't fail us in the hour of need."

Shiva heard his beloved's prayer. Instantly, he assumed the disguise of a handsome, muscular young fisherman and appeared before the chief of the Paravars.

"I have come to catch the shark," the disguised Lord announced confidently. 

"Our tribe will be indebted to you forever, if you succeed," the chief promised.

Net in hand, Shiva waded boldly down into the sea. The massive shark approached, its fin cutting through the water. But when Shiva cast his net, the shark—who was none other than the devoted Nandi—happily surrendered himself without a fight.

*My mission is fulfilled,* Nandi thought joyfully as the net closed around him.

As Shiva dragged the massive shark ashore, the villagers cheered wildly. "You are our saviour!" they chanted. 

Watching from the crowd, Parvati blushed. *How fortunate for me that this brave man has caught the shark!* she thought.

The chief embraced the hero. "Welcome to our tribe, young man. By catching the great fish you have proven yourself a great fisherman," he declared.

"I am indeed glad to hear that," Shiva smiled. 

"Accept my daughter as your wife," the chief offered happily, fulfilling his promise.

And so, Shiva, disguised as the fisherman, joyously married Parvati, the fisherwoman. With the curse fulfilled, Parvati regained her divine memories. Nandi assumed his true, glorious form as a divine bull, and carried the two blissful gods back to their home in Kailasa.


# Tales Of Shiva - 3. Shiva and Markandeya

Long ago, the devout Sage Mrikandu and his wife Marudvati were deeply saddened by their lack of children. Determined to seek divine intervention, Sage Mrikandu observed severe penances in the forest to propitiate Lord Shiva.

After many days of intense prayer, Lord Shiva finally appeared before him in a brilliant flash of light. 

"Lord, favour me with a son," Mrikandu pleaded, bowing low.

Shiva looked at him thoughtfully. "I shall grant your wish, but you must make a choice. Do you want a son who will live a very long life, but be entirely lacking in virtues... or a son who will be wise and highly virtuous, but will live for only sixteen years?"

Mrikandu didn't hesitate. "I will have the virtuous son, my Lord."

Granting the difficult wish of the sage, Lord Shiva vanished.

In due course, Marudvati gave birth to a beautiful, radiant boy. "The boy shall be named Markandeya," Mrikandu announced proudly.

Markandeya grew into a truly exceptional youth. While barely sixteen, he had already mastered the complex Vedas. Visiting sages were astounded by his wisdom. "Mrikandu, you are incredibly fortunate to have such a brilliant son," they would say.

But when the visiting sages left, Mrikandu and Marudvati would invariably break down in tears. "I cannot forget that all this brilliance will soon be put out by cruel death," Mrikandu wept.

Just then, Markandeya came home carrying a basket of fresh flowers for his daily worship. "Mother! Why are you weeping?" he asked, rushing to her side. "What can I do to make you smile?"

Marudvati only cried harder, unable to look him in the eye. 

"Why don't you speak, mother?" Markandeya pressed. "Do you think I can't get you what you want? I am not a child... I will be sixteen years old tomorrow!"

"Son, that is exactly why your mother is weeping," Mrikandu sighed heavily.

"Why? What is wrong with being sixteen?" the boy asked, confused.

"I will explain. Listen!" Mrikandu said, and he tearfully told his son about the events leading to his birth, and the fatal condition Lord Shiva had placed upon his life.

Instead of panicking, Markandeya smiled calmly. "Don't cry, mother. I will not die," he said with utter conviction. "I will seek immortality through the grace of Shiva, who is the ultimate conqueror of death."

"May you succeed by his grace!" Mrikandu prayed, placing his hands on his son's head. Looking up at the sky, Mrikandu whispered, "Shiva, I leave my son to your mercy. Protect him."

Early the next morning, on his sixteenth birthday, Markandeya went to the lonely sea-shore. With deep devotion, he made a Shiva linga out of the wet sand and carefully adorned it with fresh flowers. Then he sat down to pray, completely losing himself in meditation. 

Towards nightfall, he stood up and began to joyously sing and dance before the Lord, his heart full of pure devotion.

Suddenly, a massive, terrifying figure appeared on the beach. It was Yama, the God of Death, holding his deadly noose.

"Get ready to leave this world," Yama boomed, stepping towards the boy.

"O Lord of Death, please wait," Markandeya replied calmly. "I have not yet finished my worship."

"Foolish boy, don't you know that death waits for none?" Yama sneered.

"Please do not obstruct me in my worship of Lord Shiva," Markandeya pleaded softly.

"Fool! Do you hope to escape from me by clinging to Shiva?" Yama laughed, raising his hand. "The grip of death is fatal, as you shall now know."

Yama swung his rope and caught Markandeya's neck tightly in the magical noose. As Yama brutally dragged him away, the desperate boy threw his arms around the sand linga. "Lord, protect me!" he screamed.

The very next moment, the sand linga burst open in a blinding explosion of divine energy! Lord Shiva sprang out from the linga, his eyes blazing with fury, and viciously kicked Yama squarely on the chest.

"Lord!" Yama gasped, falling backward onto the sand.

When Yama had recovered his breath, he knelt humbly before the furious god. "Lord, pardon me," Yama begged, quickly departing the beach without his prize.

Markandeya fell to his knees in sheer awe and gratitude. "O Lord, the source of life, I salute you."

Shiva smiled gently at the devoted youth. "Markandeya, because of your unwavering faith, you will forever be free from the grasp of Yama. You will be immortal."

With a final blessing, Shiva vanished into the night.

Markandeya happily returned home to his grieving parents. "Mother! Father!" he called out joyfully as he ran through the door. "The Lord did save me!"
