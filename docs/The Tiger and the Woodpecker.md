# The Tiger and the Woodpecker

One day, deep in the forest, a fierce tiger was greedily devouring the game he had just killed. In his haste, a sharp bone got stuck painfully in his jaw. 

A woodpecker, who lived in the branches above, watched him struggle. 

"Try as I might, I cannot get the bone unstuck," the tiger moaned, clawing frantically at his mouth. "I will not be able to eat anything unless I get this bone out."

Days passed. The mighty tiger became weaker and weaker. "If someone does not come to help me, I will soon die of starvation," he thought miserably. 

The woodpecker, though puzzled by the predator's sudden weakness, was hailed by the tiger. "What's the matter with you? Why do you lie there with your mouth open?" the bird asked, fluttering down.

The tiger weakly pointed to the bone wedged deep in his jaw. 

"Oh! Oh! It's a bone," the woodpecker realized. "I shall remove it... if you will give me my fill of the flesh of the animals you kill."

The tiger nodded his head eagerly in agreement. 

Trusting the tiger's promise, the brave little woodpecker flew right into the tiger's massive, terrifying mouth, grabbed the bone with his strong beak, and pulled it out at full speed! He flew up to his tree and dropped the offending bone. 

"I'm starving. I'd better get myself some food," the relieved tiger growled, bounding away into the forest. 

A few hours later, the tiger successfully killed an animal and began devouring it ravenously. 

The woodpecker flew down. "Hey, friend, wait! Have you forgotten your promise? Give me my share."

The tiger looked at the woodpecker and pretended he had never seen him before. "Who are you? Why should I offer you any part of this?"

The woodpecker was shocked. "What!"

The tiger laughed cruelly. "You know I am a wild animal. I could easily have eaten you when you entered my mouth. I didn't. Be grateful for that and begone!"

"So that's it," the woodpecker thought, his anger rising. "You take advantage of me because I am weak. But then I have my sharp beak."

The woodpecker patiently waited in the branches until the tiger finished his heavy meal and fell asleep. "Ah! He's asleep!"

Suddenly, the bird swooped down like a dart and pecked fiercely at one of the tiger's eyes, blinding it instantly!

"AAARGH!" The tiger roared with agonizing pain, clutching his bleeding face. "Ah! You've pierced my eye. How could you be so cruel?"

The woodpecker hovered safely above him. "You know I have a sharp beak. I could easily have blinded you in both eyes. But I didn't. Be grateful for that and stop roaring so much."


# The Clever Turtle

Long ago, there lived a thriving colony of turtles on a beautiful sea coast. But their peaceful existence was shattered by a terrifying predator. Every single day, a massive eagle would swoop down from the sky and snatch one of them for food. 

This relentless hunting deeply worried an aged, wise turtle. "At this rate, not one of us will be left alive," he thought, withdrawing into his shell. He thought hard for hours until an idea struck him. "Why, that's it! Why didn't I think of it before!"

He called a secret meeting of all the surviving turtles. "We must do something to save ourselves," they pleaded. "We'll do anything you say to save our lives."

"If you do as I say, we can get rid of the eagle for good," the old turtle promised. "Hide yourselves in the water when you see the eagle tomorrow. I alone will remain on the beach."

The next day, when the eagle swooped down as usual, all the turtles vanished into the water except for the aged leader. 

When the eagle came nearer, the old turtle spoke up boldly. "O eagle, all my relatives have been devoured by you. I am the only one left now. But I'd like to save my life. Will you make a bet with me? If you lose, you must never eat turtles again."

Amused by the creature's audacity, the mighty eagle smirked. "What's the bet? If I win?"

"You may devour me first and then continue to eat turtles," the brave turtle offered. "Let's run a race. I'll swim in the sea while you fly in the sky. Let's begin the race a week from today."

"Agreed!" the arrogant eagle laughed. "A fool, this turtle. Of course, I'll win the bet."

As soon as the eagle was out of sight, the turtle called out to his companions. "Now come out of the water and do as I tell you. Post yourselves in the water in a straight line at equal distances along the coast. As the eagle flies over you, raise your head and show yourselves one by one."

The turtles accordingly took their hidden positions in the water, stretching far along the coastline. 

A week later, the grand race between the eagle and the turtle began. "Are you ready?" the eagle squawked. 

"Let's go," the old turtle replied, immediately diving under the waves. 

The eagle took off, flying with immense speed along the coastline. But to his absolute shock, every time he looked down, a turtle head popped out of the water far ahead of him! 

"...The turtle seemed to be always ahead of him by miles," the exhausted eagle gasped. "I cannot keep it up any longer. But the turtle seems fresh as ever."

Unable to comprehend the trick, the eagle pushed himself far beyond his physical limits. "No... I cannot. Let me fly away to a tree on shore before I fall down exhausted," he thought desperately. But his strength failed completely, and he crashed onto the beach, utterly defeated. 

The next day, humbled and broken, he dragged himself to see the old turtle. "You win," the eagle conceded, bowing his head. "I will never eat another turtle in my life."


# The Hare, The Partridge and The Tiger

On the peaceful banks of the Narmada river, there was a lush forest. However, the animals who lived there were anything but peaceful; they were always quarrelling over the smallest of things. 

"We quarrel because we have no king to settle our disputes," a wise old monkey observed during one particularly noisy argument. 

"Yes, we should have a king," the others agreed. 

"But who will be the king?" asked a deer.

Pandemonium immediately broke out. 

"I will be the king!" declared a boastful hare. 

"Not you. I will be the king," argued a partridge, fluttering its wings. "It should be me."

The bickering continued endlessly until an old and infirm tiger, who had recently migrated to that forest, was roused from his slumber. 

"Where is this noise from? Let me see," the tiger grumbled, limping towards the gathering. 

When the animals saw the fierce-looking (though secretly weak) tiger approaching, they were awestruck. 

"Why don't we make him our king?" whispered the hare. 

"A good idea," the deer agreed. 

So when the old tiger came to them, the animals respectfully bowed. "We need a king. Will you be our king and rule over us?" they pleaded. 

The old tiger, who had been struggling to catch prey due to his age, couldn't believe his good luck. *My days of starvation are over,* he thought gleefully.

"I do not mind being king," the tiger announced in a grand, benevolent tone. "But I dislike violence. So I will kill only if it is absolutely necessary, after making a careful study of each case."

The naive animals were delighted. "O King, we too dislike violence. But a just king has to kill the guilty ones," they assured him. "And most of the time I will find it necessary," the cunning tiger thought to himself with a smirk.

The very next day, the hare and the partridge, who were old rivals, had a bitter quarrel over a comfortable burrow. 

"This hole is mine!" the hare claimed. 

"No, it is mine! I found it first," the partridge squawked. 

"You found it all right. But it was my father who made it, so it's mine," the hare argued back. 

"But your father abandoned it. I moved in. So it's mine."

The hare was quiet for a moment, then an idea struck him. "Let's go to our King! Let him decide to whom it belongs."

"Fair enough. We'll go to him," the partridge agreed blindly. 

The two foolish creatures went to the old tiger, who was resting under a tree. They began explaining the situation loudly at the exact same time. 

"O King, we seek justice," they cried, babbling over each other. 

"Just a minute," the tiger raised a paw, feigning weakness. "I am old and can't hear properly. Come closer and explain the case to me."

As soon as the unsuspecting animals stepped right up to the tiger's face, the vicious predator sprang on them! With one swift, brutal bite, he devoured both the hare and the partridge. 

Watching from the trees above, the other animals finally realized their terrible mistake. "If only we had settled our own disputes instead of inviting a stranger to settle them for us," they mourned, knowing they had foolishly handed their lives over to a tyrant.


# The Serpent and the Rat

A snake-charmer once caught a deadly serpent in the forest and stuffed it into a sturdy cane basket. A short while later, he managed to trap a fat rat and carelessly tossed it into the very same basket, intending to use it as snake-food later. 

But when the hungry serpent eagerly approached the rat to eat it, the terrified rodent squeaked, "Please spare my life! I can free you."

The serpent paused, highly amused by the tiny creature's bold claim. "How can you succeed where I have failed?" the snake hissed mockingly. "Besides, I'm terribly hungry."

"Then what's the use of eating me?" the quick-thinking rat reasoned. "I can hardly satisfy your hunger. If you spare my life, I can lead you out to get all the food you need."

"How?" the serpent asked, intrigued.

"I can easily gnaw a hole in this box," the rat explained, pointing to his sharp teeth.

The serpent considered this. "He is right. Besides, I can eat him as well, later," the snake thought to itself. "All right," the serpent hissed aloud. "I will not eat you. Tell me how you can get me out."

"I shall chant a mantra sitting on your head," the clever rat instructed. "As soon as it is over, I shall call you. Till then you should close your eyes and stay perfectly still."

"I'll do as you say," the serpent agreed, settling down. "But please wait for me outside."

The serpent obediently closed his eyes. Taking full advantage of the snake's lowered guard, the rat jumped onto his head, quickly scrambled up to the top of the basket, and fiercely gnawed a hole in the cane roof. Without a backward glance, the rat squeezed through the hole and escaped into the jungle. 

A little later, the serpent opened his eyes. Finding the basket empty, he looked up and saw the hole. In vain did he look around for the rat. 

"The wretch has run away! But I'll find him soon," the angry snake hissed. He too slid out of the hole in the box and slithered away into the grass. 

"Ah! It's good to be free," the serpent rejoiced. "But I'm still hungry. Where is the rat?"

A few days later, while hunting, the snake finally found the small hole in the earth where the rat was now living safely. 

"Ah! There he is, the cheat. This time he won't escape," the snake thought, slithering up to the entrance. 

"O rat, why did you run away before I could thank you?" the snake called out smoothly. "Come out now. Aren't we friends?"

The rat, safely hidden deep inside his narrow burrow, laughed. "We are not. You are my enemy and will eat me up."

"But we made a deal!"

"Friendship is only between equals," the wise rat replied firmly. "I am not your equal, so we can never be friends. I pretended to be your friend only to save my life."

Realizing he had been thoroughly outsmarted, the snake sighed in defeat. "He is too wise for me. I'd better go and look for something to eat elsewhere," the serpent muttered, slithering away into the forest empty-handed.


# The Foolish Brahman

A learned but naive Brahman named Gargya lived in a quiet village near a dense forest. Deeply devoted to the divine, he once went into the forest to perform intense penance and worship Goddess Durga. 

Pleased with his unwavering devotion, the magnificent Goddess appeared before him in a blaze of light. "O pious Brahman, you deserve a boon," she said kindly. "Ask for one."

Overwhelmed, Gargya fell to his knees. "O Goddess, please grant me the *Sanjeevani*, the magical herb which is credited with the power to revive the dead."

The Goddess held out a handful of glowing green leaves. "Whenever you want to bring the dead back to life, all you need do is sprinkle the sap of these leaves on the corpse," she explained. "The body thus raised will be stronger and more vigorous than before. Besides, these leaves will neither wither nor change." 

With those instructions, the Goddess disappeared, leaving the miraculous leaves in Gargya's trembling hands.

"My importance will grow in the village. I may even be made the headman!" Gargya rejoiced as he walked back towards his home. "With this, I shall sweep away the sorrow of death from my village. There will not be a single unhappy house there."

Suddenly, his grand thoughts were interrupted. He began to have foolish doubts. "But supposing the Goddess was only teasing me? Supposing these are just ordinary leaves and..."

Just then, he saw a massive, dead tiger lying right in his path across the forest trail. 

"Ah! I can test these leaves on this dead animal," Gargya thought eagerly. 

Without stopping to think of the terrible consequences, the foolish, though learned, Brahman crushed the magical leaves in his palms and sprinkled the glowing green juice directly over the dead tiger. 

Instantly, the magic worked. The tiger stirred, took a deep breath, and sprang back to life, looking stronger and fiercer than ever before. 

"Hm... m... m! I'm hungry," the resurrected beast roared, its eyes fixing immediately on the terrified man standing before it.

"Oh, God! What have I done!" Gargya shrieked, panic finally setting in. "I'd better run for my life!"

But the tiger had seen him. With a terrifying roar, the powerful beast pounced on the fleeing Brahman and swiftly devoured him, proving tragically that even the greatest divine gifts can be fatal in the hands of a fool.


# The Female Parrot and the Hunter

There once lived a beautiful female parrot in a snug nest high up in the hollow of a massive ancient tree. In another dark hollow near the base of the same tree lived a highly venomous serpent. 

One day, the female parrot laid some eggs and sat tenderly on them, waiting for them to hatch. Unknown to her, a greedy hunter was carefully watching her from a neighboring tree. 

"Ah! She has moved from the nest. A good sign," the hunter muttered, noticing the parrot fly off to find food. Then, one day, the hunter finally heard the soft, unmistakable *cheep-cheep* of newly hatched fledgelings. 

"This is my lucky day," the hunter grinned wickedly. "The eggs have hatched. They'll fetch a tidy sum in the market."

As the parrot flew off to find food again, the hunter quickly climbed the tree. "Quiet, little ones. I'll soon bring you some food," the mother parrot had promised, before leaving.

But just as the hunter plunged his rough hand into the nest to grab the babies, the female parrot returned early! 

"Alas! He's out to snatch my young ones," she cried in horror, fluttering helplessly around the hunter's head. Weeping bitterly, she flew near his face. "Are there no birds elsewhere? My little ones have not opened their eyes as yet. Why should anybody want to harm them?"

"Why do you fear?" the hunter laughed callously. "I'm not going to kill them. I'm going to sell them as pets. Believe me, no harm will befall them."

"Not now, maybe," the mother pleaded desperately. "But as soon as you've made them plump and tasty, you'll eat them!"

Seeing his determination, the clever parrot offered a desperate bargain. "If money is all you want, leave my fledgelings alone, and I'll give you enough gold to keep you rich for the rest of your life."

The hunter paused, his greed piqued. "Gold? From where can you give me gold?"

"Recently, I saw a rich man, who was afraid of thieves, hide his wealth in this forest," she lied smoothly. "If you leave me and my fledgelings alone, I'll show you where he hid his gold."

The greedy hunter was overjoyed. "All mine! I'll take the gold, the fledgelings, and the parrot as well," he plotted secretly to himself. "But first I must win her confidence."

"Follow me," the parrot instructed, flying down towards the base of the massive tree. 

She led him straight to the dark hollow where the deadly serpent lived and pointed to the opening. "Put your hand into that hollow and take all the gold you want," she urged.

Blinded by overwhelming greed, the hunter hardly waited for her to finish. He eagerly thrust his hand deep into the dark hole. 

The next moment, a terrifying hiss echoed from the hollow. 

"EA... A... H! I'VE BEEN BITTEN BY A SERPENT!" the hunter screamed in agony, pulling back his hand to reveal two bleeding puncture wounds. 

Within minutes, the powerful venom spread through his body, and the wicked hunter fell dead at the base of the tree. 

Watching from the safety of a high branch, the mother parrot sighed softly. "It's a pity he had to die. But that was the only way I could save my young ones."


# The Foolish Crane

Long ago, there lived an old, exceptionally vain crane near a large lake, making his home high up on the branches of a tall coconut tree. Because the lake was almost dry, there were barely enough fish left for even one crane to survive on. Yet, the old crane was incredibly arrogant about his 'private' tree and his 'private' lake. 

One day, he saw a large, magnificent flock of cranes flying high in the sky past his lake.

"How I wish other cranes too would come and settle here and see how lucky I am," the old crane thought vanity getting the better of his common sense. 

He flapped his wings and called out loudly to the passing birds. "Stop! Stop. Please stop."

The leader of the flock, a wise and experienced bird, saw him. "That old bird is calling out to us. Let's fly down and see what he wants."

As the grand flock landed gracefully near the muddy water, the old crane puffed out his chest. "Ah! I'm so glad you heard me. Please be my guests. You can perch on the coconut tree and eat the fish in the lake."

The wise leader took one look at the nearly empty lake and the boastful old bird. *He is good but foolish,* the leader thought. *He wants to impress us, but at what cost! He will starve to death if we accept his invitation.*

"We are pleased by your affection for us," the wise leader declined politely on behalf of his flock. "But please permit us to go our way."

The old crane, deeply insulted by the rejection, dramatically threw himself to the ground. "If you don't accept my hospitality, I will give up my life!" he cried.

"Yes, our wise leader is right. We are so many of us," the other cranes murmured nervously. But seeing the old bird weeping, the leader reluctantly gave in. "What you have is sufficient only for you. Please permit us to go... but if you insist, we accept."

"All right. We shall be your guests," the flock agreed. 

"Come, then. Let us go to my tree," the old crane strutted proudly, his vanity completely satisfied. 

The flock settled on the coconut tree, and soon, they swooped down to the lake and began feasting ravenously on the remaining fish. 

The old crane, eager to show off even more, foolishly challenged them. "I bet I can eat more than you." 

"I bet you can't!" a young crane challenged back. 

With all their competitive betting and ravenous appetites, the massive flock finished every single fish in the tiny lake in a matter of hours. 

"Oh, dear! Not a single fish left," the old crane gasped as the sun began to set, the horrible reality of his boastfulness finally sinking in. "What shall I give them in the evening? What shall I eat when they are gone?"

That evening, the wise leader of the flock came to him. "There is nothing here for our evening meal. We are grateful to you for your hospitality, but we must leave now. Cannot let my flock starve."

As the massive flock took to the skies, the leader looked back with pity at the solitary figure by the empty lake. "He's too old to fly with us to better feeding grounds. If only he had listened to us! Poor fool! He will not survive for long."

A few days later, weak, starving, and completely alone by his ruined lake, the vain, foolish old crane breathed his last.
