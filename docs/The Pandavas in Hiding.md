# The Pandavas in Hiding - 1. The Pandavas in Hiding

After being cunningly defeated in a rigged game of dice by their wicked cousin Duryodhana, the Pandavas were forced into a thirteen-year exile. They spent twelve hard years living in the forest. However, the condition for their thirteenth year was the most difficult: they had to live completely incognito. If they were discovered by Duryodhana's spies, they would be condemned to another twelve years of exile. 

Duryodhana was determined to find them. His spies scoured the land but found nothing. Then, news arrived that Jimuta, the mightiest wrestler, and Keechaka, the dreaded commander, had both been killed in the kingdom of Virata. 

Suspecting the immense strength of the Pandava brother Bheema was behind these deaths, Duryodhana and his allies launched a massive, two-pronged attack on Virata's kingdom to steal his cattle and force the Pandavas out of hiding. 

King Virata, accompanied by his new servants— Kanka (a scholar), Valala (a cook), Arishtanemi, and Granthika— rode out to meet the first attacking army. 

Meanwhile, Duryodhana attacked the northern front. Virata's young, boastful son, Prince Uttara, was left to defend the capital. Prodded by his sister's maid, Sairandhri, the prince reluctantly took Brihannala, his sister's dance teacher, as his charioteer. 

"I am no match for the mighty Kaurava warriors!" Prince Uttara cried in terror when he saw Duryodhana's massive army. He jumped out of the chariot and tried to run away. 

Brihannala chased him down and forced him back. "Be a prince, Uttara! Don't act like a coward. You be my charioteer, and I will fight them!" 

Brihannala directed Uttara to a nearby Shami tree and ordered him to retrieve a bundle hidden in its branches. When Uttara opened it, he found magnificent, glowing weapons. 

"Whose weapons are these?" Uttara gasped. 
"They belong to the Pandavas," Brihannala revealed. "I am Arjuna, the Pandava! And your father's new servants— Kanka, Valala, and the rest— are my brothers Yudhishthira, Bheema, Nakula, and Sahadeva. The maid Sairandhri is our wife, Draupadi."

Ashamed of his cowardice but filled with new courage by the presence of his hero, Uttara gladly took the reins. 

Arjuna, wielding his legendary bow, drove into the battlefield. When Duryodhana saw him, he rejoiced, thinking he had discovered the Pandavas before the thirteenth year was over. 

"The fool has walked into my trap!" Duryodhana laughed. 
But the wise grand-uncle Bheeshma corrected him. "I am afraid not. The thirteenth year ended when the sun set yesterday. Otherwise, Arjuna would not have come out into the open."

Arjuna unleashed a terrifying shower of arrows, single-handedly routing Duryodhana's entire army and recovering all of Virata's stolen cattle. 

Meanwhile, King Virata and his army, secretly aided by Bheema and the other Pandavas, defeated the first attacking force. 

When Virata returned to his palace, he proudly praised his son Uttara for defeating the mighty Kauravas. Kanka (Yudhishthira) gently corrected him, "It must have been easy for the prince, with Brihannala as his charioteer."

Insulted that a mere scholar was giving credit to a dance teacher over his son, the furious King Virata threw a heavy dice piece at Kanka, drawing blood. Sairandhri quickly caught the blood in a golden bowl, warning that if the pious Kanka's blood touched the ground, a terrible drought would curse the kingdom. 

A few days later, King Virata entered his court to find his humble servants seated on the royal thrones. Enraged, he demanded an explanation. 

It was then that Prince Uttara revealed the truth. "The one you call Kanka is none other than King Yudhishthira! And the cook is Bheema, and the dance teacher is Arjuna!"

King Virata was stunned and deeply ashamed that he had treated the great Pandava kings as menial servants, and even struck Yudhishthira in anger. He profusely begged for their forgiveness, which Yudhishthira graciously granted. 

To cement their new friendship and alliance, King Virata offered his daughter, Princess Uttaraa, in marriage to Arjuna. 

"O King, as Uttaraa's teacher, I looked upon her as a daughter," Arjuna replied respectfully. "I can only accept her as one. Let her marry my brave son, Abhimanyu."

King Virata happily agreed. The Pandavas, having successfully completed their arduous thirteen-year exile, were now ready to march back and reclaim their rightful kingdom from the deceitful Kauravas.
