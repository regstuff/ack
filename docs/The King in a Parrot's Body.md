# The King in a Parrot's Body

Long, long ago, there was a noble king named Shuklapaksha who reigned over a city called Prithvibhushana. He was so pure of heart that he could never see bad in another. Unfortunately, his trusted minister, Krishnapaksha, was a wicked and ambitious man.

One morning, a merchant arrived at the court with a magnificent white horse. "What a fine horse!" the King exclaimed, admiring the beast. "It is the fastest steed in the world."

As the King examined the horse, an evil plan took shape in the minister's mind. *It may be fast, but it is still wild. That's it! I'll ask the king to try the horse! The steed needs to get used to him. The King will kill himself! Then I will be King!*

"Why don't you try it right away, Your Majesty?" the minister suggested smoothly. 

"An excellent idea!" the King agreed. 

Before the merchant could utter a word of warning, the King mounted the horse and galloped out of the palace. A few courtiers immediately mounted their steeds and chased after him, but the King and his wild horse were soon far, far away in a dense forest. 

Towards evening, the exhausted horse finally slowed down. "Thank God!" the King thought. "That was quite a ride." But the next moment, the horse's knees buckled, and it fell dead from exhaustion.

Leaving the dead horse, the tired, thirsty, and hungry King walked wearily on until he found a peaceful pond and some fruit. Suddenly, he spotted an old hermit. 

"O holy one, I am so happy to see you," the King greeted him respectfully, introducing himself.

The hermit was impressed by the King's regal bearing. "Why don't you come and spend the night at my hut?" he offered.

When they reached the hermitage, the King was stunned to see a breathtakingly beautiful maiden. *The very goddess of good fortune! Why does she lead the life of a recluse?* he wondered. *I must make her my queen!*

"O sage, who is that maiden?" the King asked.

"She is Nivritti, the daughter of the King of the Vidyadharas (demigods)," the sage explained. "One day, a rogue Vidyadhara flew into her father's palace and carried Nivritti away. Hearing her screams, her father rushed out and galloped after them. The rogue saw the King pursuing him, dropped the girl in front of my hermitage, and fled."

The sage continued, "A few minutes later, her father arrived. 'I'm going after the rascal and will not be back till he is caught and punished. Please look after my daughter till I return,' he told me. But it's been many years, and it seems he will not return."

"O sage, I would gladly marry her," King Shuklapaksha offered. 

"But if I am delayed, her father had instructed me to marry her to a worthy man," the sage added. "However, he must possess the power of entering a dead body."

"Well, you are certainly a deserving person," the sage considered. "If only you had the power to enter a dead body! All right. You may marry her. But till you are in possession of the power, do not take her into the inner apartments of your palace."

So the King married Nivritti. Just then, the courtiers who had been searching for him finally found the hut. As the party prepared to set off, the sage reminded him, "Remember the condition!"

"I won't forget. I will do all I can to acquire the power," the King promised.

On the way back, the King told Nivritti the whole story of the wild horse. Nivritti frowned. "The minister seems to be an evil man. If he cared for the King's safety, he would not have asked him to mount a steed that had not first been tamed," she thought, but kept her suspicions to herself for the moment. 

Meanwhile, at the palace, the wicked minister was plotting. *If he is alive, my plans have failed!* he thought nervously. When the courtiers returned with the King and his new bride, the minister pretended to be overjoyed. 

But when the minister saw Nivritti, his wicked heart skipped a beat. *She's the most beautiful woman I've ever seen,* he lusted. 

Seeing the minister's gaze, Nivritti's suspicions were confirmed. *So this is him! I'm now sure that he is an evil man. I must warn my husband about him, soon,* she decided. 

Later, brooding in his palace, the King wondered how to acquire the magical power. "I'll have to confide in Krishnapakshika," he decided, trusting his minister completely. 

When the King told him about the condition, the minister realized his golden opportunity. *That means Nivritti will belong to the man who acquires this power... But in whatever way you get it, I, too, shall get it, and make Nivritti my wife!*

"I have an idea," the minister suggested deceitfully. "Let us build a free rest-house for holy men. Among those who visit it, we might find one who could help us."

The rest-house was built, and six months passed with no luck. Then, one day, the minister met a traveling carpet-merchant who claimed to know a yogi with the power to enter dead bodies. 

"About ninety-six miles from my city, there are two palm trees on either side of a path," the merchant explained. "On one is perched a crow and on the other a swan. As you go deep into the forest, you will see a mountain. On the peak of that mountain sits Yogi Sadananda. He has the power you are looking for."

The minister excitedly told the King. "I must meet this yogi," the King declared. 

"The minister and I will set out immediately to find the yogi," the King told Nivritti later.

"I do not trust your minister," Nivritti warned him earnestly. "He is crafty, malicious, and ungrateful. Your very life will be in danger if you take him with you."

Taking her advice, the King told the minister, "I'll go alone. I want you to look after the kingdom while I am away."

But the cunning minister insisted. "How can I let you go alone, Your Majesty? The courtiers can look after the kingdom." The naive King, touched by his apparent concern, agreed to take him along. 

The two traveled far and finally found the two palm trees and the mountain. At the peak, they found Yogi Sadananda deep in meditation. For days, the yogi ignored them, but the King and the minister waited patiently. 

Finally, the yogi opened his eyes and looked at the King, ignoring the minister completely. "I am pleased with you. Ask for a boon."

"O holy one, give me the power to enter a dead body," the King requested. 

The yogi frowned at the minister. "I am willing to give it to you but not to that man. He is not fit to possess this power!"

The King, blindly loyal to his wicked friend, pleaded, "Holy sir, for my sake let my minister, too, be given the power. I cannot bear to see him so unhappy."

"If I give the power to him, your life will be in danger. He is not to be trusted," the wise yogi warned. 

"Holy sir, I trust him. Please let him have the power too," the King insisted naively. 

Reluctantly, the yogi imparted the unique, magical power to them both. 

On their return journey, they stopped to rest by a pool in the forest. Suddenly, they spotted a dead elephant behind a shrub. 

"What's this? A dead elephant! Here is an opportunity to test my newly acquired power!" the King exclaimed. "I'm going to enter the body of this elephant. Please take care of my body till I come back."

The King used the mantra, his lifeless body slumped to the ground, and the dead elephant suddenly sprang to life and wandered into the forest to explore. 

The minister watched with evil glee. "I can't believe it! I never dreamt it would be so easy to get rid of him!" The next moment, he used the mantra himself, leaving his own body and entering the King's lifeless form. 

"Now I'd better destroy my discarded body once and for all," the minister thought. He quickly built a pyre and burned his original body to ashes. Then, in the King's body, he mounted the horse and rode back towards Prithvibhushana. 

Meanwhile, the King, still in the elephant's body, returned to the pool and found both his own body and his minister missing. Only a pile of ashes remained. 

"I do not trust your minister. He is crafty..." Nivritti's warning echoed in his mind. 

"They were right!" the King realized in horror. "He wants to rob me of my kingdom and my queen. I'd better go home quickly before he succeeds in his plans."

As soon as the minister (in the King's body) reached the palace, he issued a swift order. "There is a rogue elephant about. If he enters the city, kill him immediately." 

He then rushed to Nivritti's palace. "Ah! Nivritti, I'm back," he smiled lecherously. 

Nivritti, sensing something was terribly wrong, played along. "I am happy to see you, my lord! Did you get the power? But where is my noble minister?"

"Alas! He lost his life while saving mine," the imposter lied smoothly. 

*How can I believe him? Suppose he is the wicked minister in my lord's body...* Nivritti thought shrewdly. Outwardly, she smiled. "But when you left, I took a vow, and it will take me a few more days to fulfil it. During this time, you must not meet me."

Frustrated but unable to refuse, the imposter agreed to wait. 

Meanwhile, the King (in the elephant's body) approached the city. As ordered, the royal guards armed themselves and began to chase the "rogue" elephant. 

"It would be foolish to try my strength against so many armed men. But what shall I do?" the King panicked, running for his life. 

Suddenly, he saw a dead deer. "Ah! A dead deer! If I enter its body, I can run faster!"

Using the power, he left the elephant's body, which instantly fell dead, and sprang to life as the deer, sprinting away. 

"Quick! Follow that deer and shoot it down!" the guards yelled. A volley of arrows struck the deer, fatally wounding it. "A-a-ah! My men are good shots indeed! Painfully so!" the King groaned as the deer collapsed. 

Just as the guards caught up, the King spotted a dead parrot nearby. With his last breath as the deer, he entered the parrot's body. The guards, finding only the dead deer, were satisfied and returned to the palace to claim their reward. 

"Thank God! They did not notice me!" the parrot (King) sighed in relief. He flew to the mango tree outside Nivritti's window, hoping to warn her. But as he landed, a hunter's net suddenly trapped him!

*I must do some quick thinking,* the King realized. As the hunter approached, the parrot spoke. "If you do as I tell you, you can make a lot of money."

"This bird talks! I must be dreaming!" the hunter gasped. 

"This is no dream. You can sell me for a lot of money. I could get at least a lakh of rupees," the parrot assured him. 

The hunter took the talking bird to the market. There, the parrot spotted Nivritti's loyal maid. "How is your mistress? Is she all right?" the parrot called out. 

Stunned, the maid ran back and told Nivritti everything. "I must buy that bird!" Nivritti realized instantly. "Go to the King and ask him for the money."

When the maid asked the "King" for a lakh of rupees for a parrot, the imposter scoffed. "One lakh for a mere parrot! I don't have money to throw away!"

When the maid reported this, Nivritti's suspicions were confirmed. "The King, my husband, would never deny me anything. That man is not my husband." She gave the maid her own money and ordered her to buy the bird immediately. 

Meanwhile, the minister (in the King's body) grew suspicious. "Why did the Queen want to buy such a costly bird? There must be something to it. I'll go and question her."

He arrived at the Queen's palace just as the maid brought the parrot in. A nameless fear gripped the imposter's heart as he saw the bird. "What are you doing?" he demanded, seizing the parrot from the startled maid. 

"He'll kill me. I'd better escape into the body of that dead bee," the King (in the parrot's body) thought frantically, spotting a dead insect on the floor. 

The very next minute, the cruel minister wrung the neck of the parrot, killing it instantly. But the King had successfully transferred his soul into the bee. 

"You had no right to kill my bird!" Nivritti wept bitterly. "I bought it with my own money. Restore it to life or I'll kill myself!"

Panicking that he might lose his prize, the minister tried to appease her. "All right! Give me a few minutes and I'll bring your parrot back to life."

He went into the next room alone. Leaving the King's body slumped on the bed, the minister's soul entered the dead parrot. The parrot sprang to life and flew back into the Queen's room to show off. 

But the moment the King's true body was left empty, the King (as the bee) left the insect and finally entered his own body once again! 

King Shuklapaksha strode into the Queen's room, whole and healthy. "Nivritti!"

"My Lord!" she cried in joy, rushing to him. 

The parrot (the minister) realized his fatal error. "I've been duped!" he squawked in despair. 

The King told Nivritti the whole harrowing story of his journey and his minister's betrayal. 

"Give me that parrot," Nivritti demanded angrily. "I'll wring its neck and get rid of the evil creature for ever."

But the pure-hearted King smiled gently. "Why do you want to burden yourself with the sin of killing him? Leave him to his own fate. Better still, we'll put him in an iron cage so that he will not be able to do any more mischief with this precious power."

And so, the wicked minister spent the rest of his days trapped as a parrot in an iron cage, while King Shuklapaksha and Queen Nivritti lived happily and ruled Prithvibhushana with wisdom and peace.
