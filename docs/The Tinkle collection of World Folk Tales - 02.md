# The Clever Prince

Philip, the King of Macedonia, once bought a beautiful horse for a very large sum of money. But when one of his men tried to ride it, the wild horse reared up and threw him off. Many other skilled men tried to ride the horse, but it threw them all off to the ground. 

"The trader from whom I bought it told me it was a gentle horse," the King frowned. "Why is it behaving like this?"

"I think I know, Father," a young boy said, stepping forward. "Let me ride him."

"Stop him, Sire! He'll get hurt," a courtier warned. 

"No, let him try," the King said. "All right, go ahead!"

The boy walked up to the horse, spoke gently to it, and turned the horse around to face the sun. He calmly climbed onto the saddle. Everyone expected the horse to rear up and throw off its rider, but to their amazement, it stood still. 

"He's riding it!" the courtiers gasped. 

"That's my boy!" King Philip cheered. 

"It was his own shadow that was frightening him, Father," the boy explained as he rode back. "When I turned him towards the sun, he could not see his shadow. That is why he became so gentle."

"I am proud of you, my son," the King beamed. "You deserve to be King of a larger kingdom than Macedonia."

The boy was none other than Alexander, later known as Alexander the Great. He took his father's words seriously, and by the time he died, he was master of half the world. As for the horse, he named him Bucephalus and rode him in all his legendary campaigns.


# The Story of Silk

Many, many years ago, a young Chinese Princess saw some strange worms eating the leaves of a mulberry bush in her garden. 

"See what I have found here!" she called out to her friends. 

"How funny they look!" her friends giggled. 

Every day, the girls would go to watch the worms. "They are becoming fatter day by day," the Princess noticed. 

"That's because they eat so much," a friend replied. 

"I wonder how much fatter they will become tomorrow," the Princess thought. But the next few days were wet and cold, and the children could not go out. When they finally did return to the garden, the Princess gasped. "Oh! What's the matter?"

"Alas! The poor worms have eaten too much and they are dying," her friend said sadly. 

The girls watched in wonder as the worms wove white, oval cocoons round themselves. 

"Those must be their coffins. See, some of them have covered themselves completely. They must be dead already. We will have to bury them," the Princess said softly. 

"Please bring us some tea and rice cakes," she instructed her maid. When the ancient Chinese buried their dead, they used to keep food and drink on the graves. 

Soon the tiny graves were ready. The Princess brought the cocoons... and began to drop them one by one into the grave. As she was doing this... she accidentally dropped one of the cocoons into her cup of hot tea! 

"Oh!" she cried. "What have I done! Its covering has become loose."

The soft-hearted Princess burst into tears and decided to take the ruined cocoon to her mother. Her mother, Empress Si-Ling, was a very clever woman. 

"Don't weep. Leave this worm with me and go back to your cousins," she comforted her daughter. 

"A worm which makes a covering for itself. How strange!" Empress Si-Ling marvelled. She dropped the cocoon into a bowl of hot water... then took it out again... and began to unwind it using a pair of chopsticks. 

The strand broke several times. But she joined it up each time and kept on. After some time, she had a long, glistening strand of thread in her hand. 

"I must see those worms for myself," she decided. 

When her daughter took her to the mulberry tree and showed her the cocoons, she was overjoyed. "You have found something very wonderful, my daughter," she smiled. "You have found the thread from which we shall weave the most beautiful cloth ever seen."

Lady Si-Ling taught her servants how to spin the strands into thread and to weave the thread into the shiny cloth which we now call silk. Ever since, Lady Si-Ling has been called the first maker of silk, though the name of the little Princess who discovered the cocoons has not come down to us.


# David and Goliath

Hundreds of years ago, Israel was attacked by the Philistines. It was a long war. The Philistine camp stood on one hill and the Israelite on another. 

One day, there was a great uproar in the Philistine camp. The Israelites came running out to see what the matter was. "What creature is that?" they gasped. 

"Look at me! You white-faced, lily-livered cowards! Don't run away," a massive giant roared. "Send one, just one puny Israelite to fight with me..."

It was Goliath of Gath, the champion of the Philistines. "If your champion wins, we will be your servants. But... if he is killed, you will be our servants forever!"

"He could crush us like nuts with his bare hands," the terrified Israelites whispered. "Who could kill him? Only a fool would try."

Every day Goliath came and shouted out the same challenge. "Puny men! Weak-kneed creatures! So, not one of you dares fight me? Cowards!" Many days passed, but no one dared to accept Goliath's challenge. 

One day, a young shepherd named David came to the Israelite camp looking for his brothers. 

"Come and fight, you weaklings!" Goliath's voice boomed across the valley. 

"Who is that?" David asked. 

"It's Goliath... the champion of the Philistines. He wants an Israelite to fight him in single combat."

"Then, why doesn't someone take up the challenge?" David asked. 

"Who would dare to face him?" a soldier laughed. 

"I would!" David declared. 

"You are a fool, David. You'll never have any sense in your head!" his older brother scolded. "Go and look after your sheep, David. Leave the fighting to us."

But instead of going back home, David went to a stream nearby... chose five smooth stones for his sling... and walked boldly towards the giant. "Goliath! I will fight with you!"

When Goliath saw him coming, he roared with laughter. "So they have sent a boy... HOHOHO! Run off, now, before I decide to crush you!"

"Goliath! You can't refuse to fight! It was you who demanded single combat... and I have taken up your challenge!" David shouted back. 

The giant gave a great roar of anger and advanced towards David. "You...! You dare to challenge me? I'll..."

But he did not finish his sentence. Quick as a flash, David fixed a stone in his sling and swung it. The stone came flying through the air... and hit Goliath right on his forehead with a loud *THWACK!*

Goliath swayed like a mountain in an earthquake... and fell heavily to the dirt. 

"Goliath has fallen!" the Israelites cheered. "Look, the Philistines are running away! Quick, after them!"

The Israelites pursued the fleeing Philistines and won a great victory. When David grew up, he became a legendary King of the Israelites.


# The Hunchback of Kashgar

One night, Abdulla the tailor brought a cheerful hunchback home to dinner. "Help yourself, my friend," he offered generously. 

"This fish tastes good!" the hunchback smiled. But as he was merrily eating, a sharp bone stuck in his throat. 

"ARR-ULP!" he choked, clutching his neck. "UGH-UGH-UGH..."

"My God!" Abdulla's wife gasped as the hunchback collapsed onto the floor. "He's dead!"

"Shall we call the doctor?" Abdulla panicked. 

"What's the use? He's stone dead!" his wife reasoned. 

"The police then?"

"That would be worse! The police will suspect us of killing him," she cried. "I've an idea. We'll take the body to the doctor's house and..."

Soon, they dragged the body to the doctor's clinic and knocked loudly on the door. "Please open the door. It's an emergency!" Abdulla shouted. 

"A patient!" the sleepy doctor muttered, getting out of bed. "Somebody bring me a lamp."

Not waiting for the lamp, he opened the door and stepped out in the dark. He tripped over the hunchback's body, sending them both tumbling down the stairs! 

"Bring the lamp quickly, you foolish woman!" he yelled at his wife. But when the light revealed the motionless body, the doctor gasped, "What have I done? He's dead! I've killed my patient!"

"What are we to do now?" his wife wailed. "People will say I murdered him. I will be hanged!"

"I have it! Help me carry him up to the terrace," she suggested. 

Afraid of being accused of murder, the doctor carried the body out... propped it against a shop in a dark lane, and ran away. "He may be a thief, but I had no right to kill him!" he thought guiltily. 

A little later, a merchant was passing by. The propped-up body slumped over onto him. 

"Wha...!" the merchant cried. "It's a man! The fellow is trying to murder me!" 

Thinking he was being attacked, the merchant threw a heavy punch, knocking the hunchback to the ground. "You thug!"

Hearing the thud, two policemen came running. "What's going on here, sir?" 

"Hey you! Get up!" a policeman nudged the body. "He's dead!"

"Oh, no!" the merchant gasped. 

The man was taken to the Kazi (judge). "Well, what have you to say about this?" the Kazi demanded. 

"Mercy... show me mercy... my lord..." the merchant pleaded. 

"Sir, the hunchback is the Sultan's favourite!" a guard whispered to the Kazi. 

"You wicked man! Anyone who commits murder deserves no mercy. You shall be hanged," the Kazi declared. 

When the local grocer heard of the upcoming execution, he felt uneasy. "You mean they're going to hang him? This must not happen," he muttered. 

When the merchant was about to be hanged, the grocer rushed to the platform. "Wait! Let him go! I am the real killer! I placed the body against the wall of the shop!"

The Kazi frowned. "Release him! Hang this honest killer in his place!"

But just when the grocer was about to be hanged, the doctor came rushing to the platform. "Don't hang him! Hang me! I tripped and killed him!"

"Well, this beats me!" the Kazi sighed. 

Before the doctor could be hanged, Abdulla the tailor ran up. "Wait! Don't hang him... he's innocent! It... it was I who killed the man. A fish bone got stuck in his throat at my house!"

The news reached the ears of the Sultan. "You mean, it's our friend the hunchback? And all of them say they've killed him?" he asked in disbelief. "Tell the Kazi to come here with the body and the four funny men!"

When they were brought before the Sultan, a royal barber stepped forward. "He's the talkative barber of Kashgar, Your Majesty. Don't pay heed to him. He is a bore," a courtier advised. 

But the barber had already squatted on the floor and opened his bag. "In this small armoury of mine, is a clamp... and a tiny pair of pincers."

"With one hand I open this fellow's jaw and keep this clamp firmly fixed in it," the barber explained as he worked. "With the other hand I put the pincers in his throat and... LO! The offending bone comes out!"

The barber pulled out the sharp fish bone. 

"Look! He's opening his eyes!" Abdulla gasped. 

"Wh-where am I?" the hunchback coughed. 

"Right here in my court, man! Not in a grave where you'd be lying, were it not for this clever barber!" the Sultan laughed. "Listen, my courtiers, this man may be a bore when he talks, but he's a genius when he works. As for you, my good citizens! In future, make sure you are guilty before you say you are. Go now. You are free."


# Androcles and the Lion

In ancient Rome, a slave named Androcles ran away from his cruel master. He fled into a dense jungle and took shelter in a dark cave. 

Suddenly, a terrifying roar echoed through the cavern. "ROOOARR!" A huge lion stepped inside. 

"This must be his cave! I'm trapped!" Androcles panicked. 

But the lion didn't attack. It limped forward and held out its huge paw. "He's holding out his paw... oh, there's a thorn stuck in it!" Androcles realized. 

"My poor friend... how it must pain you! Let me take it out!" Gently, Androcles pulled the sharp thorn free. The lion licked his hand and quietly went away, returning later with fresh food for his new friend. 

Androcles stayed with the lion in his cave for many peaceful days. But one day, Roman soldiers found him. "Don't try to run away," they ordered, capturing him. 

The soldiers took Androcles back to his cruel master, who sentenced him to death in the Arena. "Throw him to the lions!"

Some days later, poor Androcles was pushed into the dusty stadium. "Look at the crowds that have come to see you," a guard sneered. 

"Look, they're opening the lion's cage!" the crowd cheered as a massive, starving lion rushed out. 

The lion roared and charged at Androcles. But halfway there, it stopped and began to rub its head against the slave's legs like a gentle kitten. "It is you!" Androcles cried, hugging the beast. 

"This is amazing! The ferocious lion has become as gentle as a lamb," the Emperor marvelled. 

The crowd was deeply moved by the scene. "Free them! Yes, let them go!" they chanted. 

Androcles and the lion were set free, and it became a common, heartwarming sight thereafter to see the huge beast following Androcles around the streets of Rome like a small puppy.

According to a Chinese tale, cocks in ancient days used to have magnificent horns on their heads. 

One day, a cock was walking around in a yard when suddenly a mighty dragon flew down from the clouds. 

"Greetings, honourable cock," the dragon rumbled. 

"Greetings, noble dragon," the cock replied. 

"I am on my way to the heavenly regions to pay my respects to the Heavenly Ones," the dragon explained. "But... everybody who goes to the heavenly regions must wear horns, and I have none."

"I can see that," the cock nodded. 

"Honourable cock, will you please lend me yours?" the dragon asked politely. 

"If I do, you may not return them to me," the cock hesitated. 

"I always return what I borrow. If you don't believe me, ask the worm," the dragon said, pointing to a small earthworm wriggling nearby. 

When the cock asked the worm, the worm nodded. "Oh yes, he does."

"If I had horns, I would have given them to him," the worm added. 

Convinced, the trusting cock took off his beautiful horns and gave them to the dragon. "Thank you! Thank you, honourable cock!" the dragon smiled. "I'll return them to you in the evening, on my way home."

But the dragon did not come back that evening. Nor did he show up the next morning. The cock became deeply worried. 

At noon, he went back to the worm. "Your friend hasn't come back as yet, sir."

"Don't worry. He will come," the worm assured him. "If not today, well, certainly tomorrow."

The cock waited for several long days, looking up at the sky, but the dragon did not come. Finally, he gave up hope. 

"I'll never see my beautiful horns again," the cock sighed sadly. "I was a fool to have trusted that dragon... and that worm."

His sadness quickly turned to anger. "The dragon may never come back. But that worm... him, at least, I can punish."

The angry cock hunted down the lying worm, caught it in his beak, and gulped it right down! 

To his surprise, he found it incredibly tasty. "It's delicious!" he blinked. "Let me see if I can find another one."

From then on, the cock forgot all about his lost horns and spent all his time happily scratching the dirt looking for worms. And that is why cocks, to this day, are so fond of eating worms.


# Adventures of Kasperle

Walking through a dark German forest, a cheerful fellow named Kasperle stopped to read a sign nailed to a tree: *WANTED - NIGHT WATCHMAN FOR THE KING.*

He found the King sitting sadly on a log nearby. "Why are you here, Your Majesty?" Kasperle asked. 

"Yesterday a wicked witch cast a spell over my daughter at this very spot," the King wept. "My daughter disappeared, but I know she is here somewhere. That's why I need someone to keep a watch here."

"I will gladly stand guard for you, Your Majesty," Kasperle saluted. 

"Then please start your duties now. I will return with my soldiers to make a search of the forest," the King said, leaving Kasperle alone. 

Kasperle began to search the area. "Where could the princess be? Has she been turned into a tree? Or a bush? Or this stick?"

He noticed a stunningly beautiful red flower blooming alone in the grass. "What a beautiful flower! I must smell it." 

As he bent down, a huge, snapping jaw appeared. "Oops! Where did you come from?" Kasperle jumped back. 

A hungry crocodile crawled out of the bushes. "What do you want?" Kasperle asked. 

"I'm going to eat you!" the crocodile hissed. 

"Oh no, you won't!" Kasperle grabbed a sturdy, Y-shaped stick from the ground and jammed it propped open inside the crocodile's snapping jaws. "Now run off!" he laughed as the helpless crocodile scurried away. 

"The crocodile appeared when I was near this flower," Kasperle muttered. "I must take a closer look at it."

"Don't touch that flower, or I'll kill you!" a chilling voice whispered behind him. A terrifying ghost hovered in the air, its skeletal hands reaching for him. 

"There's certainly something fishy about that flower," Kasperle thought. "I didn't mean any harm, sir," he said aloud. "But the flower has such a lovely perfume. Take a sniff."

As the ghost leaned in, Kasperle threw a handful of fine powder right into its face. "Good thing I had that sneezing powder with me!"

"AAH... AH... TISHOO! TISHOO!" the ghost sneezed violently, blowing itself away like a cloud of dust. 

"Leave that flower alone!" another voice boomed. A red-horned devil stomped into the clearing. "Mr. Devil, I'm guarding this flower for my friend the ghost. He caught a cold and is sneezing there," Kasperle lied smoothly. 

"Oh, that's all right then! Guard the flower well till the witch comes," the devil nodded, walking away into the woods. 

"Witch? Now I'm sure the princess has been turned into that flower," Kasperle grinned. 

"Oh, oh, here comes the witch! I'd better hide," Kasperle muttered, ducking behind a thick tree. 

The ugly witch flew down on her broomstick and cackled at the red flower. "Hee hee hee! How are you my little princess? I must see your lovely face again. Abracadabra, power and might, flower turn into princess bright!"

In a flash of light, the flower transformed back into the beautiful Princess! 

"Hee hee hee! My lovely prisoner! You will be my dinner tonight," the witch sneered, grabbing her. 

"Will nobody help me?" the Princess cried. 

"I will help you, Princess!" Kasperle shouted, jumping out from behind the tree. He raised his heavy stick and whacked the wicked witch right on her head! 

"Abracadabra, might and power, witch turn into ugly flower!" Kasperle shouted an improvised spell. And instantly, the dazed witch turned into a hideous, foul-smelling weed. 

"Oh thank you, thank you!" the Princess cheered. 

Soon, the King returned with his guards. "Daughter!" he cried in joy. 

"Thank God you're safe!" the King hugged her tightly. The King rewarded the brave and clever Kasperle handsomely for rescuing his daughter from the wicked witch's spell.


# The Six Wives Who Ate Onions

One day, long ago, six American Indian women were gathering fruits in the forest. 

Suddenly, one of them pulled a bulbous root from the ground. "Oh, look what I've found! It's an onion!" She took a bite. "CRUNCH! It's delicious!"

"I'll have another one," she said. 

"I too," the others agreed. "Mmm... pungent, but tasty! SCRUNCH!"

When they returned home in the evening, each of them was carrying a large bunch of wild onions. Later, their husbands came home, weary from hunting the whole day long. 

"Ah, it's good to be home," a husband smiled, sitting by the fire. 

"I have a surprise for you," his wife beamed, holding up the pungent roots. 

"Look, what we..." she began, exhaling a deep breath. 

"ARRGHH!" her husband choked, covering his nose. "Your breath smells awful! I can't breathe in here! OUT! GET OUT!"

He shoved her out of the tent. "SLAM!"

All the other wives were driven out too by their disgusted husbands, and they had to spend the cold night out in the open, huddled together under the stars. 

"Have you been thrown out too?" one shivered. 

"Yes, she has." 

"I too."

But the next day too, instead of feeling sorry, they stubbornly went back to the forest... and ate several onions each! "Our husbands don't know what they're missing!" they laughed. "If only they would taste one!"

And again that night—"Oh, that odour! OUT! GET OUT!"

For a whole week, the wives would go to the forest and eat onions in the morning. And at night, their furious husbands would push them out of their houses. 

Finally, the women had enough. "Let's leave our husbands and go away!" the prettiest wife suggested. "They're horrid!"

"Yes, let's do that!" the others agreed. 

The next morning, the wives went up a high mountain carrying magic ropes made of eagle feathers. They climbed up to the highest rock and then the prettiest wife whispered a magic word... and threw her eagle feather rope high into the sky. 

Up went the rope... higher and higher... and then it dropped over a soft, fluffy cloud. Both ends of the rope dangled down to the women below. The other women tied their own ropes in a circle to the rope from the cloud. 

Holding hands, they began to chant, "Help us, magic ropes... help us."

The cloud sailed slowly across the sky... carrying the six women away with it into the heavens! 

As they passed over their village, their husbands saw them floating away, but they couldn't do anything about it. So they stubbornly scolded their wives' parents, "You should have kept them at home!"

But soon, the six husbands became very lonesome without their wives. 

"Let's go after them and persuade them to return," one husband finally admitted. 

"That's just what I was about to say too," the others nodded. 

So the husbands went up to the top of the mountain with their own eagle feather ropes. They chanted magic spells and threw their ropes into the clouds. And soon, they too were floating in the sky, chasing after their wives. 

"There they are!" a husband pointed as they spotted the women's cloud ahead. 

Their wives looked down and saw their husbands coming after them. "Should we let them catch up?" one asked. 

"They said they didn't want us. They pushed us out of our homes at night!" the prettiest wife frowned. "We don't want them!"

So the women chanted magic words which made their ropes go much faster. Their husbands too urged their ropes on... "Stay where you are! Wait for us!" they yelled. 

But they couldn't catch up with their wives. The chase went on and on and on. And finally, they all turned into glowing stars in the night sky!

American Indians know the higher group of six stars, 'The Pleiades', as 'Young Women'. And the lower six stars or 'Taurus' are called 'Young Men'. Every clear night, the two groups can be seen moving slowly across the sky, forever chasing, but never catching up.


# Atalanta's Race

Atalanta was the beautiful daughter of the King of Greece. She was incredibly fast, and she had agreed to marry only the man who could beat her in a foot race. 

Many noble men tried, but they all failed miserably. "She is as swift as a deer," they gasped, watching her speed past them on the track. 

One day, a handsome young man named Milanion came to the palace. 

"I wish to run against the princess," he told the King. 

"Go back, son," the King sighed wearily. "You cannot hope to beat Atalanta. She is the fastest runner in the world."

"I am a good runner myself," Milanion smiled confidently. 

"Do you know what happens to the loser? He is put to death," the King warned gravely. 

"I know. I am not afraid," Milanion replied. 

"Then come back tomorrow for the race," the King nodded. 

The next day at the starting point, the crowd murmured among themselves. "He's so handsome and he seems so sure of himself," a woman whispered. "But the poor fellow doesn't have a chance."

The race began. Instantly, Atalanta sped away, taking a clear lead. 

"She can certainly run fast," Milanion thought, trailing behind. "It's time to throw the first one."

He reached into his tunic and tossed a glittering, solid golden apple onto the track ahead of Atalanta. 

"A golden apple!" Atalanta gasped, slowing down. She couldn't resist the beautiful object and veered off the path to grab it. 

"She is chasing the apple... just as I expected," Milanion smiled as he sprinted past her. 

"And now I am ahead of her," he thought triumphantly. 

But it did not take the incredibly fast princess long to overtake the youth again. As she ran past him, Milanion threw another golden apple. She went after that one too, and he took the lead once more. 

But a little later, she had overtaken him again. 

"The winning post is in sight. I'll throw my last apple," Milanion thought, tossing his final golden treasure. 

"Another one," Atalanta smiled, stopping to snatch it up. 

But by the time Atalanta picked up the third apple and ran back to the track, Milanion had already crossed the finish line! 

"He has won!" the crowd roared in amazement. 

True to her solemn word, Atalanta married the clever youth, and they lived happily ever after.


# The Three Good-Luck Charms

It was a lovely day in Japan. A little boy-monk looked out of the monastery window at the beautiful snowy landscape. "Oh, how I long to go outside and gather chestnuts!" he sighed. 

Saying his prayers and finishing his morning chores quickly, he ran to the old Head Monk. "May I go and pick chestnuts today?" he asked eagerly. 

"No, you mustn't go out. What if the mountain witch catches you?" the old monk warned. 

"Oh, please, please let me go," the boy begged. 

"All right, go if you must..." the old monk relented. "...But take these three pieces of paper. They are good-luck charms. If you run into any trouble, ask them to help you."

The boy-monk roamed the mountain slopes happily, wandering farther and farther away from the safety of the monastery. "What a lot of chestnuts I've picked!" he smiled, his basket full. 

Suddenly, an old woman appeared from behind a tree. "Little boy, you are from the temple monastery, aren't you?" she smiled crookedly. "Come along with me. There are much better chestnuts growing in my garden."

"That sounds nice," the innocent boy thought. 

"I'll roast the big, juicy ones and boil the tiny ones for you," the old woman promised. 

She took the boy-monk to her secluded wooden house and gave him the promised treat. "They're delicious! But I can't eat any more..." he patted his full stomach. 

"You must be sleepy. Sleep here tonight. You can go back to the monastery tomorrow," she insisted. 

The boy-monk got under a thick quilt and was soon fast asleep. But at midnight, he suddenly awoke. *Pitter, patter...* "It's raining," he muttered, listening to the drops on the roof. 

The raindrops seemed to be whispering something... *Pitter patter, look and see, the old woman seems a witch to me...*

Startled, the boy-monk peeped out from under the quilt. "The old woman... there she is! What is she doing?"

The old woman was staring into a mirror, admiring herself. But she was no longer an old woman. She had transformed into a hideous monster! "How beautiful I am! How long and sharp my horns have grown!" she cackled. 

"It's the mountain witch! She'll eat me up! I must escape in the morning," the boy-monk panicked, laying as still as a stone. 

In the morning, the boy crept out of bed. "Old woman, I want to have a wash," he asked. 

"It's too cold for a wash. Stay where you are!" she snapped, boiling a massive cauldron of water over the fire. 

"But old woman, I must have a wash; I must! I simply must..." he insisted loudly. 

"Oh bother! How much you trouble me!" she growled. "All right, go! But I'll tie this rope round your waist so you don't run away."

"Now I must be quick..." the boy thought as he went out to the courtyard. He quickly untied the rope from his waist and tied it securely around a thick wooden pillar. 

"When the mountain witch calls out, answer for me," the boy whispered to the pillar, taking out one of his good-luck charms and sticking it to the wood. 

Then he tiptoed out the back door and ran off like the wind! 

Inside, the mountain witch waited and waited. "Hurry up! Haven't you finished?" she yelled. 

"Wait a little, wait a little!" the charm on the pillar called back in the boy's voice. 

"How long you take. Come out, now!"

"Wait a little, just a little..."

At last, she got tired of waiting and angrily kicked open the door. "What! He's gone! I've been tricked! I've been tricked! But I'll catch you still."

The witch flew after him and soon caught up with the boy-monk. Desperate, he brought out his second good-luck charm and threw it behind him. "Be a high sand mountain!"

*WHOOSH!* A massive mountain of loose sand erupted from the ground between them. Up the sand mountain went the witch. She slipped and sank, sank and shouted... but finally reached the top. "Wait, wait... I am going to eat you up!"

The boy-monk ran on and on. When he could finally see the monastery in the distance, he flung up his very last charm. "Be a big river!"

*SPLASH! WHOOSH!* A raging, deep river appeared. 

The witch dove in and tried to swim as fast as she could, but the roaring waves pushed her back... *Slish, slosh, slish, slosh.*

The boy-monk reached the monastery doors. "Save me, sir! Save me. The mountain witch is after me!"

"Quick! Get into the empty cupboard," the old monk instructed, shutting the boy inside. 

Seconds later, the dripping wet witch kicked open the door. "Where is that miserable boy? I'll eat him for breakfast!"

"Oh, the boy-monk? He went off to pick chestnuts yesterday. He hasn't come back yet," the old monk said calmly, toasting a rice cake over his fire. 

"LIES! LIES! Tell me where he is!" the witch screamed. But then, her huge nose twitched greedily. *Sniff, sniff...* "Rice cakes! Umm! I love rice cakes. Give me one, old monk," she drooled. 

"I'll give you as many as you want, but first you must let me test your magic powers," the clever monk smiled. 

"Anything! Ask me to do anything!" the hungry witch agreed. 

"All right, then, how big can you be? This I want to see."

"BIG... BIG..." the witch chanted, growing instantly until she was so tall her horned head touched the cloudy sky! 

"Good, good! Now show me how small... small... small you can be. This I want to see," the old monk chuckled. 

The witch shrank instantly until she was no higher than the fire tongs. 

"Can you get any smaller?" the monk asked. 

"Ah, ha! You are now as small as a soya-bean!" The witch shrank until she was just a tiny speck. 

Quick as a flash, the old monk pinched the tiny witch between his fingers, put her between two golden, roasted rice-cakes... and popped them into his mouth! 

And that was the very end of the terrifying mountain witch. 

To this day, village children in Japan sing a happy song while picking crisp and juicy chestnuts on a lovely autumn day: 
*Crisp and juicy chestnuts, go crunch! crunch! crunch!*
*The wise, old monk has gobbled up the mountain witch for lunch!*


# The Little Bun and the Old Fox

Once there lived an old man and his wife in a small cottage. One day, the old woman smiled, "There's some flour here. I'll make a nice, round bun for my husband."

So she baked a fresh bun, and left it on the window sill to cool in the breeze. But as soon as she was out of sight, the little bun came alive! 

It bounced off the window sill, rolled across the wooden bench, and dropped onto the ground. "I'm free! Free!" it cheered. 

The bun rolled on and on through the forest, till it met a fluffy hare. 

"Oh, sweet little bun, I'll eat you up!" the hare twitched its nose. 

"Don't eat me, dear little hare. I'll sing you a song," the bun pleaded, and began to sing a lively tune. "Dum-dee-dum-dee... dum..."

While the hare was distracted by the song, the bun quickly rolled away. "Hey!" the hare shouted, but the bun was gone. 

The bun got away from the hare and rolled on and on. Suddenly, a hungry wolf blocked its path. "A wolf!" the bun gasped. 

"Please don't eat me up, sir! I'll sing a song for you," the bun offered quickly. "Dum-dee-dum-dee-dum..."

The wolf scratched its head, confused by the singing bun. And before it realized, the bun had rolled safely away. 

A little later, a massive brown bear stepped onto the path. "Stop for me, O little brown bun. I want to eat you up," the bear growled. 

"Come back! ...Dum-dum-dum..." the bear yelled as the bun sang its song and swiftly rolled right past its heavy paws. 

"Dum-dum... oh-oh, a fox!" the bun suddenly skidded to a halt. A sly red fox was smiling down at it. 

"Good morning, little bun. I'm going to eat you up!" the fox grinned. 

"Let me sing for you first," the bun offered, and began its trusty tune. "Dum-dee..."

But the fox tapped its ear. "I can't hear you. Will you sit on my snout and sing again?"

"All right," the innocent bun agreed, hopping onto the fox's long nose. "Dum-dee..."

"I still can't hear you," the fox smiled wider. "Please sit on my tongue... and sing again, for the last time."

"All right," the proud little bun said, rolling right onto the fox's outstretched tongue. "Dum-dee-dum..."

But as soon as the bun rolled down to his tongue... *SNAP!* The sly fox gobbled it right up! 

And that really was the very last time the little bun ever sang.


# Echo and Narcissus

Echo, the beautiful wood nymph, was very, very talkative. 

"And then I saw the deer running away. I followed it..." she babbled endlessly to her friend. 

"Echo, please stop. You've been talking for hours. I'm tired," her friend sighed. 

"But listen, I haven't told you what happened after that," Echo insisted, grabbing her arm. 

"Echo, please, enough. I'm going," her friend groaned, walking away. 

"She's gone!" Echo muttered, disappointed. 

Just then, Hera, the majestic Mother of the Gods, came walking through the forest. "Echo, have you seen my husband, Zeus?" she asked. 

"No, Mother Hera, I haven't seen Father Zeus. But I'm sure I saw him come here. He hasn't been here, Mother Hera! I saw a deer running near the river and... I was telling my friend what happened this morning," Echo immediately began to chatter without pausing for breath. 

"But I heard voices! What happened?" Hera demanded, trying to interrupt. 

But Echo talked and talked and talked... until—

"Oh do be quiet! See what you have done!" Hera exploded in anger. "You have distracted me with your chatter. Zeus has slipped away!"

Echo was bewildered. "But what have I done?"

"You silly girl! From now on, you'll never be able to talk. You will only repeat the last word of whatever anyone says," Hera cursed her fiercely. 

"...anyone says!" Echo gasped involuntarily. 

Poor Echo could no longer speak her own thoughts and wandered about the lonely woods by herself. 

One day, a remarkably handsome youth named Narcissus came to the forest. Echo saw him and instantly fell deeply in love with him. As she followed him around, he heard rustling leaves. 

"Who are you, girl?" Narcissus asked, turning around. 

"...girl," Echo repeated softly from the bushes. 

"I know that you are a girl. What is your name?"

"...name," she repeated, stepping out. 

"Oh, go away you silly girl. You are stupid," the arrogant youth sneered. 

"...stupid," Echo whispered, tears welling in her eyes. 

"How dare you call me stupid, you wretched creature!" Narcissus shouted angrily. 

"...creature," she sobbed. 

Narcissus stormed away in anger, leaving Echo all alone. Echo, utterly heart-broken, faded away into the shadows until her body melted into a mere spirit, leaving only her echoing voice behind. 

But Nemesis, the fearsome Goddess of Revenge, had watched the whole tragic scene. The goddess sought out the arrogant Narcissus. 

"You wicked boy! You shall suffer for your cruelty!" Nemesis declared. "Look into the pond!"

Narcissus walked to the clear, still water and looked down. He saw his own beautiful reflection staring back at him. 

"Oh, what a charming face!" he gasped, instantly captivated. Narcissus stayed by the pond for a long, long time, unable to look away from his reflection. 

"I shall never leave this place. I want to look at this charming face forever," he whispered to the water. 

So Narcissus remained rooted to the spot by the pond, staring at himself until he wasted away and was magically changed into a beautiful plant bearing a delicate, golden flower that droops its head over the water. 

But Echo, a spirit now, still wanders the world in search of Narcissus. She cannot ask anyone where Narcissus is. She can only repeat what others say. 

"Hello, Echo! Now we know your story," you might shout to the mountains. 

"...story!" her lonely voice will call back.
