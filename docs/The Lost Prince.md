# The Lost Prince - 1. The Lost Prince

Kshemankara and Papankara were two princes. Kshemankara, the elder, was known for his goodness, generosity, and righteousness. Papankara, the younger, was jealous and believed his name destined him to be a villain. 

Kshemankara was a noble soul who used his archery skills only to protect, never to kill. He was so generous that he even gave away his own royal necklace to a poor, indebted widow, much to the chagrin of his father, the king, and the sneers of his brother. 

One day, a neighbouring king offered his beautiful daughter's hand in marriage to Kshemankara. "Tell him I don't intend to marry just yet, father," Kshemankara replied. "I plan to go on a voyage to make a fortune first."

When the neighbouring king heard this, he sent another letter: "My daughter is prepared to wait till you return." Touched, Kshemankara promised to marry her upon his return. 

Kshemankara set out on his voyage, taking his brother Papankara and many adventurous young men with him. They travelled to several lands and made a fortune. Finally, they visited the "Island of Jewels," where they gathered diamonds and precious stones. Kshemankara kept the most precious jewels safely tucked in his waistband. 

On their return journey, disaster struck. A giant whale attacked and shattered their ship. In the chaos, Kshemankara bravely swam towards the shore, dragging the drowning Papankara with him. 

With his last ounce of strength, Kshemankara pulled them both onto the safety of a beach, before collapsing in a deep, exhausted sleep. 

The other ships from their fleet managed to return to the capital and delivered the grim news to the king. "The ship was shattered. Many must have died... but a few might have survived," the sailors reported. The old king clung to hope for months, but eventually fell gravely ill. 

Meanwhile, back on the beach, Papankara woke up. Seeing the precious jewels in his sleeping brother's waistband, his greed overtook him. He cruelly pierced Kshemankara's eyes, blinding him, stole the jewels, and abandoned his brother on the deserted shore. 

Papankara eventually returned to the capital alone. The old king, upon hearing that Kshemankara was lost forever, died of a broken heart. Papankara was crowned king, though he felt a pang of guilt as he sat on the throne. 

Back in the neighbouring kingdom, the princess who had waited for Kshemankara was devastated. Her father insisted she marry someone else and arranged a grand *swayamvara*. 

During the ceremony, the princess ignored all the wealthy princes and was instead drawn to the sad, beautiful notes of a flute. She followed the sound and garlanded the musician— a poor, blind man dressed in rags. 

The blind man was shocked. "I cannot accept you, young lady," he said. "I love another, even if she may not accept me now. And if you truly loved your dead prince, you wouldn't have chosen me."

"I do love him," the princess declared. "If my love for Prince Kshemankara be true... let the sight of one eye be restored to you."

Miraculously, the blind man opened one eye and could see! "I believe you!" he cried joyfully. "I AM KSHEMANKARA!" 

He explained how he had survived the shipwreck, only to be blinded in his sleep and abandoned. He had wandered as a blind musician ever since, never bearing malice towards whoever had blinded him. 

"If what I have spoken be nothing but the truth, let the sight of the other eye be restored to me," Kshemankara proclaimed. Instantly, his other eye was healed. 

The overjoyed princess took him to her father, and they were married that very day. 

When Kshemankara learned that his father had passed away and Papankara was now king, he decided to return home. "I would like to go back to see my brother!" he said eagerly, refusing to believe Papankara would harm him. 

In Kshemankara's capital, King Papankara received news that his brother was alive and returning. The guilt of his terrible deed finally overwhelmed him. "Kshemankara is coming back to where he belongs. And I have no place here. I cannot show my face to him," Papankara wept. 

He ordered the city to be grandly decorated to welcome his brother, and then he rode away into the wilderness, never to return. 

When Kshemankara arrived, he understood what had happened. He ascended the throne, becoming the new king, and ruled his land with wisdom and compassion for many years.
