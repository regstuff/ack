# Bhagawat: The Krishna Avatar - The Victorious

Shukadeva was happily narrating to King Pareekshit the complex story of Krishna's various marriages and victorious battles.

Accompanied firmly by the brave Satyaki and several other noble Yadavas, Krishna paid a long-awaited visit to Indraprastha to warmly meet the Pandavas. 
"My dear Krishna!" Yudhishthira exclaimed.
"What a wonderful surprise!" Bheema boomed.
Krishna bowed respectfully to his elders Yudhishthira and Bheema, affectionately blessed the younger Nakula and Sahadeva and then turned to his dearest friend. 
"Arjuna, my dear friend!" Krishna smiled, embracing him warmly.

Their mother Kunti could not possibly contain her happy tears of joy. "O Krishna, we were truly delighted that you were so deeply concerned about us and thoughtfully sent Akrura to carefully enquire about our well-being in Hastinapura."
"I am so incredibly happy that all of you cleverly escaped from the burning house of lac," Krishna smiled. 
"And we are so absolutely happy to finally have you safely here!" Yudhishthira said. "You must definitely stay awhile with us!"

Krishna was easily persuaded to peacefully stay in Indraprastha for the entire four months of the long rainy season. 
One sunny day, Arjuna and Krishna went hunting in the deep forest. "Aah! This long hunt has definitely made me very tired," Arjuna panted. 
"Let us rest awhile by the cool river, Arjuna," Krishna suggested.

As they peacefully refreshed themselves with the cool water, Arjuna pointed. "Look, Krishna! There is a beautiful maiden meditating."
"Go and gently find out who she is," Krishna smiled. 

Arjuna approached her respectfully. "Who are you, O beautiful one? Where have you come from?"
"I am Surya the Sun god's devoted daughter," she replied softly. "My name is Kalindi. My father has kindly built a safe house for me deep in this river."
"Why are you doing such incredibly severe penance here all alone?" Arjuna asked.
"I ardently want to absolutely obtain Lord Vishnu as my beloved husband. I have solemnly vowed to faithfully remain here until I finally behold Krishna."

Arjuna went excitedly back to Krishna. "She is absolutely determined to miraculously marry you, Krishna."
"So be it!" Krishna smiled. 
Taking the delighted Kalindi with them, they returned happily to Yudhishthira's grand palace. 

While Krishna was there, asked by Krishna, Vishwakarma, the incredible celestial architect, miraculously built a truly wonderful, magical city for the Pandavas at Indraprastha. Later, with the firm help of Krishna and Arjuna, Agni, the hungry god of fire, completely devoured the massive Khandava forest. But when the dense forest was being fiercely consumed by Agni, Arjuna mercifully saved Maya, a brilliant demon architect, from the deadly flames. 
The incredibly grateful Maya magically built a truly beautiful, unparalleled assembly hall for Yudhishthira at Indraprastha. 

Then one day Krishna announced, "I have happily spent four wonderful months in your delightful company. Now I absolutely must return to Dwaraka."
After safely reaching Dwaraka, Krishna joyously married the devoted Kalindi on a highly auspicious day. 

***

Mitravinda, the beautiful princess of Ujjayini, had firmly set her pure heart entirely on Krishna. "I will absolutely marry only Krishna," she declared. 
But her arrogant brothers were strong allies of the wicked Duryodhana. "No, we absolutely cannot allow you to foolishly do so," they argued. "It would terribly annoy Duryodhana. You may freely choose absolutely anyone other than Krishna at your royal *swayamvara* (groom-choosing ceremony)."

But Krishna, who had secretly learnt of her true love, arrived boldly at the grand *swayamvara*. Before the shocked eyes of the arrogant kings, he effortlessly swept her onto his chariot. 
"Look, Krishna is brazenly taking away Mitravinda!" they cried, but none dared stop him. 

***

King Nagnajit of the grand city of Ayodhya had a beautiful daughter named Satya. He made a truly daunting public announcement. 
"I will marry my beautiful daughter Satya only to the absolute bravest of men. He must be fully able to entirely overpower these seven incredibly ferocious, wild bulls single-handedly."

When Krishna heard of this incredible challenge, he went peacefully to Ayodhya. 
"Lord Krishna is finally here!" Satya rejoiced privately. "Will my fondest dreams actually come true! Will he truly marry me?"

King Nagnajit welcomed him with deep awe. "O Lord of the entire universe! What can a mere mortal exactly like me possibly do for you?"
"I simply want to happily build a strong bond of affection with you by marrying your beautiful daughter Satya," Krishna replied. 
"We are indeed incredibly fortunate," the king beamed. "I must personally welcome him. Certainly. Where could I possibly find a better match than you? But I have a strict, unbreakable condition."
"What exactly is it?"

Nagnajit led Krishna directly to the strong enclosure of the seven truly wild, raging bulls. "You must completely control them bare-handed and display your unmatched prowess."
"I will easily do it," Krishna smiled. 

Krishna magically assumed seven different, identical forms at once. 
"Will he possibly succeed?" the worried crowd murmured. "So incredibly many mighty kings have completely failed to even control these mad bulls."
...And with utter, playful ease, the seven Krishnas effortlessly overpowered the seven ferocious animals, easily tying them up. 

"He has incredibly done it!" the crowd roared. "He is the rightful chosen groom!"
"See, with what absolute ease he's playfully leading the massive bulls. Exactly like a tiny child pulling a light wooden toy!"

The grand wedding of Krishna with Satya was joyously celebrated with truly great joy at Ayodhya. Accompanied by an enormous, royal army of decorated elephants, swift horses, and armed soldiers generously presented by King Nagnajit, the newly-weds happily left the town. 

The jealous, defeated kings who had vainly tried for Satya's hand and miserably failed now aggressively surrounded them on the road. "You shall absolutely not get safely away with her!" they roared. 
Just then, a familiar chariot appeared. "Oh, Arjuna! There you happily are!" Krishna smiled. 

Like a truly devoted friend, Arjuna came fiercely to Krishna's aid and with his incredibly powerful divine bow, Gandeeva, effortlessly defeated all of Krishna's foolish opponents. 
"There! They have all cowardly gone," Arjuna grinned. "You can peacefully proceed in absolute peace to Dwaraka."

Later, Krishna also married Bhadra, the devoted daughter of his aunt Shrutakeerti, and the beautiful Lakshmana, the princess of Madra. 
"So he had exactly eight royal wives?" King Pareekshit asked Shukadeva.
"No," the sage replied. "Besides them, he generously married the thousands of beautiful, captive women he had bravely rescued from the terrible clutches of the wicked demon Narakasura."

***

Narakasura, also terrifyingly known as Bhaumasura, was violently terrorizing the peaceful abode of the heavenly devas with his incredibly daring acts. 
"He has even brazenly stolen the priceless, magical ear-rings of mother Aditi," Indra complained to Krishna. "And he has forcefully taken away the divine royal umbrella of Varuna."

With his dear, brave wife Satyabhama, Krishna set off swiftly on his divine vehicle, the massive eagle Garuda. 
When he finally reached Pragjyotishapura, the heavily fortified capital of Narakasura—
"Hmm! The steep mountains completely surround the entire city exactly like a natural, impenetrable fortress," Krishna observed. "And then there is an incredible wall of sharp weapons, a deep moat completely filled with water, and a terrifying well of magical fire."
Besides all this, the wicked asura Mura had cleverly spread dreadful, invisible magical dragnets absolutely all around the city. 

Krishna effortlessly held aloft his heavy, divine mace... and with it, he simply broke the massive surrounding mountains completely to tiny pieces. Next he calmly took up his divine bow and rained explosive arrows. At last, he forcefully hurled his blazing *chakra*... which instantly destroyed the magical barriers of fire and water and completely tore up the invisible dragnets created by Mura. 

The demon Mura who slept deeply under the moat water woke up with a massive start. "What's that terrifying sound? Exactly like the deafening roar of thunder!"
It was Krishna powerfully blowing on the massive Panchajanya conch. 

Mura angrily took up his heavy trident and rushed violently at Krishna. "Ahhh!" he roared, and threw the heavy trident viciously at Garuda. 
Krishna effortlessly cut the flying trident completely to tiny pieces with sharp arrows shot in incredibly rapid succession... and then aimed a deadly volley of explosive arrows directly at the five terrible heads of Mura. 

The deafening roar that rose from his five ferocious heads echoed fearfully in all directions. "Ahhh!"
Whatever magical weapon the frantic asura chose, Krishna effortlessly destroyed with absolute ease. Then the desperate asura attacked Krishna directly with his bare arms outstretched to crush him. 
Playfully, Krishna released the blazing Sudarshana *chakra*... and instantly killed Mura, severing all five heads. 

The seven angry sons of Mura quickly arrayed themselves to viciously attack Krishna. "Come! Let's attack him aggressively with absolutely all our weapons! We must definitely avenge the brutal death of our father."
But they were absolutely no match for Krishna's divine might and were swiftly slain. 

Now the mighty Narakasura himself came furiously to the fore. "He has brutally killed Mura and his seven sons. He absolutely must not go unpunished."
With his massive army of literally hundreds of giant, armored elephants he set forth. "When I fiercely release my magical *Shataghnee* (a deadly weapon), you must absolutely all fire your thousands of arrows at the exact same time," he commanded. 

While Krishna effortlessly countered the massive arrow attack with his own endless arrows, Garuda set fiercely upon the enemy elephants with his incredibly powerful wings and sharp talons to entirely annihilate the elephants. The terrified elephant army simply could not stand this devastating dual attack and fled in utter panic. 

Only the furious Narakasura remained bravely on the bloody battlefield. "That giant bird has cowardly scared away my prized elephants. I will brutally kill him first with my magical *shakti* dart."
He hurled the incredibly powerful weapon directly at Garuda. Why, the divine bird did absolutely not even budge! 
"Let me attack Krishna himself," Narakasura roared, charging with his sword. 
But Krishna was incredibly quicker than him and... the blazing Sudarshana *chakra* cleanly cut off the asura's arrogant head. 

With Narakasura finally killed, Bhoomi, Mother Earth, respectfully approached Krishna. 
"Here are the stolen ear-rings of mother Aditi and the royal umbrella of Varuna, wrongfully stolen by my wicked son Narakasura." Bowing incredibly respectfully she pleaded, "Lord! My innocent grandson Bhagadatta, the young son of Bhaumasura, is entirely filled with terrible fear. Please generously protect him and make him king."
Krishna kindly blessed the trembling boy and made him the rightful king, and then peacefully entered Narakasura's grand palace. 

He found exactly sixteen thousand incredibly beautiful princesses helplessly imprisoned inside. They had been cruelly captured and hoarded by the wicked Narakasura from many defeated kingdoms. 
"Look! It is Lord Krishna himself!" they wept in relief. "I truly hope he safely rescues us."
All of them were completely enraptured by Krishna's divine beauty and gentle grace. "I want only Krishna to be my husband," they all prayed silently. "May God grant my ardent, pure wish."

Krishna perfectly realised their pure, unspoken devotion to him and gently arranged to send them all safely and honorably to Dwaraka. 
Later Krishna and Satyabhama visited Indra's heavenly abode, Amaravati, to return the stolen goods. "Here are the precious ear-rings of mother Aditi," Krishna said. 

While in heaven, Satyabhama was completely fascinated by a glowing, celestial tree—the magical *Kalpavriksha* (wish-fulfilling tree). "How incredibly lovely it perfectly looks! Can't I possibly have it?" she asked Krishna. 
Krishna fiercely forced the ungrateful Indra to part with the celestial tree and brought it completely to Dwaraka. "There! You can happily have it growing beautifully in the private garden of your own palace."

Meanwhile, the sixteen thousand damsels safely released from Bhaumasura's dark palace had joyously arrived at Dwaraka. Each of them was generously given a beautiful, separate palace to live comfortably in. Then, on a highly auspicious day, Krishna miraculously assumed sixteen thousand multiple, identical forms and was ceremoniously and honorably wedded to absolutely all of them at the exact same time, granting their pure wish.

***

One quiet day when Krishna was sitting in a perfectly relaxed mood with his beloved Rukmini—
*How incredibly, purely devoted she is completely to me,* Krishna thought fondly. *But let me playfully tease her a little.*
"Dear one!" Krishna began seriously. "So incredibly many mighty kings, immensely rich and noble, vied fiercely with one another for your beautiful hand in marriage. Yet you foolishly rejected absolutely all your wealthy suitors, and stubbornly selected me, who is absolutely not your equal in any worldly way. Why?"

Rukmini looked up in shock. 
"Why did you possibly accept me?" Krishna continued smoothly. "See how we cowardly Yadavas had to desperately take refuge deep in the sea to safely escape Jarasandha. We have absolutely no great riches, no massive, inherited possessions. Surely you must perfectly know that marriages should strictly be among absolute equals—in wealth, high social status, and age. Why did you foolishly plunge headlong into this terrible alliance? Just on the flimsy basis of some completely false praise you had mistakenly heard about me?"
Krishna sighed heavily. "As for myself, I simply kidnapped you just to publicly humiliate Rukmi, your arrogant brother."

On suddenly hearing this truly terrible, heartbreaking confession, Rukmini trembled violently and fell entirely into a deep, lifeless swoon. 
Krishna quickly realised he had foolishly gone much too far. *Poor thing,* he thought, filled with instant regret. *I had simply just meant to playfully tease her.*

In a trice he got quickly down and incredibly affectionately lifted her limp form up, reviving her. "I was just playfully teasing you, my darling. You look so incredibly lovely when you are deeply agitated."
"Are you absolutely sure you truly said all this completely in jest?" Rukmini wept, clinging to him. "I perfectly know I am absolutely not equal to you, the Supreme Lord of absolutely all worlds. But my absolute only desire is to humbly stay by your divine side and faithfully serve you absolutely all my life."
"I teased you simply just to lovingly hear you say exactly this," Krishna smiled tenderly. 

***

In due course of time, Krishna's many wives gave healthy birth to exactly ten strong sons each. I already told you the amazing story of Pradyumna, the incredibly handsome eldest son of Rukmini. Besides his first wife Mayavati, Pradyumna also surprisingly married Rukmavati, the beautiful daughter of his vengeful uncle Rukmi. 

"But didn't the arrogant Rukmi utterly hate Krishna for having completely defeated and terribly insulted him?" Pareekshit asked in surprise. "How did he possibly allow this marriage with Krishna's son?"

Shukadeva explained. Rukmavati, the incredibly lovely daughter of Rukmi, had the customary royal *swayamvara*. When she saw Pradyumna there, she thought, "Surely! That young prince is absolutely the most handsome and incredibly charming! I'll happily choose him to be my husband."

When Rukmi clearly saw the wedding garland being proudly put exactly on Pradyumna, son of Krishna, he fumed internally. *She has completely chosen Pradyumna, the son of my absolute worst enemy—that vile Krishna.* But then he relented slightly. *My dear sister Rukmini will be incredibly happy to have my daughter as her daughter-in-law. Purely for her sake I will grudgingly let them wed.*

But the other arrogant kings and princes who had hopefully gathered in the hall were entirely enraged and violently protested the choice. Pradyumna fought them absolutely bravely, effortlessly defeated them all single-handedly, and proudly carried away his beautiful bride Rukmavati to Dwaraka. 
Pradyumna and Rukmavati were happily married with completely due ceremony. They soon had a beautiful son who was wonderfully named Aniruddha, who quickly grew up to be exactly as incredibly handsome as his own father. 

***

Banasura, an incredibly powerful, thousand-armed demon who fiercely ruled over Shonitapura, was a truly great, fanatic devotee of Lord Shiva. One day, seeing Shiva dance—
"Ah! Lord Shiva is passionately dancing the furious *Tandava*! I will eagerly use my one thousand strong arms to passionately provide incredible, thunderous music for him."
Banasura powerfully played various loud musical instruments on his thousand arms to perfectly accompany Lord Shiva's wild dance. 

Shiva stopped, breathing heavily. "I am deeply pleased with your incredible, passionate devotion. Name absolutely any boon you truly desire."
"Lord, please permanently remain here and fiercely guard my kingdom yourself," Banasura asked boldly. 
"So be it," Shiva agreed. 

Thus completely and utterly protected by Lord Shiva himself, Banasura lived in absolute peace and unbeatable arrogance. One day he complained to Shiva. 
"Lord! My one thousand incredibly strong hands are of absolutely no use to me at all. There is absolutely no one besides you who can possibly even match my immense strength. Whom can I possibly fight? In fact, the other day, my heavy arms were incredibly itching to violently fight so much that I casually crushed a whole, massive range of tall mountains."

Lord Shiva was irritated by his foolish arrogance and said, "You arrogant fool! Just wait! When the high flagstaff of your golden chariot unexpectedly breaks off, you will definitely meet your absolute true match, and he will entirely and utterly crush your massive arrogance."
"At last!" Banasura rejoiced foolishly. "Now I can happily look forward to a real, good fight."

Banasura had a truly beautiful daughter named Usha. One morning she got up suddenly from a deep dream. 
"Dearest! Where exactly are you?" she cried out, searching her empty bed. 
Her maids and companions were completely surprised. "What incredibly happened, Usha?"
"Oh! I just had a truly strange, incredibly wonderful dream about a beautiful youth."

Chitralekha, who was Usha's incredibly close, magical friend said, "But whom exactly did you dream of?"
"I don't possibly know! I had absolutely never met him anywhere before. All I can clearly tell you is that he was incredibly dark and wonderfully handsome with truly large, beautiful lotus-shaped eyes."
Chitralekha, who was a truly skilled, magical artist set quietly to work. "Now, I will carefully draw the exact portraits of all famous kings and princes. See if you can possibly recognise the mysterious man of your vivid dream."

She drew face after face. "Is it any of these?"
"No, no, no," Usha sighed. 
At last, Chitralekha drew a handsome, dark face. 
"Ah! There he absolutely is!" Usha gasped in love. "That's exactly my Lord."
"This is Aniruddha, the incredibly handsome grandson of Krishna," Chitralekha identified him. "Before very long, I will absolutely bring him directly to you."

That very night, Chitralekha expertly used the magical art of yoga and stealthily brought the sleeping Aniruddha completely undetected to Shonitapura entirely from heavily-guarded Dwaraka. 
Usha and Aniruddha spent many incredibly happy, secret days blissfully in her heavily guarded private palace. 

After a long while the royal guards finally noticed that Princess Usha had absolutely not come out of the palace for many days. 
"Could she possibly be sick?"
"No! I clearly and always hear the happy sound of sweet music and joyful laughter from inside."
"And an unfamiliar man's deep voice too!"

They urgently reported the deeply shocking matter directly to Banasura. "We have perfectly guarded her palace day and entire night. Yet we definitely feel a strange man has somehow got safely inside."

Banasura charged furiously into Usha's private palace. He was utterly stunned to incredibly see the handsome Aniruddha calmly playing a peaceful game of dice with Usha. 
"Capture him immediately! Tie him entirely up with unbreakable, magical serpent-bonds (*Nagapash*)," Banasura roared. 

***

Meanwhile, far away, Dwaraka was plunged in absolute gloom. "It has been four agonizing months now since our dear Aniruddha mysteriously disappeared," his parents wept. 
"Ah! There is the wandering sage Narada. Perhaps he will finally have some news."

When Narada accurately related exactly how the arrogant Banasura had secretly captured Aniruddha, the angry people of Dwaraka were completely filled with absolute rage. 
"Attack Banasura!"
"On to Shonitapura!"

Krishna fiercely led the massive attack and expertly arranged his vast troop in a truly formidable, unbeatable military formation. 
"Surround his entire capital!" Krishna commanded. 
Banasura proudly emerged from his gates with an equally incredibly large, fierce force. With him were the mighty Lord Shiva himself and his fierce warrior son Karttikeya. 

"Come!" Krishna commanded his generals. "Pradyumna, you bravely fight Karttikeya. While I entirely confront Shiva. Satyaki, you take directly on Banasura."
The battle was truly cosmic. Lord Shiva furiously used his incredibly powerful, devastating celestial weapons but Krishna effortlessly and expertly repelled them with exactly equally strong but opposing divine weapons. 
*He is furiously using the fiery agneyastra,* Krishna noted calmly. *I'll easily quell it entirely with the watery parjanyastra.*
*I'll simply use the magical Jrumbhanastra to make Lord Shiva incredibly sleep,* Krishna decided. *Then I can easily attack the rest of the vulnerable army.*

The powerful Jrumbhanastra made the great Shiva so incredibly, magically drowsy that he simply could not possibly fight anymore. "Yawn." Shiva sat down, overwhelmed by sleep. 
Banasura finally realised in panic that his entire, massive army was being completely destroyed and he lunged furiously at Krishna. But the literal hundreds of deadly arrows released simultaneously by Banasura's thousand arms were entirely and effortlessly destroyed by Krishna's own. 

When Banasura's massive chariot, his expert charioteer, and his swift horses completely fell under Krishna's relentless attack, he cowardly ran away from the bloody field. But soon he stubbornly returned, heavily armed with various, deadly weapons in his thousand massive hands. 
Krishna calmly released his blazing Sudarshana *chakra*. 

When Banasura's numerous arms incredibly began to fall bloodily off exactly like tender, chopped branches of a soft tree, the awakened Shiva frantically intervened. 
"Banasura is strictly under my personal protection," Shiva pleaded to Krishna. "Please generously spare him his foolish life."
"I will absolutely not slay him, purely out of respect for you," Krishna promised. "But to completely humble his massive pride, I will completely cut off absolutely all but four of his thousand arms."

The humbled, bleeding Banasura bowed deeply in immense gratitude to Krishna for incredibly sparing his life and instantly ordered the immediate, safe release of Aniruddha. With the happy Usha and Aniruddha, Krishna returned incredibly victorious to Dwaraka where they received a truly joyous, grand welcome. 

***

One day, while Balarama was peacefully away visiting Vraja, Krishna received a highly arrogant messenger from Paundraka, the foolish king of Karusha, who was a close friend of the dead Narakasura. 
"My great king has sent a strict message that he is actually the absolute true Vaasudeva (God on earth). You are simply a cheap impostor who falsely carries his divine insignia. He has specifically asked you to immediately give yourself fully up or fight him to the death."

"Ha! Ha! Ha!" Krishna laughed uncontrollably. "What an incredibly silly, foolish man."
He turned to the messenger. "Tell your foolish king that I will absolutely not give up my divine disc and other insignia. In actual fact, I will gladly use my sharp disc to completely destroy him and his utterly foolish friends who constantly mislead him into this pathetic self-deception."

After the angry messenger left—"Come!" Krishna smiled. "Let us prepare to quickly attack that absolute fool Paundraka."
"But he is safely staying with his very powerful friend, the King of Kashi," a spy reported. 
Krishna confidently led his fierce army directly to Kashi. Paundraka and the arrogant King of Kashi came proudly out to meet his challenge. 

"Look at him!" Krishna roared with genuine laughter. "He is dressed exactly identically like me—with a fake conch shell, a wooden disc, a wooden mace, a dagger, and even the exact yellow garments! Ha! Ha! Ha!"
Still roaring with absolute, uncontrollable laughter at the utterly ridiculous sight of the foolish impostor Paundraka, Krishna casually attacked the combined, massive armies of Kashi and Karusha. 

Then—"Hey! You foolish Paundraka!" Krishna called out. "You desperately wanted my *chakra* and other divine weapons, didn't you? Well, here you exactly are!"
Krishna hurled his blazing disc. In a matter of mere moments, Paundraka's fake-crowned head lay bloodily on the dusty ground. 
Now Krishna calmly turned to the arrogant King of Kashi. With his returning Sudarshana *chakra*, Krishna effortlessly cut off his head also. Off flew the bloody head of the King of Kashi and landed incredibly exactly at the very gates of his own royal palace. 

Sudakshina, the young prince of Kashi, was completely shocked and horrified at the gruesome sight. "Why! This is the bloody head of my own father. I must absolutely avenge his terrible death."
He deeply concentrated all his dark energies to find a truly magical method of revenge. One day, while performing a dark, terrible *yajna* (fire sacrifice) specifically for this—a massive, terrifying fire fiend miraculously emerged from the flames and rushed aggressively directly to Dwaraka. 

The peaceful people of Dwaraka were absolutely terrified by the approaching monster of pure fire. "Lord! Save us immediately from this burning calamity!" they cried. 
Krishna barely even looked up from his casual game of dice. "Don't absolutely worry. I will completely protect you."
He casually ordered his ever-ready Sudarshana *chakra* to instantly attack the burning fiend. Whirring incredibly menacingly, the blazing, divine disc set swiftly out to utterly destroy the fire fiend. 

The fierce disk effortlessly drove the terrified fire fiend all the way back to Kashi. After utterly destroying the dark fiend and the wicked prince who summoned it, the Sudarshana *chakra* was peacefully restored to Lord Krishna's waiting hand. 

"Thus exactly ends the incredibly exciting seventh session of our detailed rendering of the tenth chapter of the sacred Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded.
