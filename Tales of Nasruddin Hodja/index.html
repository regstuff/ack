    Tales of Nasruddin Hodja - 1. The Lucky Escape

    One dark night, Nasruddin Hodja casually looked out of his bedroom window.

    Suddenly, his eyes went wide. "!?" he gasped in shock. A large, dark figure was looming in his garden!

    He ran back into the room. "Wife! Wife! Get me my hunting bow and arrow quickly!" he yelled.

    His wife rushed in, handing him the weapons. "Here! But what on earth do you want them for at this hour?" she asked, confused.

    "There's a terrifying monster out there in the garden!" Hodja whispered frantically.

    He marched bravely to the window, aiming his heavy bow.

    "Stand back!" he ordered his wife.

    He pulled the string back tight and let the arrow fly into the darkness. SWISH!

    A satisfying thud echoed from the garden.

    "I've hit it!" Hodja declared triumphantly, puffing out his chest. "Right through the heart... or maybe the stomach!"

    "Should we go check?" his wife asked nervously.

    "No, no, it's too dark," Hodja said sensibly. "We'll go out in the morning and see exactly what it looks like."

    Early the next morning, as the sun rose, Nasruddin Hodja and his wife cautiously stepped out of the house.

    "Now stay close behind me and..." Hodja warned, holding his hands up protectively.

    His wife stopped and stared at the garden wall. "So that's the terrible monster you shot!" she said, hands on her hips.

    Hodja looked over. Pinned to the wall by his arrow was a thick piece of clothing.

    "Your own heavy overcoat which I had hung out to dry!" his wife scolded him angrily.

    Hodja stared at his ruined coat, a cold sweat breaking out on his forehead. "I...I... shot at my very own overcoat?" he gulped nervously.

    He wiped his brow in sheer relief. "Thank Almighty God I was not wearing it at the time! I would have been killed!"

    Tales of Nasruddin Hodja - 2. The Thousand Coins

    Nasruddin Hodja had a very peculiar habit of praying loudly every single morning. And every day, he used to arrogantly pray for the exact same thing—and in the exact same way.

    "Oh, God, please give me one thousand gold coins," Hodja would pray loudly, his hands raised to the sky.

    He would then add stubbornly, "Exactly one thousand coins, mind you! If you give me even one coin less, I simply won't accept the money!"

    One day, his annoyed neighbour decided to play a trick on him. He carefully counted out exactly nine hundred and ninety-nine coins, put them in a small cloth bag... and secretly threw it straight through the Hodja's open window! THUD!

    Hodja heard the noise and rushed over. "A heavy bag of coins! God has finally answered my prayers!" he cheered happily.

    When the Hodja excitedly opened the bag and counted the money on his floor...

    "Wait... nine-hundred and ninety-nine coins!" Hodja frowned.

    Outside, the neighbour was hiding under the window, listening closely. Now let's clearly see what he does, the neighbour thought smugly. He will definitely have to refuse the money because it is exactly one coin short of a thousand, just as he promised!

    But to his neighbour's absolute surprise...

    "Thank you endlessly for this generous money, God!" Hodja prayed loudly, clutching the bag. "But please kindly see that you send the remaining one coin as soon as you possibly can."

    The neighbour angrily jumped up and shouted through the window. "What a lying rascal you are, Nasruddin! Even your loud prayers are entirely false!"

    The neighbour climbed inside. "Anyway, give me my money back!"

    "Your money?" Hodja asked innocently. "This money was clearly sent directly to me by God."

    "It was I who threw it into your house, you fool!" the neighbour yelled.

    "Well then, God clearly chose you as a vessel to do his holy work," Hodja smiled calmly. "Thank you for the delivery. Now please quietly go."

    "I will perfectly not leave without my money!" the neighbour fumed.

    "It's not your money, I tell you!"

    "Fine! Let's go straight to the judge," the neighbour challenged.

    Hodja suddenly looked very weak. "Oh... I am not feeling too well today. I simply can't walk all the long way to the judge's court."

    "You can have my strong mule to ride on!" the desperate neighbour offered.

    "But I can't possibly appear before the honourable judge dressed in these terribly old, ragged robes!" Hodja complained. "And I have absolutely nothing else to wear!"

    "Don't worry about clothes! I'll happily lend you my expensive new fur coat!" the neighbour yelled, completely losing his patience.

    As Hodja put on the coat, the neighbour frowned. Oh, I never truly realised this silly fellow was so incredibly tricky, he thought nervously.

    Soon, they were at the court.

    "Your honour, please intently listen to my sad story!" the neighbour begged. "This man prays loudly every morning for a thousand coins..."

    The neighbour enthusiastically spoke for a very long time, explaining the trick. When he had finally finished...

    "So, the bag of money which the Hodja found in his house is actually yours!" the judge summarized.

    "That is the absolute truth, your honour," the neighbour nodded proudly.

    Hodja calmly stepped forward. "Please don't blindly believe him, your honour. He is a known liar, always aggressively claiming other people's property as his very own."

    Hodja gestured to the window. "For example, I won't be entirely surprised if he suddenly says that the fine mule on which I rode here is actually his."

    "It is mine!" the neighbour yelled angrily.

    Hodja shook his head sadly. "See? Next, you'll falsely say that this expensive fur coat I am wearing too is yours!"

    "It is! And perfectly well you know it, you thief!" the neighbour screamed.

    Hodja turned to the judge with a knowing look. "Do you clearly see how it is with this madman, your honour?"

    "I do, indeed!" the judge said, glaring strictly at the furious neighbour. "It is perfectly clear to me that the bag of money too is completely not yours! You are a madman claiming everything in sight!"

    "Not mine?!" the neighbour gasped.

    "The case is entirely dismissed!" the judge banged his gavel.

    "But...but..." the neighbour stammered.

    Outside the court, the neighbour furiously pulled his own hair. "This is completely maddening!" he cried. He fell to his knees before Hodja. "Please have deep mercy on me, Nasruddin. I am not a rich man... please return my life's savings."

    Hodja smiled down at him. "You should have smartly thought of that before mocking a man's prayers."

    Hodja kindly handed over the heavy bag. "Anyway, here is your money." He took off the coat. "And your expensive fur coat and your healthy mule."

    His neighbour was so relieved he never dared to interfere with him ever again.

    The very next morning, the neighbour heard Hodja praying loudly again: "Oh, God, please send me a thousand coins..."

    Tales of Nasruddin Hodja - 3. A Bear on a Tree

    One sunny day, Nasruddin Hodja was peacefully walking through a dense forest when he suddenly heard a loud rustling in the bushes.

    He froze. He saw a massive, black bear lumbering straight towards him!

    Hodja gasped in pure terror. He hastily scrambled and climbed high up into the branches of the nearest, tallest tree.

    He clung to a thick branch, his heart pounding, and nervously waited for the dangerous bear to simply pass by.

    Unfortunately for Hodja, the sleepy bear lazily chose that very same tree to rest under. The huge beast curled up comfortably at the base of the trunk and quickly fell deeply asleep.

    The poor Hodja was completely trapped. He spent the entire, long afternoon terrified and cramped on the very top of the tree, too scared to even make a sound.

    Then, finally, towards the late evening...

    "Ah!" Hodja smiled weakly. "He's slowly getting up at last! Oh, what a profound relief!"

    The bear stretched its massive arms with a loud grunt. "Eh!"

    Then, to Hodja's absolute horror, the bear wrapped its thick arms around the trunk.

    "Oh, my God!" Hodja panicked, his eyes going wide. "He's actively climbing up!"

    The truth was, the bear was simply hungry and had slowly climbed up the tree to eat the sweet berries growing on the lower branches.

    But Hodja didn't know that. "He's deliberately coming after me to eat me!" Hodja cried. "I'd better quickly climb up to the absolute highest, thinnest branch!"

    CHOMP! CHOMP! The bear happily munched on the juicy berries, slowly climbing higher and higher as he ate.

    After some time, the hungry bear too reached that exact same high branch where Hodja was desperately clinging.

    "Now it's completely over for me!" Hodja whimpered, pressing himself against the thin trunk as the bear's huge face came extremely close. "This is certainly my last miserable day on earth."

    The bear picked a large handful of berries and accidentally held his massive, furry paw out right in front of Hodja's terrified face.

    Hodja squeezed his eyes shut, but then opened one. "Wait... he is generously offering me fresh fruits?"

    Not wanting to politely offend a deadly beast, Hodja shakily held up a hand. "I... I absolutely never eat fruits. Thank you very much, though," Hodja stammered politely.

    The bear had been completely focused on the berries and had perfectly not seen the Hodja hiding there till then.

    When the bear suddenly heard a loud human voice right next to his ear, he looked up and saw Hodja's face.

    The bear was so incredibly startled by the sudden, frightening appearance of a human hiding in his berry tree... that he completely lost his balance!

    With a loud yelp, the massive bear slipped off the thin branch... and heavily fell all the way down to the ground.

    CRASH!

    Then quickly picking himself up, the terrified bear ran frantically away into the deep forest as fast as his legs could possibly carry him.

    Hodja slowly and safely climbed back down to the solid ground, dusting off his robes.

    "He's completely gone! Thank God!" Hodja sighed in immense relief.

    Then he scratched his head in genuine confusion, looking into the dark forest. "But I honestly can't imagine why such a huge, fearsome beast was so incredibly frightened of little old me!"

    Tales of Nasruddin Hodja - 4. How Hodja Bought a Donkey

    One busy morning, Nasruddin Hodja decided he was completely tired of his old, stubborn donkey. So, he proudly took his donkey to the crowded town market to sell it to the highest bidder.

    He stood in the hot sun for a while... and soon found a willing buyer for it.

    "I'll give you exactly twenty-five piastres for it," the merchant offered, inspecting the old animal.

    Hodja shrugged indifferently. "All right, it's a fair deal."

    The man quickly paid Hodja the money... and then, to Hodja's great surprise, immediately turned around and loudly put the donkey up for sale again in the very same market square!

    "Come folks, gather round and closely look at this magnificent beast!" the merchant shouted loudly to the passing crowd.
