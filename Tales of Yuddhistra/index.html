    Tales of Yuddhistra - 1. The Enchanted Lake

    Deep in the thick, green forest of Dwaitavana, there happily lived a very pious, quiet Brahman. His absolute most precious possession in the entire world was a simple pair of Shami sticks, which were specially used to create bright sparks when quickly rubbed against each other.

    He proudly used these precious Shami sticks exclusively to light the holy sacrificial fires for all his daily Yagnas (religious rituals).

    When the sticks were not actively in use, he would carefully hang them up safely on a low branch of a large tree outside his small hut.

    But one quiet day, when the Brahman was briefly away from his home... a beautiful, spotted wild deer came wandering up to the quiet tree. The deer playfully butted the hanging sticks with its head, and the sticks somehow got completely entangled and stuck fast in its large, sharp antlers!

    Later, when the Brahman finally returned to his quiet cottage, he gasped in absolute horror. "My precious sticks!" he cried, seeing the deer running away with them.

    He immediately dropped his heavy bundle of dry twigs and ran desperately after the fleeing deer.

    But the old Brahman, who was absolutely no physical match for the incredibly swift wild animal, soon sadly had to give up the exhausting chase.

    I have completely lost my precious sticks forever, the old man thought miserably, tears in his eyes. What shall I possibly do? Exactly whom shall I confidently turn to for help deep in this forest?

    As he slowly turned to walk sadly back to his empty hermitage, a brilliant thought suddenly struck his mind.

    The valiant Pandava brothers! he thought happily. Why on earth didn't I cleverly think of them? They will certainly be able to powerfully help me.

    The five brave Pandava princes, who had tragically lost their great kingdom to their wily, cheating cousins the Kauravas, were currently living in quiet exile deep in the very same forest with their beautiful wife, Draupadi.

    When the panting Brahman finally stood before their humble camp, Yudhishthira, the wise and eldest Pandava, was the very first to gently speak.

    "Welcome to our quiet hermitage, holy one!" Yudhishthira smiled respectfully. "Is there absolutely anything we can possibly do for you?"

    "O King, absolutely only you can powerfully save me!" the Brahman pleaded, bowing low.

    The Brahman then urgently told him the incredibly sad story of the loss of his precious sticks. "Without my holy sticks, I will simply not be legally able to respectfully perform my important religious duties. I am completely ruined!"

    Yudhishthira nodded solemnly. "Do not worry, holy father. We will swiftly track down the wild deer and safely bring back the precious sticks directly to you."

    The five heavily armed Pandavas immediately set out into the thick forest. For a long while, they had absolutely no luck at all. Then suddenly—

    "Deer tracks!" Bhima, the incredibly strong second brother, shouted, pointing at the dirt. "Let us quickly follow them."

    At last, far, far away from their safe hermitage—

    "Look! There is the spotted deer!" Bhima yelled.

    The Pandavas aggressively chased the incredibly fast deer through the trees and expertly shot their sharp arrows at it... but entirely in vain. The deer seemed to magically dodge every single arrow.

    For, suddenly—

    "It has completely vanished into thin air!" Bhima gasped, staring at the empty forest clearing.

    "What shall we possibly do now?" Arjuna, the great archer, asked in frustration.

    "We absolutely must confidently find it again," Yudhishthira decided firmly. "Let us bravely continue the long hunt."

    But the youngest brother, Nakula, wiped sweat from his face. "I am incredibly tired, terribly hungry, and desperately thirsty," he complained weakly.

    "Then let us briefly rest here before we go proudly on," Yudhishthira agreed kindly.

    They sat down in the hot shade. "Nakula," Yudhishthira requested gently. "Quickly climb up this tall tree and carefully see if there is a cool tank or a fresh stream anywhere near by."

    Nakula nimbly climbed the tree. "Yes! There is a beautiful, sparkling blue lake not far from exactly here," he reported happily.

    "Good. Come quickly down and eagerly fetch us some cool water safely in your empty quiver," Yudhishthira commanded.

    Soon, Nakula eagerly approached the quiet lake. We are indeed incredibly fortunate to easily find fresh water so near at hand, Nakula thought, licking his dry lips.

    He rushed desperately to the water's edge.

    Suddenly, a loud, booming voice echoed from the empty air. "WAIT! THIS MAGNIFICENT LAKE PERSONALLY BELONGS ENTIRELY TO ME. YOU MAY DRINK FROM IT ONLY AFTER YOU CORRECTLY ANSWER MY SPECIFIC QUESTIONS."

    Nakula stopped and looked wildly around. There was absolutely no one in sight anywhere.

    Who could it possibly be? Nakula thought in confusion. I'll bravely find out exactly who it is... right after I have quickly quenched my terrible thirst.

    Ignoring the loud warning, Nakula aggressively dipped his hands in the cool water and drank deeply.

    The very next moment, Nakula violently clutched his throat and heavily fell completely dead on the muddy bank.

    Meanwhile, back in the hot forest, the other tired Pandavas became very impatient.

    "Exactly why hasn't Nakula quickly returned yet?" Yudhishthira wondered aloud. "Go, Sahadeva, and cleverly see if you can possibly do better."

    When Sahadeva, the youngest of the mighty Pandavas, finally neared the beautiful lake—

    "Nakula! Nakula! He's completely dead!" Sahadeva gasped in absolute horror, seeing his brother's still body. He absolutely must have sadly died of sheer thirst. I'd much better urgently drink some cool water myself before I tragically suffer the exact same terrible fate.

    "I PROUDLY OWN THIS GREAT LAKE," the loud, invisible voice boomed again. "IF YOU FOOLISHLY DRINK WITHOUT FIRST CORRECTLY ANSWERING MY SPECIFIC QUESTIONS, YOU TOO WILL IMMEDIATELY FALL COMPLETELY DEAD."

    But Sahadeva hardly even heard the strange voice over his own intense thirst. He drank deeply from the lake.

    And he too violently fell completely dead right beside his brother.

    Then came the mighty Arjuna, the third and most famous of the brave Pandavas. He came in worried search of his two missing brothers.

    When Arjuna saw them both lying still, his eyes widened in pure rage. "Nakula! Sahadeva! How... exactly who has brutally done this vile, evil deed? Whoever it is, he has confidently invited his very own painful death!"

    Arjuna fiercely raised his heavy bow and aggressively looked around the empty lake, ready to fight.

    There is absolutely no one in clear sight, Arjuna thought furiously. But before I aggressively go in violent search of the cowardly murderer, I'd much better quickly quench my own terrible thirst so I am strong.

    When he proudly knelt down and was just about to eagerly drink the cool water...

    "NO, ARJUNA, DON'T!" the booming voice warned. "NOT IF YOU DESPERATELY WANT TO SAFELY LIVE. YOU WILL ABSOLUTELY FIRST ANSWER MY IMPORTANT QUESTIONS."

    Arjuna was completely enraged by the invisible challenge. "Will I? O cowardly spirit, bravely appear right before me, if you truly dare, and then confidently lay out your arrogant conditions to my face!"

    When his angry taunt did not seem to have absolutely any effect, Arjuna furiously raised his powerful bow and aggressively shot a volley of sharp, deadly arrows directly in the direction of the invisible voice.

    "YOUR SHARP ARROWS ABSOLUTELY CANNOT MAGICALLY HARM ME, ARJUNA!" the voice laughed loudly. "WHY DON'T YOU SIMPLY ANSWER MY SIMPLE QUESTIONS?"

    "I definitely will!" Arjuna screamed in pure fury. "With infinitely more effective, deadly weapons! But completely not before I refreshingly drink some cool water from this very lake!"

    Arjuna too arrogantly disregarded the clear warning... and he violently paid the ultimate penalty. He fell completely dead.

    Then proudly came Bhima, the incredibly massive, powerful second Pandava, carrying his giant mace. When he sadly saw his three brave brothers lying completely dead near the quiet lake...

    This terrible magic absolutely must be the evil act of a powerful Yaksha (demi-god) or a cruel Rakshasa (demon), Bhima thought grimly, gripping his heavy mace. A massive, brutal battle clearly awaits me. Let me quickly quench my immense thirst and fully prepare my mighty body for it.

    Bhima too greedily drank the cool water, completely disregarding the loud warning of the invisible one, and instantly fell completely dead.

    At long last, the worried Yudhishthira himself slowly came to the quiet lake. He was completely and utterly stunned by the incredibly horrifying sight that tragically met his tearful eyes.

    "Dear, dear, brave brothers!" Yudhishthira wept loudly, falling to his knees. "Are you truly, really dead, or are my exhausted eyes cruelly playing terrible magic tricks on me!"

    Tales of Yuddhistra - 2. The Yaksha's Questions

    Yudhishthira knelt by the edge of the enchanted lake, completely heartbroken by the sight of all four of his incredibly powerful brothers lying dead.

    He carefully examined their still bodies. "Absolutely no brutal mark of weapons upon them! No signs of a fierce physical struggle! No heavy footprints visible anywhere in the mud!" he whispered in absolute shock. "Exactly who could have possibly killed them so silently?"

    Overcome by grief and terrible thirst, Yudhishthira bent down towards the cool water.

    But exactly as Yudhishthira was just about to drink...

    ...A strange, croaking voice abruptly stopped him.

    "I am the great crane who rightfully owns this magical lake," a large white bird perched on a rock announced loudly. "If you selfishly drink without first correctly answering my specific questions, you, too, will definitely die just like your rash, foolish brothers."

    Yudhishthira slowly stood up and looked incredibly closely at the white bird.

    "You, who have somehow effortlessly killed those mighty warriors who could definitively not be overcome either by the powerful Devas (gods) or the fierce Asuras (demons), absolutely cannot be a simple, ordinary bird," Yudhishthira reasoned calmly. "Please tell me exactly who you really, truly are."

    The very next moment, the crane suddenly transformed in a massive flash of blinding light!

    A huge, terrifying, green-skinned being with massive horns and a heavy club appeared where the bird had been.

    "I am actually a powerful Yaksha," the being boomed proudly. "If you correctly answer my difficult questions, you may safely drink at my lake."

    Yudhishthira bowed respectfully. "O great Yaksha, please ask your difficult questions. I shall humbly try to correctly answer them to the absolute best of my humble ability."

    The Yaksha smiled fiercely. "Then exactly here is the very first one: Exactly what makes one a true Brahman—noble birth, good conduct, or deep learning?"

    "It is only good, righteous behaviour that truly makes one a Brahman," Yudhishthira answered wisely without hesitation.

    The Yaksha nodded. "What exactly is it in this world that permanently causes ceaseless, endless wonder?"

    "Day after day, absolutely countless people sadly die," Yudhishthira answered softly. "Yet, the remaining living always foolishly wish to live forever. What could possibly be more incredibly extraordinary than that?"

    The Yaksha stepped closer. "How exactly do you successfully become rich? And how exactly do you genuinely become happy?"

    "If you entirely abandon terrible lust, you will be truly rich," Yudhishthira answered firmly. "If you completely abandon selfish desire, you will be truly happy."

    The Yaksha asked many more incredibly difficult questions, and Yudhishthira calmly answered every single one of them with perfect, profound wisdom.

    At long last—

    "Your brilliant answers immensely please me, wise mortal," the Yaksha finally announced. "Exactly one of your four dead brothers can now magically come back to life. Tell me, exactly which one do you actively want?"

    "Nakula, my lord," Yudhishthira answered simply.

    The powerful Yaksha was completely surprised. "Why Nakula? Why completely not the incredibly mighty Bhima or the brilliantly valiant Arjuna? Surely exactly one of them would be infinitely more practically useful to you when you eventually try to forcefully regain your lost kingdom from your enemies!"

    The Yaksha leaned in closely. "Besides, Bhima and Arjuna are your very own full blood brothers. Nakula is exactly only your younger step-brother. Why on earth do you strangely choose him?"

    Yudhishthira looked deeply at his dead brothers. "My great father proudly had exactly two wives—Kunti and Madri," he explained softly. "I, a proud son of Kunti, am still fully alive. It is only perfectly fair and just that exactly let one son of Madri too live. Lord, please let Nakula come back to life."

    The Yaksha's fierce face completely softened into a massive, warm smile.

    "I am incredibly deeply pleased with your absolute, perfect righteousness," the Yaksha boomed happily. "Because of your pure justice, absolutely all four of your brave brothers shall miraculously come back to life!"

    And lo! They magically did! Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva all gasped and slowly sat up, completely unharmed.

    Yudhishthira bowed low. "My great Lord, you absolutely cannot possibly be a mere Yaksha. You definitively must be a powerful Deva (Deity). Please mercifully reveal yourself to us."

    The very next moment the terrifying Yaksha vanished into thin air, and securely in his exact place proudly stood the magnificent Lord Yama, the powerful Lord of Death and Justice.

    "I am Yama," the glowing god announced warmly. "I simply wanted to strictly test your famous wisdom and justice. So, securely in the magical form of a swift deer, I playfully took away the poor Brahman's holy sticks just to deliberately draw you all to this specific spot."

    Lord Yama then generously gave Yudhishthira the completely undamaged Shami sticks.

    "You will absolutely complete the final, remaining year of your difficult exile completely successfully," Yama blessed him. "Because you have immensely pleased me, you may now freely ask for one final, personal boon."

    Yudhishthira folded his hands in deep prayer. "Lord, may I definitely never secretly succumb to terrible lust, dark avarice, and violent anger. May I absolutely ever be deeply devoted to pure virtue and absolute righteousness."

    Lord Yama smiled warmly, placing a glowing hand on Yudhishthira's head. "My dear child, you will perfectly ever be exactly what you currently are: the absolute, living embodiment of perfect Dharma (Righteousness)."

    And with a massive flash of golden light, Lord Yama vanished completely.

    The refreshed Pandavas happily took the precious Shami sticks and proudly returned them directly to the anxious Brahman in the forest.

    The old man wept tears of pure joy. "I shall be absolutely ever deeply grateful to you, O noble Yudhishthira!"

    Tales of Yuddhistra - 3. The Last Companion

    When the painful thirteen years of their forest exile were finally completely over, wise Yudhishthira desperately tried for a peaceful settlement with the Kauravas. But the arrogant Duryodhana replied only with a violent cry for war. Many were the brave, legendary warriors that were tragically slain on absolutely either side, before the Pandavas finally, bloodily won the great war.

    In spite of the massive victory, Yudhishthira was incredibly sad.

    "The vast kingdom is finally completely ours," he wept, looking at the battlefield, "but at exactly what terrible cost! Almost absolutely all our dear kinsmen and teachers are brutally slain. I shall sadly renounce the violent world entirely and simply become a peaceful ascetic."

    The wise court elders, however, strongly persuaded Yudhishthira that he had honestly only faithfully fulfilled his sacred duty. So he finally reluctantly agreed to proudly ascend the throne. Needless to cleanly say, he naturally made a completely perfect, just king.

    "Our noble king is absolutely just, incredibly generous and deeply compassionate," the happy citizens cheered. "May God safely bless him!"

    Several peaceful years happily went by. Then one quiet day, the wandering sage Narada sadly called on Yudhishthira.

    "Holy one," Yudhishthira asked eagerly. "In the long course of your vast wanderings in the deep forest, did you possibly come across my beloved mother, Kunti, and my blind uncle and his wife?"

    Narada lowered his head. "I sadly did. O King, I have incredibly bad news specifically for you. They were tragically caught and died in a massive forest fire."

    Yudhishthira was devastated. And he had hardly recovered from his terrible grief when a frantic messenger abruptly arrived from the city of Dwaraka.

    "My lord!" the messenger gasped. "The mighty Yadavas are absolutely all dead! They foolishly killed one another in a massive, drunken brawl! And Lord Krishna, too, has tragically left this mortal world."

    "Krishna entirely gone!" Arjuna wept.

    "No!" Yudhishthira cried.

    He looked at his grieving brothers. "I truly feel the sad time has finally come for us to completely leave this tragic earth and peacefully seek heaven."

    "We entirely feel exactly the same," Arjuna nodded firmly.

    So Yudhishthira proudly installed young Pareekshit, the brave grandson of Arjuna, securely on the royal throne... and quietly led Draupadi and his four brothers completely out of Hastinapura forever. As the weeping citizens stood outside the city, sadly watching their beloved king and the brave Pandavas depart, a small, dirty stray dog quietly appeared on the scene.

    It faithfully followed the little group on their extremely long, arduous trek to heaven.

    Having safely crossed the freezing Mount Himavat, they tirelessly walked completely through the great, hot desert completely beyond it... till they finally reached the snowy foot of the massive Mount Meru.

    They began to slowly climb the freezing mountain. Their incredible aim was to physically enter heaven securely in their own human form—an impossible aim perfectly only absolute, completely perfect beings could ever aspire to.

    Suddenly—Draupadi slipped and fell heavily to the ground, completely dead.

    Bhima was absolutely shocked. "O King, exactly why has Draupadi, the pure and sinless, tragically fallen?"

    Yudhishthira answered sadly without even turning back to look. "She secretly was far too partial to Arjuna."

    They kept climbing. Sahadeva was the very next to slowly fall and die.

    "He proudly lacked true humility," Yudhishthira explained quietly.

    When Nakula followed Sahadeva and died in the snow...

    "He was far too proud of his incredibly handsome features," Yudhishthira sighed.

    As Yudhishthira proceeded, followed only by Bhima and Arjuna, the little dog still faithfully continued to quietly keep them company.

    After they had painfully covered some more freezing distance... Arjuna collapsed and died.

    "Arjuna!" Bhima cried.

    "His legendary valour unfortunately made him far too vain," Yudhishthira said, a tear freezing on his cheek.

    Suddenly, Bhima too cried out and stumbled. "Wait! I am heavily falling. Where exactly did I fail?"

    "You were too incredibly greedy," Yudhishthira answered without stopping. "You proudly ate immoderately and boasted of your massive strength."

    As Yudhishthira trekked completely on alone—except, of course, for the faithful little dog—he suddenly saw a glowing, golden chariot descending securely from the sky.

    "Come, noble Yudhishthira," Indra smiled warmly. "I'll safely take you directly to heaven. Ascend this golden chariot."

    "I absolutely do not happily wish to possibly go to heaven completely without my brothers and our beloved wife," Yudhishthira firmly refused.

    Yudhishthira nodded, but pointed down. "This faithful little dog, too, will go securely with me."

    "No! No!" Indra laughed in absolute shock. "There is absolutely no place whatsoever in clean heaven for a dirty dog."

    "No place for this incredibly faithful dog?" Yudhishthira frowned. "Then I too completely have absolutely no place there."

    "Will you foolishly lose eternal heaven simply for the silly sake of an animal?" Indra asked sternly. "Forsake the dirty dog."

    "But you just did completely abandon your own dead brothers and Draupadi!" Indra argued.

    "They were completely dead," Yudhishthira explained logically. "I could do perfectly nothing more for them. But this little dog is fully alive. He has faithfully kept me quiet company. He completely depends on me. I absolutely won't abandon him."

    "It was actually I, Yama, who faithfully followed you specifically in the humble form of a simple dog," the mighty Lord of Death and Justice smiled proudly. "O my noble son, you have pure compassion for absolutely all living creatures. That is exactly why you and absolutely you alone may proudly enter heaven securely in your human form."

    THE FINAL TEST

    When Yudhishthira finally entered bright heaven, he was warmly received by all the glowing gods and wise sages. But he was completely surprised and disgusted to see Duryodhana proudly sitting there.

    "This is absolutely outrageous!" Yudhishthira yelled angrily. "Duryodhana, happily here! Duryodhana, who was completely responsible for the terrible war—for the absolute destruction of millions of men and all morality! I will absolutely not stay happily here with him."

    Sage Narada quickly stepped forward and tried gently to pacify him. "This is pure heaven, Yudhishthira. There can definitely be absolutely no earthly ill-will here. Duryodhana's brave, heroic death on the battlefield has fairly won him a proud place in heaven."

    "If beautiful heaven is the generous reward for the utterly unrighteous Duryodhana," Yudhishthira spat angrily, "I would definitely like to know exactly what infinitely higher, more beautiful world has been attained by my perfectly righteous brothers!"

    He looked desperately around the golden halls. "Where exactly are my beloved brothers? And Draupadi? Where is the noble Karna? Take me to my brothers immediately."

    Indra turned to Yudhishthira calmly. "Absolutely no one can forcefully force you to happily stay here if you completely don't warmly want to."

    He quietly called a grim messenger. "Take the righteous Yudhishthira safely to his friends and relatives."

    Yudhishthira closely followed the quiet celestial messenger. As they walked silently on, the gentle, bright glow of heaven gradually faded away completely... and they were soon entirely enveloped in freezing, blinding darkness.

    "Where exactly are we going?" Yudhishthira asked nervously.

    "Directly to your brothers," the messenger replied coldly.

    With exactly each dark step they took, the infinitely more completely revolting became the terrible scenes that met their horrified eyes. There were bloody skulls, screaming vultures, and rivers of boiling blood.

    As they bravely went on, a terribly foul stench almost completely suffocated Yudhishthira.

    "Where exactly are we?" Yudhishthira gagged. "Where exactly are my brothers? When will we thankfully leave completely behind this terrible, unpleasant path and happily meet them?"

    The celestial messenger stopped abruptly. "I was strictly told to safely lead you exactly this far and then securely stop. If you have confidently had completely enough of this foul place, you may safely return directly with me back to bright heaven."

    Completely disgusted by the foul smell and terrible sights, Yudhishthira happily began to quickly retrace his eager steps.

    Suddenly—

    "Wait! Please don't leave us!" a weak, agonizing voice cried out from the fiery darkness.

    "Your pure, holy presence confidently brings us cool comfort," another voice begged.

    "As long as you are safely here, we absolutely do not painfully feel the terrible tortures inflicted upon us," a third wept. "Please do not happily go back."

    Yudhishthira stopped, his heart breaking. "Exactly who are you?" he called out into the dark flames.

    "I'm Bhima," a weak voice cried. "I'm Nakula." "I'm Sahadeva." "I'm Arjuna." "I'm Karna." "I'm Draupadi."

    Yudhishthira was completely bewildered. "Am I deeply asleep or fully awake? Or have I completely gone mad?" he whispered.

    Then he became absolutely, furiously angry.

    "The evil Duryodhana happily in bright heaven! And my completely righteous brothers burning in terrible hell!" Yudhishthira roared furiously at the dark sky. "Are the mighty gods completely blind? Is pure Dharma completely dead?"

    He turned fiercely to the waiting messenger. "I am absolutely not going completely back with you. Loudly tell Indra that I will firmly stay exactly here, as my simple presence brings some small comfort to my dear ones."

    The very next moment, the terrible flames vanished, and Yudhishthira was suddenly bathed completely in warm, divine light!

    Indra and all the other shining deities proudly appeared before him.

    "My brave child," Indra smiled warmly. "For the exact one single lie you ever spoke to safely hasten Drona's end, you were briefly exposed to terrifying hell for exactly a single second. Your brave brothers had to safely suffer hell for a very short duration, simply for the minor human faults you yourself were already aware of. They are all safely now happily in heaven."

    "Those whose great merits far exceed their small sins, always safely experience hell completely first for a very short while to clear their debts, and then go happily to eternal heaven," Indra explained gently.

    Then Lord Yama proudly addressed Yudhishthira.

    "My brave child, this terrifying illusion was completely your third and final great test. You would heroically rather stay eternally in terrible hell for the pure sake of your brothers than live selfishly in heaven. You are deeply compassionate, absolutely righteous, and completely sinless."

    Yudhishthira bowed deeply, tears of pure joy in his eyes, to Lord Yama. "Father, it was exactly you who generously gave me the great strength to always bravely tread the completely righteous path. I humbly bow to you."

    He then happily took a refreshing, holy dip in the cool, celestial Ganga river and successfully assumed a glowing, divine form.

    He was then proudly escorted by Indra and Yama directly into the highest heaven, where he was finally happily reunited completely with all his beloved brothers and Draupadi forever.
