# Sanjaya's Mission

Following the joyous wedding celebrations of Abhimanyu and Princess Uttaraa, the illustrious descendants of the Kuru race and their allies assembled at the court of King Virata in Upaplavya. The assembly included the five Pandavas, King Virata, King Drupada, Lord Krishna, Balarama, Satyaki, and other great heroes. 

Krishna addressed the assembly, laying out the situation clearly. "You are all aware of how Shakuni deceitfully defeated Yudhishthira in the game of dice and how Yudhishthira lost his kingdom. The valorous Pandavas have completed their thirteen years in exile. Now, what is rightfully theirs should be restored."

Krishna proposed a peaceful resolution. "I suggest we send a wise and virtuous man as our ambassador to persuade Duryodhana to give half his kingdom back to Yudhishthira."

Balarama supported Krishna's proposal, urging conciliation rather than war, which angered the fiery Yadava warrior Satyaki. "How can you blame the virtuous Yudhishthira?" Satyaki roared. "We know he was cheated at dice! The Kauravas should give them what is due to them by right. And if they do not, we must bring destruction upon them all. Satyaki, Bheema, Arjuna, and Krishna make up an invincible team."

King Drupada of Panchala agreed with Satyaki's call to prepare for war, but conceded to Krishna's diplomatic approach. "Duryodhana will never willingly give up the kingdom. If we go to him as friends, the fool will think we are afraid. Let us prepare for war and send messengers to all our allies. In the meantime, I will send my learned priest to Dhritarashtra for a peaceful understanding."

Drupada's priest traveled to Hastinapura and addressed the Kaurava elders—Dhritarashtra, Bheeshma, Drona, and Vidura. "The Pandavas now demand their rightful share," the priest declared. "They hate to go to war with their kin. However, if they are denied their right and the Kauravas are in a mood to fight, Yudhishthira is prepared to take up the challenge."

Bheeshma welcomed the message. "You speak the truth, O Brahmana. The Pandavas have completed their stipulated period of exile and they may now live as dependants of Duryodhana."

But the arrogant Karna angrily interrupted the patriarch. "O Brahmana, what is the sense in repeating the same thing over and over again? Yudhishthira lost to Shakuni and had to go into exile. The Pandavas have not completed their stipulated period of stay in the forest! They must go back. Virtuous Duryodhana will not yield even a single piece of land out of fear."

Bheeshma sharply rebuked Karna for his harsh words and arrogance. Dhritarashtra, wishing to avoid an immediate confrontation, dismissed Drupada's priest with a diplomatic evasion. "I will send Sanjaya as my ambassador to convey my decision to Yudhishthira."

Sanjaya, Dhritarashtra's trusted charioteer and advisor, traveled to the Pandava camp at Upaplavya. He was given a warm welcome by Yudhishthira. 

"King Dhritarashtra has inquired after your welfare," Sanjaya began, offering pleasantries. But his core message was a plea for unilateral peace from the Pandavas, without offering any concrete return of their kingdom. "Dhritarashtra is keen that there is peace. I hope that his proposal appeals to you and that matters are settled amicably."

Yudhishthira saw through the empty words. "O Sanjaya, nobody can foretell the future. You are noble and virtuous. Yet you had to suffer greatly. But now if you wish, you are capable of establishing peace. What do you say, O Krishna? Am I right in my interpretation of Dharma?"

Krishna spoke forcefully, exposing the hypocrisy of the Kauravas. "They wish to seize and keep the kingdom rightfully inherited by the Pandavas. Theft is despicable whether it has been committed openly or otherwise. O Sanjaya, tell me how the Kauravas are different from thieves. Duryodhana wants to keep what is rightfully Yudhishthira's. Is it according to true Dharma?"

Krishna continued, his voice filled with righteous anger. "Memories of how Draupadi was molested and disgraced, and the harsh and bitter words spoken during the game of dice still rankle in the breasts of the Pandavas. The Pandavas and their allies are prepared for war if that is what Duryodhana wants. But that will be the end of the Kauravas."

Having delivered Krishna and Yudhishthira's firm but peaceful demands, Sanjaya returned to Hastinapura. He went straight to Dhritarashtra's chambers.

"O King," Sanjaya said bluntly, "I have never found a trace of falsehood in the Pandavas. Nobody can think of them with hatred... except this vile and dull-headed Duryodhana and the mean-minded Karna. It is childish of Duryodhana to think that he can rob the Pandavas of their rightful share of the kingdom while they are alive. It would be far wiser to give them what is justly theirs before being threatened by war."

"I am certain," Sanjaya warned ominously, "the Pandavas led by invincible Krishna will rout my legions. O King, in spite of being able to prevent this injustice, you did not restrain your son, out of blind love for him. Now go back to Hastinapura... I will pay a visit to Yudhishthira and then return to your capital."

Thus, Sanjaya left the blind king to ponder the inevitable ruin his son's greed was about to bring upon their entire race.


# Duryodhana Refuses to Yield

In Hastinapura, King Dhritarashtra spent a restless and tormented night after hearing Sanjaya's harsh but truthful words. Seeking solace and wisdom, he summoned his righteous brother, Vidura. 

Vidura spoke plainly to the blind king. "Restore to the Pandavas the kingdom originally given to them by you. Then alone can you avert a loss of reputation, dispute within the family, and complete subversion of righteousness. Otherwise, utter ruin will destroy the family of the Kurus."

"Your words allay my anxiety," Dhritarashtra sighed. "Please continue."

"If it is true knowledge and wisdom you desire to imbibe, then, O King, let me invoke the ancient and immortal Rishi, Sanat-Sujata," Vidura offered. Vidura mediated deeply, and the great soul Sanat-Sujata appeared, imparting profound spiritual wisdom to the troubled king. 

The next morning, in the grand court of Hastinapura, Sanjaya formally delivered the message from the Pandavas to the assembled Kauravas. 

"Listen, O King, Princes, and great warriors, to what Yudhishthira, Arjuna, and the other Pandavas had to say to me," Sanjaya announced. "Yudhishthira said: 'Tell him we desire peace but he must give us each a province... otherwise we will have no option but to fight.'"

Sanjaya recounted Arjuna's fierce warning. "Arjuna said: 'If Dhritarashtra's son does not surrender half the kingdom to Yudhishthira... then he will reap the fruits of his sins. If he desires battle with us, he shall have it.'"

Bheeshma, the wise patriarch, urged peace. "O Dhritarashtra, listen to this boastful wretch Karna. He is not equal even to a sixteenth part of the noble Pandavas. O King, the responsibility for the present calamity lies squarely on this low-born one."

Karna retaliated angrily. "It is unbecoming of you, O blessed grandsire, to talk in this manner. I have faithfully followed the Dharma of the warrior. How have I misbehaved that you should so rebuke me?" Blazing with wrath, Karna stormed out of the court. 

"The foolish Duryodhana, aided by Shakuni and with the approval of Karna and Duhshasana, wants to rule over us," Bheeshma warned. "How can the son of a charioteer undertake such a heavy responsibility? All he can do is boast."

Duryodhana, however, was completely unmoved. "O King, do not have any anxiety about us," he told his father confidently. "We are strong enough to vanquish the Pandavas in battle. I am the undisputed sovereign of all earth. Yudhishthira in his fright is begging for five villages. I will not surrender to the Pandavas even so much land as covered by the sharp point of a needle!"

Seeing that reasoning with Duryodhana was impossible, Dhritarashtra realized that war was inevitable. 

Both sides began mobilizing their forces. The numbers in the Kaurava camp swelled immensely as kings from all over India—Bhagadatta, Bhurishravas, Kritavarman, Shalya, and armies from Kamboja, Kekaya, Madra, Sindhu, and Avanti—rallied to Duryodhana's aid. 

Meanwhile, at Upaplavya, the Pandavas were deeply anxious. Yudhishthira, ever seeking peace, spoke to Lord Krishna. "O Krishna, you have heard what Sanjaya had to say. King Dhritarashtra seeks to make peace without giving us our share of the kingdom. As war would lead to bloodshed, we asked only for five villages. Even those, the wicked Duryodhana will not part with. O Krishna, is it unlikely that Duryodhana will listen to your peace proposal?"

"I will make an effort for peace," Krishna replied. "But if failing that, I must think of how the evil Duryodhana must be punished."

Draupadi, unable to contain her enduring trauma and fury, spoke up. "O Krishna, you know by what deceit my virtuous husbands were deprived of the kingdom. Dragged into the assembly, I was treated as a slave by the wicked sons of Dhritarashtra."

Her voice quivered, and she hid her face in Bheema's shoulder, weeping bitterly. "O Krishna, the Kauravas must be made to pay for their sins. I shall know no peace until they have all been slain. These are the tresses of hair on which the vile Duhshasana laid his evil hands. If my husbands are keen on peace, then my five sons led by the redoubtable Abhimanyu will cut the Kauravas into pieces!"

Krishna comforted her with a solemn promise. "O Draupadi, soon you will see the Kaurava women crying as you do now. With Bheema and Arjuna resolved on fighting, not a single Kaurava prince will be left alive. I promise you this: you will become the Queen of Kings."

The next day dawned bright, clear, and auspicious. Rising early, Krishna worshipped the sun and the holy fire. 

"Let my chariot be prepared for the journey," Krishna told his charioteer, Satyaki. "And let all my weapons be placed on it. I do not trust Duryodhana or his brothers."

And so, taking leave of the Pandavas, Lord Krishna set out for the court of Dhritarashtra at Hastinapura, undertaking one final, historic mission to secure peace—or declare a righteous war.
