# Bikal the Terrible and The Tiger-Tamers

*These stories are adapted from two popular tribal tales of Madhya Pradesh.*

## 1. Bikal the Terrible

A tiger often wandered into a village near the forest where he lived. One night, as he was prowling outside a merchant's house, he heard voices inside. 
"I am going out, father," the merchant's son said. 
"At this hour? Aren't you afraid of Bikal?" the merchant warned. (A word used by the tribals for that which is to be feared, in this case, the prowling tiger). "With Bikal around, even the bravest of men think twice before venturing out in the night."

The tiger, not knowing the word referred to himself, was startled. "Who is this Bikal?" he thought. As he walked away, he began to imagine all sorts of terrifying, monstrous creatures. 
Frightened of this unknown 'Bikal', the tiger decided, "I'd better hide somewhere for the night... but where? Ah, that shed!" He sneaked into a dark shed and fell fast asleep. 

Later that night, two thieves broke into the merchant's house and came away with two heavy sacks of loot. 
"This sack weighs more than a ton!" one thief grunted. "We've got more than we expected. We should have brought a cart along with us."
"We might find one in that shed," the other suggested. 

They entered the pitch-dark shed. Feeling around, the second thief touched the sleeping tiger. "I think it's a bullock. But there's no cart."
"Never mind. We'll put the sacks on his back," the first thief said. 

While they talked, the tiger was in the midst of a terrible nightmare, dreaming that a monstrous 'Bikal' was attacking him. Just as the dream monster pounced, one of the thieves grabbed the tiger's tail to steady him! 
"Help! Bikal has caught me!" the tiger panicked in his sleep. As the thieves loaded the heavy sacks of loot onto his back, the tiger woke up but was too terrified to move. "No ordinary man would dare treat me so. My worst fears have come true! I am in the clutches of the terrible Bikal and his friends! Ooooh! The load is heavy. But I dare not protest."

The thieves, completely unaware they were leading a tiger, walked him out into the night. "We are rich men now," they rejoiced. 
At dawn, there was finally enough light to see. "Let's divide the loot," the first thief said happily, turning around. "F-friends, am I dreaming? N-no... It's a... It's a... TIGER!"
The terrified thieves instantly bolted, abandoning the loot and running for their lives. 

The tiger was deeply embarrassed. "Oh, what a fool I have made of myself! They were just men—ordinary men! Everyone will laugh at me if they come to hear of this."
As he was wandering around feeling ashamed, the heavy sacks on his back got caught tightly between two rocks. "Ooof! Now how do I get out?" 
Just then, a cowherd named Shambhu walked by. "O cowherd... don't run away. I won't eat you," the tiger pleaded. "Please come back. Somebody has tied this sack on my back. Please take it down. I swear I won't harm you."

"It's Bikal!" Shambhu gasped, using the tribal word. 
"The sack is full of money. You can have all of it," the tiger offered. 
Shambhu made the tiger promise not to hurt him and untied the sacks. Puzzled, Shambhu asked, "Tell me, how did this happen?"
"It's all because of that Bikal!" the tiger shuddered. 
"But... but... you *are* a... how could Bikal..."
"Never utter that word again, do you hear? I hate the sound of it. If you do, I'll come and devour you. Now you may go," the tiger warned angrily. 

Shambhu rushed home with the heavy sacks of loot. "What a stroke of luck! I have suddenly become a rich man!" With the wealth, he bought cows and buffaloes, and married Bhagwati, the most intelligent, hard-working girl in the village. 

Months passed. One night, Bhagwati asked, "Shambhu, how did you become so rich?"
"It's a long story, Bhago. There was this Bikal..." Shambhu began. 
Just as Shambhu finished narrating the secret story, the tiger happened to be strolling by their window and overheard them. 

"Grr-rrr! He has uttered the forbidden word," the tiger thought furiously. "I won't spare him now."
"Shambhu, I feel like going to my parent's house," Bhagwati said. "It's the cowherd who helped me! I'll go and meet him."
"Let's go tomorrow morning, Bhago," Shambhu replied, falling asleep. 

Later that night, the tiger crept into the house, stealthily lifted the sleeping Shambhu, and carried him away to the forest, throwing him down with a thud. 
"Ow! Where am I? What happened?" Shambhu cried. 
"Oh! It's you... Yes, you broke your promise, so I am going to eat you," the tiger growled. 
Thinking quickly, Shambhu pleaded, "Wait! Eat me by all means, but in the morning. My flesh is tough now because of the cold, but it will become tender and warm with the morning sun."
"Just think it over!" Shambhu added. "To me, it's all the same whether you eat me now or a few hours later. But to you, it would mean a tastier meal."
"All right, we'll wait," the greedy tiger agreed. 

Meanwhile, Bhagwati woke up and discovered her husband missing. Seeing tiger paw prints outside, she realized what had happened. "If a tiger has carried him away, I'll have to act quickly to save him," she thought. She grabbed a heavy wooden cudgel, a drum, and some anklets with bells. 

In the forest, as the sun began to rise, the waiting tiger heard a weird, terrifying sound approaching. 
*Thak Chhannan Chhann Dham! Choom Choom Choom Choom!*
"Wh... what's that?" Shambhu asked, recognizing the sound of his wife's anklets and drum. "Why don't you answer? Is it... is it... Bikal?" he taunted, loudly enough for his wife to hear. 
"Yes, it is Bikal!" Shambhu yelled. 

Hidden in the bushes, Bhagwati heard him. "That was my husband's voice! He's alive! And the tiger is with him too. The tiger thinks *I* am Bikal, and he's terrified! Good! I'll play upon that!"
She roared in a deep, scary voice, "Who's there? Are there any tigers around for me to eat? Aha! I can smell a tiger!"
The tiger was trembling with terror. "Please help me. If you do, I won't eat you," he begged Shambhu. 
"Well... I'll try. Just lie down and keep still," Shambhu whispered. 

Bhagwati stepped into the clearing, banging her drum. "Speak up! Have you seen any tigers which I can eat?" she bellowed at Shambhu. 
"No, there aren't any here. Go away!" Shambhu replied. 
"Then what's that next to you?" she demanded, pointing her cudgel at the cowering tiger. 
"This? Oh—it's just a pile of dirty clothes."
"Really? I don't believe you! Hit the pile with a rock," she ordered. 

Shambhu hesitated. "Friend, do hit me... You must do what Bikal says in order to save me. But hit me gently," the tiger whispered frantically. 
Shambhu gently tapped the tiger's head with a stone. 
"Hit it harder if it is merely a pile of clothes!" Bhagwati commanded, stepping closer. 
"Yes. Hit a little harder, otherwise..." the tiger urged. 
Shambhu hit him harder. "Harder! Harder!" Bhagwati yelled. 
Shambhu kept hitting the tiger on the head with the rock until the poor beast finally swooned and lost consciousness. 

"No need dear, our friend has swooned," Shambhu laughed. Leaving the badly bruised tiger to his fate, the clever couple happily returned home.

## 2. The Tiger-Tamers

Two mischievous boys once lived in a village. They didn't do a spot of work but spent all their time harassing those who did. They shot arrows at women carrying water pots, shot at chickens, and drove away the villagers' goats just for fun. 

"They are becoming bolder and bolder. Let's catch them and give them a good thrashing. Come on!" the angry villagers finally decided, chasing after them with sticks. 
The boys abandoned the goats and ran for their lives. They outran the villagers and kept running until they reached the deep safety of the jungle. 
"Phew!" the older boy panted, throwing himself on the ground. "It'll take some days for those people to cool down... if at all they do."

They went deeper into the jungle. Suddenly, they stopped dead in their tracks. Seated in the middle of their path was an enormous tiger! 
Before the tiger could react, the younger boy suddenly darted forward, threw himself onto the tiger's back, and yanked the animal's ears hard, pushing his head down. At the exact same time, the older boy grabbed the tiger's tail tightly. 
"Got you!" the older boy shouted confidently. 

The tiger was completely startled out of his wits. "W-who are you? What do you want with me?"
"We are tiger-hunters," the younger boy bluffed bravely. "We need a tiger to pull our plough."
"You use tigers to pull your plough!" the tiger gasped. "There must be some mistake. What you need is a pair of bullocks."
"When you plough with bullocks you get only rice and such crops... but when you plough with tigers you get gold and silver!" the boy lied smoothly. "We have already caught one tiger, you are the second one."
"This is terrible," the tiger thought. 

The older boy pulled out a small pocket mirror and held it in front of the tiger's face. "Let's show him the tiger we've already caught!"
Seeing his own terrifying reflection, the tiger panicked. "If they've caught such a nasty-looking fellow, what chance have I of escaping? If I do escape, I'll have to feed a dozen tigers! Look friends," the tiger pleaded, "if it's gold and silver you are after, I can give you heaps of it. I have killed and eaten several princes and kings in my lifetime, and all their gold is lying around in my cave. I'll bring it for you."
"How do we know that you aren't making it all up just to escape?" the boys demanded. 
"Come with me to my cave if you don't believe me," the tiger offered. 

Trembling with fear, the tiger led the boys to his cave. "This is where I live. Now please let me..." And before the boys could say another word, the tiger bolted! He ran and ran till he had left the cave and the boys far behind. "That was a narrow escape! But for my quick thinking, I would have ended up yoked to a plough for the rest of my life."
Relieved, the tiger stalked and killed a sambhar deer, and then went to invite his tiger friends for a celebratory feast. 

Meanwhile, inside the cave, the boys were amazed. "Necklaces! Armlets! Crowns! He was not exaggerating about the wealth here." They gathered all the ornaments into a large bundle. Not finding the tiger outside, they happily went on their way. 

But soon, they realized they were lost. "Which way we came... What are you staring at?" the older boy asked. 
"Tigers! Ten-twelve-thirteen of them..." the younger boy whispered in horror. "We're done for if they see us! Let's climb up that tree."

The older boy quickly scrambled high up into the branches. But the younger boy was a little slow, and he was still only halfway up the thick trunk when the pack of tigers arrived directly underneath them! "I'd better stay where I am. If I move, it'll attract their attention," the younger boy thought, freezing against the bark. 

"Here is the sambhar, my friends. We'll drag it to that tree and eat it there," the host tiger announced, bringing the carcass right under the boy. 
The tigers made themselves comfortable. "Now before we start, please tell us why you are feasting us," the oldest tiger asked. 
"Let's say, it's to celebrate my narrow escape," the host tiger boasted. "This afternoon I was seized by two monsters. Such fiendish creatures you could never have seen before! They needed two tigers to pull their plough and they had already caught one. They were all set to take me away too."
"You don't say! How did you escape?" the others asked. 
"I used my brains... I said to them...!!" 

Just at that moment, the younger boy's arms gave out. "Yeoooew!" he yelled as he fell straight down, landing with a heavy *THUMP* right in the middle of the tiger pack!
The host tiger leaped back in shock. 
From high up in the tree, the older boy saw the disaster and instantly shouted down, "Grab the old tiger! Don't let him get away! Grab him! It's them!"
"What!" the host tiger yelled. 
"My friends, it's those two monsters I was telling you about!" he screamed to the pack. 

Shaken to the core by the sudden ambush from the sky and the terrifying yells, the thirteen tigers leaped over each other and fled blindly into the jungle in absolute terror. "Run! Run for your lives!"

The younger boy picked himself up, trembling. "Good thinking, brother. I thought it was the end for me."
"Anyway, while I was up there I saw the route we should take to get back to our village," the older boy said, climbing down. "So let's go back home. Those people are sure to forgive us when they hear about our adventure and see what we have in our bundle!"
