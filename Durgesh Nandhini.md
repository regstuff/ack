# Durgesh Nandini

During the reign of the mighty Mughal Emperor Akbar, the Pathan ruler of Orissa, Katlu Khan, marched his army to capture the province of Bengal. To halt the rebellion, the Emperor sent his trusted Rajput general, Raja Man Singh. 

Late one summer afternoon, Raja Man Singh’s son, the brave Rajput Prince Jagat Singh, was riding ahead to scout the terrain near the town of Mandaran. Suddenly, the sky darkened, and a fierce thunderstorm broke out. Seeking refuge from the torrential rain, Jagat Singh spurred his horse toward an isolated stone temple.

Inside the temple, he found two beautiful women who had also sought shelter. Startled by his sudden appearance, they drew back in fear. 
"Please, don't be afraid!" Jagat Singh reassured them gently. "As long as I am here you are safe. I am a Rajput."

As he spoke, he found himself utterly mesmerized by the younger of the two women. Her beauty was so profound that he wondered if she were an angel or a goddess. The storm eventually abated, and Jagat Singh courteously escorted the women back to their home. When the older woman, Vimla, asked whom she should thank, he proudly revealed his identity as Prince Jagat Singh of Amber. Before parting, Vimla hinted that they might meet again in fifteen days.

The young maiden was Tilottama, the beloved daughter of Raja Virendra Singh, the master of the Garh Mandaran fort. Because of her noble birth, she was affectionately known as 'Durgesh Nandini'—the daughter of the lord of the fort. Tilottama, too, had instantly fallen deeply in love with the handsome Rajput prince.

Meanwhile, Jagat Singh returned to his father's camp at Burdwan. He proposed a bold plan to drive Katlu Khan out of Bengal using a small strike force stationed at Garh Mandaran. However, there was a bitter complication: Raja Virendra Singh of Garh Mandaran harbored a deep-seated hatred for Raja Man Singh and stubbornly refused to ally with the Mughals, insisting on remaining neutral. 

Ignoring the political tensions, Jagat Singh secretly met Vimla at the temple a week later. Desperate to see Tilottama again, he convinced Vimla to sneak him into the heavily guarded Garh Mandaran fort at midnight. 

That night, as Jagat Singh scaled a tree to enter the fort, he spotted shadows moving in the dark. Katlu Khan's nephew, Usman Khan, was secretly infiltrating the fort with his Pathan troops! Furious at Raja Virendra Singh's refusal to join the Pathan rebellion, Katlu Khan had ordered a surprise attack.

Unaware of the impending doom, Vimla led Jagat Singh to Tilottama's chamber. The two young lovers finally met, but their joyful reunion was violently interrupted by the clash of swords and battle cries. Usman Khan’s troops had stormed the fort!

Jagat Singh drew his sword and fought like a lion, cutting down several Pathan soldiers, but he was eventually overpowered and gravely wounded. Raja Virendra Singh was also captured. The Pathans seized the fort, taking the Raja, Vimla, Tilottama, and the bleeding Jagat Singh as prisoners to a nearby stronghold.

Tragedy struck a few days later. Despite Vimla's tearful pleas for mercy, the ruthless Katlu Khan ordered Raja Virendra Singh to be beheaded. As the executioner's axe fell, Vimla stood in stony silence, secretly vowing, "I will avenge your death, my lord." 

Later, safely hidden in their cell, Vimla made a shocking revelation to a grieving Tilottama. "Raja Virendra Singh was not just my master," Vimla confessed. "He was my husband. We were married in secret... which makes me your stepmother, my poor child."

Meanwhile, Jagat Singh had been rescued from the brink of death, nursed back to health by the tender and devoted care of Ayesha, Katlu Khan's own daughter. Ayesha had fallen deeply in love with the brave Rajput prince. Her cousin, Usman Khan, offered Jagat Singh his freedom and a peace treaty if the Mughals agreed to leave Orissa to the Pathans. 
"I am a Rajput," Jagat Singh refused fiercely. "I cannot ask my father to betray the Emperor." Consequently, he was thrown into a prison cell.

As Katlu Khan's birthday approached, Vimla finalized her plan for vengeance. Usman Khan, who secretly sympathized with Vimla, handed her a special ring to help her escape the fortress during the birthday festivities. Vimla gave the ring to Tilottama, instructing her to wait outside the zenana (women's quarters) at midnight to be escorted to safety. 

That night, amidst the drunken revelry of the birthday celebrations, Vimla dressed in mesmerizing finery and managed to gain access to Katlu Khan's private bedchamber. Striking with the fury of a grieving widow, she assassinated the Pathan ruler in his own bed! 

Panic erupted in the fortress. Guards rushed toward the bedchamber, carelessly leaving the main gates open, allowing Vimla to escape into the night. 

As Katlu Khan lay dying from Vimla's fatal strike, he realized the futility of his rebellion. He urgently summoned Jagat Singh from his cell. With his final breaths, the dying Nawab begged for peace. "Please... forget and forgive the past. I will request Emperor Akbar to leave Orissa to you... My sons... promise me a treaty," he gasped, before closing his eyes forever.

With the rebellion's leader dead, a peaceful agreement was swiftly reached between the Pathans and the Mughals. Raja Man Singh accepted the treaty on behalf of Emperor Akbar. 

In the joyous aftermath of peace, Jagat Singh and Tilottama were finally united in a grand royal wedding. Ayesha, hiding her own broken heart with immense grace and dignity, attended the wedding and presented Tilottama with a casket of priceless jewelry as a bridal gift. 

As Ayesha left Garh Mandaran to return to her homeland, she stood on the banks of a gently flowing river. She took out a poison-filled ring she had kept for herself, realizing she no longer needed it. She had found a quiet, profound peace in seeing the man she loved live happily with his chosen bride. Tossing the ring into the water, Ayesha walked away, leaving the legacy of Durgesh Nandini—the brave daughter of the fort—to live on in history.
