# Ashwini Kumars: Tales of Devotion from the Vedas

## 1. Bhujyu

Ships laden with riches were sailing back to Sindhu, the land of Raja Tugra. The merchants on board were quite pleased with themselves. 
"We have done better on this voyage than we did ever before," one boasted. 
"Our raja will be pleased with the money the royal treasury will get as its share. I am impatient to land," another said. 
"Patience, my friend. Since we are taking the route through the strait, we should get home faster than usual," the captain reassured them.

However, meanwhile, at a rocky hideout on an island by the strait, a gang of ruthless pirates lay in wait. The pirate chief watched from a high cliff as the seven merchant ships approached. "Let them come nearer... all seven of them!" he smirked. "Now attack!"
The pirates ambushed the merchants, capturing the ships. "Take everything but spare our lives," the terrified merchants begged. The pirate chief commanded his men to transfer all the cargo to their own ships. 

A few days later, the ruined merchants stood at the court of Raja Tugra. "We were ambushed by some islanders and robbed of all our cargo. These islanders have become a menace. Unless they are subdued no trade is possible with the outside world, Maharaj."
"As your king it is my duty to make the seas safe for you. And I shall do it," Raja Tugra promised. 

Later, Tugra spoke to his young son, Prince Bhujyu. "Bhujyu, I am going to lead an expedition to the island."
"You? No father. As your son, that should be my privilege," Bhujyu protested. 
"But you are young, inexperienced and unacquainted with the hazards of the sea, my son."
"All the more reason why I should go, father. It would be a good beginning. How else will I gain experience?" 
His mother was fearful. "Oh, no, my dear child! The island chief is wicked, wily, treacherous..."
"I am the son of a king... born to destroy the wicked and protect the virtuous," Bhujyu replied confidently. 
Proud of his son's courage, Tugra agreed. "You make me happy and you make me proud, my son."

Bhujyu's men assembled before him. Before setting sail, Bhujyu made offerings to various deities. "Let us pray to Pushan to show us the right path. This is for Pushan, the path-finder. And this for Indra. And this for the Ashvin twins!"

The great fleet set sail. After a voyage of three days and three nights, they saw seabirds flying overhead. "Birds! We must be nearing the island!" 
But just then, a veteran sailor of a hundred voyages came to Bhujyu. "Prince Bhujyu! I fear we are in for a storm."
"A storm!"
Undeterred, Bhujyu blew a mighty conch, and his soldiers emitted war cries, ready for battle. But the very next moment, a massive tempest hit the fleet. Huge waves battered the ships. 
"Help!" a sailor cried as he fell overboard. Even as Bhujyu tried to pull the man up, a towering wave crashed directly onto their ship. 

Most of the ships sank, and many of the men drowned in the raging sea. Bhujyu, thrown into the churning water, swam with great effort. "A ship! One of our ships! Help! Help!" he shouted. But the frightened sailors on the surviving vessel ignored him. "Keep going. Let us get away from this accursed place," they yelled, abandoning their prince. 

The storm at last subsided, leaving Bhujyu all alone, adrift on the high seas. Exhausted, he thought, "No... I can't keep it up... any longer... O Ashvins, come to my rescue!" Just as he was losing hope, a large wooden log floated by. Bhujyu slumped onto the log of wood and closed his eyes. 

For some time, Bhujyu drifted, clinging to the log. When he opened his eyes, he was horrified to see a shark circling him. "A killer fish!" he panicked. "I have neither the strength... nor the weapons..." Then, he realized he still had his wooden log. As the shark lunged, Bhujyu shoved the thick log straight into the shark's gaping jaws. "Come! Come my friend. Sink your teeth into it. Sink them hard," he grunted as the shark got hopelessly stuck.

But Bhujyu was severely dehydrated. Overcome by thirst, he took a mouthful of salt water, only to spit it out. "Oh, for some water!" he choked. "O Ashvins, if I must die, let it be on a battlefield... with arrows piercing my heart... not of thirst in this watery grave. O Ashvins, please help me! Don't let me die a futile death. O Ashvins, you saved Antaka, Vandana, Rebha, Deerghatamas and many others. Save Bhujyu who worships you."

At that exact moment, not far off, a magnificent divine boat appeared. It was the Ashvin twins! "Can you see that speck on the water? It seems to be a human being," one said. "It must be some hapless, ship-wrecked sailor! Row hard in that direction."

When they pulled Bhujyu aboard, he was unconscious. Upon waking, he looked at his glowing rescuers. "You must be the Ashvins, the mighty heroes, helpers of all. Accept my salutations!" 
The Ashvins smiled and gave Bhujyu some divine honey, milk, and *soma* to restore his strength. 

After three days and three nights, their divine boat reached the shores of the Sindhu land. Hearing of the arrival, Raja Tugra and his queen rushed to the shore. "Bhujyu! Oh, Bhujyu! My son!" his mother wept, embracing him. "O mother, I am safe! Alive!" 
By the time the reunited family looked up to thank the rescuers, the glowing boat with the Ashvin twins had already receded into the horizon. 
"O Ashvins, ever-vigilant helpers of mankind, you should have waited for an offering of *soma* and honey!" Tugra prayed gratefully.

Later, Bhujyu went to his father. "Father, permit me to resume the campaign against the island chief."
"Are you not afraid of the sea?" Tugra asked. 
"No, father. On the contrary, I am more confident. With the Ashvins on my side, I have nothing to fear." 
Tugra blessed him, and Bhujyu set sail with a new fleet. 

A few weeks later, the pirates on the island spotted the incoming ships. "It is very kind of him to send us a fresh supply of riches so soon!" the pirate chief laughed. "Mount an attack at the usual spot!"
But this time, the islanders were in for a surprise. Fully prepared and fearless, Bhujyu's men fiercely overpowered the treacherous pirates, recovered the stolen riches, and returned home in absolute triumph. 
"The seas are now safe for the traders of Tugra," Raja Tugra beamed with pride. "In performing your *dharma*, you have helped me perform mine. I am proud of you, my son."

## 2. Atri

Sage Atri lived with his family in a peaceful hermitage on the outskirts of a dense forest. 
One day, his sons warned him, "Father, I heard the Dasyus (bandits) have moved into the forest. You must not go about in the forest anymore."
"How does that concern me?" Atri smiled calmly. "Why not? Surely the forest is large enough for all of us."
Unconvinced, his worried sons decided to secretly follow him at a distance. 

Atri walked deep into the thickest part of the forest. Finding an immensely tall tree, he wondered, "What a tall tree! I wonder what the earth looks like from above!" 
Atri climbed higher and higher until he reached the top. "What a magnificent view. The earth seems to expand when seen from such a height. I wonder how it would look when seen from the skies." Inspired, he closed his eyes and began to meditate on the idea right there in the branches. 

He meditated until nightfall. When he finally opened his eyes to see the moonlit sky, he breathed, "What a glorious sight!" 
Atri slid down the tree, walked to a nearby stream, had his bath, and sat down to pray to the rising sun. But just as he opened his eyes from prayer, armed Dasyus leaped out from the bushes! "Aa.." Atri gasped as they grabbed him. 

The hidden sons, seeing their father captured, cried out, "Father is in trouble!" and ran as fast as their legs could carry them to help. But the wily Dasyus had set traps. The boys tripped and fell straight into a heavy hunting net. 
"I knew you were anxious to join your father," the cruel Dasyu chief laughed. 

The bandits dragged Atri and his sons deep into the forest to a massive, ancient tree with a deep, hollow trunk. 
"You will now go on a long journey," the Dasyu chief mocked. They callously picked up Atri and threw him headfirst down into the dark, bottomless hollow. They then threw his screaming sons in after him. 

Falling through the pitch-black darkness, Atri wondered, "Is this infinity... or am I in the womb of mother earth?" Finally, he landed softly at the bottom. The next moment, his sons tumbled down beside him. 
"Father!" they cried in panic. "Father, they are going to set this place on fire. I heard them say so. What shall we do?"
"Let us pray to the Ashvins!" Atri said calmly. 

High above, the Dasyus tossed burning torches down into the hollow. Thick smoke and flames began to engulf the trapped family. "Ee-a-ah!" the boys coughed and screamed as the fire spread. 
Standing amidst the flames, Atri raised his hands to the sky. "O Ashvins, save the boys. O Ashvins! Come to our rescue! Now, this very moment! You must come. Now!"

Instantly, the call was heard. The Ashvin twins descended from the heavens in a magnificent, glowing chariot drawn by two flying asses! 
They swooped down over the forest. The Dasyus who dared to put up a fight against the divine twins quickly fell, while the wiser ones fled in sheer terror. 
The Ashvins instantly extinguished the raging fire within the hollow tree and magically lifted Atri and his sons to safety, offering them refreshing water. 

"We salute you, O twins," Atri bowed deeply. His unwavering faith and courage had paid off; thanks to the Ashvins, he had lost neither his freedom nor his life.
