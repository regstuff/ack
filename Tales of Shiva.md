# Tales of Shiva: The Mighty Lord of Kailasa

## 1. Shiva and Arjuna

During their thirteen-year exile, the Pandavas faced many hardships. Knowing that a great war with the Kauravas was inevitable, the Pandava prince Arjuna traveled to Indrakeela, a quiet and peaceful retreat of ascetics in the Himalayas. There, he performed severe penances to propitiate Lord Shiva, hoping to obtain powerful celestial weapons. 

For four months, Arjuna stood in deep meditation, chanting "Om Namo Shivaya." The heat generated by his intense penance was so terrible that it began to choke the entire forest. Unable to bear the heat, the sages of Indrakeela traveled to Mount Kailasa, the abode of Lord Shiva. "Lord, grant Arjuna his wish, and relieve us of this suffering," they pleaded. 
"So be it," Lord Shiva replied.

When the sages departed, Parvati asked, "What does Arjuna want, my lord?"
"He wants celestial weapons," Shiva answered. 
"Can he wield them, my lord?" 
"I will find out by testing him," Shiva smiled. "I'll appear before him as a *Kirata* (hunter) and engage him in a duel."
Parvati and Shiva's *ganas* (attendants) accompanied him, disguised as Kirata women. 

As they approached Indrakeela, Shiva spotted a wild boar running toward Arjuna's hermitage. It was actually the demon Mookasura in disguise! The boar charged into the quiet hermitage, driving the ascetics helter-skelter. Disturbed by the din, Arjuna opened his eyes, raised his bow, and took aim. 
"Stop! It's mine. Leave it to me. I was stalking it," Shiva (disguised as the Kirata) shouted.
"Shoot it and claim it. Hunters do not beg," Arjuna retorted proudly. 

Both archers fired simultaneously. The boar was caught between the two unerring arrows and fell dead, revealing its true demonic form. 
"Your arrow killed it," the Kirata women cheered for Shiva. 
Arjuna was enraged. "It was our chief's arrow that killed the boar," he insisted. "All right, Kirata. We will settle once and for all who is the better shot."

A fierce archery duel began. Arrows whizzed past as the two matched their incredible skills. But after a while, Arjuna realized his quiver was empty, and there wasn't a single scratch on the Kirata! 
"O mighty archer, shall I lend you a few arrows?" the Kirata teased. 
Infuriated, Arjuna tried to strangle the Kirata with his bowstring. In a deft move, the Kirata wrestled the bow from Arjuna and casually threw it away. 

Undaunted, Arjuna drew his heavy sword and rushed at the Kirata. But as he smote the Kirata's head, his sword shattered to pieces! Shorn of his arms, Arjuna continued the fight with uprooted trees, but the Kirata remained completely unscathed. In a desperate final attempt, Arjuna charged with his bare hands. With a simple flick of his wrist, the Kirata lifted Arjuna and flung him to the ground. 

Humbled and exhausted, Arjuna realized he could not win. He immediately made a small *linga* out of clay on the spot, draped it with flowers, and prayed to Lord Shiva for strength. Instantly, a new power surged through his limbs. Rejuvenated, he stood up and challenged his rival. "Kirata, your time is up!"

But as Arjuna raised his fist, he stopped, transfixed. He saw the very same flowers he had just offered to the clay *linga* resting on the Kirata's head! Realizing the truth, Arjuna fell to his knees. "O Lord, pardon me and my vanity."
Shiva and Parvati revealed their true, divine forms. "I am pleased with your devotion and courage," Shiva smiled. "I shall impart to you the secret of the *Pashupata* missile, which will help you in the hour of need."

Shiva's blessing proved crucial. Later, in the great Mahabharata war, it was only with the *Pashupata* weapon that Arjuna was able to defeat his arch-rival, Karna.

## 2. Shiva the Fisherman

Once, in Mount Kailasa, Lord Shiva started expounding the deep mysteries of the Vedas to his wife, Parvati. For years, Shiva continued his discourse without a single break. Gradually, despite her best efforts, Parvati's attention flagged, and her mind began to wander. 

Noticing this, Shiva grew annoyed. "The Vedas are not for you," he snapped. "Since you are no better than a common fisherwoman... may you be born as one!"
Instantly, Parvati vanished from Kailasa. The very next moment, Shiva realized his mistake. "What have I done! In thoughtless haste, have I cast away one whose love for me was peerless."

Shiva's trusted servant, Nandi the bull, noticed his master's deep sorrow. "Now my master will know no peace until mother Parvati returns. I will see to it that he is forced to become a fisherman and go down to earth to claim her," Nandi decided. 

Meanwhile, Parvati was found as an abandoned infant under a tree by the chief of the Paravars, a clan of fishermen on earth. Overjoyed, he named her Parvati and raised her as his own. She grew up to be a beautiful, strong, and clever fisherwoman, helping her father row his boat. 

To reunite the divine couple, Nandi assumed the form of a gigantic, terrifying shark and began terrorizing the Paravar coast. The shark tore their nets, smashed their boats to pieces, and dragged fishermen into the sea. Days turned into weeks, and the shark became an unstoppable menace. 

Desperate to save his people, the chief of the Paravars announced a grand reward: "I will offer the hand of my daughter to the one who captures the shark."
Many brave young men tried, but all failed. Finally, the hopeless fishermen and Parvati prayed to Lord Shiva for divine intervention. "Lord, come to our rescue. Don't fail us in the hour of need."

Hearing their prayers, Lord Shiva disguised himself as a handsome young fisherman and arrived at the coast. Net in hand, Shiva waded into the sea. As soon as he cast his net, the giant shark (Nandi) happily surrendered himself, his mission fulfilled. 

As Shiva dragged the massive shark ashore, the fishermen cheered wildly. "You are our savior!" the chief declared. "By catching the great fish, you have become a great fisherman. Accept my daughter as your wife."
Shiva, the fisherman, happily married Parvati, the fisherwoman. Then, Nandi assumed his true divine form, and the reunited couple returned triumphantly to Kailasa.

## 3. Shiva and Markandeya

Sage Mrikandu and his wife Marudvati were childless. The sage performed severe penances to propitiate Lord Shiva. Pleased, Shiva appeared before him and offered a difficult choice: "Do you want a son who will live long though lacking in virtues, or a son who will be wise and virtuous but will live for only sixteen years?"
"I will have the virtuous son, my lord," Mrikandu answered without hesitation. 

In due course, they were blessed with a beautiful baby boy whom they named Markandeya. The boy was exceptionally brilliant; by the time he was barely sixteen, he had mastered the Vedas. But as his sixteenth birthday approached, his parents grew increasingly depressed, knowing his cruel death was imminent. 

Seeing his mother weeping, Markandeya asked her why. Mrikandu sadly explained the terms of Shiva's boon. "I will be sixteen years old tomorrow," Markandeya realized. But the brave boy did not despair. "Don't cry, mother. I will not die. I will seek immortality through the grace of Shiva, who is the conqueror of death."

Early the next morning, on his sixteenth birthday, Markandeya went to the seashore. He built a Shiva *linga* out of wet sand, adorned it with flowers, and sat down to pray in deep devotion. 

Towards nightfall, the terrifying figure of Yama, the God of Death, suddenly appeared holding a noose. "Get ready to leave this world, foolish boy," Yama commanded. "Death waits for none."
"O lord of death, please wait," Markandeya begged calmly. "I have not yet finished my worship. Please do not obstruct me."
"Fool! Do you hope to escape from me by clinging to Shiva? The grip of death is fatal!" Yama sneered. He callously threw his noose, catching Markandeya's neck, and began to drag the boy away. 

"Lord, protect me!" Markandeya cried out. 
The very next moment, the sand *linga* burst open, and Lord Shiva himself sprang forth in blazing fury! With a mighty kick, Shiva struck Yama square on the chest, knocking the God of Death to the ground. Terrified and humbled, Yama quickly departed without the boy's soul. 

Shiva then turned to the devoted boy. "Markandeya, you will forever be free from death. You will be immortal," the Lord blessed him. 
Markandeya returned home joyfully to his parents. "Mother! The lord did save me!" he smiled, and they lived happily, knowing their brilliant son would live forever.
