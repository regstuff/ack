# Bhagawat: The Krishna Avatar - The Lord of Dwaraka

Shukadeva continued his incredibly detailed narration of the divine exploits of young Krishna to King Pareekshit.

"After finally killing the tyrant Kamsa, Krishna and Balarama went immediately to meet Vasudeva and Devaki in the prison and respectfully bowed deeply before them. However, deeply thinking of their sons as the divine lords of the world who had just effortlessly slain the greatest warriors, Vasudeva and Devaki completely forgot that they were simply their sons and hesitated to embrace them."

"Krishna instantly divined the reason for their unnatural, hesitant behaviour. *They are treating me entirely as God, not as their own son,* he realized. 
Krishna then used his powerful *maya* (illusory power) to temporarily make them completely forgetful of his absolute divinity for a while."

"Dear father, dear mother!" Krishna wept, running to them. "We are the loving sons you were so incredibly anxious to meet. But alas, we had been cruelly separated from you in our infancy and boyhood. We feel we have lived entirely in vain since we have not been actively able to serve you. That cruel Kamsa treated you very badly, yet we could do absolutely nothing to help you all these years."
Overwhelmed completely by Krishna's loving, human words, Vasudeva tearfully clasped Krishna in a fond, tight embrace.

After thoroughly comforting his parents, Krishna went straight to his old grandfather Ugrasena who had also been kept cruelly in prison by Kamsa for many years. 
"You are now the rightful king of the Yadavas again," Krishna told the old man, respectfully placing the crown on his head. "Please treat me simply as one of your loyal subjects."
"Yes," Ugrasena wept. "Now that Kamsa is finally killed we can happily look forward to a safe and joyful life under Krishna's ultimate protection."

Kamsa's cruel, oppressive reign had made many of his terrified subjects flee desperately to the neighbouring kingdoms. Krishna warmly welcomed them all back and generously settled them safely in their own homes. 
"Not only are we safely back home but we have been generously given enough wealth by Krishna to easily start life all over again," the citizens rejoiced.

Then Krishna and Balarama went respectfully to meet their foster father, Nanda. 
"Father, please proceed safely back to Vrindavana with the cowherds. We will definitely come to meet you there as soon as our urgent work here is entirely over."
Nanda hugged the two boys incredibly tenderly and, with a heavy heart, made quick preparations to return home with his cowherds. 

Thereafter, at the formal request of Vasudeva, the sage Gargacharya properly performed the *upanayana* (sacred thread ceremony) of Balarama and Krishna. 
"Now I will secretly tell you the powerful Gayatri mantra in your ear," the sage whispered.

Krishna and Balarama then immediately set out for the distant ashrama of Sage Sandipani in Avanti (modern Ujjayini). With Sage Sandipani as their respected guru, the boys formally began their traditional education. The guru properly taught them the complex vedas. He also expertly taught them how to perfectly wield all weapons and meticulously taught them all the sixty-four arts. 
So incredibly attentive were the boys, that they perfectly learnt each and every subject the very first time it was taught to them. Thus, within exactly sixty-four days, they completely mastered all the sixty-four arts and crafts.

At the successful end of their training, they humbly addressed their guru. "Tradition strictly demands that we offer you a *gurudakshina* (tribute offering made to the teacher), now that we have successfully completed our education. Please tell us what tribute would you like us to generously offer you?"
Guru Sandipani was completely aware of the extraordinary, divine powers of his pupils. After quietly consulting his grieving wife he said, "Please successfully restore to me my young son, who was tragically drowned long ago in the sea at Prabhasa."
"I will," Krishna promised instantly. 

The boys climbed swiftly onto their chariot and went directly to Prabhasa in Gujarat. They sat quietly on the seashore for a while. Assuming the form of a human, the mighty ocean appeared respectfully before Krishna. 
Krishna addressed the god of the ocean thus: "O powerful king of the oceans, return immediately to us the precious son of our guru, whom you had violently swept away from this very spot."

The ocean replied nervously, "O Lord, it was actually not I who deliberately took him away. There is a mighty, wicked asura called Panchajana who dwells deep in my waters in the deceptive guise of a giant conch-shell. It was he who violently took the boy away."
As soon as he heard this, Krishna fearlessly dived deep into the rough sea. 

After some tense moments he came back up. "I thoroughly killed the asura but I could not find the boy anywhere inside him. Here is the massive shell in which the demon lived." This incredibly powerful conch-shell came to be famously known as Panchajanya. 

Krishna and Balarama then went straight to the dark abode of Yama, the dreaded lord of death. Krishna blew powerfully on the Panchajanya to loudly announce his arrival. On hearing the terrifying, earth-shaking sound of the conch-shell, Yama rushed out to respectfully meet them. 
"Welcome to my dark kingdom, O Krishna and Balarama. What can I possibly do for you?"
"My guru's son has been wrongly brought here. Obey my strict command and bring him safely to me immediately."
Soon, the boy was restored to them. "Here he is."

Krishna and Balarama went joyously back to Sandipani's ashram with the boy. "Gurudeva, what else can we possibly do for you?" they asked. 
"No, you have miraculously done all you possibly can," the tearful guru blessed them. "Go now safely to your own homes. May your vast knowledge remain ever fresh."
With the deep blessing of their guru, the two boys returned happily to Mathura. 
"Welcome safely back, O Krishna!" the citizens cheered. "We feel exactly as if we have joyfully regained our lost wealth."

***

O Pareekshit, Uddhava was a very dear friend of Shri Krishna, and a highly respected minister too. One day Krishna bade him to go to Vrindavana. 
"Dear Uddhava, go to my beloved parents Nanda and Yashoda. Tell them I am perfectly well. The sweet gopikas of Vrindavana are very much deeply attached to me. They must be missing me terribly and eagerly awaiting my return. Go and gently console them."
"I will gladly leave at once," Uddhava bowed. 

When Uddhava reached Vrindavana it was late dusk, the exact time for the cows to return from grazing in the woods. The thick dust raised by their many hooves entirely enveloped his chariot. The comforting sound of the calves lowing mingled beautifully with the melodious music of the cowherds' flutes. Warm lamps shone brightly in the simple homes. The evening air was heavily filled with sweet incense. 

Nanda warmly welcomed Uddhava exactly as if he were his own son. After he had been generously fed a truly sumptuous meal and seated comfortably, Nanda asked him eagerly, "Does Krishna ever remember us at all? Does he miss his old parents and his simple friends? Oh! If he would only come just once to meet us. We do so desperately long to behold his beautiful face and his incredibly endearing smile."
Nanda's eyes moistened heavily with unshed tears. "We miss him so unbelievably much. Absolutely everything in Vrindavana fiercely reminds us of him—the fast rivers, the tall mountains, the gentle cows, the young cowherds."
Soon hot tears began to trickle rapidly down Nanda's face. Yashoda too began to cry bitterly. 

Uddhava said gently, "Indeed, you both are truly the absolutely luckiest of people to have such incredibly pure love and devotion towards Krishna. He will surely soon be with you."
Uddhava and Nanda spent the entire long night happily praising Krishna and his amazing deeds. 

Early the next morning the gopikas arose to do their daily chores. When the bright sun rose, they suddenly noticed a spectacular golden chariot standing on Nanda's humble doorstep. 
"Whose incredibly grand chariot could it be?"
"I desperately hope it is not that wicked Akrura who took away our beloved Krishna."
While they were speaking thus, they suddenly saw Uddhava walking at a distance. 
"Look closely at that handsome stranger. He is built and dressed exactly like our beloved Krishna."

The curious gopikas immediately surrounded Uddhava and eagerly plied him with rapid questions. "Where have you come from?" "Why are you here?"
When they excitedly learnt that Uddhava was an official emissary of Krishna, they quickly took him aside. 
"Oh! You have finally come with a message from your Lord," one gopika said bitterly. "But he must have simply sent you to kindly comfort his old parents, for there is absolutely nothing else here worthwhile for a great king like him to remember."

"Only the strong parental bond endures," another wept. "All other relationships are terribly weak and short lived. Fickle citizens eagerly abandon a king who becomes weak. Selfish students casually forsake their dedicated teacher when their studies are completely over. Why, even the birds ungratefully fly away from the tree when its sweet fruits are entirely exhausted."

Then, changing their mood, the gopikas were so thrilled at the sudden arrival of Krishna's messenger that they babbled entirely incoherently. "Tell us how our dear Krishna is! Does he even mention us? Will we ever have the unbelievable good fortune of seeing him again?"

Uddhava was deeply moved by the intense, pure devotion of the women. "You are incredibly fortunate to be so absolutely close to Lord Krishna in your hearts. Your pure devotion can easily serve as an inspiring, perfect example for even the most learned sages. That is exactly why Krishna has specifically sent me here with a message for you."

Uddhava then faithfully related the divine message of Krishna to the anxious, weeping women. 
"You can never really be truly separated from me, for I am completely everywhere. I pervade the entire vast universe in many different forms. I am the ultimate creator, sustainer and destroyer. I am the eternal, immortal soul. Just as all flowing rivers, ultimately reach the single ocean after meandering endlessly on their course, so also all various forms of pure worship finally culminate in the deep desire to attain me. Physical separation from me will only make your pure love more incredibly intense. But I can absolutely never forsake you. Be completely assured."

For many long months Uddhava stayed happily in Vrindavana regaling the eager people with exciting stories of Krishna's many heroic deeds in Mathura. When at last Uddhava was finally about to return to Mathura, the tearful people of Vrindavana generously presented him with many gifts and bade him a deeply tearful farewell. 
"Dear Uddhava, our absolute only desire is to completely dedicate all our thoughts and actions solely to Lord Krishna," they sobbed. 

Uddhava returned to Mathura and faithfully told him what he had heard and seen in Vrindavana. "So incredibly engrossed are the devoted gopikas in you, that they magically see you completely everywhere, in absolutely all objects and beings. People in Vrindavana constantly pine to have a mere glimpse of you."

***

After they had successfully delivered the generous presents sent by Nanda to Vasudeva, Balarama, and King Ugrasena, Krishna suddenly remembered a firm promise made earlier. 
"I specifically told Sairandhri (Trivakra, the hunchback) that I would definitely visit her house after my urgent mission here was completely over. Come Uddhava, let's go right now."

Krishna and Uddhava found Sairandhri's house to be beautifully and heavily adorned with fresh flowers, long strings of white pearls, colorful banners, and bright buntings in eager anticipation. 
"Lord Krishna? At my humble doorstep!" she gasped. 
At first Sairandhri was completely surprised and totally confused by his actual arrival. But soon she came eagerly forward to welcome Krishna. "Welcome, O Lord!" 

After a short while, she beautifully bedecked herself with incredibly fine garments and expensive ornaments and sat happily with her divine guests. 
"Dear Krishna, please stay here securely with me for a few days. I simply cannot bear to let you go now," she pleaded. 
"I absolutely must leave now," Krishna smiled gently. 

Later one day, he unexpectedly visited Akrura with Balarama and Uddhava. Akrura ran eagerly forward to completely welcome them. 
"Welcome! I am incredibly fortunate to have you as my honored guests. You two have been divinely born on earth purely to entirely rid it of terrible wickedness."
Krishna smiled. "You are our dear uncle and strong well-wisher. Indeed you are truly one of our absolutely closest friends. That is exactly why I urgently want you to go to Hastinapura and meet the young Pandavas."
"After the tragic death of their brave father Pandu, young Yudhishthira and his brothers have gone to Hastinapura to live with their blind uncle Dhritarashtra. His eldest son Duryodhana is incredibly wicked."
"Therefore I am deeply anxious about the safety of the Pandavas. I would very much like to ensure their continued happiness and safety."

As requested urgently by Krishna, Akrura went swiftly to Hastinapura and met King Dhritarashtra, the wise Bheeshma, the minister Vidura, and the Pandavas. 
For the next few months, Akrura stayed quietly at Hastinapura to carefully find out exactly how Dhritarashtra was actually treating the Pandavas. 

*Why, the blind Dhritarashtra simply does not dare to do absolutely anything against the terrible wishes of his extremely wicked sons,* Akrura observed. *That vile uncle Shakuni is also a terribly bad influence on the weak old king.*

Akrura spoke secretly to his dear cousin Kunti, the mother of the Pandavas. 
"Yes it is absolutely true that my poor sons are treated terribly badly," Kunti wept. "Duryodhana is incredibly jealous of them and deeply resents their immense popularity with the common people."
Vidura, who was also secretly present, added grimly, "Indeed in sheer valour, pure strength and perfect manners, the Pandavas are far, far superior to the cruel Kaurava brothers. Duryodhana and his wicked brothers are absolutely forever plotting secretly to brutally harm the Pandavas. They even desperately tried to poison them once."

Before finally leaving, Akrura boldly addressed the entire royal court at Hastinapura and strongly conveyed the direct message of Krishna and Balarama. 
"May you greatly enhance the glorious glory of the Kuru clan," Akrura addressed Dhritarashtra. "Strive deeply to keep all your subjects completely happy. Treat absolutely all your kith and kin equally alike. Be strictly impartial in your daily behaviour towards your own sons and the orphaned Pandavas."

King Dhritarashtra replied smoothly. "O Akrura, I definitely know you mean extremely well. But my blind heart is already so incredibly biased completely towards my own sons, that your good, wise counsel unfortunately has absolutely no effect on me. I hear that God himself has been incredibly incarnated in the Yadava dynasty specifically for entirely ridding the world of evil. Let his divine will be completely done."
Akrura returned quickly to Mathura and reported everything accurately to Krishna. "Now that I have closely observed his weak behaviour myself, there is absolutely no doubt that Dhritarashtra entirely favours the wicked Kauravas and the noble Pandavas are completely not treated fairly at all."

***

Meanwhile, Kamsa's two grieving queens, Asti and Prapti, went tearfully back to their powerful father's house after the shocking death of their husband. They bitterly told their father, Jarasandha, the mighty king of Magadha, exactly how Kamsa had been brutally killed by the boy Krishna. 

Jarasandha was absolutely enraged. "So, father," they wept, "after effortlessly killing the mighty, unbeatable wrestlers, Krishna brutally killed our dear husband."
"I will severely punish him!" Jarasandha roared. 
Jarasandha was completely filled with terrible bitterness and deep sorrow. "Let my massive army fully prepare for immediate war. I will absolutely not rest till I completely wipe out the entire, hated race of the Yadavas from the face of the earth."

Soon, with an unbelievably massive army of 23 Akshauhinis (each Akshauhini contains 21,870 elephants, 21,870 chariots, 65,610 cavalry, and 109,350 infantry), Jarasandha completely surrounded the entire city of Mathura, the capital of the Yadavas. 

Krishna beheld the truly awesome, terrifying sight from the high walls. 
"Why, Jarasandha's endless army looks exactly like a veritable, massive ocean of fierce soldiers completely around our city," Krishna observed calmly. 
*Perhaps, it is actually good that Jarasandha has foolishly collected this impossibly large army and conveniently brought them directly to my door. I can completely destroy them all at once and greatly lighten the terrible burden of evil from the earth.*
*But I will absolutely not kill Jarasandha himself yet. Let him furiously collect more of his wicked men to repeatedly attack me in the future. I can completely kill them too and perfectly fulfil the divine purpose of my incarnation.*

While Krishna was deeply engrossed in this thought, two gleaming, magnificent chariots miraculously descended completely from the sky. They were expertly driven by able celestial charioteers and heavily equipped with wonderful, divine weapons of absolutely all description. 
"Behold, dear brother!" Krishna pointed.
The celestial and utterly eternal weapons of Balarama also miraculously appeared that very instance. 
"Not only the divine chariots," Balarama marvelled, "even your favourite, absolute weapons—the mighty plough and the heavy pestle—are perfectly here."

"Go forth bravely on this chariot and completely destroy the enemy," Krishna commanded. "Protect our terrified people from this massive calamity. For we are truly here on earth to completely protect the good and utterly destroy the wicked."
Krishna and Balarama then quickly donned their impenetrable armour and ascended the magnificent celestial chariots. 

There was only a very small Yadava army with them. On the very outskirts of the besieged city, Krishna powerfully blew his massive conch-shell, the Panchajanya. The terrifying, booming sound was loud enough to instantly put absolute fear deeply in the hearts of even the bravest of seasoned warriors in Jarasandha's vast army. 

Jarasandha rode forward and said mockingly to Krishna, "O tiny Krishna, you are a mere lad. I truly feel deeply embarrassed to unfairly fight with you alone."
Then Jarasandha turned arrogantly to Balarama. "Balarama, if you truly believe that a swift death on the bloody battlefield will successfully ensure you a high place in heaven, come bravely fight with me."

Krishna calmly responded, "True warriors absolutely don't brag vainly like you, they simply just act. Go ahead! Your empty words don't bother me at all."

Just as dark clouds temporarily covering the sun or thick smoke enveloping a bright fire cannot possibly do so for very long, so also the enormous, endless army of Jarasandha could absolutely not successfully surround Balarama and Krishna for long. 

The terrified women of Mathura were anxiously watching the terrifying scene from their high balconies and safe terraces. 
"See! Jarasandha's endless soldiers have completely crowded in from absolutely all sides."
"We cannot even get a tiny glimpse of Krishna's small army now."
"Oh! The magnificent chariot of Krishna with the high pennant of Garuda has completely disappeared into the mass of soldiers."
"The tall flag of the palm atop Balarama's chariot is absolutely not seen either."
Fearing the absolute worst, the desperate women began to sob incredibly loudly. 

But down on the battlefield, the deadly arrows of Jarasandha's soldiers were completely falling uselessly on Krishna's army like mere rain-drops during a normal downpour. Krishna now casually picked up his divine bow. 
He instantly began sending massive shower after shower of deadly arrows directly at Jarasandha's army. The unbelievable speed with which Krishna smoothly removed the arrows from his bottomless quiver, effortlessly stringed his bow, and rapidly shot the arrows, was so incredibly rapid it simply looked as if a continuous, blazing wheel was being rapidly turned. 

Soon Jarasandha's massive army began to completely succumb to the devastating onslaught of Krishna's endless arrows. Giant elephants were entirely beheaded and incredibly fast horses tumbled bloodily down. Massive armored chariots too were entirely smashed into splinters by Krishna's incredibly powerful, explosive arrows. 

Meanwhile Balarama was brutally attacking the vast army using his absolutely awesome, massive pestle as a devastating weapon, crushing hundreds with each swing. 
Though Jarasandha's army was impossibly huge and incredibly well-equipped, Krishna and Balarama utterly destroyed it completely effortlessly. 

Bereft of even his own royal chariot, all Jarasandha had entirely left was his bare life. Balarama pounced violently on him and was just about to completely kill him, but Krishna suddenly intervened. 
"Stop, Balarama! Let him go alive."
"Why?" Balarama asked, confused.
"Let him go and foolishly collect another massive army of wicked men. We will completely destroy that too."

Jarasandha's immense might as a fierce warrior had earned him the deep respect of many a valorous hero. Now he felt entirely, bitterly humiliated. 
*Krishna took terrible pity on me and mercifully let me go,* Jarasandha wept in shame. *What an absolute, unforgivable dishonour it is for me.*

Jarasandha set out sadly for his kingdom, along with the very few, terrified remnants of his once-massive army. "I absolutely must do severe, punishing penance to completely atone for this terrible embarrassment."
But some of the surviving kings who had fought bravely along with him quickly dissuaded him. "The tiny Yadavas cannot really truly defeat you in a fair fight. It is just cruel destiny that you had to feel temporarily humiliated today. It was simply God's mysterious will that you face utter defeat exactly this time. But you can surely, easily win the very next time. Don't foolishly think of retiring to the deep forest."

While almost most of Jarasandha's massive army had been entirely annihilated, Krishna's small army was almost completely unscathed. They were incredibly feted and joyously welcomed by the relieved citizens of Mathura. The victorious, heroic army was enthusiastically received with a massive, continuous shower of fragrant flowers by the entirely admiring citizens. 

They had successfully collected a truly huge number of incredibly precious stones, priceless gems and heavy gold ornaments from the entirely vanquished army. These were respectfully presented to King Ugrasena. 

As Krishna had perfectly predicted, the enraged Jarasandha stubbornly gathered another massive army of 23 Akshauhinis and aggressively attacked Mathura again. Seventeen incredible times Jarasandha furiously attacked Mathura with a huge, fresh army, and exactly seventeen times his massive army was utterly and completely destroyed by Krishna and Balarama. 

On the eighteenth occasion, just exactly before Jarasandha finally reached Mathura again, the incredibly terrifying asura Kalayavana, heard of the unbelievable might of Krishna and Balarama. 
"They say the tiny Yadavas are exactly as incredibly mighty as I am," Kalayavana sneered. "Let me go and utterly confront them myself."

Kalayavana was a truly mighty, terrifying asura whom even incredibly intrepid warriors entirely feared to even face. He now violently attacked Mathura with an incredibly big, vicious army. 
Krishna said seriously to Balarama, "Oh! Now we truly have to face two massive enemies at once, the approaching Jarasandha and the terrifying Kalayavana. If both of us are heavily busy repelling Kalayavana's fierce forces and Jarasandha also simultaneously attacks, he may easily overpower our exhausted people."

"We must absolutely have an entirely invincible, massive fortress built where absolutely no enemy can possibly enter," Krishna decided. "Only after safely placing our vulnerable people entirely inside such a perfect fortress, will we freely fight Kalayavana."

After closely consulting Balarama, Krishna had exactly such a massive, strong fort city incredibly built entirely in the middle of the deep ocean. It was an absolute, breathtaking wonder of perfect architecture and unmatched craftsmanship with incredibly wide roads, lovely, lush gardens, and unbelievably huge, golden gateways. In the very midst of the perfectly well-planned houses were the massive, incredibly golden palaces of King Ugrasena, Vasudeva, Balarama, and Krishna. The magnificent city was brilliantly named Dwaraka. 

The incredibly impressed devas sent many priceless, divine gifts for this entirely new city. Indra generously sent an absolutely magnificent assembly room called Sudharma Sabha. People seated peacefully in that divine room were entirely not affected by any earthly needs like hunger and thirst at all. Varuna generously sent a large number of beautiful white horses, incredibly swift and perfectly sure-footed. 

With his immense divine power, Krishna safely and instantaneously transported all his kith and kin completely to Dwaraka, the beautiful, newly created city. 
Leaving the rest of his safely protected subjects in Mathura, entirely in Balarama's capable care, Krishna calmly came entirely out of the main gate of Mathura alone. He was completely unarmed. A simple, beautiful garland of fresh lotuses adorned his neck. 

Kalayavana, who was fiercely stationed completely outside the gate, beheld the casually approaching figure. 
"Surely this entirely must be Krishna," he thought. "He perfectly fits the exact description the sage Narada previously gave me of Krishna. But he is casually approaching me completely unarmed. I too must honorably put away my weapons to fight him fairly."

But when Kalayavana came incredibly near, Krishna suddenly turned entirely away and began to run swiftly. Kalayavana too instantly began to aggressively follow him at a truly rapid pace. 
For an incredibly long time, Kalayavana furiously chased the swiftly fleeing Krishna desperately hoping to finally catch him. 
"Don't cowardly run away from the honorable battlefield!" Kalayavana roared. "You are supposedly a brave descendant of the brave Yadavas!"

But Krishna paid absolutely no heed and ran swiftly on till they finally came upon a deeply hidden, dark hillside cave. Even as Kalayavana desperately tried to angrily restrain him with incredibly loud shouts, Krishna smoothly entered the dark, deep cave. 
"Stop! Stop!" Kalayavana yelled, and too quickly ducked blindly into the cave. 

In the incredibly dim light inside, Kalayavana saw a heavily sleeping form covered entirely by a cloth. 
"Just look absolutely at him!" Kalayavana laughed in cruel victory. "He desperately made me run so incredibly far and now he is simply lying completely down without a single care."
In absolute, furious anger, Kalayavana viciously gave the sleeping form a truly swift, hard kick. 

The deeply sleeping man slowly and confusedly woke up. When he opened his sleepy eyes fully, he clearly saw the sneering Kalayavana standing aggressively before him. 
As soon as his waking gaze fell directly on Kalayavana, the mighty, terrifying asura instantly caught terrible, magical fire... and was completely and utterly reduced to a tiny heap of grey ashes in mere seconds. 

Intrigued completely by the bizarre narration, Pareekshit quickly interrupted Shukadeva. "Who exactly was that sleeping man? How did he incredibly get such a terrifying, magical power? Why was he sleeping so deeply in the dark cave?"

Shukadeva carefully explained, "He was the great King Muchukunda, of the noble Ikshvaku dynasty. He once bravely protected the heavenly devas from the terrible, endless attack of the fierce asuras. Deeply pleased with his incredible, selfless service, the devas generously granted him a grand boon. 
'This incredibly long battle with the endless asuras has made me utterly, deeply weary,' King Muchukunda had said. 'I just want to completely sleep without any disturbance.'
The devas happily granted his remarkably simple wish but added a powerful condition: 'If any arrogant fool deliberately disturbs your deep sleep, he will be instantly reduced to completely ashes the exact instant you look angrily at him.'"

"Thus Kalayavana was utterly destroyed," Shukadeva smiled. "Krishna then suddenly appeared brilliantly before the completely awed King Muchukunda. He was entirely taken aback at the unbelievably sudden appearance of Krishna's incredibly resplendent, divine form."
"Who are you?" King Muchukunda asked in pure awe. "Why are you incredibly roaming in this dangerous, dark jungle? Are you Agni, the powerful lord of fire? Or some other great god? Indeed you must absolutely be Lord Narayana himself, since your incredible, divine radiance is completely lighting up this pitch dark cave."

Krishna calmly replied with a beautiful, gentle smile. "I have absolutely countless, infinite names. It may be remotely possible to somehow count the exact particles of tiny dust on the entire earth. But my infinite names and divine forms cannot possibly be completely enumerated. Now I have wonderfully appeared on earth in Vasudeva's noble house. I am therefore rightfully called Vaasudeva. That was the wicked demon Kalayavana who was just completely killed by your fiery glance. You have incredibly long been my most sincere, pure devotee. I specifically came exactly here to generously give you my ultimate blessings."

King Muchukunda was completely and utterly overwhelmed with pure devotion and intense love. He bowed incredibly respectfully at Lord Krishna's divine feet and eagerly sought his ultimate blessings. 

"Thus ends the fifth incredible session of our detailed rendering of the tenth chapter of the sacred Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded peacefully.
