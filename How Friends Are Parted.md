# How Friends Are Parted: Tales from the Hitopadesha

Hitopadesha is a collection of ancient Sanskrit fables written by Narayana Pandit. The four stories chosen here have simple moral tales to tell about judging for oneself, minding one's own business, avoiding greed, and not succumbing to rumour-mongers.

## How Friends Are Parted
There was once a rich merchant travelling through a forest in a cart loaded with goods. Suddenly, one of his bulls broke its foreleg. Unable to take him further, the merchant sadly left the bull behind in the forest. 

The forest was full of tender grass, and the bull fed happily. As the days passed, his leg healed, and he grew strong and robust. He bellowed with joy, his roar echoing through the trees. 

In that same forest lived a mighty lion and two jackals, Damanaka and Karataka, who were out of the lion's favour. One day, as the lion went to drink from a pool, he heard the bull's thundering roar. Terrified, the lion ran back to his cave. 

Damanaka saw this as an opportunity to win back the king's favour. He approached the lion and boldly offered to find out what beast was making the noise, in exchange for a royal feast for him and his brother. The lion agreed. 

Damanaka soon discovered the source of the roar was merely a grass-eating bull. However, to maintain his importance, he told the lion the bull was a dangerous beast but that he could persuade it to be friends. Damanaka then went to the bull and terrified him with tales of the fierce lion, offering to safely guide him to the king to pay homage. 

When Damanaka brought them together, the lion and the bull, both relieved, became inseparable friends. The lion appointed the grass-eating bull as his treasurer, effectively stopping the jackals from stealing and wasting the meat from his hunts. 

Deprived of their free meals, Damanaka grew jealous and decided to turn the friends against each other. He went to the lion and lied, "My Lord, the bull is a traitor. He plans to seize your kingdom. He will come here with his horns lowered as bulls do when they attack."

Then, Damanaka went to the bull and lied again, "His majesty's heart has turned against you. He plans to kill you. Go to him, and when he glares at you with his tail stiff and paw raised, you'll see for yourself."

The bull went to the lion's den. Expecting an attack, he lowered his horns. The lion, expecting an attack, stiffened his tail and raised his paw. Seeing this "proof," both attacked. The mighty lion killed the bull. As the lion stared sadly at his dead friend, he heard the gleeful howl of the jackals. He realised too late that he had let cunning rumor-mongers outwit him and part him from a true friend.

## The Washerman's Jackass
Long ago, there lived an ass and a dog who belonged to a washerman in Varanasi. One night, a thief broke into the yard. 
The dog, resentful because his master often neglected to feed him, refused to bark. "A little fright will remind him that he needs me," the dog said. 

The loyal but foolish ass was furious. "How can you let our master down, you unfaithful cur? I will wake him up!" 
The ass began to bray as loudly as he could: HE-HAW! HE-HAW!

The noise woke up the washerman, but the thief managed to escape before he was seen. Finding no thief and furious at having his sleep ruined by the braying ass, the washerman grabbed a cudgel and beat the ass mercilessly. 

The dog shook his head in pity. "Poor ass. If only you had stuck to your duties instead of trying to perform mine, would you have suffered this fate?"

## The Cat Who Served a Lion
A mighty lion was constantly annoyed by a tiny mouse that nibbled on his magnificent mane while he slept. Unable to catch the speedy mouse, the lion brought a cat to live in his den to act as a bodyguard. He treated the cat to the freshest fish every day. 

With the cat prowling around, the terrified mouse stayed hidden in its hole. The lion slept in peace, and the cat enjoyed a life of luxury. 

However, after a week of starvation, the desperate mouse crept out to find food. The cat immediately pounced and killed it. 

The next day, the lion noticed he no longer heard the mouse. Realizing he no longer needed the cat's services, the lion stopped providing fish. "Friend lion, I'm hungry," the cat complained.
"Then go and catch yourself some mice. Don't disturb me," the lion replied. The cat left the den, a wiser animal.

## The Terrible Bell
A thief stole a bell from a village temple but was killed by a tiger while escaping through the forest. The bell was left behind. 

The next day, a troop of monkeys found the bell and began playing with it, making a tremendous clanging noise. Travellers passing through the forest heard the noise, saw the thief's bones, and ran to the village, spreading rumours of a terrible, man-eating demon named Ghantakarna who swung ringing bells from his ears. 

The terrified villagers went to their headman, begging to leave the village. An opportunistic woman offered to chase the demon away if paid a large sum of money. The desperate headman agreed. 

However, a wise village woman reasoned, "I refuse to believe there's a demon... The noise sounds like the clanging of a bell. Some monkeys must have got hold of it." 

She bought a basket of mangoes and went to the forest. She threw the mangoes near where the sound was coming from and hid. The monkeys immediately dropped the bell and scampered for the fruit. The woman grabbed the bell and presented it to the headman. 

"Here is your demon! The terrible sound came from this harmless temple bell," she revealed. The headman rewarded her handsomely for her common sense, and she became the most respected person in the village.
