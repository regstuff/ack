# Harsha: The Great Ruler of Thaneshwar

About 1,400 years ago, Thaneshwar, a powerful kingdom in Northern India, was ruled by King Prabhakar Vardhan. During his reign, the Huns had started harassing the people of Thaneshwar. So, the king sent his able sons, Prince Rajyavardhan and Prince Harsha, to punish them. 

After a bitter fight, the Huns retreated. "Victory to Prince Rajyavardhan! Victory to Prince Harsha!" the soldiers cheered. The brothers decided to camp for the night. Since there was no fighting for several days, Harsha decided to go on a hunt. While hunting far away from the camp, Harsha saw a rider approaching in a great hurry. 
"Prince Harsha, your father is very ill. He wants to see you and Prince Rajya," the messenger informed him. Both princes rushed back to Thaneshwar, but they were too late. Their noble father had passed away. 

"Prince Rajya, you will now have to sit on the throne," Harsha said a few days later. 
"Oh, no! Let Harsha be the king," Rajyavardhan replied. 
"Certainly not. Being the elder brother, it is your right and duty to rule," Harsha insisted. The ministers agreed, and Rajyavardhan became king. "My brothers are young and inexperienced. Please guide them," their sister Rajyashri, married to King Grahavarman of Kannauj, urged her husband. 

Meanwhile, Devgupta, the greedy king of Malwa, was busy plotting. "We can now grab the kingdoms of Thaneshwar and Kannauj," he schemed. "If we kill Grahavarman, the inexperienced Rajyavardhan will not be able to defend both kingdoms."
During the spring festival in Kannauj, Devgupta and his soldiers disguised themselves as peasants. As King Grahavarman was entering a temple, they attacked and killed him. Devgupta then captured Kannauj and forcibly imprisoned Rajyashri in Malwa. 

When the news of Devgupta's treachery reached Thaneshwar, Rajyavardhan was furious. "Maharaj, allow me to attack Malwa," Harsha pleaded. 
"No, Harsha! Being the elder brother and the king, it is my duty to do so," Rajyavardhan declared and marched towards Malwa. 

Fearing Rajyavardhan's strength, Devgupta schemed with King Shashank of Gaud. "Win Rajyavardhan's confidence and accompany him in his attack on me. When the battle begins, attack Rajyavardhan from the rear," Devgupta proposed, promising Shashank half the kingdoms. 
Shashank agreed and deceitfully marched with Rajyavardhan. "I am grateful to you for your help, King Shashank. You shall always be my most trusted friend," Rajyavardhan said warmly. 

However, during the fierce battle, Shashank was helpless as he was surrounded by Rajyavardhan's vigilant personal guards. Rajyavardhan fought fiercely, eventually killing Devgupta and winning the battle. Shashank was deeply upset that his treacherous dream was shattered. 

Meanwhile, back in Malwa's prison, Rajyashri was agonised by the cries of other prisoners. Due to the disorganization caused by the battle, only one soldier was left to guard the prison. This soldier possessed a noble heart. He opened a secret passage for Rajyashri, leading her to the jungle. She escaped safely, avoiding Devgupta's patrolling soldiers. 

Overwhelmed by her tragic fate, Rajyashri wandered through the jungle until she decided to end her life by throwing herself off a cliff. "There is nobody to help me. Why should I live any more?" she cried. 
Just in time, Harsha found her. "Stop! Sister stop!" he shouted. He rescued her and vowed, "Your bad days are now over, dear sister. I pledge that I will uproot the cause of these sufferings."

To bring lasting peace to Northern India, Harsha set out to conquer the warring, petty kingdoms. Very soon, all the small kingdoms came under one flag. "There would be no more battles between them. Poor peasants will live in peace at last," Harsha thought. 

Unaware of Harsha's peace-plans, the great South Indian king, Pulakeshin, felt threatened by Harsha's expanding empire. "Harsha has won kingdoms as far as Kathiawar. He may attack us one day. We must attack before he does," Pulakeshin decided. 
As Harsha was returning home after conquering Kathiawar, his tired soldiers were ambushed by Pulakeshin's army. The fighting continued for several days with heavy losses on both sides. Harsha's tired soldiers had to retreat. 

Sometime later in Thaneshwar, his generals urged him to attack Pulakeshin again. "This time we are fully prepared to crush him."
"I have had enough of this blood-shed," Harsha declared, deeply saddened by the loss of life. "There shall be no more wars. In future, I shall use my strength to protect the weak and the poor." His subjects hailed him as a noble king. 

To fulfill his dream, Harsha gave immense financial help to the poor, appointed physicians to treat the sick free of cost, and helped students pursue their studies. During his reign, followers of different faiths lived in peace and harmony, and traders came from as far as China. 

During this period, Hiuen Tsang, the famous Chinese pilgrim and scholar, came to India to learn about Buddhism. Harsha welcomed him warmly, and Hiuen Tsang stayed in India for several years. Harsha even organized a grand seminar on religions, inviting eminent scholars of Hinduism, Jainism, and Buddhism. 

During the seminar, Harsha praised Hiuen Tsang as "the most noble scholar I have ever known." Out of jealousy, some greedy participants conspired to harm the foreign scholar. When Harsha learned of this, he issued a stern warning: "Anybody who tries to harm Acharya Hiuen Tsang shall be punished. I believe in non-violence... Those who preach violence shall pay for it with their own lives." Nobody dared to harm Hiuen Tsang. 

At the end of the seminar, Harsha distributed gifts to the scholars and priests, and then to the poor peasants. When the gifts ran out, he ordered the royal treasury to be emptied. Even after donating all the royal wealth, some people were left out. Harsha then gave away his royal ornaments. 
In the end, only one poor man remained. "I am sorry that nothing is left with me except the clothes I am wearing," Harsha said, and turned to his sister. "Sister Rajyashri, can you lend me a cloth so that I can donate my dress." 
Receiving a simple cloth from his sister, Harsha donated his royal clothes to the man. 

In this manner, every five years, Harsha gave away all his accumulated wealth to scholars, priests, and the poor, earning eternal reverence from his people. 

After completing his studies, Hiuen Tsang returned to China. He carried with him memoirs of his visit, which became world-famous testimonies of India's great ancient heritage. "I have known many kings but there is none like Harsha," Hiuen Tsang recorded. "I have seen many countries but there is none like Bharat. Truly Bharat is a great country and her greatness lies in her people and in her rulers like Harsha."
