# Devi Choudhurani: The Bandit Queen

In Durgapur, a small village of North Bengal, there lived a poor Brahman widow with her beautiful daughter, Prafulla. The fame of Prafulla's beauty reached the ears of Haraballabh, the wealthy Zamindar of Bhuthnath. 

"She is the girl for my son. Poor she certainly is. But she is a Kulin Brahman girl, and they are hard to come by," Haraballabh thought. He went to see Prafulla's mother. 
"I wish to marry my only child Brajeshwar to your daughter," he proposed. 
"I am honoured, sir," her mother agreed joyfully. 

The wedding soon took place. Prafulla's mother did her best to please all. But she could not afford a grand feast for everyone. 
"She gave a grand feast to the groom's party, and we were left out. We should avenge this insult," the envious neighbours muttered. 
So Prafulla had to return to her mother after the wedding. "I spent all I had on your wedding. We are left with nothing. What will we live on?" her mother wept. 
"Is there no one to fight this act of injustice?" Prafulla wondered. 

Two days later, the envious neighbours met Haraballabh at his house. "Sir! Prafulla's mother has no character. If only you had consulted us..." they lied maliciously. 
Angry and agitated, Haraballabh believed the village gossip. He went to his wife and told her everything. "Send Prafulla back to her mother. And she need not return."
"But why? Just because of some village gossip?" his wife reasoned. 
"There can be no smoke without fire," Haraballabh declared stubbornly. 

The two women spent two years in dire poverty. Then one day, Prafulla's mother said, "Mother! I can't see you starving for my sake. I'd rather go to my in-laws..."
"...And be humiliated? No, my child!" Prafulla objected. 
"Why not? They are obliged to maintain me."
Prafulla made her way to Haraballabh's house and stood before her mother-in-law. "Mother! I have come to stay with you."
"She is so beautiful... and looks innocent too. I wish she could stay here," her mother-in-law thought. She ushered Prafulla into a room where she met Sagar, Brajeshwar's second wife. 

"I am Sagar, the second wife. So you are the one who has taken my place in the family!" Sagar smiled warmly. "I would like to have you with us, my child! But I fear society. I live with my father. Our husband lives with his third wife, Nayantara."
"How unfortunate he is! In spite of having a beautiful wife like you..." Prafulla sighed. 
"What about you? There is none prettier than you!" Sagar laughed. 

Meanwhile, Haraballabh found out Prafulla was in the house. "That woman! Throw her out," he roared. 
Brajeshwar found Prafulla in the room where Sagar had left her. She came forward and touched his feet. 
"Forgive me, Prafulla. I am helpless. My father does not want you here and I cannot maintain you," Brajeshwar apologized. "How can I? She is a sweet child..." he thought sadly. "I'll soon take up a job, however."

Brajeshwar brought out his diamond ring and gave it to her. "Sell it and use the money till I am in a position to maintain you."
"It has your name engraved on it. I won't part with it even if I have to starve," Prafulla said firmly, returning it. 
"Braja! It is your duty to drive her out," Haraballabh yelled from outside. 
Brajeshwar had no choice. Prafulla was forced to leave. 

When Prafulla returned to Durgapur, she found her mother seriously ill. Soon after, her mother died, leaving her completely alone. 
One dark night, a few days after her mother's death, the evil Zamindar's men broke into Prafulla's house. They carried Prafulla away in a palanquin through an unused jungle path. 
Suddenly, they heard a shout. "Look! Dacoits! Run for your lives!"
Dropping the palanquin, the cowardly men ran away. Prafulla saw her chance to escape and fled into the deep forest. 

As she wandered about in the forest, Prafulla came upon the ruins of a house. Inside, she found a dying old man who asked for water. Prafulla fetched him some water. 
"I am grateful to you. I never thought that I'd see a human face again. Now listen... I'm dying. I've buried some gold in the ground near that window - you may take it," the old man rasped, and breathed his last. 

The next morning, Prafulla dug the ground at the spot he had indicated and found an ewer full of gold coins. She buried the dead body in the pit, carried the pitcher inside and hid it. 
Hungry, she went out into the forest and met a pious-looking Brahman. "What are you doing here, mother? This forest is no place for a lady like you."
"I want to buy some food - I'm hungry," Prafulla said, offering a gold coin. 

The Brahman looked at the coin. "I am Bhavani Pathak. And here is the proof," he smiled. He sounded a gong, and fifty fierce dacoits instantly assembled in front of Prafulla. 
Bhavani Pathak was popularly known as 'Bhavani Thakur', a legendary bandit who robbed the rich to help the poor. "Regard this lady as your mother and let no harm befall her. Now disperse," he ordered his men. 
"You cannot leave this place unless I wish it. But trust me - no harm will come to you," Bhavani told her. 

Prafulla told him her story but hid the facts about her marriage. 
"Now that you know all about this treasure, tell me what to do with it," she asked. 
"You need training to handle it properly. That should take some five years. I can train you if you are willing to follow my instructions without questioning them and... if you agree not to touch that money before the training is over."
"I accept the conditions, sir!" Prafulla agreed. 

"I've at last found a 'Queen' for us," Bhavani Pathak told his deputy, Rangaraj. "She will be trained to rule within five years. She has beauty, money and courage."
For five years, Prafulla underwent rigorous training. She was instructed in various arts, literature, sciences, and even wrestling and martial arts. She lived an austere life, eating coarse rice and sleeping on the bare floor, hardening her body and sharpening her mind. 

When her training was complete, Bhavani Pathak said, "Now you can deal with any situation with a calm and courageous mind. I did expect you to join us; not as my assistant but as our Queen!"
Prafulla thus became the leader of the dacoit gang. In a short period, she became well-known all over Bengal as the legendary 'Devi Choudhurani' (the Bandit Queen). 

Meanwhile, in Haraballabh's house, disaster had struck. 
"I am ruined, my son! Once again the dacoits have looted my revenue collections," Haraballabh wept. "My tax arrears have mounted to Rs. 50,000. If I do not pay, I will be arrested."
"I will try to borrow the amount from Sagar's father," Brajeshwar offered. 
Brajeshwar went to his wealthy father-in-law, but the old man callously refused to help Haraballabh. Disgusted, Brajeshwar severed ties with him. 

Brajeshwar left by boat on the river Trisrota. That night, Devi Choudhurani's men intercepted his boat. Following Devi's strict orders of no bloodshed, they simply tied up the boatmen and captured Brajeshwar. 
Brajeshwar was led to a waiting, luxurious barge and presented before a veiled lady. 
"I do not know why you've brought me here, Devi!" he said. 
"I am not Devi! I have bought you from her. You are my slave now," the veiled Prafulla said playfully. "Now, start your work. First tend my feet."

As soon as Brajeshwar touched her feet, she removed her veil with a laugh. 
"Oh!... Sagar!" Brajeshwar gasped. Sagar had secretly come to Devi's barge. "But how did you come here?"
"Devi is my cousin. Now let us go to meet her," Sagar smiled. 
A little later, Brajeshwar noticed a familiar diamond ring on the veiled Devi's finger. "My name engraved on it! Tell me! Who is this Devi?"
"She is Prafulla! I recognised her the moment I saw her," Sagar revealed. 

Brajeshwar was led to Devi in another cabin. She got up and bowed respectfully to him. 
"If my men bothered you, let my money compensate. Take this and pay off your father's debt," Prafulla said, handing him the 50,000 rupees. 
Back at home, Brajeshwar gave the money to his father and naively told him he got it from Devi Choudhurani, promising to repay her in the month of Baisakh. 

"Return the tainted money of a dacoit? Nonsense," Haraballabh schemed. "On the contrary, hand her over to the police and collect the prize-money."
As the month of Baisakh drew near, Haraballabh treacherously went to the District Collector's office and returned with Lieutenant Brennan and five hundred soldiers to arrest Devi. 

Unaware of his father's vile move, Brajeshwar went to meet Devi on the appointed day. Devi told him the whole story of his father's betrayal. 
"Now you know everything. But leave the barge immediately. The British are coming to arrest me," Prafulla warned him. 
"Then I should be here by your side," Brajeshwar insisted out of loyalty. 
"But I have to let them arrest me. Otherwise your father would be in danger," Prafulla explained. 

Lieutenant Brennan and Haraballabh boarded Devi's barge. To gain time, Prafulla, Sagar, and Nishi all claimed to be Devi Choudhurani, thoroughly confusing the British officer. 
As Devi had anticipated, a violent storm soon broke out. Devi's expert boatmen were prepared for it. Before anyone could realise what was happening, her barge pierced the cordon of English boats and was soon out of sight. 

The next morning, the gale stopped. Devi graciously released her captives, Lieutenant Brennan and the treacherous Haraballabh, unharmed. 
"You may go now, sir! The groom will stay on with us. The couple will come to your house tomorrow," she told Haraballabh. 
The next day, Prafulla took leave of Bhavani Pathak and her gang. "Rangaraj! Take charge of my money and continue to help the poor. Farewell to all of you," she said, giving up her life as a bandit queen. 

Later in the afternoon, at Haraballabh's house, Brajeshwar arrived with Prafulla. 
"I found her by God's grace," Brajeshwar told his father. 
Sagar came running in. "You look like a goddess, Devi!" she whispered. 
"Shh! Sagar! Devi is dead and so is Prafulla. I am the new bride," Prafulla smiled. 
And so, having conquered poverty, betrayal, and the British army, the brilliant Prafulla returned to live a peaceful life with her husband, her true identity as the legendary Devi Choudhurani remaining a closely guarded secret.
