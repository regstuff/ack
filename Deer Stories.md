# Deer Stories: The Gentle Wisdom of the Jataka

## True Friendship
In a thicket near a lake, lived three friends: a tortoise, a woodpecker and an antelope. One day a hunter came that way. "Ah! Footprints! I'll set the trap right here. I'll come back for him tomorrow," the hunter smirked, setting a strong leather snare. 

At dawn, the antelope came bounding to the lake. "It's good to be alive. I must have a drink." But suddenly... *clack!* He stepped into the trap. 
"Help! I'm caught. A...a...ah!" he cried. 
Meanwhile the hunter too had heard the antelope's shouts for help and rushed out of his house. The woodpecker spied him. "Eyoow! Ah... ah, he's caught. I'd better get indoors," the hunter said as the woodpecker swooped down and pecked at him furiously. 

The tortoise and the woodpecker had heard his cries and rushed to the antelope. 
"I'll tell you what - you have strong teeth," the woodpecker told the tortoise. "You free him while I keep the hunter away. When he comes out of his hut, I'll give him a good pecking."
The tortoise was as slow as a tortoise could be. "Will you please hurry," the antelope pleaded. "I hope, friend tortoise is safe," he thought as he finally broke free. 

Just then the hunter arrived. The antelope rushed back to the spot where the tortoise had been abandoned. "Don't worry, my friend, I'll soon get you out of the bag!" the antelope promised. He showed himself to the hunter. 
"Ah, so you are the culprit. You freed my antelope. I'll teach you not to interfere. Since you robbed me of my dinner... I'll put you in this sack, take you home and eat you," the hunter said, grabbing the tortoise. 

"Once he is in he will not know how to get out. I'll lead him into my favourite old cave," the antelope thought. The hunter dropped his sack and ran fiercely after the antelope. 
"Huh - where did he go?" the hunter wondered, entering the dark cave. 
"What a fool! He'll be trapped in the cave. He's done for," the antelope smiled, sneaking out and freeing the tortoise from the sack. 
"What happened to the hunter?" the tortoise asked. 
"Ha! Ha! Ha! He is running around in circles in our old cave!"

## Ruru, the Golden Deer
Mahadhanaka was the son of a rich merchant of Varanasi. His father, instead of sending him to school, spoilt him with all the pleasures of the world. When he came of age, Mahadhanaka was married to a beautiful girl. Soon his parents died and he was left alone. His greedy friends were after his money. Mahadhanaka had never handled money before, so he did not know how to use it. 
One day, he realised he had no money left. "If I don't do something I'll soon have no friends either," he worried. He went from one money-lender to another and borrowed money, promising to pay it back. But he knew he would never be able to repay the money. 

"I would rather die than face the money-lenders," he finally decided. He led all his creditors to the bank of a river. "I have some treasure hidden in the river bed. I will return all that I have borrowed from you," he lied. Then he jumped into the fast-flowing river to drown himself. "Help! Glub! Gasp! Help!" he panicked as he sank. 

Not very far away, on the opposite bank, lived Ruru, a golden deer. "Somebody's drowning. I must save him," Ruru thought. He swam out and saved the drowning man. Before parting, Ruru asked, "Promise me you will tell no one about me and where I live."
"I promise, friend," Mahadhanaka swore. 

That night Khema, the Queen of Varanasi, had a dream. "I dreamt that a golden deer preached dharma to me. If such a deer exists, I would like to hear its discourse," she told the king. 
The king sent for his councillors, who confirmed that such animals existed. The king immediately sent around a proclamation offering a large reward to any man who could tell him of the whereabouts of a golden deer. 
Mahadhanaka heard the town crier. "Hmm, I shall soon be rich again," he thought greedily. He went to the palace and led the king's hunting party to the grove where Ruru lived. 

"Surround the grove," the king ordered his men. When the deer heard the noise of the beaters, it tried to escape, but found the king waiting with his bow drawn. 
"Put down your bow, O king. I've something to ask you," Ruru spoke in a human voice. 
The king was astonished. "I promise I won't shoot. Come out of your hiding place."
"Your majesty, who told you about me and where I live?" Ruru asked. 
"He did," the king pointed to Mahadhanaka. 
"I thought as much. It would have been better to save a log of wood than a man like him," the deer sighed. "I once saved this man from drowning, and this is how he has repaid me."

"Is that so? He shall be hanged for it," the furious king declared. 
"No, don't do that. I don't want him to die because of me. Let him go," Ruru pleaded. 
"You are lucky. But for him I would have had you hanged. Now go away," the king told the cowardly Mahadhanaka. 
The king then turned to Ruru. "Ask for anything, and I'll grant it to you."
"Your majesty, from now on, let no animal or bird be harmed by anyone in your kingdom," the golden deer asked. 
The king immediately sent around a proclamation banning hunting, and Ruru discoursed to the Queen and returned to the forest.

## The Hunter Outwitted
There once lived a stag in the forest near Rajagriha. One day his sister came to him with her son. "Brother, will you teach your nephew the tricks of our herd?"
"Certainly. Go home now, my boy, and come back tomorrow," the stag said. 
Every day for two months the little deer went to his uncle to learn the tricks of survival. 

One day while wandering through the forest with his friends, he stepped into a trap. "Help! I'm caught."
His friends hurried to the stag. "Brother, please help me. My son has been trapped."
"Do not fear, my sister. I must remember what uncle taught me. I must lie stiff and pretend to be dead," the young deer thought. He pretended so well that even the flies were fooled. 

Along came the hunter. "I hope an animal has been trapped today. Aha! It's a lucky day for me. He must have died this morning. He's stiff already."
He opened the trap. "I'll cut him up and take the flesh home. My wife will be pleased. On second thoughts I'll roast some of the flesh and eat it here."
As the hunter went to collect some wood to make a fire, the young deer sprang up and ran away. "Hee..hee..hee..."

## Caution Pays
In a forest near Varanasi, there lived an antelope which was very fond of the fruits of a particular tree. In a village nearby lived a hunter, who knew this. 
Early that morning the hunter went to the jungle. "Hmm. He is a big animal. I will get quite some money by selling his meat," the hunter thought. He put up a 'machan' (platform) on the tree... and waited for the antelope. 

As usual the antelope came strolling along. "Mm..m..m.. just thinking of that fruit makes me hungry. Hey! A man's footprints! There must be a hunter near by, waiting to kill me."
He looked closely at the tree. "Why doesn't he come near? I shall try throwing some fruit to him. Maybe that will lure him to the tree," the hunter thought, dropping some fruits. 
"Oh oh, there is someone up the tree. The fruit is being thrown to me. It isn't dropping as it usually does," the antelope realised. "Yes. Now I am sure. I can see the corner of a 'machan'."

The antelope looked up at the tree. "Listen, tree. You have always dropped your fruit on the earth. Why do you throw it at me today? And so tree, since you have stopped behaving like a normal tree, I too will behave differently. I shall eat elsewhere."
The hunter knew he had lost his chance and threw his javelin at the antelope in anger. It missed. 
"Hee...hee, too bad, hunter! Better luck next time," the antelope laughed and bounded away.

## The Golden Antelope
Long ago there lived a king in Varanasi who had a gardener named Sanjaya. Every day an antelope came to graze in the royal garden. After some time the antelope became accustomed to seeing Sanjaya about and stopped running away. 
One day while he was taking fruit and flowers to the palace, the king stopped him. "Have you noticed anything strange in the garden, Sanjaya?"
"Nothing, your majesty, except that a wild antelope comes to graze here from time to time."
"Do you think you could catch him?"
"Oh yes. If I had a little honey I would bring him right into your palace."

The king ordered some honey to be given to Sanjaya. 
"I shall coat the grass where he grazes with honey, then hide myself and see what happens," Sanjaya planned. 
Along came the antelope. "Hmm. This grass tastes exceptionally sweet today."
The antelope was so snared by the taste of the sweetened grass, he would eat nowhere else. "Hmm. Now to show myself gradually," Sanjaya thought. 

At first the antelope was startled at the appearance of Sanjaya. But soon he was eating the sweet grass that Sanjaya offered him. 
"Now I must lead him towards the palace. He hasn't even noticed we are near the palace," Sanjaya smiled. 
Once he was inside the palace, Sanjaya quickly shut the heavy door. At the sight of so many men and knowing that he was trapped, the antelope began trembling with fright and dashed to and fro. 

When the king came down from his chamber and saw the antelope he felt sorry for it. 
"Antelopes are so timid that they never revisit a place for at least a week if they have seen a man there," the king observed. "Sometimes they never go back again. This antelope was carried away by the taste of the sweetened grass and forgot himself. Surely there is nothing viler in the world than the lust of taste. You have taught us a valuable lesson."
He opened the doors. "Go my friend. You are free to go wherever you please."

## The Banyan Deer
In two different parts of a forest lived two handsome golden stags. One was called the Banyan Deer and the other the Branch Deer. Each had a herd of its own. 
Now the King of Varanasi was very fond of hunting deer and in his love of it he disturbed all his townsfolk. "Come on, everyone, let's go on a hunt," he would declare constantly. 
One day the people got together and hit upon a plan. "Let us chase all the deer into the king's garden. Then he will not disturb us at odd hours to go into the forest."

They gathered sticks, pots, pans and anything that made a noise and drove the deer into the garden. A few townsmen went to the king. "Sir, you distract us from work when you go hunting, so we have driven all the deer into your garden. Shoot them whenever you wish."
"And so the king would go out and shoot deer now and again. Sometimes the head cook would accompany him on his hunt. "Look, my lord! There goes a plump one."

But the deer were being slain in such large numbers that the Banyan Deer decided to do something about it. 
"My friend, our deer cannot escape death but let them not be killed needlessly," the Banyan Deer told the Branch Deer. "We will send them to the block by turns - one from my herd one from yours - each day."
In this way the deer whose turn it was would go to the block and wait for the cook to take him away. 

One day it was the lot of a doe who was about to be a mother. She went to the Branch Deer. "My lord, let me escape this time. When I have had my little one there will be two of us to go to the block."
"No, no, I cannot do this. Go away," the Branch Deer refused. 
So she went to the Banyan Deer and told him her story. 
"Very well. You don't go this time. I will do something about it," he promised. 

He himself went to the block that day. When the cook came along he was surprised to see one of the golden deer at the block. "Oh, oh! I must turn back and tell the king."
He rushed to the spot. When the king heard the news, he was surprised. "Why are you here? You are free to live."
"Your majesty, a doe who was about to become a mother came to me and asked to be spared. I have come in her place. Please spare her."

The king was astonished at such love. He turned to the Banyan Deer. "Both of you shall live. You deserve it."
"If you can spare our lives, can't you spare the lives of all the deer, your majesty?" the golden stag asked gently. 
"And couldn't you spare the lives of all the creatures in your forest, your majesty?"
The king thought for a while and then - "All right! From this day, there shall be no hunting or killing of any kind in my kingdom."
And so all the animals in the forest lived in peace and harmony for a long time to come.
