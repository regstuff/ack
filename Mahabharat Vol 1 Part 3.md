# The Conspiracy

The Pandava princes grew up to be extraordinary warriors, beloved by the citizens of Hastinapura for their virtue, strength, and humility. Yudhishthira, the eldest, was so scrupulously honest and fair that King Dhritarashtra, recognizing the public sentiment, had no choice but to install him as the heir-apparent. 

The Pandavas continually proved their worth. Bheema mastered the mace and chariot steering under Balarama, while Arjuna mastered diverse weapons under Drona. Nakula excelled in unusual weapons, and Sahadeva immersed himself in spiritual lore. To expand the Kuru empire, Arjuna vanquished the Yavana kings, while Bheema marched South and East, returning with immense wealth and treasures. 

Seeing the Pandavas' phenomenal success and the citizens' adoration, King Dhritarashtra's heart grew heavy with jealousy and fear for his own hundred sons, the Kauravas. His envy was relentlessly stoked by his eldest son, Duryodhana, who constantly whispered venomous thoughts against his cousins. 

Deeply distressed, Dhritarashtra summoned his chief political counselor, Kanika. "The Pandavas are men of might and ample resources. Should I treat them as allies or as rivals?" he asked. 

Kanika’s response was chilling. "O King, slay your foe with gifts and camaraderie. Kill him with discord or with force. A clever jackal once demolished his foes—a strong tiger, a cunning mouse, a swift wolf, and a mongoose—solely through the art of diplomacy and deceit. Be like the jackal."

Convinced by Kanika's ruthless advice, Dhritarashtra threw his full support behind Duryodhana, his brother Duhshasana, and their uncle Shakuni. Together, they hatched a sinister plot to eliminate the Pandavas without drawing suspicion to the Kuru throne. 

Dhritarashtra began to passionately describe the upcoming festival of Pashupati in the town of Varanavata, praising its brilliant attractions. Intrigued, the innocent Pandavas expressed a desire to attend. Dhritarashtra happily granted them permission, instructing them to live in Varanavata with their mother, Kunti.

Secretly, Duryodhana summoned his most loyal and ruthless henchman, Purochana. "The time is ripe for our escape from fear," Duryodhana declared. "Go swiftly to Varanavata. Build a grand mansion for the Pandavas, but construct its walls using highly inflammable materials—hemp, resin, ghee, oil, and lacquer. Welcome them warmly, and when they are fast asleep, set fire to the mansion’s entrance."

Purochana eagerly agreed and sped to Varanavata. 

When the time came for the Pandavas to depart, the citizens of Hastinapura gathered to bid them a tearful farewell. Among the crowd was the wise and deeply concerned Vidura. Knowing of Duryodhana’s lethal plot, Vidura approached Yudhishthira. 

To prevent anyone else from understanding, Vidura spoke to Yudhishthira in the obscure Mlechchha dialect. 
"He who knows that weapons of fire can burn forests but not singe those in forest hideouts, will survive," Vidura warned cryptically. "A burrow with many exits saves one from fire. He who is aware of the planned schemes of foes should protect himself from foreseen calamities."

"I have understood," Yudhishthira replied solemnly, fully grasping that they were walking into a deadly fire trap and that they must dig a secret tunnel to survive. Armed with Vidura's cryptic but life-saving counsel, the Pandavas set forth toward Varanavata.


# The Escape

Upon arriving in Varanavata, the Pandavas were warmly welcomed by the citizens and formally received by Purochana, Duryodhana's wicked henchman. Purochana proudly led them to a grand new mansion built exclusively for their stay. 

However, the moment they stepped inside, Yudhishthira's sharp senses detected a strong, unmistakable odor of ghee, oil, fat, and lacquer. Recalling Vidura's cryptic warning, he realized instantly that the entire beautiful structure was a highly flammable death trap. 

"We should continue to live here simulating innocence, and yet be cautious and alert," Yudhishthira whispered to his brothers, warning them that fleeing immediately would only cause Duryodhana to send assassins after them. 

A few days later, a friend of Vidura, an expert miner, secretly arrived at the mansion. He confirmed Yudhishthira's worst fears: "On a moonless night of a waning fortnight, Purochana will set fire to the door of this mansion."
The miner quickly set to work. Starting from the center of the mansion's floor, he excavated a deep, wide subterranean tunnel that stretched far beyond the city walls, concealing the entrance with clever levelling. 

For an entire year, the Pandavas lived in the mansion of lacquer, feigning complete ignorance to lull Purochana into a false sense of security. During the day, they hunted extensively in the surrounding forests, memorizing the treacherous terrain to ensure their eventual escape route. 

When the time was finally ripe, Kunti organized a massive almsgiving ritual, feeding countless Brahmanas and citizens in the mansion. Among the crowd was a destitute Nishada woman and her five sons. Seeking shelter, they consumed a large amount of wine and fell into a deep, death-like sleep within the mansion's courtyard. 

That very night, a fierce windstorm swept through Varanavata. Knowing Purochana intended to burn them alive while they slept, Bheema acted first. He set fire to Purochana's sleeping quarters and then ignited the highly flammable walls of the mansion itself. As the flames roared to life with terrifying speed, the Pandavas and Kunti swiftly descended into their secret tunnel. 

The fire consumed the mansion entirely, burning the wicked Purochana alive. The next morning, the horrified citizens sifted through the ashes and discovered the charred remains of the Nishada woman and her five sons. Believing them to be Kunti and the five Pandavas, they sent grief-stricken messengers to Hastinapura. 

Upon hearing the news, King Dhritarashtra wailed in feigned sorrow, while Bheeshma and the entire Kuru court wept genuine tears of devastation. Only Vidura remained secretly calm, knowing the truth of their escape, though he still prayed for their safe passage through the wilderness.

Meanwhile, the Pandavas emerged from the tunnel into a dense, desolate jungle. The horrific roar of wild animals, the lack of water, and the sheer exhaustion of their sleepless escape quickly took their toll. Kunti and the younger Pandavas collapsed on the jungle floor, completely unable to walk further. 

Seeing their despair, the mighty Bheema hoisted his mother onto his shoulder and gathered his four brothers into his massive arms. With the furious power of the wind god Vayu, Bheema crashed through the dense forest, snapping trees and leaping over rivers, carrying his entire family until they finally reached the safety of a giant banyan tree. 

Leaving his exhausted family to rest, Bheema followed the cries of water birds to a nearby lake, fetching water to quench their desperate thirst as they began their perilous life in exile.


# The Birth of Ghatotkacha

While the exhausted Pandavas and Kunti slept peacefully under the sprawling roots of a banyan tree, they were completely unaware that they had entered the territory of a terrifying, man-eating Rakshasa named Hidimba. 

Scenting human flesh from miles away, the famished cannibal sent his fierce sister, Hidimbaa, to kill the trespassers and drag their bodies back for a feast. 

However, when Hidimbaa reached the sleeping humans, her eyes fell upon Bheema, who was keeping watch over his family. Instantly struck by his broad, leonine shoulders and radiant power, she fell hopelessly in love. Using her magical powers, the Rakshasi transformed herself from a terrifying demoness into a breathtakingly beautiful human maiden. 

Approaching Bheema, she warned him of her brother's evil intent. "I have chosen you as my husband," she pleaded. "I shall save you from the Rakshasa. Please come with me."

"I am not afraid of any Rakshasa," Bheema replied confidently, refusing to abandon his sleeping mother and brothers or even wake them. "I shall slay him myself."

Tired of waiting for his meal, the colossal Hidimba crashed through the forest. When he saw his sister transformed into a beautiful woman and speaking affectionately with his prey, he flew into a murderous rage. "You traitor!" he roared, lunging at her with the intent to kill. 

Bheema smoothly intercepted the giant. "Strike me first!" he challenged with a laugh. Not wanting to disturb his family's much-needed rest, Bheema grabbed the roaring Rakshasa and dragged him away from the camp, pulling the massive creature as easily as a lion drags a small animal. 

A brutal, earth-shattering wrestling match ensued. They uprooted trees and smashed boulders until the tremendous noise finally woke the other Pandavas. Seeing the stunning Hidimbaa standing nearby, Kunti asked who she was. Hidimbaa quickly explained her identity and her love for Bheema. 

Arjuna rushed forward to help his brother, but Bheema waved him off. "Stand back! I will kill him with my bare hands."

With a sudden burst of immense strength, Bheema lifted the giant Rakshasa high into the air, whirled him around a hundred times, and violently dashed him to the ground, instantly killing the cannibal. 

With the threat eliminated, the Pandavas prepared to resume their journey. Hidimbaa threw herself at Kunti’s feet. "I have forsaken my friends, my tribe, and my tradition for your son," she wept. "I beg you to accept me. I shall carry you on my back across all obstacles and fly you through the skies."

Moved by her devotion and the fact that she had saved them from her brother, Yudhishthira agreed to the marriage, but with a strict condition: she could spend the days with Bheema, showing him the beautiful mountains and forests, but she must return him to the Pandava camp every night. 

Bheema and Hidimbaa were married. They spent their days blissfully flying over snow-capped mountains, crystal lakes, and lush forests. In due time, Hidimbaa gave birth to a mighty son. 

The child was born incredibly strong, resembling a young man the moment he was born. Because his large, bald head was completely smooth and shaped like a water-pot (Ghatam), his mother named him Ghatotkacha. Though born to a Rakshasa, Ghatotkacha possessed divine, superhuman strength and was utterly devoted to his father's family, completely lacking the cruelty of his mother's tribe. 

He quickly mastered all martial arts under the Pandavas' guidance. However, the time soon came when Hidimbaa realized her permitted stay with Bheema was over. Demonstrating true devotion, she respectfully saluted Kunti and the Pandavas and flew away into the sky. 

Ghatotkacha also bowed to his family. "I shall return whenever you need me," the loyal son promised before departing northward to rule his own realm.

Shortly after, Sage Veda Vyasa appeared before the Pandavas to comfort them. He guided them to the peaceful town of Ekachakra, advising them to live quietly in disguise as poor Brahmanas to ensure their survival and prepare for their eventual glorious return.
