# Princess Sukanya

Long, long ago, in a dense jungle on the outskirts of King Sharyaati's vast kingdom, there lived a great ascetic named Sage Chyavana. He spent his days and nights deeply immersed in severe penances and austerities near a beautiful, serene lake. 

As years rolled into decades, Sage Chyavana remained entirely motionless in his deep meditation. Unaware of the passing time, a massive anthill slowly built up around his stationary body, eventually covering him completely. From the outside, there was absolutely no sign of a human being inside the mound of earth. 

King Sharyaati had many beautiful daughters, but he loved Princess Sukanya the most. One bright, sunny day, the king, Sukanya, and a large retinue of royal attendants decided to visit the beautiful forest lake for a leisurely picnic. 

While the others relaxed by the water, the curious young Princess Sukanya wandered off into her favorite grove. Suddenly, a strange heap of mud caught her attention. As she stepped closer, she noticed two bright, shining objects deeply embedded within the dark earth of the anthill. 

"Could they be fireflies?" she wondered, completely fascinated. "I'll drive them out and catch them. The others would be delighted to see them."

Picking up two sharp, sturdy twigs from the ground, the innocent princess forcefully poked both the shining objects simultaneously. 

Instantly, a horrifying, agonizing cry of pain erupted from within the anthill! 

Terrified out of her wits, Sukanya dropped the twigs and ran for her life, rushing blindly back to the royal camp. But an even stranger, more horrifying sight awaited her there. The king's soldiers, ministers, and attendants were all collapsing to the ground, writhing in sudden, inexplicable, and excruciating physical pain. 

King Sharyaati was completely perplexed by this sudden, invisible scourge that had struck his men. "We were near the hermitage of Sage Chyavana when we went to the lake," the king realized with a sinking heart. "Has anyone offended the great sage? Find out immediately!"

Hearing her father's desperate questions, a trembling, tearful Sukanya came forward. "Father... I was curious," she sobbed. "I saw an anthill with two bright objects that looked like fireflies. I poked at them with twigs. Suddenly, from the anthill came a terrible cry of pain..."

The king was absolutely horrified. "You have blinded the great Sage Chyavana!" he cried in despair. 

Without wasting a single moment, the king rushed into the forest and approached the anthill, falling to his knees before the bleeding, blinded sage. 
"Pardon me! O great one, pardon me!" the king pleaded. "I have not cursed anybody," the agonizing sage replied weakly. "You are reaping the fruits of your daughter's terrible actions. She has destroyed my meditation and blinded me forever."

"How can we make amends for this great sin? Command me! Anything! Anything!" the desperate king begged. 

Sage Chyavana sighed heavily. "I will need someone to look after me, blinded and helpless as I am. I shall send a hundred men to serve you," the king offered eagerly. 
"I will not have servants," the sage interrupted firmly. "I want your daughter, Sukanya, to serve me. Think about it and let me know."

King Sharyaati returned to the palace with a heavy, broken heart. How could he possibly send his favorite, beautiful, young daughter to serve a frail, blind, and elderly sage for the rest of her life? But if he refused, his entire kingdom would suffer the terrible consequences of the unlifted curse. 

Seeing her beloved father's unbearable distress, Princess Sukanya made a courageous, selfless decision. She walked proudly into the grand royal assembly, stood before the painful, writhing courtiers, and raised her hands in prayer. 

"In the name of Goddess Durga, I vow that I shall marry only Sage Chyavana!" Sukanya declared in a clear, ringing voice. "If my words be true, let all be relieved of the scourge!"

Instantly, the terrible, invisible pain miraculously vanished from the bodies of the royal attendants. A collective sigh of immense relief swept through the palace. 

True to her solemn oath, the young princess willingly left the luxurious comforts of her royal home. Adorned in simple garments, she was married to the blind, elderly sage. King Sharyaati returned to his palace with a heavy, sorrowing heart, leaving his precious daughter to her harsh new life. 

But Sukanya never once complained. She completely dedicated herself to the service of her blind husband. She woke before dawn, cheerfully fetched fresh water from the lake, meticulously picked flowers for his daily rituals, cooked his simple meals, and carefully guided his every step, becoming the absolute light of his darkened world. 

So the years passed. One day, as Sukanya was returning home from the lake after a bath, she was suddenly approached by two spectacularly handsome, glowing youths. They were the Ashwini twins—the divine, celestial physicians of the gods. 

"Fair maiden, tell us who you are," the twins asked. "Are you a goddess or a human being? Are you alone in this lonely forest?"
"I am Sukanya, the wife of Sage Chyavana," she replied modestly. 

The twins laughed in utter disbelief. "Surely your father must be a cruel man to have given you in marriage to a blind, old sage! You are wasting your youth. Leave that old man and marry one of us, and be truly happy!"

Sukanya's eyes flashed with fierce, furious indignation. "How dare you speak these words to a chaste woman!" she flared. "Gods you claim to be. Go away from here before I curse you!"

Impressed by her unshakeable, fierce loyalty, the Ashwini twins smiled. "Pray, wait! We merely wanted to test your absolute devotion. As a reward, we are eager to do you a good turn. We shall restore your husband's lost vision... and give him back his youth, too!"

Sukanya gasped in sheer hope. "But...?"
"But there is a test," the twins continued mischievously. "After giving him back his youth and vision, we shall make him look exactly like us. All three of us will be identical. You must successfully spot your true husband among us. If you fail, you must agree to marry one of us. Will you accept this test?"

Sukanya immediately rushed back to the ashrama and explained the incredible offer to her blind husband. 
"Advise me, my lord. Shall I submit myself to this dangerous test of the gods?" she asked anxiously. 
Sage Chyavana smiled warmly. "Sukanya! You have all my blessings. Lead me to the place."

Sukanya gently guided her husband back to the edge of the sparkling lake. Without a word of hesitation, the blind, elderly sage waded into the deep waters alongside the two glowing, celestial twins. 

As soon as the three figures were completely submerged, they suddenly vanished under the rippling water. Sukanya waited anxiously on the bank, her heart pounding in her chest. 

A few moments later, the water broke. Sukanya gasped in absolute astonishment. Wading toward her from the depths of the lake were three spectacularly handsome, powerfully built, radiant young men. They were completely identical in every single physical detail—their faces, their height, their bright eyes, and their glowing smiles were indistinguishable from one another. 

"Can you spot me?" all three voices asked in perfect unison. 

Sukanya stared intensely at the three identical faces. Panic momentarily gripped her heart. *If I choose the wrong one, I lose my husband forever,* she thought. 

Then, she remembered an ancient, sacred truth she had once learned about the gods. She looked carefully, scrutinizing their faces one by one. Suddenly, she noticed a tiny, crucial detail. 

The eyes of the two men on the outside remained perfectly, unnervingly still. But the eyes of the handsome young man standing exactly in the center... *blinked*. 

"Gods do not blink," she realized with a surge of triumphant joy. 

Without a moment's hesitation, she stepped forward and joyfully threw her arms around the man in the center. "This is my husband!" she declared proudly. 

Her brilliant guess was absolutely right! The Ashwini twins smiled, deeply moved by her incredible intelligence and unwavering devotion. 

"Sukanya, you, a chaste and brilliant woman, have humbled the very gods!" the twins blessed her warmly. "Your glory shall live forever." With a flash of divine light, the celestial physicians departed, returning to the heavens. 

Sage Chyavana, now completely restored to vigorous, radiant youth and perfect vision, embraced his brilliant wife. They happily returned to their ashrama, their lives transformed forever. 

"I have everything a wife could ever wish for," Sukanya sighed happily, looking at her handsome husband. "There is only one thing left... I long to see my father."

She did not have to wait long. Having heard rumors of a miraculous transformation in the forest, King Sharyaati personally arrived at the ashrama with his royal retinue. When he saw his beloved daughter standing happily beside a radiant, handsome young sage, the king wept tears of sheer, overwhelming joy. 

"Sukanya, you have made me proud!" the king declared, embracing her tightly. "Nay! You have made all womankind proud! As long as the sun and the moon exist, let her be known forever as *Sati Sukanya*—the ultimate symbol of devotion and absolute purity."
