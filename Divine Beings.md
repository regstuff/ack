# Divine Beings: Airavata, Jatayu, Nandi & Others

## Jatayu
Many stories are told about the great vulture, Jatayu. He was the son of Aruna, the Sun God's charioteer, and the nephew of Garuda. He was a bird of majestic proportions, and when he spread his wings, it seemed as though they covered the whole sky. 
Even as hatchlings, Jatayu and his older brother Sampati loved flying at dizzying heights. They would often fly up to meet their father. "My sons, you must not come near us," Aruna warned. "It's dangerous. You must not fly so close to Surya unless you want to be scorched."

But as they grew older, they raced each other higher and higher. One day, they flew dangerously close to the sun. "I can hear the sound of Surya's horses. The chariot will be here any moment... it's too late to fly away... we'll be burnt to death!" Jatayu panicked. 
With a final burst of speed, Sampati flew over Jatayu, stretching his wings wide to shield his beloved brother. The flames ate up Sampati's wings, and he hurtled to the ground in pain, having lost the power of flight. Jatayu also fell, exhausted. Sampati fell on Mount Mahendra, where he was cared for by Sage Nishakara, while Jatayu fell in the Panchavati forest. The brothers never met again. 

Years later, King Dasharatha of Ayodhya was chasing Shani (Saturn) across the sky to stop a terrible famine. Shani's fiery gaze set Dasharatha's chariot on fire, and the king fell. Jatayu swooped down and caught the king, saving his life. "I am indebted to you for life," Dasharatha thanked him, and they became close friends. 

Years after Dasharatha's death, his sons Rama and Lakshmana, along with Rama's wife Sita, were exiled to the Panchavati forest. Jatayu met them and promised, "I will always be there for you and your family as long as I have breath in my body."
One day, the demon king Ravana abducted Sita in his flying chariot while the brothers were away. Jatayu heard Sita's cries and attacked Ravana. He fought bravely with his talons and beak, destroying Ravana's chariot. But Ravana was too powerful, and he mercilessly sliced off Jatayu's wings. 

When Rama and Lakshmana returned and found Sita missing, they searched the forest and found the dying Jatayu. "Forgive me, Rama. I tried my best to save Sita, but he was too powerful for me. Ravana took her south," Jatayu gasped before breathing his last. 
Deeply moved by the noble bird's sacrifice, Rama and Lakshmana built a pyre and performed the last rites for Jatayu, granting his soul salvation.

## Nandi
Sage Shilada had been praying to Shiva for many years in deep meditation for a child. Pleased, Shiva appeared and granted him a son who glowed like the sun. He named him Nandi. 
Nandi grew up to be very bright, memorising all the shlokas. One day, two sages, Mitra and Varuna, visited Shilada. "Your son does not have long to live," they prophesied. 

Heartbroken, Shilada wept. But Nandi sat on the banks of the river Bhuvana and meditated on Shiva. Shiva appeared before him. 
"I wish to be by your side forever," Nandi asked. 
Shiva blessed Nandi, transforming the boy into a divine, immortal bull. He took Nandi to his home on Mount Kailash, making him his vehicle and closest aide. 

Once, when the gods and asuras churned the ocean to obtain Amrita, a deadly poison called Halahala spewed out. To protect the world, Shiva drank the poison. As he drank, some of the poison fell from his hands. Before it could touch the ground and destroy the world, Nandi quickly drank it. Miraculously, it didn't harm him at all. 

Nandi's devotion was absolute. One day, Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka, came to Mount Kailash and tried to force his way in while Shiva and Parvati were resting. Nandi blocked his path. 
"You fool! How dare you insult me!" Ravana sneered, mocking Nandi's monkey-like face (in some versions). 
"Your arrogance will be your end. You will one day be defeated by monkeys," Nandi cursed him. Nandi's curse came to pass when an army of monkeys led by Rama defeated Ravana. 
Every Shiva temple has a statue of Nandi directly in front of the main sanctum, guarding his Lord through eternity.

## Shyama and Sabala
A group of asuras called the Kalakanjas were trying to get to heaven by building a massive brick altar. "We will build an altar all the way up to Indra's kingdom!" they boasted. 
The gods were worried. Indra disguised himself as a laborer and went down to the earth. He picked up a brick and placed it on the altar. "I hope this works," he thought. 

The altar grew taller and taller. Eventually, the stairway to heaven was ready. As the asuras excitedly began to climb, Indra watched and waited till they had climbed high enough. 
Then, Indra pulled his brick out. The structure lost its balance and came tumbling down. As the asuras fell towards the ground, they started turning into spiders. 
The two asuras who were almost at the very top turned into dogs. They came to be known as Shyama and Sabala, representing dusk and dawn. Yama, the god of death, made them the eternal guards of his gates.

## Airavata
Before earth existed, there was endless water. Out of this expanse, a golden egg came into being. The egg split and Brahma emerged from it. He created heaven, earth, and creatures of all sizes. 
Among them were sixteen majestic winged elephants. Brahma declared the magnificent four-tusked white elephant, Airavata, as the leader. Brahma decreed that the elephants would guard the eight directions. Airavata and his mate Abhramu were assigned to guard the East with Indra. They roamed the skies and helped form the rain clouds. 

One day, the venerable sage Dirghatapas was meditating in the shade of a large banyan tree. A winged elephant rested on a branch, which gave way with a loud crack, sending the elephant tumbling down near the sage. 
Furious at the disturbance, the sage cursed them. "You, and all the other elephants, will lose your wings. You will never be able to fly again."
And so, elephants began to live in forests on earth. 

In time, Indra came to depend on Airavata as his mount. During a fierce battle against the powerful asura Surapadma, Indra's son Jayanta was struck down. Seeing this, Airavata charged angrily at Surapadma's chariot and smashed it to pieces. But Surapadma struck back, cutting off two of Airavata's tusks and throwing him to the earth below. 

Lost and defeated, Airavata prayed to Shiva. Touched by his devotion, Shiva appeared and not only sent Airavata back to Indraloka but also restored his tusks. 

*(Another legend says that Airavata was born from Iravati, a descendant of Kashyapa. Yet another version from the Mahabharata says Airavata emerged as one of the divine gifts during the churning of the Ocean of Milk).*

## Gandaberunda and Sharabha
The evil king Hiranyakashipu had just been slain by Vishnu's fourth incarnation, Narasimha. However, the killing had given Narasimha a taste for blood, and he lost sight of his purpose, continuing on a fearful rampage. 
The terrified gods went to Shiva for help. Shiva took the form of Sharabha, a fearsome half-bird, half-lion creature, to subdue Narasimha. 

But the act angered Narasimha. From his body emerged a creature bigger and more fearsome than Sharabha - Gandaberunda, a massive two-headed bird of unimaginable strength. 
The two mighty beasts fought a terrible battle for eighteen days, destroying everything in their wake. Finally, seeing the world reduced to ashes, they stopped. A calm and peaceful Vishnu emerged from the fearsome bird, and Shiva resumed his form. Peace was finally restored. 

*(Gandaberunda is the official emblem of the Government of Karnataka, originally the royal insignia of the Wodeyars of Mysore).*

## Navagunjara
This fascinating mythical being finds its mention in Oriya folklore based on the Mahabharata. Once when Arjuna was out hunting, he came across a creature beyond his imagination. It was made up of nine animals! This magnificent creature had the head of a rooster, the neck of a peacock, the hump of a bull, the waist of a lion, legs of a human, an elephant, a deer and a tiger, and with a snake as its tail. 
Though initially frightened, Arjuna soon realised that such a being could only be a manifestation of the divine Krishna. Arjuna laid down his weapons and saluted the wondrous creature.

## Vahanas - Companions of the Gods
The gods in Indian mythology have vahanas or vehicles on which they ride. These represent the strength and beauty of the deity and at times symbolise various concepts.
- **Durga** travels on a fierce tiger.
- **Yamuna** is depicted riding a tortoise.
- **Saraswati** rides a swan.
- **Varuna** (Lord of waters) rides Jaladhi (Makara), a creature with the body of a crocodile and the trunk of an elephant. 
- **Shani** rides a crow.
- **Surya** rides a chariot drawn by seven white horses.
- **Lakshmi** rides an owl (Uluka) or sometimes an elephant.
- **Kaalratri** (a form of Kali) rides a donkey.
- **Vayu** (wind god) rides a deer.
- **Yama** rides Paundraka, a black buffalo.
- **Kubera** (god of wealth) has a man for a vahana.
- **Agni** (fire god) rides a ram.
- **Kama** and Rati ride a parrot (or sometimes a pigeon). 
- **Ashwini Kumaras** ride horses or a donkey.

## Ananta
Kadru and Vinata, wives of Kashyapa, placed a wager over the colour of the divine horse Uchhaisravas's tail (or Airavata's tail in some versions). To win by deceit, Kadru ordered her serpent sons to hang from the tail to make it look black. 
Ananta and a few others refused to be part of this dishonest act. Enraged, Kadru cursed them to die in King Janmejaya's snake sacrifice. 
Perturbed, Ananta sought help from Brahma. Brahma told him to go to the netherworld and support Mother Earth on his hood. Ananta readily agreed. He carries the entire world on his hood, maintaining its balance, and forms the mighty coils on which Lord Vishnu rests.

## Kamadhenu
Kamadhenu is the caring and majestic mother of all cattle in the world. Once, on seeing how hard her children on earth were being made to work, she wept with sorrow. 
Indra saw her weeping and asked what the matter was. She told him that her children were being beaten and overworked. Touched by her sorrow, Indra commanded the heavens to open and the rain to fall. It poured for days, making it impossible for the fields to be ploughed, giving Kamadhenu's children some much-needed rest. Kamadhenu lives in Goloka.
