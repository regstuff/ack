# How the Jackal Ate the Elephant

Mahachaturaka, the jackal, was very happy. He had found a dead elephant in the middle of the jungle. "Enough food for weeks!" he rejoiced. 

But as he looked closer at the massive carcass, he realized a problem. "But I'm going to need help to cut into his thick hide and reach the flesh," he muttered, scratching his chin.

At that moment, a mighty lion happened to come by. The jackal quickly bowed low. "I'd better offer him the elephant and win his favour," he thought. 
"My lord," the jackal said loudly, "your humble servant has been guarding this dead elephant for you."

The lion frowned. "I eat only what I have killed. Don't I know that!" he thought. To the jackal, he declared, "I gift this elephant to you, my loyal servant."
"Just as I had expected," the jackal smirked as the lion walked away. "But how am I to get to the flesh of the elephant?"

As he wondered what to do, a fierce tiger came by.
"Oh! Oh! I sent off one fellow by pretending to be humble," the jackal panicked. "How shall I send this one packing? He's not one to be flattered. I'll have to think fast. Cunning! That's it! I'll resort to cunning!"

"Uncle!" the jackal cried out, running towards the tiger in mock terror. "It's dangerous for you to be here! The lion who killed this elephant has gone to bathe, leaving me here to guard it. He has sworn to kill every tiger he meets in this forest!"
He added slyly, "He hates tigers. One of them once ate the elephant he had killed and he had to eat the leavings."

The tiger froze, his eyes wide. "Thank you for warning me, dear nephew. Don't tell him you saw me. I'm off!" And the mighty tiger ran for his life.
"Ha! Ha! Ha! Ho! Look at him run," the jackal laughed. "But I still haven't got to the flesh of this elephant!"

Just then, he saw a leopard coming towards him.
"That's the fellow I'll use!" the jackal thought. "He has strong teeth and will cut through the hide in no time. Then I'll send him packing."

"Well, well, my nephew! You seem hungry," the jackal greeted the leopard.
"I am. That's a fine elephant you have there," the leopard eyed the carcass.
"Isn't it? It was killed by a lion who left me here to guard it. But you may eat some of it before he gets back, if you're quick enough," the jackal offered generously.

"A lion, did you say? No! No!" the leopard stepped back nervously. "This meat would be the death of me, if I don't escape in time!"
"Come on. Be bold and eat," the jackal coaxed. "I'll keep watch and warn you if I see him coming."

The leopard needed no more coaxing. He jumped onto the elephant and began to tear away furiously at the thick hide. As soon as the hide was cut through, the jackal shouted, "Here comes the lion. Quick! Run off!"
The leopard did not even stop to look up. Turning on his heels, he ran for his life.

The jackal was just about to feed on the exposed flesh when another jackal came by.
"Oh! This one is my equal," Mahachaturaka thought. "I'll fight him off."
Baring his fangs, he charged at the newcomer, fighting fiercely and chasing away the unwelcome guest.

"All this meat! All for myself! I need not look for food for weeks," the clever jackal rejoiced, finally digging into his hard-earned meal.

**Moral: Mighty brawn is no match against nimble brain.**


# The Frog King and the Snake

Gangadatta was the king of the frogs who lived in a deep well. Despite his royal title, he could not get along with some of his relatives because they often treated him badly and mocked his authority. 

One day, furious after another insult, he turned to his wife. "How dare they treat me, their king, in this way! I must teach them a lesson."
"Take care, dear husband," his wife warned gently, "that in trying to harm them you don't get us into trouble."

But, ignoring her advice, Gangadatta leapt from the pail attached to the water-wheel... and rode it out of the well. Just as he landed outside, he saw Priyadarshana, the snake, slithering into his hole.

"Ah! I'll ask him to be my guest and eat my wicked relatives," the frog king plotted. He hopped over to the hole. "Hey, Priyadarshana! Come out."

Inside, the snake was puzzled. "That's no snake calling me. And I don't have any friends apart from snakes. Perhaps it's a snake charmer!" 

"Come out, Priyadarshana," the frog called again. "I am Gangadatta, the frog-king. I want to make friends with you."
"Impossible!" the snake replied from the darkness. "Can hay ever make friends with fire? What you say makes no sense."

"I agree that we are born enemies," Gangadatta admitted. "Nevertheless, I need your help. I want you to eat my enemies."
"Who are these enemies?" the snake asked, intrigued.
"My own relatives. They live in the same well as I do."

"Oh! You live in a well!" the snake scoffed. "How am I to get in? And even if I did, where would I perch while killing your enemies? Get away with you!"
"Wait, my friend," the frog urged. "I'll show you how to get into the well. And I'll also show you a comfortable hole, a little above the water, where you can rest. Please come out."

The snake emerged and fell deep in thought. "I am no longer as young as I used to be. Once in a while I catch a mouse, and that too with difficulty. His offer is tempting." 

"Well, Gangadatta," the snake finally agreed, "I have decided to come with you! Lead and I'll follow."
"Thank you, my friend," the frog smiled. "But there's one thing. You must spare my own family and eat only those whom I tell you to eat."
"You are my friend now, Gangadatta. I wouldn't touch your family," the snake promised solemnly.

When they reached the edge of the well, the frog jumped onto the water-wheel. "Follow me. It's very easy." The snake slithered down the rope. 
"That's the hole," the frog pointed. "Settle yourself there."

When the snake was comfortably settled, Gangadatta pointed out his targets. "There! Those are the frogs that torment me."

One by one, over the next few days, the snake ate up all the enemies of the frog-king. 
When Gangadatta came to see him, the snake said, "I've eaten all your enemies!"
"Good! Now you may return to your hole, the way you came, my friend," the frog king declared.

"Return to my hole? You can't be serious," the snake hissed. "Some other snake would have moved into it the very day I left. No, my friend, I will have to stay here. And since you took me out of my hole, it is your duty to feed me." 
The snake's eyes narrowed. "You must give me one frog at a time, from your friends and your own family. If you don't, I'll eat you all up."

"What a fool I have been!" Gangadatta wept to himself. "Why did I ever bring him here? Now I have no choice but to give him a frog every day."

The snake, however, not only ate the frog sent to him... but another too behind the frog-king's back. 
One day, the extra frog he ate was Gangadatta's own son; and Gangadatta caught him in the act.
"No! No! Not that one, my friend. It's my son!" the king cried.

But it was too late. All Gangadatta's wailing could not bring his son back.
"What's the use of wailing now?" his wife scolded him through her tears. "Who is there to help you? You wanted to destroy your own kin! You'd better escape from here or think of a plot to kill him."

The days went by. Gangadatta had no plan, and eventually, all the frogs in the well were eaten. All but himself.

"Dear Gangadatta, I'm hungry," the snake complained one morning. "Please find me something to eat. It's your duty to do so."
"This is my chance to escape," the frog thought quickly. 
"My friend, as long as I'm alive you won't go hungry," Gangadatta said aloud. "Permit me to leave this well and I'll bring you all the frogs from other wells."
"You, who have been like a brother to me, I'll never eat now," the snake said fondly. "If you do as you promise, you will be like a father to me."

Not wasting another moment, the frog escaped from the well. 

Meanwhile, the snake waited in vain for his return. "I'd better find myself another well to live in," he sighed, realizing he had been tricked. "I should not have let him go."

Many days later, the old snake turned to a lizard who lived in the same well. "Madam, you and Gangadatta are old friends. Please find him and ask him to return quickly. Never mind if he can't get other frogs to come. Tell him that I will not hurt him; that I can't live without him."

After hunting in all the neighbouring wells, the lizard at last found the frog-king. 
"Dear Gangadatta, what are you doing here?" the lizard asked. "Your friend, Priyadarshana, is anxiously awaiting your return. He promises not to harm you. So come home."

Gangadatta shook his head firmly. "A starving man is not to be trusted. I've learnt my lesson. He will never see me again."

**Moral: Don't cut off your nose to spite your face.**


# The Lion, the Jackal and the Donkey

In a jungle, there once lived a powerful lion who had a cunning jackal for a servant. Whenever the lion killed an animal, he would first have his fill... and leave the rest for the jackal.

One day, the lion made the mistake of attacking a fierce king-elephant. The elephant fought back and wounded him so badly that he could barely walk. 

For a week, both master and servant starved. At last, the lion had an idea.
"If you can bring some animal to me which I can kill without much effort, we won't have to starve," the lion told his servant.

The jackal slowly rose to his feet and set out. He soon spotted a deer. "That's a fine deer, but too fast for my wounded master," he thought. 

A few hours later, he saw a donkey grazing by a river. "Ah! A donkey! Just the animal I am looking for!"

"Good day uncle! Why do you look so feeble?" the jackal asked sweetly.
"How else would I look, dear nephew?" the donkey sighed. "I have a cruel, miserly dhobi for a master. I am overworked and underfed. Not a handful of rich fodder have I eaten in ages! All that I live on is this dry grass."
"If that is so, why don't you come and live with me in the forest?" the jackal invited. "There's plenty of rich, green grass near my cave."

"It is very kind of you to invite me," the donkey hesitated. "But I am a village dweller. The wild animals of the jungle would soon kill me."
"You don't have to worry about that, uncle. No one dares to come near my cave. They fear my powerful paws and claws." 
The jackal leaned in closer and whispered, "Do you know, three she-donkeys who were tortured like you by their masters are now living under my protection."

"She-donkeys?" the donkey perked up.
"Yes. Three of them! They have grown plump on the rich, green grass and now want to get married. In fact, I have come in search of a husband for them. That's why I want to take you back with me."

Hearts floating in his eyes, the foolish donkey eagerly agreed. "Lead me to them, dear nephew. I too am looking for a bride."

And the poor donkey followed the jackal into the jungle. 
As they approached the cave, the starving lion saw them. "Food at last!" he thought excitedly. "What was that?" the donkey wondered as he heard a rustle.

The lion leaped, but in his eager and weakened state, he misjudged his jump. "The hasty fool!" the jackal cursed as the lion missed completely.
The terrified donkey took one look at the ferocious lion... and ran for his life!

The jackal turned on his master. "A stupid donkey and you couldn't kill him! O master, how did you dare attack an elephant?"
"It's not my fault. I wasn't ready for him. I didn't expect you to return so soon," the lion defended himself, embarrassed. "Bring him back! I'll be ready now."
"Bring him back? Impossible!" the jackal scoffed. "He saw me and ran away. You'll have to bring some other animal."
"I will bring back that very donkey," the jackal challenged. "Be ready for him this time."

When the jackal went back to the riverbank, the donkey was furious. 
"So you're back! A fine spot you took me to! It's my luck that I escaped from that horrible creature!"

The jackal simply laughed. "Uncle, that was a lovesick she-donkey. When she saw you, she sprang forward to welcome you. But you were shy and ran away. You must come back and marry her. If you don't, she says she'll starve herself to death."
"She can't bear to be separated from you," the jackal continued his lies. "So have mercy on her and return. If you don't, you will be guilty of killing a lady and Kamadeva (the god of love) will be angry with you."

Believing all that the jackal said, the foolish donkey went back with him to the jungle. 

When they were near enough, the lion—wondering at the marvel of the jackal bringing him back—leaped perfectly. "This time I won't fail him."

The lion struck true, and the donkey was killed instantly. 
"Didn't I tell you I would succeed if I were ready?" the lion boasted. "Now guard this donkey while I go to bathe."

"I cannot wait till he returns," the hungry jackal thought as the lion left. "While he is away, I'll eat up the donkey's heart and ears."

When the lion returned, he began sniffing at the donkey. Suddenly, he began to roar. "You scoundrel! You have eaten the ears and heart! Am I to eat your leavings?"

But the jackal was not frightened. Calmly he faced the lion.
"O king, this creature was born without ears or heart. If not, would he have come here, heard your roar, run away in terror... and then come back again? What he says must be true, or else why should the donkey have come back?" the lion pondered.

"All right," the lion grumbled. "I shall first have my fill of this donkey. You may then eat the rest."

**Moral: Don't lose your head in the face of calamities and you'll overcome them.**


# The Dhobi's Donkey

Shuddhaphata, a poor dhobi (washerman), loved his donkey but could not afford to feed it well. 

One day, as he was returning home through a thick jungle, the donkey stumbled under its heavy load, so weak had it become from starvation.
"My poor feeble beast! If only I could give you better fodder!" the washerman cried, trying to help it up.

As he looked around the jungle, something caught his eye. "What's that? Oh! A dead tiger! Thank god it wasn't a live one!"
He was about to walk on, but suddenly stopped, struck by a brilliant idea. "That's it! I'll flay this fellow and take the skin home. My donkey will no longer lack food."

He quickly skinned the tiger and returned home. "You will soon become a fearful tiger, my gentle donkey, and eat all the millet you want," the dhobi smiled.

That night, the dhobi draped the tiger skin over the donkey. "There! Now, in this garb, go into the millet fields at night." 

The dhobi led him to the edge of the rich fields. "Go, my pet, and eat to your heart's content. I'll come back for you in the morning."
An hour later, when the farmer and his men came to make their usual rounds, they panicked. "There's an animal in the millet field! It's a tiger! Run!"

And the disguised donkey munched away completely undisturbed. 
In the morning, the dhobi led him safely home. This went on for many days. 

"You've grown so plump, my donkey," the dhobi chuckled, patting his well-fed beast. "If you grow any plumper, you will not be able to enter your stall."

One night, the farmers spotted the 'tiger' again. "There he is again! What shall we do? We're helpless," they lamented, too scared to approach.
Just then, the donkey heard a familiar sound in the distance—the bray of a she-donkey. "EE-AW! EE-AW!"

Overjoyed and completely forgetting his disguise, the plump donkey threw back his head and replied loudly, "EE-AW! EE-AW!"

The farmers stopped in their tracks. "We've been duped," one of them growled, gripping his stick. "It's only a donkey in disguise!"
The angry farmer and his men charged at the donkey and beat him to death for eating their crops.

In the morning, the dhobi was shocked to find his beloved donkey dead in the field. "Alas, my friend! How did it happen?" he wept.

**Moral: Silence is golden.**


# The Lioness and the Jackal Cub

A lioness once gave birth to two healthy cubs. For a time, she could not go out hunting to feed her family, so her husband, a mighty lion, went out every day to bring home the game he killed.

One day, the lion could not find a single animal to kill. As he was returning home empty-handed and frustrated, he spotted a tiny creature near the path. "What's that? A jackal cub?" he wondered.

He raised his huge paw to strike it, when pity for the tiny, helpless creature overcame him. "No! He's just a cub," the lion thought. 
Picking the cub up gently with his teeth, he took it home alive. 

When he reached their cave, the lioness asked, "What have you brought today?"
"I couldn't find a single animal. Then I saw this cub," the lion explained. "I didn't have the heart to kill him. But you may kill and eat him if you like."
"When you didn't have the heart to kill him, how can I?" the motherly lioness replied. "He shall grow up as my third son."

The three cubs soon grew plump and frisky, playing happily together every day. 

One day, while the three brothers were exploring near their den, they saw a huge elephant approaching. 
"Who is that intruder? Come, brothers, let's go and attack him!" the two young lion cubs roared bravely, getting ready to charge.

But the young jackal panicked. "Wait, brothers. Don't! That's an elephant, an enemy of our race. Let's run away!" 
The two young lions burst out laughing. "Run away? Ha! Ha! What's so funny? I'm going to run away. Let them follow if they want to," the jackal thought angrily, slinking back to the den.

Later at the den, the lion cubs excitedly told their parents about the encounter. "...And, mother, it was so funny! Ho! Ho! The way he put his tail between his legs. Ha! Ha! Ho! Ho! And ran for his life... Ha! Ha!"

The jackal cub was furious. "You are fools!" he snapped at the lion cubs. "I should have let that elephant kill you. I wish I hadn't warned you. I..."
"Calm down, my son. Let us go outside. I want to talk to you," the lioness interrupted gently.

When they were alone, she said, "You must never again speak like that to them. They are your older brothers."
"So what?" the jackal bristled. "Do you think I am inferior to them in any way? Why do they make fun of me? I'm going to kill them!"

The lioness held back the smile that came to her lips. *Poor cub. I will have to tell him the truth—before it's too late.*

"My cub, you are the son of a jackal," she revealed gently. "I brought you up because you were helpless. As long as my sons are cubs, they will not harm you. Run away and join your own pack before they know you to be a jackal. If you don't, my sons will sooner or later fight you and kill you."

The poor jackal was so terrified when he heard this, that without a word he slunk away into the forest to find his own pack.

**Moral: You are best off with your own kind.**
