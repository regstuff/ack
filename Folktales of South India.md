# Folktales of South India

## The King Who Stopped the River
Once upon a time, there was a foolish king who had a wise Diwan. One warm summer night, the king could not sleep. Hearing the gong sound every hour made him restless. At six o'clock, he sat up. "I'll have to get up now. But I feel so dull. I think I'll ride out into the countryside and get some fresh air."

So the king and his wise Diwan set out. After an hour's ride, the king felt thirsty. "Let's stop by that river. Such clear, sparkling water! Where does this river flow?"
"It flows down to that kingdom in the east, your majesty," the Diwan replied. 
"Our river flowing into their country? We must stop it at once!" the king commanded. "I want a dam built here."

The dam was built. But since the river could not flow down its usual course, it overflowed its banks and flooded the countryside. The people were worried about the upcoming monsoon, but the king didn't care. "We've got our river all to ourselves, haven't we?" he reasoned. 
The wise Diwan knew their neighbours would soon attack them for stopping their water supply. "I must get him to break down that dam," he thought, and soon came up with a plan. 

That evening, the Diwan went to the gong tower. "After midnight, I want you to sound the gong every half-hour. Not every hour, as you do now," he instructed the guard. 
Because of the Diwan's order, the sixth gong sounded when it was actually only 3 o'clock in the morning. The guards woke up the palace. The commander rushed to the king's chamber in panic. 
"Wake up, your majesty! A terrible thing has happened! It's six o'clock but the sun hasn't risen as yet."

The king looked out of the window. "You're right. Summon the Diwan! Why hasn't the sun come up as yet?"
"I have been thinking about it, your majesty. There can be only one reason," the Diwan said seriously. "Somebody has caught the sun and is not letting it come over our land. It must be the king of the eastern kingdom, who else? I heard he was angry because we stopped the river from flowing into his country. And as you know, the sun passes over his kingdom before it comes over ours."

The king was terrified. "Your majesty, we must do something about it soon or we'll be in darkness forever," the Diwan warned. "Do you think he would let the sun go if we let the river flow into his country again?"
"What a brilliant idea, your majesty!" the Diwan praised. 
The king immediately led his men to the river and broke the dam down before dawn. As the river flowed to the neighbouring country, the sun naturally rose. 
"The sun! Look! They've let the sun go!" the king rejoiced. "Your plan worked, your majesty."
"Oh, it was nothing," the foolish king boasted, never realising how he had been fooled by his wise Diwan.

## Punyakoti
Hulia the tiger was weak with hunger. He hadn't eaten for days. One evening, he watched the cattle returning home. "I'll catch a silly cow!" he thought. 

He jumped out in front of a cow named Punyakoti. "I am going to kill you and eat you up."
"Kill me! Eat me! But not immediately," Punyakoti pleaded. "My child is waiting for me. I'll go home, feed him and come back to you."
Hulia laughed. "Do you take me for a fool? As if you'll come back, if I let you go!"
"Believe me, Hulia, I will. I give you my word with Mother Earth as a witness."
Hulia agreed to let her go. 

Punyakoti ran home and fed her calf. "Drink as much milk as you can. For this is the last time I will be feeding you. I have to go back to Hulia."
The calf begged her not to go, suggesting they avoid the tiger's route. 
"I must go. I promised him that I would return. Truth is my mother. Truth is my father. Truth is my God. Truth is everything to me," she explained. She entrusted her calf to the other cows, ignoring their pleas to stay, and went back to the tiger. 

Meanwhile, Hulia was getting impatient and didn't expect her to return. When Punyakoti appeared, he was stunned. "She has kept her promise... even though death awaits her here. What a noble creature!"
"Hulia, my brother, come! Here I am. Eat me," Punyakoti said calmly. 
"Eat you? Never, my noble sister. Never. Go back to your child," Hulia said, deeply moved. He turned away, and Punyakoti joyfully rejoined her child.

## How an Elephant was Weighed
One day a king sent for his mahout. "I want to know how much my elephant weighs, and if you don't give me the answer by tomorrow morning... I'll have you beheaded."
The mahout was in despair. In those days, there were no scales big enough to weigh an elephant. A sadhu saw him crying and offered a solution. 

"Go and bring the animal to the river," the sadhu instructed. "Now get him into that boat. That's it. Now mark the level to which the boat has sunk in the water. Now bring the elephant out and fill the boat with sand till it sinks to the mark you have made."

The mahout did as told. "Do you know why?" the sadhu smiled. "It's because the sand you have put into it is equal to the weight of the elephant. Now weigh that sand on a pair of scales and... you'll know the weight of the elephant!"
The next day, the mahout confidently told the king, "It weighs 900 pounds." The king rewarded him, and the mahout never forgot the clever sadhu.

## The Two Pandits
A king invited two scholars, Pandit Gyanraj and Pandit Vidyaraj, to his palace. They were learned men, and the king decided to reward them in a grand ceremony. However, the king realised he didn't know much about either man to decide their rewards. 

He asked Pandit Vidyaraj privately, "Please tell me all about your friend."
Vidyaraj thought, "If I praise Gyanraj, the king might give him a better reward." So he lied, "I do not like to say it, but Pandit Gyanraj is an ass! He doesn't know anything. He just repeats what he hears from me."

Next, the king asked Pandit Gyanraj the same question. Gyanraj also thought selfishly and replied, "Pandit Vidyaraj is a donkey! He doesn't know anything."

The king was disappointed. "These scholars have read many books but what is the use of all their learning? They are no better than any of us here." 
During the ceremony, servants placed a plate before each pandit. 
"What is this, your majesty? Fresh, green grass!" they exclaimed in shock. 
"You yourselves have told me that one of you is an ass and the other a donkey. So I have given you the reward asses and donkeys would like best - the finest grass that could be found in the kingdom!" the king declared. 
Ashamed of their pettiness, the pandits left the palace and were never heard from again.

## Dodda's Dubuka
Dodda was invited to his friend's house for tea and was served a delicious sweet. "I'll ask my mother to make some at home. What is it called?"
"Kadubu," his friend said. 
Dodda kept repeating "Kadubu... Kadubu..." all the way home so he wouldn't forget. Suddenly, he jumped over a stream and stubbed his toe. "Oww! Now what was that sweet called? Dubuka!" he misremembered in pain. 

He ran home crying, "Mother! I want Dubuka!" 
"Enough of your jokes! What is it?" his mother asked. 
"I want Dubuka!" Dodda insisted, annoying his mother until she slapped his face. 
"Silly boy! Now your poor face looks like a Kadubu," she scolded. 
"Kadubu! That's it! That's what I want! Kadubu!"
"Then why didn't you say so?" his mother sighed, and made him so many Kadubus that he ate nothing else for three days.

## The Heavenly Elephant
A labourer was working in the fields when he saw a magnificent flying elephant descending from the sky. "It must have come from heaven. I'll go to heaven with it!" he thought, grabbing its tail as it flew back up. 

He spent the day working in heaven and returned the next morning by holding onto the elephant's tail again. He boasted to the villagers, "It's such a wonderful place! They pay one measure of rice for every hour of work! And their measure is so large! See how much rice I got for half a day's work!"

The greedy villagers all wanted to go. The next morning, the man took them to the field. When the elephant was about to leave, the man grabbed its tail, another man grabbed his legs, and soon all the villagers formed a human chain flying towards heaven. 
"How much did you say they pay for one hour's work?" a villager asked from the bottom of the chain. 
"One measure of rice!"
"How big is the measure?"
"THIS BIG!" the man holding the tail exclaimed, enthusiastically opening his arms to show the size. 

He let go of the tail, and the entire human chain plunged into the river below. The elephant was so startled by the splash that it never returned to that village again.

## The Man Who Loved Vadas
Khandoba, a notorious miser, felt like eating vadas. His wife asked him to buy oil and dal. He went to the market to buy oil, wearing his turban. 
"Let me smell it and see if it's the best," Khandoba said, bending over the oil drum. His turban fell into the oil! 
He quickly picked it up, soaking with expensive oil, and told the shopkeeper, "Never mind. I'm in a hurry. I'll come back later for the oil."

Back home, he proudly squeezed the oil from his turban into a vessel. He then went back to the market for dal. While inspecting the dal, his turban fell into the dal sack! He picked it up, covered in dal, and ran home. He shook the dal out of his turban and gave it to his wife. "Here you are! All the dal you need."

His wife made twenty delicious, hot vadas. She tasted one. "M-m-m-m...! Delicious! I'll have just one more, while it's hot." 
When Khandoba returned, he asked for his vadas. 
"They were so tasty I ate all of them... except this one," his wife admitted. 
"You ate nineteen vadas! How could anyone eat so many vadas!" Khandoba screamed. 
"Like this!" she said, popping the last vada into her mouth. Poor Khandoba's miserly tricks left him with nothing.

## Rama to the Rescue
Rama was the village kotwal (chief of police). One night, a thief dug a hole and broke into a house in the village. The husband and wife woke up, realising the thief had bypassed Rama the Kotwal. 
"What should we do?" the husband whispered. 
"I'll tell you. Listen..." the wife replied. 

They started talking loudly. "What should we name our child? If he is a boy we'll call him Rama."
"Rama? Yes... that's a good name. When he's in the house, I'll call out softly to him. Rama! Rama!" the husband said softly. 
"But what if he's in the yard? Then I'll call out a little louder. Rama, Rama!"
"Or fall asleep at least," the thief grumbled from his hiding spot. 
"But my dear, what if the boy is not in the house or in the yard, but in the street? Oh, then I'll call out very loudly... RAMA! RAMA!" the wife screamed at the top of her lungs. 

Rama the Kotwal heard his name being shouted in the dead of night and came running with his guards. They caught the thief red-handed. The clever couple had saved themselves from being robbed.

## How the Miser Outsmarted Himself
A miserly king had a son, which meant he had to host a feast for the villagers. "And they eat so much! What should I do?" he worried. 

He came up with a cunning plan. At the feast, each guest was served just two laddoos. 
"As soon as you finish those two, you will be served another two, and so on," the king announced. "Besides, for every laddoo you eat you will be paid two rupees. But... you must eat the laddoos without bending your arm at the elbow and without putting your mouth to your plate."

The villagers were baffled, but one clever man figured it out. "I know how we can do it!" 
The villagers started feeding each other with straight arms! They ate and ate, much to the dismay of the miserly king. At the end of the feast, each guest had eaten at least fifty laddoos and earned a hundred rupees. The king tried to save money and ended up paying out a fortune.

## Words for Words
A clever city dweller was passing through a village. Seeing a rich-looking man, he thought, "The people here are so simple. I could easily make some money here if I tried."

He approached the rich man. "Oh, what a noble face you have, sir! I can tell that you are a man of great generosity."
The rich man smiled. "Wife, bring ten measures of rice for my friend here!" he called out. 
Encouraged, the city dweller continued, "Men like you are rare, sir. You shine like a jewel on a heap of coal."
"Wife, bring a hundred rupee note," the rich man ordered. 
The city dweller then recited a flattering poem. "Wife! Bring two silk dhoties," the rich man shouted. 

"How easily I have fooled this stupid fellow!" the city dweller thought. But after waiting a while, he asked, "Er... why haven't the gifts come as yet, sir?"
"Gifts? Now, what did you do with your words?" the rich man asked. 
"I... er... made you happy."
"Well, I have made you happy with mine. What more do you want?" the rich man smiled. 
The city dweller realised that the villagers were not as simple as they looked and hastily left the village.

## Raman's Cat
The emperor, Krishna Deva Raya, wanted to test his ministers' cleverness. "Can any of you bring me a cat... which runs away from milk?" he challenged. 
"No cat will ever run away from milk," the ministers murmured. 

But Tenali Raman spoke up. "I will bring you such a cat, your majesty, within 30 days."
Raman went home and adopted a cat. But instead of normal milk, he placed a saucer of boiling hot milk before it. The cat eagerly dipped its tongue into it and jumped back in pain and alarm. 
After doing this for thirty days, Raman brought the cat to the court. When presented with a bowl of normal milk, the cat, remembering the burnt tongue, ran away from it in sheer terror, winning Raman the challenge.
